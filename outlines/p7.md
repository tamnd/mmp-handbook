# Outline of P7: Intersection numbers, positivity and vanishing

This is the outline of P7 for milestone S2 (spec 15) and question Q-20 (spec 16). It lists the sections of each chapter, the page estimate of each chapter, and the place where the program volumes use the chapter. The writer of P7 starts from it. A change to the outline is a pull request to this file.

The spine texts are Lazarsfeld, *Positivity in algebraic geometry* I and II (Laz04), Debarre, *Higher-dimensional algebraic geometry*, Ch. 1 (Deb01), and Kollár-Mori, Ch. 2 (KM98). The volume needs P4, P5 Ch. 5 to 8, and P6 Ch. 6 for Ch. 8 to 11 (spec 11.1).

The rule of spec 11.4 fixes the scope. P7 works on a projective variety over an algebraically closed field, or on a smooth projective variety, and proves each result in that case. It does not treat a base $Z$, a boundary $\Delta$ or singularities worse than those of a normal variety. Those cases are in Volumes 1 and 5, which cite P7.

## The sections

### 1. Intersection numbers (40 pages)

1. Intersection numbers of Cartier divisors, defined from Euler characteristics (Snapper, Kleiman).
2. Multilinearity, symmetry and the projection formula.
3. Intersection with a subvariety and the degree of a subvariety in $\mathbb{P}^N$.
4. Asymptotic Riemann-Roch: $\chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \frac{D^n}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1})$.
5. Examples: $\mathbb{P}^n$, products, the blow-up of a point of $\mathbb{P}^n$ with $E^n = (-1)^{n-1}$, a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle.

### 2. Numerical equivalence and $N^1$, $N_1$ (35 pages)

1. Numerical equivalence of divisors and of curves.
2. $\mathbb{Q}$- and $\mathbb{R}$-divisors; $N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}$ and $N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}$; the perfect pairing.
3. The theorem of the base: $N^1(X)$ has finite dimension, the Picard number $\rho(X)$. The proof over $\mathbb{C}$ goes through $H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})$ (P6); the general proof is cited (Kleiman).
4. Pullback and pushforward of classes under a morphism.
5. Examples: $\rho$ of $\mathbb{P}^n$, of products, of $\mathbb{F}_n$, of blow-ups; $E \times E$ with and without complex multiplication.

### 3. The criteria of Nakai-Moishezon, Kleiman and Seshadri (45 pages)

1. Ample and very ample divisors again (P3 Ch. 10, P4 Ch. 2); Cartan-Serre-Grothendieck.
2. The Nakai-Moishezon criterion, with proof.
3. Nef divisors; Kleiman's theorem that $D^k \cdot V \ge 0$ for a nef $D$; amplitude is open: the ample cone is the interior of the nef cone.
4. Kleiman's criterion: $D$ is ample if and only if $D$ is positive on $\overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$. The hypothesis "projective" and Hironaka's example.
5. Seshadri constants and Seshadri's criterion.
6. Mumford's example: a divisor positive on every curve that is not ample.

### 4. Nef and ample cones and $\overline{NE}(X)$ (45 pages)

1. Convex cones: duality, faces, extremal rays, polyhedral and round cones.
2. $\mathrm{Nef}(X)$, $\mathrm{Amp}(X)$ and $\overline{NE}(X)$ as dual cones.
3. Surfaces: the Hodge index theorem and the cone of curves of a surface (P5 Ch. 5).
4. Worked cones: $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, $\mathbb{F}_n$, $\mathrm{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2$, the del Pezzo surfaces, $E \times E$.
5. Faces and morphisms: a morphism with connected fibres gives a face of $\overline{NE}(X)$; semiample divisors and the morphisms they define. The contraction theorem is Vol. 7.

### 5. Big and pseudo-effective divisors (40 pages)

1. Big divisors; Kodaira's lemma: big is ample plus effective.
2. The big cone and the pseudo-effective cone; big is the interior of pseudo-effective.
3. Movable curves; the theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell as a statement (BDPP13).
4. Examples: a big and not nef divisor on $\mathrm{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2$; the effective cone of a del Pezzo surface.

### 6. Section rings and Iitaka dimension (40 pages)

1. The section ring $R(X, D)$ and the semigroup $N(X, D)$.
2. The Iitaka dimension $\kappa(X, D)$ and its basic properties.
3. The Iitaka fibration, with proof.
4. Finite generation: semiample divisors give finitely generated rings; Zariski's example of a ring that is not finitely generated.
5. The Kodaira dimension $\kappa(X)$ and its invariance under birational maps.

The numerical dimension $\kappa_\sigma$ of Nakayama is in Vol. 1, because the program uses it for pairs.

### 7. Volume and base loci (40 pages)

1. The volume $\mathrm{vol}(D) = \limsup \frac{h^0(mD)}{m^n/n!}$; homogeneity and continuity on $N^1(X)$.
2. Fujita's approximation theorem as a statement; Okounkov bodies as a remark.
3. The stable base locus $\mathbf{B}(D)$, the augmented base locus $\mathbf{B}_+(D)$ and the restricted base locus $\mathbf{B}_-(D)$.
4. Nakamaye's theorem as a statement.
5. Examples on surfaces: Zariski decomposition and the volume (P5 Ch. 8).

### 8. Vanishing I: Kodaira and Kawamata-Viehweg (45 pages)

1. Kodaira vanishing over $\mathbb{C}$ from Hodge theory (P6 Ch. 6), and its algebraic proof by Deligne-Illusie cited from Ch. 11.
2. Cyclic covers and the covering trick.
3. Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for a nef and big divisor on a smooth projective variety, with the snc fractional part.
4. Ramanujam's form on surfaces.
5. Examples: vanishing on $\mathbb{P}^n$ and on a blow-up; failure without bigness.

Kawamata-Viehweg for pairs and over a base is Vol. 5 Ch. 2.

### 9. Vanishing II: Grauert-Riemenschneider and Kollár (35 pages)

1. Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing for a resolution.
2. Kollár's injectivity theorem on a smooth projective variety.
3. Kollár's torsion-freeness and vanishing for $R^i f_* \omega_X$, for a smooth $X$.
4. The decomposition of $R f_* \omega_X$ as a statement (Kollár, Saito).

The relative and log forms (Ambro, Fujino) are Vol. 5 Ch. 3.

### 10. Multiplier ideals (45 pages)

1. The multiplier ideal $\mathcal{J}(X, \mathfrak{a}^c)$ on a smooth variety, from a log resolution; its independence of the resolution.
2. The log canonical threshold as the first jump; examples on curves and on the cusp ($\mathrm{lct} = \frac56$).
3. Nadel vanishing on a smooth projective variety.
4. Monomial ideals and Howald's theorem.
5. The restriction theorem, subadditivity and Skoda's theorem, on a smooth variety.
6. Asymptotic multiplier ideals of a linear series.

Multiplier ideals on pairs, adjoint ideals and their use in extension theorems are Vol. 5 Ch. 4 and 5, and Vol. 9.

### 11. Characteristic $p$: Raynaud, Deligne-Illusie, Frobenius splitting (40 pages)

1. The Frobenius morphism and the Cartier operator.
2. Raynaud's counterexample to Kodaira vanishing on a surface, in outline.
3. Deligne-Illusie: lifting to $W_2(k)$ gives the degeneration of Hodge to de Rham and Kodaira vanishing.
4. Frobenius splitting and vanishing on $F$-split varieties; the flag varieties as an example.

$F$-singularities and the program in characteristic $p$ are Vol. 11.
