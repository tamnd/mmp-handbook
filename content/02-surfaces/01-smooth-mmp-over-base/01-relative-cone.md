## The relative cone theorem and the relative contractions

### Statement

Throughout, $k$ is algebraically closed of any characteristic, $S$ is a smooth projective surface over $k$, and $f \colon S \to Z$ is a morphism to a projective variety $Z$. As in Definition P5.12.1, $N^1(S) = \operatorname{Num}(S) \otimes \mathbb{R}$ carries the intersection pairing, and the class of a curve is its class in $N^1(S)$.

**Definition 2.1.1 (the relative cone).** An integral curve $C \subseteq S$ is *$f$-contracted*, or *vertical*, if $f(C)$ is a point, and *horizontal* otherwise. The *relative cone* $NE(S/Z) \subseteq N^1(S)$ is the convex cone spanned by the classes of the $f$-contracted integral curves, and $\overline{NE}(S/Z)$ is its closure. The subspace $N_1(S/Z) \subseteq N^1(S)$ spanned by these classes has dimension $\rho(S/Z)$, the *relative Picard number*. An $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $D$ is *$f$-nef* if $D \cdot C \geq 0$ for every $f$-contracted integral curve $C$, and for a class $D$ write $\overline{NE}(S/Z)_{D \geq 0} = \{ x \in \overline{NE}(S/Z) : D \cdot x \geq 0 \}$.

If $Z$ is a point, every curve is contracted, $\overline{NE}(S/Z) = \overline{NE}(S)$, $\rho(S/Z) = \rho(S)$, and $f$-nef means nef. If $f$ is the identity, $NE(S/Z) = 0$ and every divisor is $f$-nef.

**Definition 2.1.2 (relative extremal rays and their contractions).** An *extremal ray* of $\overline{NE}(S/Z)$ is a ray $R = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} x$, $x \neq 0$, such that $a, b \in \overline{NE}(S/Z)$ and $a + b \in R$ imply $a, b \in R$. It is *$K$-negative* if $K_S \cdot x < 0$. A *contraction* of $R$ over $Z$ is a morphism $\varphi_R \colon S \to T$ onto a normal projective variety with $\varphi_{R*} \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_T$, together with a morphism $g \colon T \to Z$ with $f = g \circ \varphi_R$, such that an integral curve $C$ is contracted by $\varphi_R$ if and only if $[C] \in R$.

For the structure of the relative cone, let $f = g \circ p$ be the Stein factorization (Theorem P4.6.15), with $p \colon S \to Y$, $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_Y$, and $g \colon Y \to Z$ finite. A *fibration* is a surjective morphism $p \colon S \to B$ onto a smooth projective curve with $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_B$. For a closed point $b \in B$ the fibre is the effective divisor $S_b = p^* b$, and $F$ denotes the class of a fibre.

**Lemma 2.1.3 (Zariski's lemma).** Let $p \colon S \to B$ be a fibration and $S_b = \sum_{i=1}^r n_i C_i$ a fibre, with $C_i$ distinct integral curves and $n_i > 0$.

1. All fibres are numerically equivalent, $F^2 = 0$, $F \cdot C = 0$ for every vertical integral curve $C$, and $F \cdot C > 0$ for every horizontal integral curve $C$.
2. The intersection matrix $(C_i \cdot C_j)$ is negative semidefinite, and an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $D = \sum_i a_i C_i$ has $D^2 = 0$ if and only if $D = t S_b$ for some $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
3. If $r \geq 2$, then $C_i^2 < 0$ for every $i$.

**Proposition 2.1.4 (the structure of the relative cone).** Let $f = g \circ p$ be the Stein factorization, $p \colon S \to Y$.

1. The $f$-contracted curves are the $p$-contracted curves, and $\dim Y = \dim f(S)$.
2. If $\dim f(S) = 2$, then $p$ is birational onto the normal projective surface $Y$, its exceptional curves $C_1, \dots, C_r$ are linearly independent in $N^1(S)$, $NE(S/Z) = \sum_i \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [C_i]$ is a closed simplicial cone, its extremal rays are the $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [C_i]$, and $\rho(S/Z) = r$.
3. If $\dim f(S) = 1$, then $p \colon S \to B = Y$ is a fibration. Only finitely many fibres $S_{b_1}, \dots, S_{b_s}$ are reducible. Let $S_{b_j}$ have $r_j \geq 2$ components $C_{j,1}, \dots, C_{j,r_j}$. Then $NE(S/Z) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F + \sum_{j, i} \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [C_{j,i}]$ is a closed polyhedral cone, and $\rho(S/Z) = 1 + \sum_j (r_j - 1) \leq \rho(S) - 1$. If $s = 0$, then $NE(S/Z) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$ is a single extremal ray. If $s \geq 1$, the extremal rays are exactly the $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[C_{j,i}]$, each of negative square, and $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$ is not extremal.
4. If $f(S)$ is a point, $\overline{NE}(S/Z) = \overline{NE}(S)$.

**Theorem 2.1.5 (the relative cone theorem).** Let $\{R_i\}_{i \in I}$ be the set of $K$-negative extremal rays of $\overline{NE}(S/Z)$.

1. Each $R_i$ is spanned by the class of an $f$-contracted integral curve $C_i \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ with $0 < -K \cdot C_i \leq 3$, and $$\overline{NE}(S/Z) = \overline{NE}(S/Z)_{K \geq 0} + \sum_{i \in I} R_i.$$
2. If $f(S)$ is not a point, then $\overline{NE}(S/Z) = NE(S/Z)$ is polyhedral, $I$ is finite, and each $C_i$ is either a $(-1)$-curve, with $-K \cdot C_i = 1$, or the reduced fibre of a fibration all of whose fibres are irreducible, with $C_i^2 = 0$ and $-K \cdot C_i = 2$.
3. $K$ is $f$-nef if and only if $I$ is empty.

**Theorem 2.1.6 (the relative contractions).** Let $R$ be a $K$-negative extremal ray of $\overline{NE}(S/Z)$. Then the contraction $\varphi_R$ of $R$ over $Z$ exists, and exactly one of the following holds.

1. $R = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [E]$ for an $f$-contracted $(-1)$-curve $E$, the only integral curve with class in $R$. Then $\varphi_R \colon S \to S'$ is the contraction of $E$ of Theorem P5.7.4, $S'$ is a smooth projective surface, $f = f' \circ \varphi_R$ for a unique $f' \colon S' \to Z$, $\rho(S') = \rho(S) - 1$ and $\rho(S'/Z) = \rho(S/Z) - 1$.
2. $\dim f(S) = 1$, every fibre of the Stein factorization $p \colon S \to B$ is irreducible, and $R = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$. Then $\varphi_R = p$, every scheme-theoretic fibre of $p$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, $-K \cdot F = 2$, $\rho(S) = 2$ and $\rho(S/Z) = 1$.
3. $f(S)$ is a point and $R$ is of type 2 or 3 of Theorem P5.12.15, so that $\varphi_R$ is a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over a curve with $\rho(S) = 2$, or $S \cong \mathbb{P}^2$ and $\varphi_R$ is the map to a point.

In case 1 the contraction is *divisorial*, and the morphism $f' \colon S' \to Z$ is again a morphism of a smooth projective surface, so the theorem applies to it. In cases 2 and 3 the contraction $\varphi_R \colon S \to T$ is a *Mori fibre space over $Z$*: $\dim T < \dim S$, $-K$ is positive on every curve contracted by $\varphi_R$, and $\rho(S/T) = 1$.

### Proof in words

The Stein factorization separates the base into a finite part, which contracts no curve, and a part with connected fibres. If the connected part is birational, only the finitely many exceptional curves are contracted. Mumford's theorem makes them negative definite, so their classes are independent and each spans an extremal ray of a simplicial cone. If the connected part is a fibration, the contracted curves are the fibre components. Zariski's lemma says that the components of one fibre form a negative semidefinite configuration whose only isotropic direction is the fibre itself. So each component of a reducible fibre has negative square and spans an extremal ray, the fibre class is their sum and is not extremal, and the independence of the components modulo the fibre bounds the number of reducible fibres by the Picard number.

The cone theorem is then a statement about finitely many explicit generators. A $K$-negative generator of negative square is a $(-1)$-curve by the genus formula. A $K$-negative generator of square $0$ is a reduced fibre of a fibration with irreducible fibres, and the argument of Theorem P5.12.15 shows that such a fibration is a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle with $\rho = 2$. The contraction of a $(-1)$-curve in a fibre is Castelnuovo's blow-down, and the morphism to $Z$ factors through it by the universal property. Over a point everything is the absolute theorem.

### Examples

**Example 2.1.7 (a blown-up Hirzebruch surface over $\mathbb{P}^1$).** Let $n \geq 0$, let $x$ be a closed point of the fibre $F_c$ of $F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1$, and let $S = \operatorname{Bl}_x F_n$ with exceptional curve $E$ and $f \colon S \to \mathbb{P}^1$ the composite, a fibration. By Proposition P5.6.4, $f^* c = \tilde F_c + E$, with $\tilde F_c^2 = 0 - 1 = -1$ and $K \cdot \tilde F_c = -2 + 1 = -1$, and $E^2 = K \cdot E = -1$ by Theorem P5.6.3. Every other fibre is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$. By Proposition 2.1.4, $NE(S/\mathbb{P}^1) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [\tilde F_c] + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [E]$, with $F = \tilde F_c + E$ in the interior, and $\rho(S/\mathbb{P}^1) = 1 + (2 - 1) = 2$, while $\rho(S) = 3$. Both rays are $K$-negative of type 1. Contracting $E$ gives back $F_n$. Contracting $\tilde F_c$ gives, by Proposition P5.9.10, the surface $F_{n+1}$ if $x \in C_-$, the surface $F_{n-1}$ if $x \notin C_-$ and $n \geq 1$, and $F_1$ if $n = 0$. In each case the next step is the contraction of the fibre class, of type 2, so both outcomes are Mori fibre spaces over $\mathbb{P}^1$, and they are not isomorphic by part 3 of Proposition P5.9.9. The absolute cone of $S$ is larger: for $x \in C_-$ the strict transform $\tilde C_-$ has $\tilde C_-^2 = -n - 1$ and $K \cdot \tilde C_- = n - 1$, it is horizontal, and it spans an extremal ray of $\overline{NE}(S)$ by Lemma P5.12.11 that does not lie in $NE(S/\mathbb{P}^1)$.

**Example 2.1.8 (the resolution of the quadric cone).** Let $T = V_+(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$ be the quadric cone with vertex $v$, and $f \colon S = \operatorname{Bl}_v T \to T$, as in Example P5.8.9. There $S$ is a smooth projective surface, $f$ is birational onto the normal surface $T$, and its only exceptional curve is $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ with $E^2 = -2$. The genus formula gives $K \cdot E = -2 - E^2 = 0$. So $NE(S/T) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[E]$, $\rho(S/T) = 1$, there is no $K$-negative relative ray, and $K_S$ is $f$-nef although $f$ is not an isomorphism. Now let $x \in E$ and $S' = \operatorname{Bl}_x S \to T$, with exceptional curve $E_x$ and strict transform $\tilde E$. By Proposition P5.6.4, $\tilde E^2 = -3$, $K \cdot \tilde E = 0 + 1 = 1$ and $\tilde E \cdot E_x = 1$. The intersection matrix $\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $2 > 0$ and negative trace, so it is negative definite, as Theorem P5.8.3 requires. By Proposition 2.1.4, $NE(S'/T) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\tilde E] + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[E_x]$. The ray of $E_x$ is the only $K$-negative ray, and $NE(S'/T)_{K \geq 0} = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\tilde E]$. Theorem 2.1.5 reads $NE(S'/T) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\tilde E] + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[E_x]$, and the contraction of $E_x$ returns $S$, on which $K$ is $f$-nef.

**Example 2.1.9 (the rational elliptic surface over $\mathbb{P}^1$).** Let $f \colon X = X_9 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be the rational elliptic surface of Theorem P5.10.21, defined by a pencil of plane cubics satisfying the hypothesis (H) there. Its fibres are integral curves of arithmetic genus $1$, the fibre class is $F = -K$, and $K \cdot F = 0$. By Proposition 2.1.4, $NE(X/\mathbb{P}^1) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$, $\rho(X/\mathbb{P}^1) = 1$, and $K = -F$ is $f$-nef: there is no $K$-negative relative extremal ray, and $X$ is its own relative minimal model over $\mathbb{P}^1$. But $K$ is not nef: each $E_i$ is a section with $K \cdot E_i = -1$, and by Theorem P5.10.22, $X$ contains infinitely many $(-1)$-curves, all of them sections, hence all horizontal. An absolute run of the program on $X$ contracts $E_9, \dots, E_1$ and ends at $\mathbb{P}^2$. So relative minimality over a curve is much weaker than minimality, and $\rho(X/\mathbb{P}^1) = 1$ while $\rho(X) = 10$.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 2.1.3.* (1) The morphism $p$ is flat by Theorem P3.13.4, since $S$ is integral and $p$ is dominant. Step 3 of the proof of Theorem P5.12.15 uses only this flatness: for a divisor $D$, $D \cdot S_b = \chi(\mathcal{O}_{S_b}) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_{S_b}(-D))$, and both terms are independent of $b$ by Theorem P4.5.13. So $S_b \equiv S_{b'}$ for all closed points $b, b'$. Let $C$ be an integral curve. If $C$ is vertical, $C \subseteq S_b$, choose $b' \neq b$; then $C \cap S_{b'} = \emptyset$ and $F \cdot C = S_{b'} \cdot C = 0$ by Proposition P5.5.5. If $C$ is horizontal, $p(C) = B$ and $C$ meets $S_{b'}$ for every $b'$ without being contained in it, so $F \cdot C > 0$ by Proposition P5.5.5. Finally $F^2 = F \cdot S_b = \sum_i n_i F \cdot C_i = 0$.

(2) Let $M = (C_i \cdot C_j)$ and $z = (n_1, \dots, n_r)$, with every $n_i > 0$. Off the diagonal $C_i \cdot C_j \geq 0$, with equality if and only if $C_i \cap C_j = \emptyset$, by Proposition P5.5.5. The support of $S_b$ is connected by Theorem P4.6.7, so the graph $\Gamma(M)$ is connected. By (1), $(M z)_j = C_j \cdot S_b = 0$ for every $j$. Part 4 of Lemma P5.8.1 gives that $M$ is negative semidefinite and $a^T M a = 0$ only for $a \in \mathbb{R} z$, which is the claim.

(3) If $r \geq 2$, the unit vector $e_i$ is not a multiple of $z$, so $C_i^2 = e_i^T M e_i < 0$ by (2). $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 2.1.4.* (1) The fibres of $g$ are finite, so $f(C)$ is a point if and only if $p(C)$ is a point, and $\dim Y = \dim g(Y) = \dim f(S)$. The scheme $Y$ is projective over $k$, since $g$ is finite and $Z$ is projective. It is integral and normal, by the argument of Step 2 of the proof of Theorem P5.12.15: a rational function on an open $W \subseteq Y$ integral over $\mathcal{O}_Y(W)$ pulls back to a rational function on the normal $p^{-1}(W)$ integral over $\mathcal{O}_S(p^{-1} W)$, hence regular, and $\mathcal{O}_Y(W) = \mathcal{O}_S(p^{-1} W)$.

(2) Let $\dim Y = 2$. The points of $Y$ over which the fibre of $p$ has dimension $1$ form a closed subset (Hartshorne, Exercise II.3.22), which is not all of $Y$ since $\dim S = \dim Y$, so it is a finite set $Y_0$. Over $V = Y \setminus Y_0$ the morphism $p$ is projective with finite fibres, hence finite by Corollary P4.6.17. A finite morphism is affine, and $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_Y$ says that $p^{-1}(V) = \operatorname{Spec}_V \mathcal{O}_V = V$. So $p$ is birational. By Theorem P5.8.3 its exceptional curves $C_1, \dots, C_r$ are finite in number and form a negative definite configuration. A negative definite Gram matrix is invertible, so the classes $[C_i]$ are linearly independent, and $\rho(S/Z) = r$. The $f$-contracted curves are exactly the $C_i$, so $NE(S/Z) = \sum_i \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [C_i]$, a closed simplicial cone. If $a = \sum_i a_i [C_i]$ and $b = \sum_i b_i [C_i]$ lie in it, with $a_i, b_i \geq 0$, and $a + b \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [C_1]$, independence gives $a_i + b_i = 0$ for $i \geq 2$, so $a_i = b_i = 0$ for $i \geq 2$. So each $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[C_i]$ is extremal, and an extremal ray of a cone spanned by finitely many classes is spanned by one of them.

(3) Let $\dim Y = 1$. Then $Y$ is a normal projective curve, hence a smooth projective curve $B$, and $p$ is a fibration. A vertical integral curve lies in a fibre and is one of its components. If $S_b = n C$ is irreducible, $[C] = F / n$. So $NE(S/Z)$ is spanned by $F$ and the components of the reducible fibres, once it is shown that there are finitely many of these.

Let $b_1, \dots, b_s$ be distinct points with reducible fibres, and in each $S_{b_j}$ omit one component, say $C_{j, r_j}$. The claim is that $F$ and the $C_{j,i}$ with $1 \leq i \leq r_j - 1$ are linearly independent in $N^1(S)$. Let $a F + \sum_j D_j \equiv 0$ with $D_j = \sum_{i < r_j} a_{j,i} C_{j,i}$. For a component $C$ of $S_{b_j}$, $F \cdot C = 0$ by part 1 of Lemma 2.1.3, and $D_{j'} \cdot C = 0$ for $j' \neq j$, since distinct fibres are disjoint. So $D_j \cdot C = 0$ for every component $C$ of $S_{b_j}$, and $D_j^2 = 0$. By part 2 of Lemma 2.1.3, $D_j = t S_{b_j}$. The coefficient of $C_{j, r_j}$ in $D_j$ is $0$ and in $S_{b_j}$ it is positive, so $t = 0$ and $D_j = 0$. Then $a F \equiv 0$, and $F \cdot H > 0$ for an ample $H$ by Lemma 2.1.3, so $a = 0$. These $1 + \sum_j (r_j - 1)$ classes are independent and orthogonal to $F$, while $H \cdot F > 0$, so $1 + \sum_j (r_j - 1) \leq \rho(S) - 1$. In particular $s \leq \rho(S) - 2$, so there are finitely many reducible fibres. The components of $S_{b_j}$ together with $F$ span the same space as $F$ and the $C_{j,i}$ with $i < r_j$, because $F \equiv \sum_i n_{j,i} C_{j,i}$. So $\rho(S/Z) = 1 + \sum_j (r_j - 1)$.

The cone $NE(S/Z)$ is spanned by finitely many classes, so it is polyhedral and closed. If $s = 0$, it is the ray $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$, which is extremal, as a sum $a + b \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$ of elements of $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$. Let $s \geq 1$ and $C = C_{j,i}$, so $C^2 < 0$ by part 3 of Lemma 2.1.3. Let $a, b \in NE(S/Z)$ with $a + b = \lambda [C]$. Write $a = \alpha [C] + a'$ and $b = \beta [C] + b'$, where $\alpha, \beta \geq 0$ and $a', b'$ are nonnegative combinations of classes of vertical integral curves other than $C$. Then $a' + b' = (\lambda - \alpha - \beta)[C]$. The left side has nonnegative intersection with $C$ and the right side has intersection $(\lambda - \alpha - \beta) C^2$, so $\lambda - \alpha - \beta \leq 0$. Intersecting $a' + b' + (\alpha + \beta - \lambda)[C] = 0$ with $H$, every term is nonnegative, so $\alpha + \beta = \lambda$ and $H \cdot a' = H \cdot b' = 0$, which forces $a' = b' = 0$. So $a, b \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[C]$ and the ray of $C$ is extremal. The ray of $F$ is not: $F = \sum_i n_{j,i} [C_{j,i}]$ with $[C_{j,i}] \notin \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$, since $C_{j,i}^2 < 0 = F^2$. Every extremal ray of a cone spanned by finitely many classes is spanned by one of them, so the extremal rays are the rays of the $C_{j,i}$.

(4) If $f(S)$ is a point, every curve is contracted. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 2.1.5.* If $f(S)$ is a point, $\overline{NE}(S/Z) = \overline{NE}(S)$ and $f$-nef means nef, so (1) and (3) are parts 1 and 4 of Theorem P5.12.14, with $C_i \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ by Theorem P5.12.15, and (2) is empty.

Let $f(S)$ be a curve or a surface. By Proposition 2.1.4, $\overline{NE}(S/Z) = NE(S/Z)$ is spanned by finitely many classes of vertical integral curves, it is the sum of its extremal rays, and each extremal ray is spanned by a vertical integral curve $C$, with $C^2 < 0$ or with $C$ the reduced fibre of a fibration all of whose fibres are irreducible. In the second case $C^2 = 0$, since $F^2 = 0$ and $[C]$ is a positive multiple of $F$. Let $R = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[C]$ be $K$-negative. If $C^2 < 0$, then $C$ is a $(-1)$-curve by part 2 of Corollary P5.5.15, and $-K \cdot C = 1$. If $C^2 = 0$, then $C^2 + K \cdot C = 2 p_a(C) - 2$ is even, negative, and at least $-2$ by part 1 of Corollary P5.5.15, so $K \cdot C = -2$ and $C \cong \mathbb{P}^1$. The rays with $K \geq 0$ lie in $NE(S/Z)_{K \geq 0}$, and the sum of all extremal rays is the cone, which gives (1) and (2), with $I$ finite.

(3) If $K$ is $f$-nef, then $K \geq 0$ on $NE(S/Z)$ and on its closure, so no ray is $K$-negative. If $K \cdot C < 0$ for a vertical integral curve $C$, then $[C]$ is a nonnegative combination of generators of the extremal rays, and one of them is $K$-negative. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 2.1.6.* If $f(S)$ is a point, Theorem P5.12.15 gives the contraction $\varphi_R$ and its three types; type 1 is case 1, with $\rho(S'/Z) = \rho(S') = \rho(S) - 1$, and types 2 and 3 are case 3. The morphism $g$ to the point $Z$ is unique. So let $f(S)$ be a curve or a surface, and let $R = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[C]$ as in Theorem 2.1.5.

*Case $C^2 < 0$.* Then $E = C$ is an $f$-contracted $(-1)$-curve. An integral curve $C'$ with $[C'] = t[E]$, $t > 0$, has $C' \cdot E = -t < 0$, so $C' = E$ by Proposition P5.5.5. Let $\psi \colon S \to S'$ be the contraction of Theorem P5.7.4. Then $S'$ is a smooth projective surface, $\psi_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_{S'}$, and $\psi$ contracts exactly $E$. As $f(E)$ is a point, part 1 of Corollary P5.7.5 gives a unique $f' \colon S' \to Z$ with $f = f' \circ \psi$. So $\psi$ is the contraction of $R$ over $Z$, and $\rho(S') = \rho(S) - 1$ by part 2 of Corollary P5.7.5. For the relative Picard number, an integral curve $C' \subseteq S'$ is $f'$-contracted if and only if its strict transform $\tilde C'$ is $f$-contracted, and $\psi^* C' = \tilde C' + (\tilde C' \cdot E) E$, since $\psi^* C' \cdot E = 0$ by part 2 of Corollary P5.7.5. So $\psi^* N_1(S'/Z) \subseteq N_1(S/Z)$, and $N_1(S/Z)$ is spanned by $[E]$ and the classes $\psi^*[C'] - (\tilde C' \cdot E)[E]$. As $\psi^*$ is injective on $N^1$ with image orthogonal to $E$, and $E^2 \neq 0$, $N_1(S/Z) = \psi^* N_1(S'/Z) \oplus \mathbb{R}[E]$, and $\rho(S'/Z) = \rho(S/Z) - 1$.

*Case $C^2 = 0$.* By Proposition 2.1.4 and Theorem 2.1.5, $\dim f(S) = 1$, every fibre of $p \colon S \to B$ is irreducible, $R = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} F$, and $C$ is a reduced fibre with $K \cdot C = -2$. Every fibre is $S_b = m_b G_b$ with $G_b$ integral and $[G_b] \in R$, so $G_b^2 = 0$ and $K \cdot G_b < 0$, and as for $C$, $G_b \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ and $K \cdot G_b = -2$. Since $K \cdot S_b = -2 m_b$ is independent of $b$ by part 1 of Lemma 2.1.3, $m_b = m$ is constant. Step 4 of the proof of Theorem P5.12.15 applies verbatim: by Riemann-Roch $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S_b}) = -\frac{1}{2} S_b \cdot (S_b + K) = m$, by Theorem P4.5.13 this equals $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S_\eta})$ for the generic fibre, and by part 2 of Corollary P4.5.2 $h^0(\mathcal{O}_{S_\eta}) = 1$, so $m \leq 1$. So every scheme-theoretic fibre $S_b = G_b$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, and $-K \cdot F = 2$. Step 5 of the same proof uses only that the fibres of a fibration are integral with $F^2 = 0$ and $K \cdot F = -2$, as Example P5.12.19 notes, and gives $\rho(S) = 2$. The morphism $p$ has $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_B$ and $f = g \circ p$. It contracts exactly the vertical curves, whose classes lie in $R$. Conversely an integral curve with class in $R$ has $F \cdot C' = 0$, so it is vertical by part 1 of Lemma 2.1.3. So $p$ is the contraction of $R$ over $Z$, and $\rho(S/Z) = 1$ by Proposition 2.1.4.

The three cases are exclusive, since they are distinguished by $C^2 < 0$, by $C^2 = 0$ with $f(S)$ a curve, and by $f(S)$ a point. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $x, y$ be distinct points of one fibre $F_c$ of $F_0 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$, and $S = \operatorname{Bl}_{x, y} F_0$. Show that the fibre over $c$ is $\tilde F_c + E_x + E_y$ with $\tilde F_c^2 = -2$, compute $NE(S/\mathbb{P}^1)$ and $\rho(S/\mathbb{P}^1)$, and show that exactly two of its three extremal rays are $K$-negative.
2. (A) Let $S = \operatorname{Bl}_q \mathbb{P}^2$. Compute $NE(S/Z)$, $\rho(S/Z)$ and the $K$-negative relative rays for $Z = \mathbb{P}^2$ with the blow-down, and for $Z = \mathbb{P}^1$ with the projection from $q$. Which of the two is a Mori fibre space over $Z$?
3. (B) Let $S_b = 2C_0 + C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + C_4$ be a fibre with $C_i^2 = -2$ for all $i$, $C_0 \cdot C_i = 1$ for $i \geq 1$, and $C_i \cdot C_j = 0$ for $1 \leq i < j$. Check that $S_b \cdot C_i = 0$ for every $i$, and verify part 2 of Lemma 2.1.3 directly by writing the quadratic form as a sum of squares.
4. (B) Let $p \colon S \to B$ be a fibration and $D$ an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor supported on a fibre $S_b$ with $D \cdot C \geq 0$ for every component $C$ of $S_b$. Show that $D = t S_b$ for some $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
5. (B) Let $p \colon S \to B$ be a fibration with $K$ $p$-nef and $K \cdot F = 0$. Show that $K \cdot C = 0$ for every vertical integral curve, and that every component of a reducible fibre is a smooth rational curve of square $-2$.
6. (C) Let $f \colon S \to T$ be a birational morphism onto a normal projective surface. Show that $K_S$ is $f$-nef if and only if no exceptional curve of $f$ is a $(-1)$-curve, and that in that case every exceptional curve $C$ with $K \cdot C = 0$ is a smooth rational curve of square $-2$. Give an example with an exceptional curve $C$ with $K \cdot C > 0$.

### Notes and history

The relative cone theorem is the case of a smooth surface of the cone theorem for a projective morphism, Kollár-Mori, Theorem 3.25, and the relative point of view is the one of Kollár-Mori, Section 1.4, and of Matsuki, Chapter 1. For surfaces the relative cone is polyhedral as soon as the base is not a point, which is special to dimension 2: for a 3-fold over a curve the relative cone can have infinitely many extremal rays. Zariski's lemma is in Zariski's 1962 paper on the Riemann-Roch problem for surfaces, and in Beauville, Lemma VIII.4, and Barth, Hulek, Peters and Van de Ven, Lemma III.8.2. The literature state of the results of this section is theorem.
