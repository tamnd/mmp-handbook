# The smooth surface MMP over a base

The minimal model program of P5 Chapter 12 runs on a smooth projective surface by itself. In higher dimension the program is always run over a base: a contraction is a morphism $X \to Z$, and the next step looks only at the curves that $Z$ contracts. This chapter sets up the relative program for a smooth projective surface $S$ with a morphism $f \colon S \to Z$ to a projective variety. Three cases occur, according to the dimension of $f(S)$. If $f$ is birational onto a normal surface, only finitely many curves are contracted. If $f(S)$ is a curve, the contracted curves are the components of the fibres of a fibration. If $f(S)$ is a point, the program is the absolute one.

Section 1 defines the relative cone $\overline{NE}(S/Z)$ and proves Zariski's lemma on the fibres of a fibration. It proves that the relative cone is polyhedral when $f(S)$ is not a point, states and proves the relative cone theorem, and classifies the contractions of $K$-negative relative extremal rays: the blow-down of a $(-1)$-curve contracted by $f$, a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over a curve, and the absolute case over a point. Section 2 treats the Mori fibre spaces over a curve. It defines the elementary transformation of a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle at a point, computes how it changes the self-intersections of sections, deduces the moves $F_n \dashrightarrow F_{n \pm 1}$ of the Hirzebruch surfaces, and proves that every birational map between $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundles over the same curve is a composite of elementary transformations. Section 3 runs the relative program, proves that a relative minimal model with $K$ nef over $Z$ is unique, and shows that a surface with $\kappa \geq 0$ never ends at a Mori fibre space.

The examples are the blow-up of a Hirzebruch surface over $\mathbb{P}^1$, with two $K$-negative relative rays whose contractions give $F_n$ and $F_{n \pm 1}$; the resolution of the quadric cone, which is a relative minimal model that is not an isomorphism; the rational elliptic surface, which is relatively minimal over $\mathbb{P}^1$ although it contains infinitely many $(-1)$-curves; the blow-up of a smooth quintic surface in a point, a surface of general type with exactly one $(-1)$-curve, whose cone, canonical ring and Zariski decomposition are computed; and the conic bundle on a cubic surface given by the lines through one of the six points, with five singular fibres and thirty-two relative minimal models, each isomorphic to $F_0$ or $F_1$ according to a parity. The problems compute relative cones, elementary transformations and relative minimal models by hand.
