## Problems

Each problem is a computation with the tools of this chapter: Kodaira's canonical bundle formula and Corollary 2.5.8, the contraction of $(-2)$-curves to the canonical model, the section rings of Lemma 2.5.15, and the log canonical class of an explicit pair. The ground field is $\mathbb{C}$ in the problems on elliptic fibrations.

1. (A) Let $f \colon S \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be a relatively minimal elliptic fibration with $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 2$ and exactly one multiple fibre, of multiplicity $3$. Compute $\delta$, show that $\kappa(S) = 1$, and show that $P_1 = 1$, $P_2 = 2$ and $P_3 = 3$.
2. (A) Let $W \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be the Weierstrass fibration of Example 2.5.9 with $N = 4$. Show that $K_W \sim 2F$, $p_g = 3$, $q = 0$, $P_n = 2n + 1$, and $h^1(nK_W) = 2n - 3$ for $n \geq 2$, and check the values against Riemann-Roch.
3. (B) Let $E$ be an elliptic curve, $\tau$ the translation by a point of order $2$, and $C$ a curve with an involution $\iota$ that has $2r \geq 2$ fixed points, with quotient $C' = C/\iota$ of genus $g'$. The group generated by $(\tau, \iota)$ acts freely on $E \times C$; let $S$ be the quotient, with the elliptic fibration $S \to C'$. Show that this fibration has exactly $2r$ multiple fibres, all of multiplicity $2$, that $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 0$, and that $\delta = 2g' - 2 + r$. Check this by pulling back along the étale double cover $\pi \colon E \times C \to S$: show $\pi^* K_S = K_{E \times C}$, and compare with Example 2.5.11, using $2g(C) - 2 = 2(2g' - 2) + 2r$. Show that $\delta = 0$ exactly when $g' = 0$ and $r = 2$, and that then $C$ is elliptic and $S$ is a bielliptic surface.
4. (B) Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$ be the quintic $x_0^3 x_1 x_2 + x_0^2 x_3^3 + G(x_1, x_2, x_3) = 0$, with $G$ a quintic form, and assume that $p = [1 : 0 : 0 : 0]$ is the only singular point of $X$. Show that $p$ is a rational double point of type $A_2$, that its minimal resolution $f \colon S \to X$ (Theorem 2.3.8) has two $(-2)$-curves meeting transversally in one point, that $K_S = f^* \mathcal{O}_X(1)$, and that $R(S, K_S) \cong k[x_0, \dots, x_3]/(F)$ with $K_S^2 = 5$ and $p_g(S) = 4$.
5. (B) Let $C_1$ and $C_2$ be curves of genus $2$ and $S = C_1 \times C_2$. Compute $K_S^2$, $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S)$, $p_g$, $q$ and $P_n$ for $n \geq 2$. Show that $\varphi_{|K_S|}$ is the product of the two hyperelliptic maps followed by the Segre embedding $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$, a morphism of degree $4$ onto a quadric, so that $K_S$ is ample and free but not very ample.
6. (B) Let $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, and let $B \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be a curve of degree $10$ with one ordinary node and no other singular point. Let $X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the double cover branched along $B$ and $f \colon S \to X$ the minimal resolution (Theorem 2.3.8). Show that $X$ has one singular point, of type $A_1$, that $K_S = f^* \pi^* \mathcal{O}(2)$, that $K_S^2 = 8$ and $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 7$, and that $X$ is the canonical model of $S$.
7. (B) On $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ let $A_1, \dots, A_4$ be distinct fibres of class $F_1$ over points $a_i$, and $B_1, \dots, B_4$ distinct fibres of class $F_2$ over points $b_j$, and put $\Delta = \frac{1}{2} \sum A_i + \frac{1}{2} \sum B_j$. Show that $(X, \Delta)$ is klt and that $K_X + \Delta \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} 0$. Let $E_1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ and $E_2 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be the double covers branched over the $a_i$ and the $b_j$, and $p \colon E_1 \times E_2 \to X$ their product. Show that $p^*(K_X + \Delta) = K_{E_1 \times E_2} \sim 0$.
8. (C) Let $X_9$ be the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in nine points $P_1, \dots, P_9$ of a smooth cubic $C$ with $\sum P_i$ a point of exact order $m \geq 2$ in the group law with a flex as origin. Show that $|-mK|$ is a base point free pencil, that the induced elliptic fibration has $m \tilde C$ as a multiple fibre of multiplicity $m$, and, granting that it is the only multiple fibre, that Corollary 2.5.8 with $\chi = 1$ gives $\delta = -\frac{1}{m}$ and $K \equiv -\frac{1}{m} F$, in agreement with $-mK \sim F$ and $\kappa = -\infty$.
9. (C) Let $S$ be the Dolgachev surface of Example 2.5.10, with multiple fibres $2F_1$ and $3F_2$. Show that $3K_S \sim F_1$ and $2K_S \sim F_2$, and let $\sigma_2$, $\sigma_3$ be sections of $2K_S$ and $3K_S$ with these divisors. Show that $\sigma_3^2 / \sigma_2^3$ is a nonconstant rational function, and deduce that $R(S, K_S) = k[\sigma_2, \sigma_3]$ is a polynomial ring with generators in degrees $2$ and $3$: compare $\dim R_n$ with the number of pairs $(a, b)$ with $2a + 3b = n$, using $P_n = \lfloor n/6 \rfloor$ for $n \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ and $P_n = \lfloor n/6 \rfloor + 1$ otherwise. Conclude that $\operatorname{Proj} R(S, K_S) = \mathbb{P}(2, 3) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ is the base of the Iitaka fibration.
10. (C) Let $L_1, \dots, L_5 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be five lines in general position and $\Delta_c = c \sum L_i$ for $0 \leq c < 1$. Find the values of $c$ for which $(\mathbb{P}^2, \Delta_c)$ is klt with $K + \Delta_c$ nef, and for $c = \frac{4}{5}$ compute $h^0(\lfloor n(K + \Delta_c) \rfloor)$ for $n = 1, \dots, 5$. Show that the log canonical ring has a Veronese subring isomorphic to $k[x, y, z]$, so that the log canonical model is $\mathbb{P}^2$.
