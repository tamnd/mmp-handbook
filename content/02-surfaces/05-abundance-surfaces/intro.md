# Abundance for surfaces

The minimal model program for a smooth projective surface $S$ ends in one of two ways (Theorem P5.12.21). Either it reaches a Mori fibre space, and then $\kappa(S) = -\infty$, or it reaches a minimal surface $S_0$ with $K_{S_0}$ nef. Abundance is the statement that in the second case $K_{S_0}$ is semiample: some multiple $mK_{S_0}$ is base point free, and the morphism it defines is the Iitaka fibration of $S_0$. For surfaces abundance is a theorem, and this chapter proves it, partly by citing the Enriques-Kodaira classification (Theorem P5.11.26) and partly by direct arguments.

Section 1 proves that a nef canonical divisor has $\kappa \geq 0$, the first step of abundance, by comparing a ruling with the canonical class. It then treats $\kappa = 0$, where $K$ is torsion, and $\kappa = 1$, where $K$ is the pullback of a divisor of positive degree under an elliptic fibration. Kodaira's canonical bundle formula makes the second case explicit, and the section computes the plurigenera of an elliptic surface from it, with the Dolgachev surface and the Weierstrass fibrations as examples.

Section 2 treats $\kappa = 2$. The curves on which $K_S$ is trivial are $(-2)$-curves, they form a negative definite configuration, and Artin's theorem contracts them to rational double points. On the contracted surface $X$ the canonical class is Cartier and ample, so $K_S = f^* K_X$ is semiample. The section proves that the canonical ring $R(S, K_S)$ is finitely generated with $\operatorname{Proj} R(S, K_S) = X$, in the tradition of Zariski's paper of 1962 and Mumford's appendix to it, and states Bombieri's effective bounds. The examples are a quintic surface with a node, a product of two curves, and a blow-up, where the canonical ring is still finitely generated but the canonical class is not semiample.

Section 3 states log abundance for lc and klt surface pairs, with the references to Fujita, Fujino and Tanaka, and proves the big case from the base point free theorem. It ends with the Enriques-Kodaira classification seen from the minimal model program: run the program, and either stop at a Mori fibre space, or stop at a minimal model whose canonical class is semiample, with Iitaka fibration onto $\operatorname{Proj}$ of the canonical ring. The failure example is a rational elliptic surface with a boundary of coefficient bigger than $1$, where the log canonical class is nef and not semiample.

The problems compute plurigenera from the canonical bundle formula, canonical rings and canonical models of explicit surfaces, and log canonical classes of explicit pairs.
