## Problems

Each problem computes a Mumford pullback, a discriminant group, a local class group or a canonical class by hand. The tools are the linear system $M c = -u$ of Section 2, the system $M a = k$ of Section 3, and the formula $K_X^2 = K_Y^2 - A^2$. Check every rational answer against $(\det M) c \in \mathbb{Z}^r$.

1. (A) Let $x$ be an $A_n$ point, with exceptional chain $E_1, \dots, E_n$ of $(-2)$-curves. Show that the vector $c_x(D)$ for a divisor with $u_x(D) = e_j$ is the $j$-th column of $-M^{-1}$, with entries $\frac{\min(i,j) \, (n + 1 - \max(i,j))}{n+1}$. For $n = 3$, list the three columns, find the order of each class in $\Phi_x \cong \mathbb{Z}/4$, and show that the classes for $j = 1$ and $j = 3$ are inverse to each other.
2. (A) Let $x$ be a $D_4$ point, with central $(-2)$-curve $E_0$ and three $(-2)$-curves $E_1, E_2, E_3$ each meeting $E_0$ once. Show that $\det M = 4$. Show that $u = e_1$ gives $c = (1, 1, \frac12, \frac12)$ in the order $E_0, E_1, E_2, E_3$, that $u = e_0$ gives $c = (2, 1, 1, 1)$, and deduce that $\Phi_x \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ and not $\mathbb{Z}/4$. Conclude that every Weil divisor is Cartier at $x$ after multiplication by 2.
3. (A) Compute $\det M$ for the configurations $E_6$ and $E_7$ of $(-2)$-curves, and show that $|\det M| = 3$ and $2$. For $E_6$, with the chain $E_1, E_3, E_4, E_5, E_6$ and $E_2$ meeting $E_4$, show that $u = e_1$ gives $c = (\frac43, \frac53, 2, \frac43, \frac23, 1)$ in the order $E_1, E_3, E_4, E_5, E_6, E_2$, and deduce that $\Phi_x \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$.
4. (A) The minimal resolution of $\frac15(1,2)$ is a chain of a $(-3)$-curve $E_1$ and a $(-2)$-curve $E_2$ (Example 4.3.21). Show that $\det M = 5$, that $u = e_1$ gives $c = (\frac25, \frac15)$ and $u = e_2$ gives $c = (\frac15, \frac35)$, and that each class generates $\Phi_x \cong \mathbb{Z}/5$. Compute $a = (-\frac25, -\frac15)$ from $M a = k$, and show that $K_X$ has index 5 at $x$.
5. (A) In Example 2.3.22 with $n = 3$, show that $K_{X_3} = -5\ell$, $f^*K_{X_3} = -\frac53 (C_- + 3F)$, $A^2 = -\frac13$, $K_{X_3}^2 = \frac{25}{3}$, and that $K_{X_3}$ has index 3. Check the inequality $K_X \cdot C \ge K_Y \cdot \tilde C$ for $C = \ell$ and for $C = H$.
6. (B) Let $T \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be the projective cone over a smooth plane cubic and $f \colon Y \to T$ the blow-up of the vertex, as in Example P5.8.21. Use the following facts (Har77, Section V.2): $Y$ is a ruled surface over the cubic with a section $E_0$, the exceptional curve, with $E_0^2 = -3$; with $F$ a fibre, $K_Y \equiv -2E_0 - 3F$ and the pullback of a plane section is $E_\infty \equiv E_0 + 3F$. Show that $a = -1$, $f^*K_T = K_Y + E_0 \equiv -f^*H$, and $K_T^2 = 3 = K_Y^2 - A^2$ with $K_Y^2 = 0$ and $A^2 = -3$. Check that adjunction for a cubic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ also gives $K_T = \mathcal{O}_T(-1)$, of square 3.
7. (B) Let $x$ be an $E_8$ point, with the chain $e_1, e_3, e_4, e_5, e_6, e_7, e_8$ and $e_2$ meeting $e_4$. Run Laufer's algorithm (part 2 of Proposition P5.8.13) and show that $Z_f = 2e_1 + 3e_2 + 4e_3 + 6e_4 + 5e_5 + 4e_6 + 3e_7 + 2e_8$, with $Z_f \cdot e_8 = -1$, $Z_f \cdot e_j = 0$ for $j \ne 8$ and $Z_f^2 = -2$. Show that $\det M = 1$, for instance by computing the determinants of the chains and expanding along the row of $e_2$, and that $u = e_1$ gives the integer vector $c = (4, 5, 7, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2)$.
8. (B) Compare the index of $K_X$ with $|\det M|$ for cyclic quotient singularities. Show that the index is $\frac{n}{\gcd(n, 2)}$ for $\frac1n(1,1)$, that it is 1 for $\frac1n(1, n-1)$, whose resolution is the chain $A_{n-1}$, and that it is 5 for $\frac15(1,2)$, while $|\det M| = n$ in each case. Find the index of $K_X$ for $\frac17(1,3)$, whose minimal resolution is the chain $[3, 2, 2]$ by Problem 1 of Chapter 3 of Volume 4.
9. (B) The weighted projective plane $X = \mathbb{P}(1, 2, 3)$ is a normal projective rational surface with $\rho = 1$ whose only singular points are an $A_1$ point and an $A_2$ point, and $K_X^2 = 6$. Assuming these facts, show that the minimal resolution $Y$ has $K_Y^2 = 6$ and is crepant over $X$. Using that every smooth projective rational surface other than $\mathbb{P}^2$ is a blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ or of some $F_n$, show that $K_Y^2 = 10 - \rho(Y)$, so $\rho(Y) = 4$, and check that $\rho(Y) - r = 1 = \dim N^1_W(X)$, as Proposition 2.3.5 says.
10. (C) In Counterexample 2.3.18, extend $q \mapsto \ell_q$ linearly to a homomorphism $\lambda \colon \operatorname{Div}(V) \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X,0})$. Show that a nonzero section $s \in H^0(V, L)$, viewed as a function of degree 1 on $X$, has $\operatorname{div}_X(s) = \lambda(\operatorname{div}_V(s))$ near $0$, so that $\lambda$ factors through a homomorphism $\operatorname{Pic}(V) / \mathbb{Z}L \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X,0})$. Using the argument of Counterexample 2.3.18, show that this homomorphism is injective, and deduce that $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X,0})$ contains a copy of the uncountable group $\operatorname{Pic}^0(V) \cong V(\mathbb{C})$, while $\Phi_0 = \mathbb{Z}/d$.
