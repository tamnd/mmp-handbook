# Normal surfaces

The log minimal model program for surfaces leaves the smooth category at its first step: the contraction of a curve of negative square that is not a $(-1)$-curve gives a normal surface with a singular point. This chapter builds the intersection theory and the divisor theory that the program needs on such a surface. The tools are a resolution $f \colon Y \to X$, the negative definite intersection matrix $M$ of its exceptional curves, and linear algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ on $M$.

Section 1 turns Mumford's pullback of Corollary P5.8.5 into an intersection pairing on all Weil divisors of a normal projective surface. The pairing is $\mathbb{Q}$-valued, it does not depend on the resolution, it satisfies the projection formula, and it satisfies the Hodge index theorem. The numerical classes of Weil divisors of $X$ are the classes on $Y$ that are orthogonal to the exceptional curves. The section ends with the minimal resolution, a resolution with no exceptional $(-1)$-curve: it exists by Castelnuovo's criterion, every resolution factors through it by the factorization theorem for birational morphisms, so it is unique, and it is the resolution on which $K_Y$ is nef over $X$. Section 2 asks which Weil divisors are Cartier. A Weil divisor whose Mumford pullback has integer coefficients is *numerically Cartier*. At a rational singularity, numerically Cartier means Cartier, by Lipman's generation theorem, so the local class group embeds in the discriminant group $\mathbb{Z}^r / M\mathbb{Z}^r$ of order $|\det M|$. In particular a surface with rational singularities is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial. Section 3 applies this to the canonical class. It computes the Mumford pullback of $K_X$ from adjunction on each exceptional curve, gives $K_X^2 = K_Y^2 - A^2$ for the discrepancy divisor $A$, compares $K_X \cdot C$ with $K_Y \cdot \tilde C$ on the minimal resolution, and records that a klt surface has rational, hence $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, quotient singularities.

The worked examples are the cone $X_n$ over the rational normal curve of degree $n$, which is the quotient $\frac1n(1,1)$ at its vertex, with $\ell^2 = \frac1n$, $\operatorname{Cl} = \mathbb{Z}/n$, $a = -\frac{n-2}{n}$ and $K_{X_n}^2 = \frac{(n+2)^2}{n}$; the quadric cone, with a line of square $\frac12$ (Example P5.8.9); the cone over an elliptic curve, which has a Cartier canonical divisor and is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, because a general line through the vertex is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier; and the $E_8$ singularity, whose intersection matrix has determinant 1, so that its local ring is factorial. Volume 4, Chapter 3, treats the same singularities locally, as germs, with the emphasis on discrepancies and their classification. This chapter adds the global pairing, the class groups and the canonical class of a projective surface, and cites Volume 4 for the local classification. The chapter ends with problems that compute pullbacks, class groups and canonical classes by hand.
