## Root systems and the number of extremal rays

### Statement

Let $3 \leq r \leq 8$ and $S = S_r$ as in Definition 2.2.1, with $K = k_r = -3\ell + \sum_i E_i$ and $K^2 = 9 - r$. By Theorem 2.2.2 the extremal rays of $NE(S_r)$ are the rays of the $N_r$ exceptional classes. This section counts them with the root systems of Definition P5.10.10: a *root* is $\alpha \in I^{1,r}$ with $\alpha^2 = -2$ and $\alpha \cdot K = 0$, $R_r$ is the set of roots, $s_\alpha(x) = x + (x \cdot \alpha)\alpha$ is the reflection in $\alpha$, and $W_r = W(R_r)$ is the group generated by the $s_\alpha$. Each $s_\alpha$ is an isometry with $s_\alpha(K) = K$, so it permutes the exceptional classes. Put
$$\alpha_0 = \ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3, \qquad \alpha_i = E_i - E_{i+1} \quad (1 \leq i \leq r - 1).$$

**Proposition 2.2.7 (the lattice $K^\perp$).** Let $3 \leq r \leq 8$.

1. The form on $K^\perp \subseteq I^{1,r}$ is negative definite, and $\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{r-1}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $K^\perp$ consisting of roots.
2. For $i \neq j$, $\alpha_i \cdot \alpha_j = 1$ if $i, j \geq 1$ and $|i - j| = 1$, or if $\{i, j\} = \{0, 3\}$, and $\alpha_i \cdot \alpha_j = 0$ otherwise. So the matrix $(-\alpha_i \cdot \alpha_j)$ is the Cartan matrix of the Dynkin diagram with a chain $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{r-1}$ and the extra node $\alpha_0$ joined to $\alpha_3$. Its type is $A_2 \times A_1$, $A_4$, $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$ for $r = 3, \dots, 8$, and its determinant is $9 - r = K^2$.
3. $R_r$ is finite.

**Proposition 2.2.8 (the roots).** The $240$ roots of $I^{1,8}$ are
$$\pm(E_i - E_j) \ (56), \qquad \pm(\ell - E_i - E_j - E_k) \ (112), \qquad \pm(2\ell - \textstyle\sum_6 E) \ (56), \qquad \pm(3\ell - 2E_i - \textstyle\sum_{j \neq i} E_j) \ (16),$$
where $\sum_6 E$ is a sum of six distinct $E_j$. For $r \leq 8$, $R_r$ is the set of roots of $I^{1,8}$ involving only $E_1, \dots, E_r$, and
$$|R_r| = r(r - 1) + 2\binom{r}{3} + 2\binom{r}{6} + 16\,[r = 8] = 8, \ 20, \ 40, \ 72, \ 126, \ 240 \qquad (r = 3, \dots, 8).$$

**Theorem 2.2.9 (the Weyl group is transitive on the extremal rays).** Let $3 \leq r \leq 8$. Then $W_r$ acts transitively on the exceptional classes of $I^{1,r}$, that is, on the extremal rays of $NE(S_r)$. More precisely, every exceptional class $D = (a; b_1, \dots, b_r)$ with $a > 0$ and $b_1 \geq b_2 \geq b_3$ the three largest $b_i$ has $b_1 + b_2 + b_3 > a$, and $s_{\alpha}(D)$, for $\alpha = \ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3$, has degree $2a - (b_1 + b_2 + b_3) < a$.

**Corollary 2.2.10 (the number of extremal rays).** For $3 \leq r \leq 8$ the stabilizer of $E_r$ in $W_r$ is $W_{r-1}$, and
$$N_r = \frac{|W_r|}{|W_{r-1}|}.$$
With the orders $|W_2| = 2$, $|W(A_2 \times A_1)| = 12$, $|W(A_4)| = 120$, $|W(D_5)| = 1920$, $|W(E_6)| = 51840$, $|W(E_7)| = 2903040$ and $|W(E_8)| = 696729600$, this gives the table

| $r$ | $\rho(S_r)$ | $K^2$ | type of $R_r$ | $\lvert R_r \rvert$ | $N_r$ | $\lvert W_r \rvert$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 8 | none | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 3 | 7 | $A_1$ | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| 3 | 4 | 6 | $A_2 \times A_1$ | 8 | 6 | 12 |

| 5 | 6 | 4 | $D_5$ | 40 | 16 | 1920 |
| 6 | 7 | 3 | $E_6$ | 72 | 27 | 51840 |

### Proof in words

The anticanonical class is ample and has positive square, so by the Hodge index theorem its orthogonal complement is negative definite, and the vectors of square $-2$ in it are finite in number. Writing a vector of $K^\perp$ as a multiple of $\alpha_0$ plus a vector with coefficient sum zero in the $E_i$ shows that the $\alpha_i$ are a basis, and their products are the Dynkin diagram $T_{2,3,r-3}$. The roots of $I^{1,8}$ are found by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, which bounds the degree by $4$, and a case check. For the transitivity, the Cremona reflection in $\ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3$ lowers the degree of an exceptional class whenever the three largest multiplicities sum to more than the degree, and the bound $r \leq 8$ forces this. So every class descends to some $E_i$, and the reflections in $E_i - E_j$ permute the $E_i$. The stabilizer of $E_r$ is the parabolic subgroup generated by the reflections that fix it, the Weyl group of $R_{r-1}$, and the orbit-stabilizer formula gives the counts.

### Examples

**Example 2.2.11 (the Geiser and Bertini involutions).** For $r = 7$ let $\gamma(x) = -x + (x \cdot K)K$. Then $\gamma(K) = -K + 2K = K$, and $\gamma(x) \cdot \gamma(y) = x \cdot y - 2(x \cdot K)(y \cdot K) + (x \cdot K)(y \cdot K) K^2 = x \cdot y$, since $K^2 = 2$. So $\gamma$ is an isometry fixing $K$, it acts as $-1$ on $K^\perp$, and it sends an exceptional class $D$ to $-D - K$. For $D = E_7$ this is $3\ell - E_1 - \dots - E_6 - 2E_7$, the class $3\ell - 2E_i - \Sigma_6$ of Theorem P5.9.26, and for $D = \ell - E_1 - E_2$ it is $2\ell - E_3 - E_4 - E_5 - E_6 - E_7$. The $56$ classes fall into $28$ pairs $\{D, -K - D\}$, and $D \cdot (-K - D) = 1 + 1 = 2$. Since $-1$ is in $W(E_7)$ (Bourbaki, Lie groups, Chapter VI, Plate VI), $\gamma \in W_7$. For $r = 8$ the same computation with $K^2 = 1$ gives the isometry $\beta(x) = -x + 2(x \cdot K)K$, with $\beta(D) = -D - 2K$ and $D \cdot \beta(D) = 2 + 1 = 3$. The $240$ classes fall into $120$ pairs. For example $\beta(E_8) = 6\ell - 2E_1 - \dots - 2E_7 - 3E_8$, and $\beta(\ell - E_1 - E_2) = 5\ell - E_1 - E_2 - 2E_3 - \dots - 2E_8$. Section 4 realizes $\gamma$ and $\beta$ as the covering involutions of the anticanonical double covers.

**Example 2.2.12 (how the $(-1)$-curves meet a given one).** Fix the $(-1)$-curve $E_r$. An exceptional class $D \neq E_r$ has $D \cdot E_r = b_r \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, and the classes with $b_r = 0$ are the exceptional classes of $I^{1,r-1}$. A computer enumeration of the solutions of $a^2 - \sum b_i^2 = -1$, $3a - \sum b_i = 1$ with $|a| \leq 12$ gives these counts:

| $r$ | $D \cdot E_r = 0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 16 | 10 | 0 | 0 |
| 7 | 27 | 27 | 1 | 0 |
| 8 | 56 | 126 | 56 | 1 |

The first column is $N_{r-1}$, in accordance with Corollary 2.2.10. The single class meeting $E_7$ twice is $\gamma(E_7) = -K - E_7$, and the single class meeting $E_8$ three times is $\beta(E_8)$. On the cubic surface each line meets ten others and is disjoint from sixteen. The rows add up to $N_r - 1$: $26$, $55$ and $239$.

### Proof

*Proposition 2.2.7.* (1) By Theorem P5.9.26 (1), $-K$ is ample on $S_r$, and $\operatorname{Num}(S_r) = I^{1,r}$. By Theorem P5.5.22, $x^2 < 0$ for every $x \neq 0$ with $x \cdot K = 0$, so the form on $K^\perp$ is negative definite. Each $\alpha_i$ is a root: $\alpha_0^2 = 1 - 3 = -2$ and $\alpha_0 \cdot K = -3 + 3 = 0$, and $\alpha_i^2 = -1 - 1 = -2$ and $\alpha_i \cdot K = -1 + 1 = 0$ for $i \geq 1$. Let $x = a\ell - \sum_i b_i E_i \in K^\perp$, so $x \cdot K = -3a + \sum_i b_i = 0$. Then
$$x - a\alpha_0 = -(b_1 - a)E_1 - (b_2 - a)E_2 - (b_3 - a)E_3 - \sum_{i \geq 4} b_i E_i = \sum_i c_i E_i, \qquad \sum_i c_i = 3a - \sum_i b_i = 0.$$
A vector $\sum_i c_i E_i$ with $\sum_i c_i = 0$ is $\sum_{i=1}^{r-1} (c_1 + \dots + c_i) \alpha_i$, by summation by parts. So the $\alpha_i$ span $K^\perp$ over $\mathbb{Z}$. They are $r$ vectors in a lattice of rank $r$, since $K^\perp$ is the kernel of the nonzero linear form $x \mapsto x \cdot K$ on $I^{1,r} \cong \mathbb{Z}^{r+1}$, so they are a basis.

(2) For $1 \leq i < j$, $\alpha_i \cdot \alpha_j = (E_i - E_{i+1}) \cdot (E_j - E_{j+1})$ is $-E_{i+1}^2 = 1$ if $j = i + 1$ and $0$ if $j > i + 1$. For $i \geq 1$, $\alpha_0 \cdot \alpha_i = (\ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3) \cdot (E_i - E_{i+1})$ equals $1 - 1 = 0$ for $i = 1, 2$, equals $1 - 0 = 1$ for $i = 3$, and $0$ for $i \geq 4$. So $-\alpha_i \cdot \alpha_j$ is $2$ on the diagonal, $-1$ for the edges $\alpha_i \alpha_{i+1}$ and $\alpha_0 \alpha_3$, and $0$ otherwise. For $r = 3$ there is no $\alpha_3$, and the diagram is the chain $\alpha_1 \alpha_2$ and the isolated node $\alpha_0$, of type $A_2 \times A_1$. For $r = 4$ it is the chain $\alpha_1 \alpha_2 \alpha_3 \alpha_0$, of type $A_4$. For $r \geq 5$ the node $\alpha_3$ has three neighbours $\alpha_2$, $\alpha_4$ and $\alpha_0$, with arms of $2$, $r - 4$ and $1$ nodes, which is $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$ for $r = 5, 6, 7, 8$. The determinants of the Cartan matrices of these types are $3 \cdot 2 = 6$, $5$, $4$, $3$, $2$, $1$ (Bourbaki, Lie groups, Chapter VI, Plates I to VII), and a direct elimination of the $r \times r$ matrices gives the same numbers. They equal $9 - r = K^2$. This is a general fact: $I^{1,r}$ is unimodular and $K$ is primitive, so the discriminant of $K^\perp$ is $\pm K^2$.

(3) A negative definite lattice has finitely many vectors of a given square. $\square$

*Proposition 2.2.8.* Let $\alpha = a\ell - \sum_i b_i E_i \in I^{1,8}$ be a root. Then $\sum_i b_i = 3a$ and $\sum_i b_i^2 = a^2 + 2$. Replacing $\alpha$ by $-\alpha$ assume $a \geq 0$. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, $9a^2 = (\sum_i b_i)^2 \leq 8 \sum_i b_i^2 = 8a^2 + 16$, so $a \leq 4$.

For $a = 0$: $\sum b_i = 0$ and $\sum b_i^2 = 2$, so $b$ has one entry $1$ and one entry $-1$, and $\alpha = E_j - E_i$, giving $8 \cdot 7 = 56$ roots. For $a = 1$: $\sum b_i^2 = 3$ forces three entries $\pm 1$, and $\sum b_i = 3$ forces all three to be $1$, giving $\binom83 = 56$ roots. For $a = 2$: $\sum b_i^2 = 6$ and $\sum b_i = 6$. Since $b_i^2 \geq b_i$ with equality only for $b_i \in \{0, 1\}$, all $b_i$ are $0$ or $1$, so six entries are $1$, giving $\binom86 = 28$ roots. For $a = 3$: $\sum b_i^2 = 11$ and $\sum b_i = 9$, so $\sum b_i(b_i - 1) = 2$. Each term $b_i(b_i - 1)$ is $0$ for $b_i \in \{0, 1\}$, $2$ for $b_i \in \{-1, 2\}$, and at least $6$ otherwise. So exactly one $b_i$ is $-1$ or $2$ and the others are $0$ or $1$. If it is $-1$, the others sum to $10 > 7$, impossible. If it is $2$, the other seven sum to $7$, so all are $1$, giving $8$ roots $3\ell - 2E_i - \sum_{j \neq i} E_j$. For $a = 4$: $\sum b_i = 12$ and $\sum b_i^2 = 18$, and Cauchy-Schwarz is an equality, $144 = 8 \cdot 18$, so all $b_i$ are equal to $\frac32$, which is not an integer. So there are $56 + 2(56 + 28 + 8) = 240$ roots, as listed.

The inclusion $I^{1,r} \subseteq I^{1,8}$ preserves the form and sends $k_r$ to a class with $x \cdot k_8 = x \cdot k_r$ for $x \in I^{1,r}$, since $E_j \cdot x = 0$ for $j > r$. So $R_r$ is the set of roots of $I^{1,8}$ with $b_j = 0$ for $j > r$. In the list, $\pm(E_i - E_j)$ gives $r(r - 1)$ of these, $\pm(\ell - E_i - E_j - E_k)$ gives $2\binom r3$, $\pm(2\ell - \sum_6 E)$ gives $2\binom r6$, and the last family involves all eight $E_j$, so it contributes only for $r = 8$. The numbers are $6 + 2 = 8$, $12 + 8 = 20$, $20 + 20 = 40$, $30 + 40 + 2 = 72$, $42 + 70 + 14 = 126$ and $56 + 112 + 56 + 16 = 240$. $\square$

*Theorem 2.2.9.* Let $D = (a; b_1, \dots, b_r)$ be an exceptional class with $a > 0$. By Theorem P5.9.26 (2), $D$ is the class of a $(-1)$-curve $C$ on $S_r$, and $C \neq E_i$ since $D \cdot \ell = a > 0$, so $b_i = C \cdot E_i \geq 0$ by Proposition P5.5.5. Renumber so that $b_1 \geq b_2 \geq \dots \geq b_r \geq 0$; the reflections $s_{E_i - E_j}$ realize the renumbering inside $W_r$, since $s_{E_i - E_j}$ exchanges $E_i$ and $E_j$ and fixes $\ell$ and the other $E_m$. Put $s = b_1 + b_2 + b_3$. Suppose $s \leq a$. Then $b_3 \leq \frac{s}{3}$ and $b_i \leq b_3$ for $i \geq 3$, so
$$3a - 1 = \sum_i b_i \leq b_1 + b_2 + (r - 2) b_3 \leq b_1 + b_2 + 6 b_3 = s + 5 b_3 \leq \tfrac{8}{3} s \leq \tfrac83 a,$$
which gives $a \leq 3$. The exceptional classes of degree $a \leq 3$ are those of Theorem P5.9.26 (2): $\ell - E_i - E_j$ with $s = 2 > 1$, $2\ell - \Sigma_5$ with $s = 3 > 2$, and $3\ell - 2E_i - \Sigma_6$ with $s = 4 > 3$. This contradicts $s \leq a$, so $s > a$.

Let $\alpha = \ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3 \in R_r$, which needs $r \geq 3$. Then $D \cdot \alpha = a - s < 0$ and $s_\alpha(D) = D + (a - s)\alpha$ has degree $a + (a - s) = 2a - s < a$. It is again an exceptional class, and its degree is nonnegative by Proposition P5.10.17 (1). By induction on the degree, some $w \in W_r$ sends $D$ to an exceptional class of degree $0$, which is some $E_i$ by Proposition P5.10.17 (1), and $s_{E_i - E_r}$ sends $E_i$ to $E_r$. So every exceptional class is in the orbit of $E_r$. By Theorem 2.2.2 (2) the exceptional classes are the extremal rays of $NE(S_r)$. $\square$

*Corollary 2.2.10.* Every $w \in W_r$ fixes $K$, so $W_r$ preserves $V = K^\perp \otimes \mathbb{R}$, on which $-(\ ,\ )$ is a Euclidean inner product by Proposition 2.2.7 (1). An element of $W_r$ that is the identity on $V$ and fixes $K$ is the identity, because $V$ and $K$ span $I^{1,r} \otimes \mathbb{R}$, as $K^2 \neq 0$. So $W_r$ is the finite reflection group of the root system $R_r$ in $V$. Write $E_r = \frac{E_r \cdot K}{K^2} K + e$ with $e \in V$. An element of $W_r$ fixes $E_r$ if and only if it fixes $e$. By Steinberg's theorem on finite reflection groups (Humphreys, Reflection groups and Coxeter groups, Theorem 1.12 (c)), the stabilizer of $e$ is generated by the reflections $s_\alpha$ that fix $e$, that is, by those with $\alpha \cdot e = 0$, equivalently $\alpha \cdot E_r = 0$ since $\alpha \cdot K = 0$. The roots of $I^{1,r}$ orthogonal to $E_r$ are the roots with $b_r = 0$, which form $R_{r-1}$ by the second part of the proof of Proposition 2.2.8. So the stabilizer of $E_r$ is $W_{r-1}$, and Theorem 2.2.9 with the orbit-stabilizer formula gives $N_r = |W_r| / |W_{r-1}|$.

The orders are those of Bourbaki, Lie groups, Chapter VI, Plates I to VII: $|W(A_n)| = (n + 1)!$, $|W(D_5)| = 2^4 \cdot 5! = 1920$, and $|W(E_6)|$, $|W(E_7)|$, $|W(E_8)|$ as in the table; $|W(E_6)| = 51840$ is also Theorem P5.10.12 (3). The quotients are $\frac{12}{2} = 6$, $\frac{120}{12} = 10$, $\frac{1920}{120} = 16$, $\frac{51840}{1920} = 27$, $\frac{2903040}{51840} = 56$ and $\frac{696729600}{2903040} = 240$. They agree with Theorem P5.9.26 (2). As an independent check, a computer enumeration of the exceptional classes of $I^{1,r}$ with $|a| \leq 12$, which contains all of them by the Cauchy-Schwarz bound $(3a - 1)^2 \leq r(a^2 + 1)$, finds $1, 3, 6, 10, 16, 27, 56, 240$ classes for $r = 1, \dots, 8$, distributed by degree as $8 + 28 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 28 + 8$ for $r = 8$, and it finds $0, 2, 8, 20, 40, 72, 126, 240$ roots. The rows $r = 1, 2$ of the table are Examples P5.12.7 and P5.12.17. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) For $r = 6$, write the exceptional class $G_1 = 2\ell - E_2 - E_3 - E_4 - E_5 - E_6$ as $w(E_6)$ for an explicit product $w$ of reflections, following the proof of Theorem 2.2.9.
2. (A) Show that $L - L'$ is a root for any two disjoint $(-1)$-curves $L, L'$ on $S_r$, and that $L + L'$ is a class of square $-2$ and $K$-degree $-2$ that spans no extremal ray.
3. (B) Show that for $r = 2$ the roots $\pm(E_1 - E_2)$ do not span $K^\perp$, and find a basis of $K^\perp \subseteq I^{1,2}$ and its Gram matrix. What is its determinant?
4. (B) Verify the count $|R_7| = 126$ by listing the roots of $I^{1,7}$ by degree, and check that the number of positive roots, $63$, is the number of reflections in $W(E_7)$.
5. (B) Show that every exceptional class $D$ of $I^{1,8}$ gives a root $D + K$, and that this map is a bijection, as in Proposition P5.10.17 (3). Use it and Proposition 2.2.8 to recount the exceptional classes of $I^{1,8}$ by degree.
6. (C) Let $\mathcal{C}_r$ be the set of ordered sequences $(D_1, \dots, D_r)$ of pairwise orthogonal exceptional classes of $I^{1,r}$. Show that $W_r$ acts simply transitively on $\mathcal{C}_r$ for $3 \leq r \leq 8$, generalizing Theorem P5.10.12 (2), and deduce $|\mathcal{C}_r| = |W_r|$.

### Notes and history

The link between the $27$ lines and $W(E_6)$ goes back to Jordan and to Coble. The systematic treatment by exceptional classes, roots and the Weyl groups $W(E_r)$ is Manin, Cubic forms, Chapter IV, Sections 23 to 26, and Demazure, Surfaces de del Pezzo, Séminaire sur les singularités des surfaces, 1980. Dolgachev, Classical algebraic geometry, Chapter 8, has the tables. The descent argument of Theorem 2.2.9 is the standard Noether inequality for exceptional classes: the three largest multiplicities of a curve of degree $a$ with $-K \cdot C = 1$ sum to more than $a$. For $r = 9$ the same argument fails and the orbit is infinite, which is Section 4.
