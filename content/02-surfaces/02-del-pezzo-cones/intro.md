# Del Pezzo surfaces and their cones

A del Pezzo surface is a smooth projective surface with ample anticanonical divisor. Chapter P5.9 classifies them as $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and the blow-ups $S_r$ of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in $r \leq 8$ points in general position, and Chapter P5.10 studies the cubic surface and the blow-ups in eight and nine points. This chapter reads the same surfaces from the point of view of the minimal model program. Since $-K$ is ample, every curve is $K$-negative, the cone theorem has no $K$-positive part, and the cone of curves is a rational polyhedral cone whose rays are all contractible. The question is then what these cones look like, how many extremal rays they have, and in how many ways the program can be run.

Section 1 proves that the cone of curves of $S_r$ is spanned by the $(-1)$-curves once $\rho \geq 3$, describes the nef and ample cones dually, and works out the prism of $S_3$ and the failure of general position for three collinear points. Section 2 identifies $K^\perp$ with the root lattices $A_2 \times A_1$, $A_4$, $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, proves that the Weyl group acts transitively on the exceptional classes, and counts the extremal rays as $N_r = |W_r| / |W_{r-1}|$, which gives $6, 10, 16, 27, 56, 240$ for $3 \leq r \leq 8$. Section 3 lists the extremal contractions and counts the runs of the program from $S_r$: every run ends at $\mathbb{P}^2$, at $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ or at $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$, and each end is reached by exactly $|W_r|$ runs. Section 4 treats degrees $2$ and $1$ through the anticanonical double covers of $\mathbb{P}^2$ and of the quadric cone, and then the plane blown up in nine base points of a cubic pencil, where the cone of curves is no longer polyhedral and the program has infinitely many runs.

The examples are the $36$ runs from $S_3$, the relative runs of a conic bundle, the conic bundle structures on $S_r$, the Geiser and Bertini involutions, the $28$ bitangents of a plane quartic, the $120$ tritangent planes of a space sextic, and the $(-1)$-curves of the nine-point blow-up indexed by the lattice $E_8$. The chapter ends with problems that compute nef cones, involutions, conic bundles and the sections of the elliptic fibration by hand.
