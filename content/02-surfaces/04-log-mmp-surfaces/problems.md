## Problems

Each problem runs part of the log minimal model program by hand: compute the intersection numbers on the minimal resolution of Theorem 2.3.8, decide the singularities with one log resolution, and follow the discrepancies through the contractions.

1. (A) Let $p_1, \dots, p_4 \in \mathbb{P}^2$ be four points with no three on a line, and $L_1, \dots, L_6$ the six lines through two of them. Show that each $p_i$ lies on exactly three of the lines and that the lines meet in three further points, each on exactly two lines. For $\Delta = a\sum_j L_j$ with $0 \le a \le 1$, show that $(\mathbb{P}^2, \Delta)$ is klt if and only if $a < \frac23$, lc if and only if $a \le \frac23$, and that $K + \Delta$ is nef if and only if $a \ge \frac12$. Deduce that $(\mathbb{P}^2, \frac12 \sum_j L_j)$ is a klt pair with $K + \Delta \equiv 0$.
2. (A) Let $L_1, L_2, L_3$ be concurrent lines and $L_4$ a line not through their common point, and $\Delta = a(L_1 + L_2 + L_3) + bL_4$. Show that no klt pair of this form has $K + \Delta \equiv 0$, and find the lc pairs of this form with $K + \Delta \equiv 0$.
3. (A) On $F_5$ let $\Delta = bC_-$ with $0 \le b < 1$. Find the values of $b$ for which the ray of $C_-$ is $(K + \Delta)$-negative, compute the number $a$ of Theorem 2.4.10 for $b = \frac45$, and check that $a(C_-, \mathbb{P}(1,1,5), 0) = -\frac35$ in two ways: by part 4 of Theorem 2.4.10 and by Example 4.3.7.
4. (A) For $X = \mathbb{P}(1,1,3)$ compute $\ell^2$, $K_X^2$, $-K_X \cdot \ell$ and the smallest $m \ge 1$ such that $mK_X$ is Cartier. Check that $K_X^2 = \frac{(n+2)^2}{n}$ with $n = 3$.
5. (B) On $F_n$ with $n \ge 2$ let $\Delta = bC_- + \sum_{j=1}^m \beta_j F_j$ for distinct fibres $F_j$ and $0 \le b, \beta_j < 1$. Show that the pair is klt, that the ray of $F$ is always negative, so that $(F_n, \Delta)$ has no log minimal model, and that the ray of $C_-$ is negative if and only if $\sum_j \beta_j < 2 - n(1 - b)$. In that case contract $C_-$ and check that the image $(\mathbb{P}(1,1,n), \sum_j \beta_j \ell_j)$ is klt, as Theorem 2.4.10 predicts, by comparing with the condition $\sum_j \beta_j < 2$ of Example 2.4.23.
6. (B) Let $n \ge 3$ and $(X, \Delta) = (F_n, C_-)$, an lc pair that is not klt. Show that the ray of $C_-$ is negative with $-(K + \Delta) \cdot C_- = 2$, that the contraction $\varphi \colon F_n \to \mathbb{P}(1,1,n)$ of Example 2.4.13 satisfies $K + C_- = \varphi^*K_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)} + aC_-$ with $a = \frac2n$, and that $a(C_-, \mathbb{P}(1,1,n), 0) = -1 + \frac2n$. Which hypothesis of Lemma 2.4.8 fails for the configuration $\{C_-\}$ with $\Theta = C_-$, and why does the contraction exist anyway?
7. (B) In Example 2.4.15, let $\sigma_p$ be the fibre of the second projection of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ through the first blown-up point, $\tilde\sigma$ its strict transform on $W$ and $s = f(\tilde\sigma)$. Show that $\tilde\sigma$ is a $(-1)$-curve meeting $E_1'$ once and no other exceptional curve of $f$, that $f^*s = \tilde\sigma + \frac12 E_1'$, $s^2 = -\frac12$ and $K_X \cdot s = -1$. Deduce that $\overline{NE}(X) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}[\Gamma] + \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}[s]$, compute $a = 2$ for the contraction of $s$, and show that the contraction is $X \to \mathbb{P}(1,1,2)$ by contracting $\tilde\sigma$ and then $E_1'$ on $W$ and identifying the result with $F_2$.
8. (C) Let $n \ge 2$, $q \in C_- \subset F_n$, $b \colon W \to F_n$ the blow-up of $q$ with exceptional curve $E$, $C'$ and $F'$ the strict transforms of $C_-$ and of the fibre through $q$, and $f \colon W \to X$ the contraction of $C'$, a $(-(n+1))$-curve, to a point of type $\frac{1}{n+1}(1,1)$. Show that $\rho(X) = 2$ and that $\overline{NE}(X)$ is spanned by $e = f(E)$ and $f(F')$. Compute $e^2 = -\frac{n}{n+1}$, $K_X \cdot e = -\frac{2}{n+1}$, $f(F')^2 = -1$ and $K_X \cdot f(F') = -1$. Show that contracting $e$ gives $\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)$ with $a = \frac2n$, that contracting $f(F')$ gives $\mathbb{P}(1,1,n+1)$ with $a = 1$, and check part 4 of Theorem 2.4.10 on the divisor $C'$ in the first case: $-\frac{n-1}{n+1} + \frac2n \cdot \frac{1}{n+1} = -\frac{n-2}{n}$.
9. (C) Let $X$ be the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in the four points of Problem 1, with exceptional curves $E_1, \dots, E_4$, and $\Delta = a\sum_j \tilde L_j$ for the strict transforms of the six lines, $0 \le a < 1$. Show that $\Delta$ has simple normal crossings, that $(K + \Delta) \cdot \tilde L_j = -1$ for every $j$ and $(K + \Delta) \cdot E_i = 3a - 1$. For $a < \frac13$, run the program by contracting $E_1, \dots, E_4$, identify the output with $(\mathbb{P}^2, a\sum_j L_j)$, and check that $a(E_i, \mathbb{P}^2, a\sum_j L_j) = 1 - 3a$ agrees with Example 2.4.5. For $a = \frac12$, show that $K + \Delta = -\frac12 \sum_i E_i$ and that $(K + \Delta) \cdot (-K_X) < 0$, and deduce that $(X, \Delta)$ has no log minimal model.
10. (C) On $\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)$ with $n \ge 1$, let $G_1', \dots, G_m'$ be the images of $m$ general members of $|C_- + nF|$ on $F_n$, which avoid $C_-$, meet pairwise transversally and have no point in common to three of them. Show that $(\mathbb{P}(1,1,n), \gamma\sum_j G_j')$ is klt for $0 \le \gamma < 1$ and that $K + \Delta \equiv (m\gamma n - n - 2)\ell$. Find, for each $n$, the least $m$ for which some $\gamma < 1$ makes $K + \Delta$ ample, and check the answer for $n = 1$, where $\mathbb{P}(1,1,1) = \mathbb{P}^2$ and the $G_j'$ are lines.
