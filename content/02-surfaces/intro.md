# The MMP for surfaces

The minimal model program in dimension 2 is the model for every later volume. Its steps are contractions of curves, there are no flips, and every statement can be checked on explicit surfaces by intersection numbers. Part P5 of the prerequisites proves the absolute smooth case: Castelnuovo's criterion (Theorem P5.7.4), the cone theorem and the three types of extremal rays (Theorems P5.12.14 and P5.12.15), and the existence and uniqueness of minimal models (Theorem P5.11.14 and Corollary P5.11.19). This volume takes these results as known and proves the versions that the program in higher dimension needs: over a base, for singular surfaces, and for pairs.

> **The surface box.** For a smooth projective surface $S$ and a projective morphism $f \colon S \to Z$, a curve $C$ is $f$-contracted if $f(C)$ is a point, and $K_S$ is $f$-nef if $K_S \cdot C \geq 0$ for every $f$-contracted curve. A $K_S$-negative extremal ray of the relative cone is spanned by a smooth rational curve, and its contraction is one of three kinds: the blow-down of a $(-1)$-curve in a fibre, a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over a curve, or $\mathbb{P}^2$ over a point. A run of the program over $Z$ contracts $(-1)$-curves in fibres until $K$ is $f$-nef or a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle or $\mathbb{P}^2$ appears.

Chapter 1 proves the relative cone and contraction theorems for a morphism of a smooth surface, treats the Mori fibre spaces over curves and the elementary transformations that connect them, and proves that a relative minimal model with $K$ nef over the base is unique. Chapter 2 studies the del Pezzo surfaces, the plane blown up in at most eight points, whose cones are spanned by finitely many $(-1)$-curves arranged by the root systems, together with the plane blown up in nine points, where the cone has infinitely many rays. Chapter 3 passes to normal surfaces: Mumford's intersection theory, the minimal resolution, and the quotient singularities, which are the klt surface germs. Chapter 4 proves the cone and contraction theorems for log canonical and klt surface pairs and runs the log program. Chapter 5 proves abundance for surfaces, the semiampleness of a nef canonical divisor, and reads the Enriques-Kodaira classification as the output of the program.

The spine is Reid's *Chapters on algebraic surfaces* (Rei97), Beauville's *Complex algebraic surfaces* (Bea96), and Matsuki's *Introduction to the Mori program*, Chapter 1 (Mat02). Volume 1 supplies the language of divisors, cones and contractions in every dimension, and Volume 4, Chapter 3, treats the singularities of surfaces from the point of view of discrepancies. This volume uses both and does not repeat them.
