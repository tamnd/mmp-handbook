# The rationality theorem

This chapter proves Kawamata's rationality theorem. Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a projective klt pair of dimension $n$ with $K_X + \Delta$ not nef, $a \ge 1$ an integer with $a(K_X + \Delta)$ Cartier, and $H$ an ample Cartier divisor. The nef threshold $r = \max\{ t : H + t(K_X + \Delta) \text{ nef} \}$ is then a rational number $u/v$ with $v \le a(n + 1)$. Chapter 4 applies the theorem to a family of ample divisors to show that the $(K_X + \Delta)$-negative part of the cone of curves is cut out by rational hyperplanes, and the denominator bound is what makes the extremal rays discrete. The ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$.

Section 1 proves the lemma on polynomials that converts the theorem into a statement about base loci. A polynomial of degree at most $n$ in two variables that vanishes at all lattice points of the strip $0 < ay - rx < 1$, far enough out, is zero unless $r = u/v$ with $v \le a(n + 1)$: for an irrational $r$, or a denominator that is too large, the lattice points of the strip lie on too many parallel lines.

Section 2 proves the theorem. On the strip, Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing identifies $h^0(pH + qa(K_X + \Delta))$ with a polynomial $P(p, q)$ of degree at most $n$, and the divisors there are not nef, so their base loci are nonempty. A minimal base locus among them, tie-breaking and vanishing on a resolution give a second polynomial $Q$ on a divisor $F_0$, and the lemma, applied to $Q$, produces a lattice point of the strip whose base locus is strictly smaller. The corollary is the nef value morphism, the contraction defined by $H + r(K_X + \Delta)$, and the examples compute $r$ on projective space, where the bound is sharp, on the plane with a boundary, on surfaces with two rulings or a cone point, and on the plane blown up at a point with two choices of $H$.
