# The nonvanishing theorem

This chapter proves Shokurov's nonvanishing theorem: on a smooth projective variety, a nef Cartier divisor $D$ such that $aD + G - K_X$ is nef and big for a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $G$ with snc support and $\lceil G \rceil \ge 0$ has $H^0(X, mD + \lceil G \rceil) \neq 0$ for every $m \gg 0$. The corollary for klt pairs, $H^0(X, mD) \neq 0$ when $aD - (K_X + \Delta)$ is nef and big, is Step 1 of the base point free theorem of Chapter 2. Throughout, the ground field $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$, since the proofs use Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing in the form of Corollary 5.1.14 and resolution of singularities in the form of Theorem 4.2.10 and Theorem P7.8.15.

Section 1 proves three lemmas used in all three chapters. The first compares a nef divisor with an ample one through intersection numbers. The second produces a log resolution on which the pullback of an ample divisor stays ample after subtracting a small effective divisor with snc support. The third is the tie-breaking lemma: given a log resolution, the discrepancies $a_j > -1$ and the multiplicities $r_j$ of a divisor through a base locus, it picks a threshold $c$ and a small perturbation so that exactly one coefficient of $\sum_j (a_j - c r_j) F_j$ equals $-1$. The examples compute the threshold for a cusp and break a three-way tie for two crossing lines.

Section 2 proves the nonvanishing theorem by induction on the dimension, following KM98, Theorem 3.4. If $D$ is numerically trivial, Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing makes $h^0(mD + \lceil G \rceil)$ a polynomial in $m$ that is positive at $m = 0$. Otherwise a divisor in a multiple of $eD + A$ with high multiplicity at a point gives a threshold $c$, the tie-breaking lemma isolates a divisor $F_0$ on a resolution, the hypothesis holds on $F_0$ in dimension one less, and vanishing lifts the sections from $F_0$. The examples show that bigness cannot be dropped.
