# The cone theorem for klt pairs

This chapter proves the cone theorem for a projective klt pair $(X, \Delta)$ over a field of characteristic $0$. The input is the rationality theorem of Chapter 3 and the base point free theorem of Chapter 2, which rest on the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing of Volume 5. No rational curves enter the proof of the decomposition: the extremal rays appear as faces cut out by nef divisors of the form $A + r(K_X + \Delta)$, and the curves in them are found afterwards.

Section 1 proves the cone theorem, Theorem 7.4.6. Thresholds of ample divisors are rational with bounded denominators, the cone is the closure of the $K_X + \Delta$-nonnegative part and the negative exposed rays, the closure is not needed, and the negative rays are discrete away from the hyperplane $(K_X + \Delta)^\perp$. Every negative extremal face has a supporting divisor $L$ with $L - (K_X + \Delta)$ ample, and the cone of a log Fano variety is polyhedral.

Section 2 puts curves in the rays. The base point free theorem gives an integral curve in every negative face, and Kawamata's theorem, cited, gives a rational curve $\Gamma$ in every negative ray with $-(K_X + \Delta) \cdot \Gamma \le 2 \dim X$. The relative cone theorem over a projective base follows, because the relative cone is a face.

Section 3 applies the theorem to log del Pezzo surfaces. Their cones are polyhedral, spanned by rational curves of length at most $4$. The examples are the smooth del Pezzo surfaces of Volume 6, the weighted projective planes $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$, and the Hirzebruch surfaces $\mathbb{F}_n$ with $n \ge 3$, which carry a log del Pezzo boundary although they are not del Pezzo surfaces.
