# Cone and contraction theorems

This volume proves the cone and contraction theorems for projective klt pairs over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, by the method of Kawamata, Reid, Shokurov and Kollár, which replaces the rational curves of Volume 6 with vanishing theorems. The input is Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for klt pairs from Volume 5, the discrepancies of Volume 4, and, for comparison and for the length of extremal rays, the cone theorem for smooth varieties and bend-and-break from Volume 6. Four theorems are proved in turn: nonvanishing, base point freeness, rationality, and the cone theorem; the contraction theorem follows from the first three and the cone theorem.

> **The discrepancy box.** For a projective birational $f \colon Y \to X$ and a pair $(X, \Delta)$, write $K_Y + \Delta_Y = f^*(K_X + \Delta)$ with $f_*\Delta_Y = \Delta$. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the *discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) = -\operatorname{mult}_E \Delta_Y$, and the *log discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) + 1$. The pair is terminal or canonical if $a > 0$ or $a \ge 0$ for every exceptional $E$ over $X$. It is klt or lc if $a > -1$ or $a \ge -1$ for every prime divisor $E$ over $X$, exceptional or not. For a divisor $E$ on $X$ itself, $a(E, X, \Delta)$ is minus the coefficient of $E$ in $\Delta$, so klt asks for coefficients less than 1 and lc for coefficients at most 1. The Lean code states the same conditions with the log discrepancy, over all divisorial valuations.

In this volume the box is used in one way above all. On a log resolution $f \colon Y \to X$ of a klt pair, $K_Y = f^*(K_X + \Delta) + \sum_j a_j F_j$ with every $a_j > -1$, and the round-up $\lceil \sum_j a_j F_j \rceil$ is effective and $f$-exceptional. Adding a multiple $c$ of a divisor through a base locus lowers the $a_j$, and the largest $c$ that keeps every coefficient at least $-1$ is a log canonical threshold. After a perturbation exactly one coefficient equals $-1$, and that divisor $F_0$ is where the induction on dimension takes place: Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing on $Y$ lifts sections from $F_0$.

Chapter 1 proves the nonvanishing theorem of Shokurov. For a nef Cartier divisor $D$ on a smooth projective variety and a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $G$ with snc support and $\lceil G \rceil \ge 0$ such that $aD + G - K_X$ is nef and big, the divisor $mD + \lceil G \rceil$ has sections for $m \gg 0$. The chapter also proves the tie-breaking lemma that the next two chapters use, and the corollary for klt pairs.

Chapter 2 proves the base point free theorem of Kawamata and Shokurov. If $(X, \Delta)$ is klt, $D$ is nef Cartier and $aD - (K_X + \Delta)$ is nef and big, then $|bD|$ is free for $b \gg 0$. The proof is a Noetherian induction on base loci, and the corollaries give the contraction defined by $D$, the semiampleness of a nef and big $K_X + \Delta$, and the equality of linear and numerical equivalence on klt pairs with $-(K_X + \Delta)$ nef and big.

Chapter 3 proves Kawamata's rationality theorem. For $H$ ample and $K_X + \Delta$ not nef, the nef threshold $\max\{t : H + t(K_X + \Delta) \text{ nef}\}$ is a rational number $u/v$ with $v \le a(\dim X + 1)$, where $a(K_X + \Delta)$ is Cartier. The proof compares a polynomial of degree at most $\dim X$ on the lattice points of a thin strip with the base loci of the divisors it counts. The corollary is the nef value morphism.

Chapter 4 proves the cone theorem for klt pairs: the $(K_X + \Delta)$-negative part of $\overline{NE}(X)$ is spanned by countably many extremal rays, discrete away from $(K_X + \Delta)^\perp$, each spanned by a rational curve of $(K_X + \Delta)$-degree at least $-2 \dim X$, the Kawamata length bound. The rationality theorem gives the rays and bend-and-break from Volume 6 gives the curves.

Chapter 5 proves the contraction theorem. Every $(K_X + \Delta)$-negative extremal face has a contraction, given by a supporting divisor and the base point free theorem, and the chapter sorts the contractions of extremal rays into divisorial, small and fibre type. The examples are the del Pezzo cones again, a smooth Fano threefold of Picard number $2$, a divisorial contraction to a curve and a fibre type contraction to a surface.

Chapter 6 sketches the versions for lc pairs, due to Ambro and Fujino, and compares the proof of the cone theorem in this volume with Mori's proof in Volume 6: what each method needs, what each gives, and where each works.

The spine is Kollár and Mori, Chapter 3 (KM98), with Matsuki, Chapters 6 to 8 (Mat02), and Fujino's foundations (Fuj17d). The original sources are Kawamata (Kaw84, Kaw85), Shokurov (Sho85) and Kawamata, Matsuda and Matsuki (KMM87).
