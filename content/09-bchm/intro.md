# BCHM: existence of minimal models

This volume proves, or outlines with precise references, the theorem of Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan (BCHM10): a klt pair $(X, \Delta)$ whose boundary $\Delta$ is big, or whose log canonical divisor $K_X + \Delta$ is big, has a log terminal model when $K_X + \Delta$ is pseudo-effective, and its log canonical ring is finitely generated. In particular klt flips exist in every dimension, the canonical ring of a smooth projective variety is finitely generated, and a projective klt pair with $K_X + \Delta$ not pseudo-effective has a program that ends with a Mori fibre space. Volume 7 gives the cone, contraction, base point free and nonvanishing theorems, and Volume 8 constructs the program in dimension 3 and sets up flips, pl flips, the restricted algebra, Shokurov's reduction and special termination. This volume runs the same machine in every dimension.

> **The induction box.** BCHM10 prove six statements by a single induction on the dimension $n$: $A_n$, pl flips exist; $B_n$, special finiteness; $C_n$, log terminal models exist for a big boundary; $D_n$, nonvanishing for a big boundary; $E_n$, finiteness of models; $F_n$, finite generation. The implications are
> $$F_{n-1} \Rightarrow A_n, \quad E_{n-1} \Rightarrow B_n, \quad A_n + B_n \Rightarrow C_n, \quad D_{n-1} + B_n + C_n \Rightarrow D_n, \quad C_n + D_n \Rightarrow E_n, \quad C_n + D_n \Rightarrow F_n,$$
> and the six statements in dimension $1$ are elementary. The program with scaling is the device that turns existence into termination: it is the only program that the proof needs, and its termination for a big boundary is a consequence of the finiteness of models.

The box is the plan of the volume. Each chapter proves one or two of the arrows, and the arrows in the lowest dimension that is not elementary, dimension $3$, are the theorems of Volume 8.

Chapter 1 is the map of the induction. It states the six theorems $A$ to $F$ and the main theorem with their definitions, weak log canonical models, log terminal models and the sets $\mathcal{L}_A(V)$ of boundaries with a general ample part, proves the base case and the formal induction, and works out the toric program with scaling on the flip of the weighted blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^3$ with weights $(1, 2, 3)$, where two choices of the scaling divisor give the two different programs of Volume 3.

Chapter 2 treats pl flips in every dimension. A pl flip exists if its restricted algebra is finitely generated, the flip of a klt flipping contraction is a relative log canonical model, and the Hacon-McKernan extension theorem identifies the restricted algebra with an adjoint algebra on the plt centre $S$, a variety of dimension $n - 1$. Finite generation in dimension $n - 1$ then gives the pl flip.

Chapter 3 treats special termination and the program with scaling. Special termination near the reduced boundary is a statement in dimension $n - 1$ by adjunction. The program with scaling is defined, its thresholds are shown to decrease and its models to be weak log canonical models of the scaled divisors, and its termination for a big boundary is deduced from the finiteness of models.

Chapter 4 proves the finiteness of models: the Shokurov polytope and the geography of models, the decomposition of a polytope of boundaries into finitely many chambers with a single model each. It also outlines the proof of nonvanishing for a big boundary, with the Diophantine approximation step.

Chapter 5 assembles the theorem of BCHM and its consequences: finite generation of the canonical ring of a klt pair and of adjoint rings, a surface computed by hand, the existence of log terminal models, of flips and of $\mathbb{Q}$-factorializations.

Chapter 6 treats Mori dream spaces in the sense of Hu and Keel, computes the Mori chambers of the Hirzebruch surfaces and of a del Pezzo surface, proves that log Fano varieties are Mori dream spaces, outlines the route of Cascini and Lazić and of Corti and Lazić, and points to Volume 10.

The spine is BCHM10 itself, with the accounts of Corti's seminar, Flips for 3-folds and 4-folds (Cor07), and in it the chapter of Hacon and McKernan (HM07), the notes of Corti, Kaloghiros and Lazić, Introduction to the minimal model program and the existence of flips, and the book of Hacon and Kovács, Classification of higher dimensional algebraic varieties. Fujino's account of special termination is Fuj11, and Birkar's extension of the method to log canonical flips is Bir12.
