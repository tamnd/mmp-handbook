# Finiteness of models

The program with scaling of Chapter 3 terminates for a big boundary once one knows that a fixed family of klt pairs has only finitely many log terminal models. This chapter proves that finiteness statement in the form it is used, and the second ingredient of the induction that feeds it, nonvanishing for a big boundary.

The first section sets up Shokurov's polytopes. The boundaries $\Delta$ supported on a fixed finite set of prime divisors, with $(X, \Delta)$ log canonical, form a rational polytope; the boundaries in such a polytope with $K_X + \Delta$ nef form a rational polytope; and the boundaries for which a fixed birational contraction is a weak log canonical model form a rational polytope. These three facts are proved in full, the second from the cone theorem and Kawamata's length bound of Volume 7 by a finiteness argument in a lattice.

The second section states the finiteness of models of Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan, their Theorem E, and deduces the geography of models: the pseudo-effective part of a polytope of big klt boundaries is a finite union of the rational polytopes of the first section, and so is itself a rational polytope. The blow-up of the plane at a point serves as the worked example, with every chamber computed.

The third section treats nonvanishing for a big boundary, Theorem D of BCHM. Its statement, the structure of the proof and the Diophantine approximation step are given; the approximation step is proved completely, since it is the point where real coefficients enter and are removed.
