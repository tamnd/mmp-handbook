## Log Fano varieties are Mori dream spaces

### Statement

A *log Fano pair* is a projective klt pair $(X, \Delta)$, with $\Delta$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor, such that $-(K_X + \Delta)$ is ample. Throughout, $X$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial and the ground field $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic zero.

**Lemma 9.6.8 (the Picard group of a log Fano).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a log Fano pair. Then $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = 0$ for $i > 0$, every numerically trivial line bundle on $X$ is torsion, and $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} = N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space of finite dimension.

**Theorem 9.6.9 (log Fanos are Mori dream spaces, BCHM).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial log Fano pair. Then $X$ is a Mori dream space. In particular $\overline{\mathrm{Eff}}(X)$ and $\overline{\mathrm{Mov}}(X)$ are rational polyhedral, $X$ has finitely many Mori chambers, and the minimal model program for any divisor on $X$ runs and terminates.

### Proof in words

The Cox ring of $X$ is a ring of sections of all line bundles, and line bundles have no reason to be adjoint. The point is that on a log Fano every line bundle is adjoint up to a multiple: since $-(K_X + \Delta)$ is ample, a small multiple $\pm \varepsilon L$ of any divisor $L$ can be written as $K_X + \Delta'$ with $\Delta'$ equal to $\Delta$ plus a general ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor, so $(X, \Delta')$ stays klt and $\Delta'$ contains an ample divisor. Applied to $\pm L_1, \ldots, \pm L_\rho$ for a basis, this presents a Veronese subring of the Cox ring as a quotient of an adjoint ring, which is finitely generated by Chapter 5. Hu and Keel's theorem then turns finite generation into the Mori dream space property.

### Examples

**Example 9.6.10 (del Pezzo surfaces).** A smooth del Pezzo surface $S$ is a log Fano pair with $\Delta = 0$, so it is a Mori dream space by Theorem 9.6.9. This matches Theorem 2.2.2: $\overline{\mathrm{Eff}}(S) = \overline{NE}(S)$ is spanned by finitely many classes, the $(-1)$-curves when $\rho(S) \ge 3$, for instance the $N_2 = 3$ curves of Example 9.6.7 and the $N_6 = 27$ lines on a cubic surface. On a surface a small $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial modification is an isomorphism, so a Mori chamber of $S$ is the closure of the set of big classes whose Zariski decomposition has negative part supported exactly on a fixed set of pairwise disjoint $(-1)$-curves, and its ample model contracts that set.

**Example 9.6.11 (the plane blown up in nine points is not a Mori dream space).** Let $X = X_9$ be as in Theorem 2.2.20, the blow-up of the base points of a cubic pencil satisfying (H). On a surface $\overline{\mathrm{Eff}}(X) = \overline{NE}(X)$, and by part 2 of Theorem 2.2.20 this cone has infinitely many extremal rays, spanned by the $(-1)$-curves. A rational polyhedral cone has finitely many extremal rays, so by part 2 of Theorem 9.6.3, $X$ is not a Mori dream space. Theorem 9.6.9 does not apply: $-K_X = F$ is nef with $F^2 = 0$, so it is not big, and no boundary $\Delta$ makes $-(K_X + \Delta)$ ample, since $-(K_X + \Delta) \cdot F = -\Delta \cdot F \le 0$ for every effective $\Delta$ because $F$ is nef. The surface also has infinitely many runs of the program (part 3 of Theorem 2.2.20), and infinitely many contractions to del Pezzo surfaces of degree $1$, against the finiteness of Exercise 6 of the previous section.

**Example 9.6.12 (log del Pezzo structures on $\mathbb{F}_n$).** Let $n \ge 3$ and $\Delta = c\, C_-$ with $\frac{n - 2}{n} < c < 1$ rational. By Example 7.4.22, $(\mathbb{F}_n, \Delta)$ is klt with $-(K + \Delta)$ ample. Directly: $-(K + \Delta) \equiv (2 - c) C_- + (n + 2) F$, and for $aC_- + bF$ ampleness means $a > 0$ and $b > na$, here $n + 2 > n(2 - c)$, which is $c > \frac{n - 2}{n}$. So $\mathbb{F}_n$ is a Mori dream space by Theorem 9.6.9, as Example 9.6.6 showed directly, although $\mathbb{F}_n$ is not a del Pezzo surface for $n \ge 3$. The ample model of the chamber $\langle C_- + nF, C_- \rangle$ is $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$, whose vertex is the quotient singularity $\frac{1}{n}(1, 1)$. Writing $K_{\mathbb{F}_n} = \pi^* K_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)} + a C_-$ and intersecting with $C_-$ gives $n - 2 = -an$, so the discrepancy is $a = -\frac{n - 2}{n} > -1$ and the vertex is klt.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 9.6.8.* Apply Theorem 5.1.13 to the structure morphism $X \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ and $N = 0$: since $0 - (K_X + \Delta)$ is ample, hence nef and big, $H^j(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = 0$ for $j > 0$. The identity component $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$ of the Picard scheme is a connected group scheme, reduced in characteristic zero, with tangent space $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = 0$ at the origin, so $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$ is a point. By a theorem of Kleiman (Laz04, Section 1.1), the numerically trivial line bundles form a group $\operatorname{Pic}^\tau(X)$ containing $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$ with finite quotient, so $\operatorname{Pic}^\tau(X)$ is finite and every numerically trivial line bundle is torsion. Hence $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} \to N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is injective, and it is surjective by definition of $N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$; this space has finite dimension by the theorem of the base of Néron and Severi (Laz04, Section 1.1). $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 9.6.9.* By Lemma 9.6.8, $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} = N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has finite dimension $\rho$; choose Cartier divisors $L_1, \ldots, L_\rho$ whose classes form a basis. By Theorem 9.6.3 it suffices to show that $\operatorname{Cox}(X) = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{m} \in \mathbb{Z}^\rho} H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\sum m_i L_i))$ is finitely generated.

Let $H = -(K_X + \Delta)$, an ample $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor. Since the ample cone is open, there is a rational $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $\frac{1}{2} H + \varepsilon L_i$ and $\frac{1}{2} H - \varepsilon L_i$ are ample for $i = 1, \ldots, \rho$. Fix an integer $c \ge 2$ such that $\frac{c}{2} H$ and all $c(\frac{1}{2} H \pm \varepsilon L_i)$ are very ample Cartier divisors. Let $A_0$ be a general member of $|\frac{c}{2} H|$ and $G_i^{\pm}$ general members of $|c(\frac{1}{2} H \pm \varepsilon L_i)|$, and set
$$A = \tfrac{1}{c} A_0, \qquad \Delta_i^{\pm} = \Delta + A + \tfrac{1}{c} G_i^{\pm}.$$
By Bertini's theorem for klt pairs (KM98, Lemma 5.17), adding $\frac{1}{c}$ times a general member of a base point free linear system to a klt pair keeps it klt, as $\frac{1}{c} < 1$; applying this twice, every pair $(X, \Delta_i^{\pm})$ is klt. Each $\Delta_i^{\pm}$ is an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with $\Delta_i^{\pm} \ge A$, and $A$ is ample. Further
$$K_X + \Delta_i^{\pm} \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} K_X + \Delta + \tfrac{1}{2} H + \tfrac{1}{2} H \pm \varepsilon L_i = \pm \varepsilon L_i.$$
Choose a positive integer $r$ such that $D_i^{\pm} = r(K_X + \Delta_i^{\pm})$ is Cartier, $e = r\varepsilon$ is an integer, and $D_i^{\pm} - (\pm e L_i)$ is linearly equivalent to zero; the last is possible because this difference is $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly trivial, so a multiple is numerically trivial and Cartier, hence torsion by Lemma 9.6.8, and enlarging $r$ by the order kills it. Fix rational functions $f_i^{\pm}$ with $D_i^{\pm} = \pm e L_i + \operatorname{div}(f_i^{\pm})$.

By Theorem 9.5.6 of Chapter 5, the adjoint ring
$$R' = R(X; K_X + \Delta_1^+, K_X + \Delta_1^-, \ldots, K_X + \Delta_\rho^+, K_X + \Delta_\rho^-),$$
graded by $\mathbb{N}^{2\rho}$, is finitely generated. Its subring $R$ of degrees in $r \mathbb{N}^{2\rho}$ is finitely generated: if $g_1, \ldots, g_s$ are homogeneous generators of $R'$, then $R'$ is a finite module over $k[g_1^r, \ldots, g_s^r] \subseteq R$, generated by the monomials $g^{\mathbf{n}}$ with all exponents below $r$, so $R$ is a submodule of a finite module over a noetherian ring, hence a finite module over $k[g_1^r, \ldots, g_s^r]$ and a finitely generated $k$-algebra. The graded piece of $R$ of degree $r(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b})$, with $\mathbf{a} = (a_i)$ indexing the $+$ divisors and $\mathbf{b} = (b_i)$ the $-$ divisors, is $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\sum_i a_i D_i^+ + b_i D_i^-))$. Multiplication by $\prod_i (f_i^+)^{a_i} (f_i^-)^{b_i}$ identifies it with $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\sum_i e(a_i - b_i) L_i))$, the piece of $\operatorname{Cox}(X)$ of degree $e(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b})$. These identifications are compatible with products, so they define a homomorphism of $k$-algebras
$$\Phi \colon R \to \operatorname{Cox}(X)^{(e)} = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{m} \in e\mathbb{Z}^\rho} \operatorname{Cox}(X)_{\mathbf{m}},$$
and $\Phi$ is surjective, since a degree $e\mathbf{m}$ is the image of $r(\mathbf{m}^+, \mathbf{m}^-)$ with $\mathbf{m}^{\pm}$ the positive and negative parts of $\mathbf{m}$, and on that piece $\Phi$ is an isomorphism. So $\operatorname{Cox}(X)^{(e)}$ is finitely generated, and $\operatorname{Cox}(X)$ is a domain, a subring of $k(X)[t_1^{\pm 1}, \ldots, t_\rho^{\pm 1}]$, so it is finitely generated by Lemma 9.5.1 of Chapter 5. By Theorem 9.6.3, $X$ is a Mori dream space, and the remaining assertions are part 2 of that theorem. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (B) Show that the cone over a smooth plane cubic is not a log Fano variety with any boundary, and that $\mathbb{P}^2$ blown up at nine general points has no boundary $\Delta$ making $(X, \Delta)$ log Fano.
2. (B) Let $X$ be a smooth projective toric variety and $\Delta_X$ its toric boundary. Show that $(X, (1 - \delta)\Delta_X)$ is log Fano for small rational $\delta > 0$ only if $X$ is Fano, and find instead a boundary $\Delta$ supported on $\Delta_X$ with $(X, \Delta)$ log Fano for every projective toric $X$.
3. (B) For $\mathbb{F}_n$ with $n \ge 3$ and $\Delta = cC_-$, show that $(\mathbb{F}_n, \Delta)$ is not log Fano for $c \le \frac{n - 2}{n}$, and describe the ample model of $-(K + \Delta)$ for $c = \frac{n - 2}{n}$.
4. (B) Carry out the proof of Theorem 9.6.9 for $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with $\Delta = 0$ and $L_1$, $L_2$ the two rulings: choose $\varepsilon$, $c$ and the boundaries $\Delta_i^{\pm}$, compute $r$ and $e$, and identify the map $\Phi$ explicitly.
5. (C) Let $X$ be the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at $r \le 8$ points in general position. Using the cone of Theorem 2.2.2, show that the Mori chambers of $X$ correspond to the sets of pairwise disjoint $(-1)$-curves, the model of a chamber being the contraction of its set, and compute the number of chambers for $r = 3$.
6. (C) Show that a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial klt variety $X$ of Fano type, that is, admitting some $\Delta$ with $(X, \Delta)$ klt and $-(K_X + \Delta)$ big and nef, is a Mori dream space, by reducing to Theorem 9.6.9 with a perturbed boundary.

### Notes and history

Hu and Keel (HK00) conjectured that log Fano varieties are Mori dream spaces and proved it for surfaces and in some threefold cases. Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan proved it in all dimensions as Corollary 1.3.1 of BCHM10, by the argument above: the finite generation of adjoint rings with an ample part, together with the observation that on a log Fano every divisor class is a small multiple of an adjoint class. The converse fails: there are K3 surfaces with finitely generated Cox ring, which are Mori dream spaces but not rational, hence not of Fano type. The characterization of Mori dream spaces of Fano type through Cox rings is due to Gongyo, Okawa, Sannai and Takagi.
