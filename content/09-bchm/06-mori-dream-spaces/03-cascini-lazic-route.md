## The route of Cascini, Lazić and Corti

### Statement

In BCHM10 finite generation of adjoint rings is a consequence of the minimal model program with scaling: the program produces the models, finiteness of models makes them finitely many, and the section rings are read off from them. Cascini and Lazić reversed the order. They proved finite generation first, by an induction on dimension that uses only the extension theorem of Hacon and McKernan, Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing and Diophantine approximation, and Corti and Lazić then recovered the minimal model program from it. This section states the result and outlines the argument. The outline is marked as such and nothing in it is proved here.

**Theorem 9.6.13 (Cascini and Lazić).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety, $A$ an ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $B_1, \ldots, B_l$ effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors such that $\lfloor B_i \rfloor = 0$ and the support of $\sum_i B_i$ has simple normal crossings. Then the adjoint ring
$$R(X; K_X + A + B_1, \ldots, K_X + A + B_l)$$
is a finitely generated $k$-algebra. The proof does not use the minimal model program.

### Proof in words

*Outline.* The argument of CL12 runs by induction on $\dim X$, and proves two statements together: finite generation of adjoint rings as in Theorem 9.6.13, and a nonvanishing statement for adjoint divisors $K_X + A + B$, the analogue of Theorem 9.4.12 of Chapter 4.

1. *Restriction to a component.* Given the cone $\mathcal{C}$ of divisors $K_X + A + B$ with $B$ in a rational polytope of boundaries, choose a prime divisor $S$ in the support and restrict sections to $S$. The extension theorem of Hacon and McKernan, the result of Chapter 2, shows that the restricted algebra is, up to a Veronese truncation, an adjoint algebra on $S$, finitely generated by induction.
2. *Lifting generators.* The kernel of the restriction is generated by the section defining $S$. Finite generation on $S$ and of this principal ideal give finite generation of the part of the ring supported near the boundary of $\mathcal{C}$; a Gordan lemma argument over the cone covers all of it.
3. *Convex geometry of the asymptotic order.* The functions $\sigma_S$ of asymptotic vanishing order along $S$ are shown to be piecewise linear on rational polyhedral subcones of $\mathcal{C}$, by the Diophantine approximation lemma, Lemma 9.4.14 of Chapter 4, used as in the proof of nonvanishing.
4. *Nonvanishing.* The induction also gives nonvanishing in dimension $n$, so the support of the adjoint ring is a rational polyhedral cone and the induction closes.

Corti and Lazić (CL13) then deduce the main results of BCHM10 from Theorem 9.6.13: given finite generation, the ample models of the divisors in a rational polytope of adjoint divisors are finitely many, which is Theorem 9.4.9 of Chapter 4 in a form proved without the program, and each step of the program with scaling is a passage between adjacent chambers, so it terminates because the chambers are finitely many. This is the picture of Example 9.6.14 below in general.

### Examples

**Example 9.6.14 (the program with scaling on $S_2$ as a walk through chambers).** Let $X = S_2$ as in Example 9.6.7, $D = 2L + E_1 + 2E_2$ and the ample divisor $H = -K_X = 3L - E_1 - E_2$. Then
$$D + tH = (2 + 3t)L + (1 - t)E_1 + (2 - t)E_2 = (2 + 3t)L - (t - 1)E_1 - (t - 2)E_2.$$
With $a = 2 + 3t$, $b_1 = t - 1$, $b_2 = t - 2$, the conditions of Example 9.6.7 give: $D + tH$ is nef if and only if $t - 1 \ge 0$, $t - 2 \ge 0$ and $a - b_1 - b_2 = t + 5 \ge 0$, that is, $t \ge 2$. So the program with scaling starts with $t_0 = 2$, where $(D + 2H) \cdot E_2 = 0$ and $D \cdot E_2 = 2E_2^2 = -2 < 0$. The step contracts $E_2$ and gives $X_1 = \mathbb{F}_1$, the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at $p$, with $D_1 = 2L + E_1$ and $H_1 = 3L - E_1$. On $X_1$, $D_1 + tH_1 = (2 + 3t)L - (t - 1)E_1$ is nef if and only if $t \ge 1$ and $2 + 3t \ge t - 1$, that is, $t \ge 1$, so $t_1 = 1$, with $(D_1 + H_1) \cdot E_1 = 0$ and $D_1 \cdot E_1 = -1 < 0$. The step contracts $E_1$ and gives $X_2 = \mathbb{P}^2$ with $D_2 = 2L$ ample, where the program stops. In the chamber picture of Example 9.6.7, the path $t \mapsto D + tH$ for $t \ge 2$ lies in $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$; for $1 \le t \le 2$ it lies in $\langle L, L - E_1, E_2 \rangle$, as $D + tH = (2t + 3)L + (t - 1)(L - E_1) + (2 - t)E_2$ there, the chamber whose model is the contraction of $E_2$; and for $0 \le t \le 1$ it lies in $\langle L, E_1, E_2 \rangle$, with model $\mathbb{P}^2$. The program has crossed the two walls $\langle L, L - E_1 \rangle$ and $\langle L, E_2 \rangle$, one step for each.

### Proof

The proof of Theorem 9.6.13 is CL12, Theorem A, and is cited. The deduction of the minimal model program for klt pairs with big boundary, including the existence of flips and termination of the program with scaling, is CL13, and is also cited. An account of both, with the comparison with BCHM10, is CKL11. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (B) Redo Example 9.6.14 with $D = L + 2E_1 + E_2$ and $H = -K_X$, and show that the program contracts $E_1$ first.
2. (B) On $S_2$ with $D = 2(L - E_1) + E_{12}$ and $H = -K_X$, run the program with scaling, show that it contracts $E_{12}$ and stops on $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with a nef divisor that is not big, and identify the ample model of $D$.
3. (B) For $\mathbb{F}_n$ and $D = C_- + F$, run the program with scaling of $H = C_- + (n + 1)F$ and compare with the chambers of Example 9.6.6.
4. (B) Show that if $\mathcal{C}$ is a rational polyhedral cone and the divisorial ring over $\mathcal{C} \cap \mathbb{Z}^l$ is finitely generated, then $\mathcal{C}$ is a finite union of rational polyhedral subcones on each of which the ample model is constant in the interior.
5. (C) Explain why Theorem 9.6.13, applied on a log resolution, implies Theorem 9.5.6 of Chapter 5 for klt pairs $(X, \Delta_i)$ with $\Delta_i \ge A$ ample.
6. (C) In Example 9.6.14, show that the final model does not depend on the ample divisor $H$, but the order of the steps does: find an ample $H'$ on $S_2$ for which the program contracts $E_1$ before $E_2$, and describe the chambers its path crosses.

### Notes and history

Cascini and Lazić (CL12) proved finite generation of adjoint rings with an ample part by induction on dimension, without flips. Corti and Lazić (CL13) showed that the minimal model program with scaling for klt pairs with big boundary follows. The survey of Corti, Kaloghiros and Lazić (CKL11) introduces both and explains the relation with BCHM10. Hacon and Kovács (HK10) give a book account of the BCHM argument.

Volume 10 takes up what lies beyond this volume. Abundance, which says that a nef $K_X + \Delta$ of a klt pair is semiample, is an open conjecture in dimension at least four, and so is the nonvanishing conjecture for pseudoeffective $K_X + \Delta$ without a big boundary. The program for log canonical pairs, and termination of flips in general, the termination conjecture, are also treated there; termination is open in dimension at least four outside the cases with big boundary and scaling covered by BCHM10.
