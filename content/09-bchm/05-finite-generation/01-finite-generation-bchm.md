## Finite generation of log canonical and adjoint rings

### Statement

All varieties are over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic zero. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ on a normal projective variety $X$, the section ring is $R(X, D) = \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\lfloor m D \rfloor))$, a graded subring of $k(X)[t]$, hence a domain.

**Lemma 9.5.1 (Veronese subrings).** Let $R = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{m} \in \mathbb{Z}^\rho} R_{\mathbf{m}}$ be a $\mathbb{Z}^\rho$-graded domain containing $k$, let $e \ge 1$ be an integer and $R^{(e)} = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{m} \in e \mathbb{Z}^\rho} R_{\mathbf{m}}$. If $R^{(e)}$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra, then $R$ is a finite $R^{(e)}$-module, and in particular a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

**Definition 9.5.2 (log canonical rings and adjoint rings).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a projective pair with $\Delta$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $K_X + \Delta$ $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier. Its *log canonical ring* is $R(X, K_X + \Delta)$. For $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors $D_1, \ldots, D_l$ the *divisorial ring* is
$$R(X; D_1, \ldots, D_l) = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{m} \in \mathbb{N}^l} H^0\bigl(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\lfloor m_1 D_1 + \cdots + m_l D_l \rfloor)\bigr),$$
graded by $\mathbb{N}^l$; when $D_i = K_X + \Delta_i$ for boundaries $\Delta_i$ it is an *adjoint ring*. For a projective morphism $\pi \colon X \to U$ the relative ring $R(\pi, D) = \bigoplus_m \pi_* \mathcal{O}_X(\lfloor m D \rfloor)$ is a sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_U$-algebras.

**Theorem 9.5.3 (BCHM, Theorem 1.2).** Let $\pi \colon X \to U$ be a projective morphism of normal quasi-projective varieties and $(X, \Delta)$ a klt pair with $\Delta$ an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor. Suppose that either $\Delta$ is $\pi$-big and $K_X + \Delta$ is $\pi$-pseudo-effective, or $K_X + \Delta$ is $\pi$-big. Then:

1. $K_X + \Delta$ has a log terminal model over $U$;
2. if $K_X + \Delta$ is $\pi$-big, it has a log canonical model over $U$;
3. if $K_X + \Delta$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, the ring $R(\pi, K_X + \Delta)$ is a finitely generated sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_U$-algebras.

**Theorem 9.5.4 (finite generation of the log canonical ring).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a projective klt pair with $\Delta$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor. Then $R(X, K_X + \Delta)$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra. If $K_X + \Delta$ is big, then $\operatorname{Proj} R(X, K_X + \Delta)$ is the log canonical model of $(X, \Delta)$.

**Corollary 9.5.5 (finite generation of the canonical ring).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety. Then the canonical ring $R(X, K_X) = \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m K_X))$ is finitely generated.

**Theorem 9.5.6 (finite generation of adjoint rings).** Let $X$ be a normal projective variety, $A$ an ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $\Delta_1, \ldots, \Delta_l$ effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors with $\Delta_i \ge A$ and $(X, \Delta_i)$ klt for every $i$. Then the adjoint ring $R(X; K_X + \Delta_1, \ldots, K_X + \Delta_l)$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

### Proof in words

Finite generation is insensitive to Veronese subrings, by Lemma 9.5.1, so one may pass to a multiple $r(K_X + \Delta)$ that is Cartier. If $K_X + \Delta$ is big, Theorem 9.5.3 gives a log terminal model $\varphi \colon X \dashrightarrow Y$. On a common resolution, $p^*(K_X + \Delta) = q^*(K_Y + \Gamma) + E$ with $E \ge 0$ exceptional over $Y$, and Lemma 7.1.6 identifies the sections of multiples of $K_X + \Delta$ with those of $K_Y + \Gamma$, compatibly with multiplication. On $Y$ the divisor $K_Y + \Gamma$ is nef and big with $(Y, \Gamma)$ klt, hence semiample by Corollary 7.2.5, and the section ring of a semiample divisor is finitely generated by Lemma 2.5.15.

If $K_X + \Delta$ is not big but has some section, the Iitaka fibration $X \dashrightarrow Z$ and the canonical bundle formula of Fujino and Mori produce a klt pair $(Z, \Delta_Z)$ on a lower-dimensional base with $K_Z + \Delta_Z$ big and with the same log canonical ring up to Veronese subrings, and the big case applies on $Z$. This step is cited. If $K_X + \Delta$ has no sections in positive degree, the ring is $k$.

Adjoint rings are finitely generated by a refinement of the same argument across a polytope of boundaries: by the geography of Chapter 4 the polytope spanned by the $\Delta_i$ is covered by finitely many rational polytopes on each of which a single log terminal model works, on each piece the ring is controlled by a semigroup of lattice points in a rational cone, which is finitely generated by Gordan's lemma (Theorem P8.6.5), and the pieces are glued. This is outlined, not proved.

### Examples

**Example 9.5.7 (a log canonical ring on $\mathbb{F}_1$).** Keep the notation of Example 9.4.11 in Chapter 4, with $(s, u) = (\frac{3}{4}, \frac{1}{8})$, so $\Delta = \frac{1}{2} A'' + \frac{3}{4} E + \frac{1}{8} F$ with $A'' \in |6L - 3E|$. Then
$$8(K_X + \Delta) = 8K_X + 4A'' + 6E + F \sim 8(-3L + E) + 4(6L - 3E) + 6E + (L - E) = L + E.$$
Let $G \in |m(L + E)|$ be effective with $m \ge 1$. If $G \ge jE$ with $0 \le j < m$, then $(G - jE) \cdot E = -(m - j) < 0$, so $E$ is a component of $G - jE$ and $G \ge (j + 1)E$. By induction $G \ge mE$, so multiplication by the $m$-th power of the section of $\mathcal{O}(E)$ is an isomorphism $H^0(X, mL) \to H^0(X, m(L + E))$, and $H^0(X, mL)$ is the space $S_m$ of forms of degree $m$ on $\mathbb{P}^2$. Hence
$$R(X, K_X + \Delta)^{(8)} \cong R(X, L + E) \cong k[x_0, x_1, x_2],$$
a polynomial ring, and $\operatorname{Proj} R(X, K_X + \Delta) = \mathbb{P}^2$. This agrees with the geography of Example 9.4.11: the point $(\frac{3}{4}, \frac{1}{8})$ satisfies $u < s - \frac{1}{2}$, where the blow-down to $\mathbb{P}^2$ is the log terminal model and the ample model.

**Example 9.5.8 (a pair whose log canonical ring is trivial).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $\Delta = \frac{1}{2} H$ for a hyperplane $H$. Then $\lfloor m(K_X + \Delta) \rfloor = -(n + 1)m H + \lfloor \frac{m}{2} \rfloor H$ has negative degree for $m \ge 1$, so $R(X, K_X + \Delta) = k$, finitely generated with $\operatorname{Proj}$ empty. Theorem 9.5.4 includes this case: the ring is trivial exactly when $K_X + \Delta$ has no effective multiple, here because $K_X + \Delta$ is anti-ample.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 9.5.1.* For a coset $c \in \mathbb{Z}^\rho / e \mathbb{Z}^\rho$ put $M_c = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{m} \in c} R_{\mathbf{m}}$. Then $R = \bigoplus_c M_c$ is a finite direct sum, $M_0 = R^{(e)}$, and each $M_c$ is an $R^{(e)}$-submodule. Fix $c$ with $M_c \ne 0$ and a nonzero homogeneous $x \in R_{\mathbf{a}}$ with $\mathbf{a} \in c$. For homogeneous $y \in R_{\mathbf{b}}$ with $\mathbf{b} \in c$, the product $x^{e - 1} y$ has degree $(e - 1)\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} \equiv e \mathbf{a} \equiv 0$ modulo $e \mathbb{Z}^\rho$, so $x^{e-1} M_c \subseteq R^{(e)}$. Since $R$ is a domain, multiplication by $x^{e-1}$ is injective, so $M_c$ is isomorphic to an $R^{(e)}$-submodule of $R^{(e)}$, that is, to an ideal. As $R^{(e)}$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra it is Noetherian, and the ideal is finitely generated. So each $M_c$ is a finite $R^{(e)}$-module, hence so is $R$, and the generators of $R^{(e)}$ together with module generators of the $M_c$ generate $R$ as a $k$-algebra. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 9.5.3.* This is Theorem 1.2 of BCHM, the main theorem of the volume, obtained at the end of the induction of Chapter 1. It is cited. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 9.5.4.* If $H^0(X, \lfloor m(K_X + \Delta) \rfloor) = 0$ for all $m \ge 1$, then $R(X, K_X + \Delta) = k$. Suppose first that $K_X + \Delta$ is big. By Theorem 9.5.3 with $U = \operatorname{Spec} k$ there is a log terminal model $\varphi \colon X \dashrightarrow Y$, with $\Gamma = \varphi_* \Delta$; then $(Y, \Gamma)$ is klt by Lemma 9.4.4 of Chapter 4, and $Y$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial. Let $p \colon W \to X$, $q \colon W \to Y$ be a common resolution with $p$ projective. By Lemma 9.4.4, part 3, $E = p^*(K_X + \Delta) - q^*(K_Y + \Gamma)$ is effective and $q$-exceptional. Choose $r \ge 1$ such that $r(K_X + \Delta)$ and $r(K_Y + \Gamma)$ are Cartier; then $rE$ is an integral Cartier divisor.

For $m \ge 0$, since $X$ is normal and $p$ is birational and proper, $p_* \mathcal{O}_W = \mathcal{O}_X$, and the projection formula gives $H^0(X, mr(K_X + \Delta)) = H^0(W, p^* mr(K_X + \Delta))$ by pullback. By Lemma 7.1.6, part 2, applied to $q$, the Cartier divisor $N = mr(K_Y + \Gamma)$ and the effective $q$-exceptional divisor $mrE$, with $\sigma$ the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}_W(rE)$, the map $s \mapsto q^* s \cdot \sigma^{m}$ is an isomorphism
$$H^0(Y, mr(K_Y + \Gamma)) \to H^0(W, q^* mr(K_Y + \Gamma) + mr E) = H^0(W, p^* mr(K_X + \Delta)),$$
Pullback is multiplicative and $\sigma^{m} \sigma^{m'} = \sigma^{m + m'}$, so these isomorphisms assemble into an isomorphism of graded rings
$$R(Y, r(K_Y + \Gamma)) \cong R(X, r(K_X + \Delta)) = R(X, K_X + \Delta)^{(r)}.$$
The divisor $K_Y + \Gamma = q_* p^*(K_X + \Delta)$ is big, as the pushforward of a big divisor under a birational morphism, and it is nef. By Corollary 7.2.5 applied to the klt pair $(Y, \Gamma)$, there is $b \ge 1$ such that $br(K_Y + \Gamma)$ is a free Cartier divisor. By Lemma 2.5.15, $R(Y, br(K_Y + \Gamma))$ is finitely generated, and it is the $b$-th Veronese subring of $R(Y, r(K_Y + \Gamma))$. Hence $R(X, K_X + \Delta)^{(br)}$ is finitely generated, and so is $R(X, K_X + \Delta)$ by Lemma 9.5.1 with $\rho = 1$. By Lemma 2.5.15, $\operatorname{Proj} R(Y, br(K_Y + \Gamma))$ is the target of the semiample fibration of $K_Y + \Gamma$, which is birational because $K_Y + \Gamma$ is big; it is the log canonical model, and $\operatorname{Proj}$ is unchanged under Veronese subrings.

If $K_X + \Delta$ is not big but $H^0(X, \lfloor m(K_X + \Delta) \rfloor) \ne 0$ for some $m \ge 1$, then by the canonical bundle formula of Fujino and Mori (FM00) there are a projective klt pair $(Z, \Delta_Z)$ with $\Delta_Z$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $K_Z + \Delta_Z$ big, of dimension the Iitaka dimension of $K_X + \Delta$, and an integer $a \ge 1$ with $R(X, K_X + \Delta)^{(a)} \cong R(Z, K_Z + \Delta_Z)^{(a)}$. The big case applies to $(Z, \Delta_Z)$, and Lemma 9.5.1 transfers finite generation to $R(X, K_X + \Delta)$. This step is cited, and is how BCHM deduce finite generation for all klt pairs. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 9.5.5.* The pair $(X, 0)$ is klt because $X$ is smooth, and $R(X, K_X)$ is its log canonical ring. Apply Theorem 9.5.4. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 9.5.6.* This is the finite generation of adjoint rings of BCHM, Section 1.1, in the form given by Cascini and Lazić (CL12), and it is cited. The outline above indicates how it follows from Theorem 9.4.10 of Chapter 4 and Gordan's lemma. $\square$

### Exercises

3. (B) In Example 9.5.7 take $(s, u) = (\frac{5}{8}, \frac{1}{2})$, find $r$ with $r(K_X + \Delta)$ integral up to linear equivalence, and compute $R(X, K_X + \Delta)^{(r)}$ and its $\operatorname{Proj}$.
4. (B) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $\Delta = \frac{2}{3}(F_1 + F_1' + F_1'') + \frac{3}{4}(F_2 + F_2' + F_2'' + F_2''')$ with distinct fibres $F_1, F_1', F_1''$ of the first projection and $F_2, \ldots, F_2'''$ of the second. Compute $\lfloor m(K_X + \Delta) \rfloor$ for every $m$ and the ring $R(X, K_X + \Delta)$, and show that its $\operatorname{Proj}$ is $\mathbb{P}^1$.
5. (C) Prove the converse of Lemma 9.5.1 for $\rho = 1$: if $R$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{N}$-graded $k$-algebra, then so is $R^{(e)}$. Hint: $R^{(e)}$ is the ring of invariants of the action of the group of $e$-th roots of unity.
6. (C) Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a projective klt pair with $K_X + \Delta$ big and let $X \dashrightarrow Y_1$, $X \dashrightarrow Y_2$ be two log terminal models. Show that $R(Y_1, K_{Y_1} + \Gamma_1) \cong R(Y_2, K_{Y_2} + \Gamma_2)$ as graded rings and deduce that the log canonical model does not depend on the choice.

### Notes and history

Finite generation of the canonical ring of a variety of general type was proved by Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan (BCHM10), and independently, by an analytic method, by Siu. The reduction of the general klt case to the big case is the canonical bundle formula of Fujino and Mori (FM00). Cascini and Lazić (CL12) gave a proof of the finite generation of adjoint rings directly, by induction on the dimension, without running the minimal model program; Chapter 6 outlines their route. Zariski's work on the section rings of divisors on surfaces, taken up in the next section, is the origin of the question.
