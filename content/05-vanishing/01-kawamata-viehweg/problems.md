## Problems

Each problem applies the vanishing theorems of the chapter to an explicit pair or morphism and checks the prediction by a direct computation.

1. (A) Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be the union of $r \geq 3$ general lines and $\Delta = cA$. Show that $(\mathbb{P}^2, \Delta)$ is snc, that it is klt if and only if $c < 1$, and use Corollary 5.1.14 to find all $d$ with $H^i(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(d)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ that the theorem predicts. Compare with Theorem P4.2.1.
2. (A) Let $r \geq 3$ lines pass through one point $p \in \mathbb{P}^2$, with sum $A$, and $\Delta = cA$. Show that $(\mathbb{P}^2, cA)$ is klt if and only if $c < \frac{2}{r}$, and compute $N_Y$ and $\Theta$ of Corollary 5.1.16 on the blow-up of $p$ for $N = d\ell$.
3. (A) Let $\mu \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^3$ be the blow-up of a point with exceptional divisor $E \cong \mathbb{P}^2$. Show that $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(kE) = 0$ for $j > 0$ and $k \leq 2$, that $R^1 \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(kE) = 0$ for every $k$, and that $R^2 \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(3E) \cong k$. Deduce $h^i(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(dH + kE))$ for $0 \leq k \leq 2$ and every $d$, with $H$ the pullback of a plane.
4. (B) In Example 5.1.20, let $\Delta = c \sum_{i < j} L_{ij} + b \ell_0$, with $\ell_0$ a general line and $0 \leq b < 1$. Find the region of $(b, c)$ where the pair is klt, and for $N = d\ell$ the region where Theorem 5.1.13 predicts $H^i(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y)) = 0$ for $i > 0$. Show that $b$ only shrinks the predicted range.
5. (B) Let $X = \mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$, the quadric cone in $\mathbb{P}^3$, and $L$ a ruling through the vertex, so $L \sim D_1$ in the notation of Example 5.1.18. For $\Delta = cL$ with $0 \leq c < 1$, show that $(X, \Delta)$ is klt, and find the integers $a$ for which Theorem 5.1.13 gives $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1)) = 0$ for $i > 0$. Compare with the toric computation of Example 5.1.18.
6. (B) Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve, $P_1, \dots, P_r$ distinct points and $m_1, \dots, m_r \geq 2$. Show that a Kawamata cover $g \colon C' \to C$ for $(P_j, m_j)$ with $R_0 = 0$ exists only if $2 g(C) - 2 + \sum_j (1 - \frac{1}{m_j})$ is $\frac{1}{d}(2 g(C') - 2)$ for an integer $d$, and decide for which $(m_1, m_2, m_3)$ with $C = \mathbb{P}^1$ and $r = 3$ the sign of $2g(C') - 2$ is negative, zero or positive.
7. (B) Let $f \colon X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to Z = \mathbb{P}^1$ be the second projection, and $F_1, \dots, F_4$ fibres of the first projection, which dominate $Z$. Show that for $\Delta = \frac12 (F_1 + F_2)$ and $N = 0$ the hypothesis of Theorem 5.1.13 holds over $Z$, and that for $\Delta = \frac12 (F_1 + \dots + F_4)$ it fails, because $-(K_X + \Delta)$ is $f$-nef and not $f$-big. Check $R^1 f_* \mathcal{O}_X = 0$ directly.
8. (C) Let $X = \mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ and $\mu \colon Y \to X$ the minimal resolution, the Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb{F}_2$, with exceptional curve $E$. Show that $\mu$ is crepant and that $\mu^* D_1 = D'_1 + \frac12 E$ in the notation of Example 5.1.18. Using Corollary 5.1.16 with $\Delta = 0$, show that $H^i(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y)) \cong H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N))$ for every Weil divisor $N$ on $X$, compute $N_Y$ for $N = a D_1$, and compute both sides for $a = 1$ and $a = -3$.
9. (C) Let $\mu \colon Y \to X$ be the blow-up of a smooth centre $W$ of codimension $r$ in a smooth projective variety, $E$ the exceptional divisor and $D$ a nef and big Cartier divisor on $X$. Show that $H^i(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(K_Y + \mu^* D - kE)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and $0 \leq k \leq r - 1$, using Example 5.1.21, the Leray spectral sequence and Corollary P7.8.19. For $X = \mathbb{P}^2$, $W$ a point, $D = \ell$ and $k = 2$, show that $H^1 \cong k$.
