## Kawamata covers and the covering trick

### Statement

Throughout the chapter, $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$. A *smooth divisor* on a smooth variety is a reduced effective divisor whose support is smooth; it may be disconnected. A sum $\sum_j D_j$ of smooth divisors without common components is *reduced snc* if it is a reduced divisor with simple normal crossings. The covering trick replaces a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor by an integral divisor on a finite cover. Volume P7 builds the two basic covers, the cyclic cover along a smooth divisor (Proposition P7.8.10) and the Bloch-Gieseker cover that extracts a root of a line bundle (Proposition P7.8.11). This section composes them.

**Definition 5.1.1 (Kawamata cover).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety, $D = D_1 + \dots + D_r$ a reduced snc divisor with every $D_j$ a smooth divisor, and $m_1, \dots, m_r \geq 1$ integers. A *Kawamata cover* for $(D_j, m_j)$ is a finite flat surjective morphism $g \colon X' \to X$ from a smooth projective variety such that, for every $j$,
$$g^* D_j = m_j D'_j$$
with $D'_j$ a smooth divisor, and $D'_1 + \dots + D'_r$ is reduced snc.

**Theorem 5.1.2 (Kawamata's covering lemma).** In the setting of Definition 5.1.1 a Kawamata cover exists.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Proposition 5.1.3 (the ramification of a Kawamata cover).** Let $g \colon X' \to X$ be a Kawamata cover for $(D_j, m_j)$. Then
$$K_{X'} = g^* K_X + \sum_j (m_j - 1) D'_j + R_0,$$
with $R_0$ an effective divisor that has no component in common with $D'_1 + \dots + D'_r$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.1.4 (pairs on a Kawamata cover).** Let $g$ be a Kawamata cover for $(D_j, m_j)$, and $\Delta = \sum_j a_j D_j$ with $a_j \in \mathbb{Q}$, $a_j < 1$ and $m_j a_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ for every $j$. Put $\Delta' = g^* \Delta - \sum_j (m_j - 1) D'_j - R_0$. Then
$$K_{X'} + \Delta' = g^*(K_X + \Delta), \qquad \Delta' = \sum_j \big(1 - m_j (1 - a_j)\big) D'_j - R_0,$$
so $\Delta'$ is an integral divisor with $\Delta' \leq 0$. For every Cartier divisor $N$ on $X$, $g^*(N - \Delta)$ is a Cartier divisor on $X'$. For the standard coefficients $a_j = 1 - \frac{1}{m_j}$, $\Delta' = -R_0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Proposition 5.1.5 (the trace splits off the structure sheaf).** Let $g \colon X' \to X$ be a finite surjective morphism of normal varieties, of degree $d$. There is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear map $\tau \colon g_* \mathcal{O}_{X'} \to \mathcal{O}_X$ with $\tau(1) = 1$. So for every locally free sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ and every $i$, the map $H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) \to H^i(X', g^* \mathcal{F})$ is injective, with a left inverse. In particular $H^i(X', g^*\mathcal{F}) = 0$ implies $H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

### Proof in words

The covering lemma is built in two stages. The first stage makes each line bundle $\mathcal{O}_X(D_j)$ an $m_j$-th power after a finite flat pullback, by $r$ applications of the Bloch-Gieseker construction, which keeps the pullback of $D$ reduced snc. Once $\mathcal{O}(D_j) \cong L_j^{m_j}$, the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}(D_j)$ is a section of $L_j^{m_j}$ with smooth reduced divisor, and the cyclic cover of Proposition P7.8.10 extracts its $m_j$-th root: the pullback of $D_j$ becomes $m_j$ times a smooth divisor. Part 5 of that proposition keeps the other pullbacks smooth and in normal crossing, so the $r$ cyclic covers can be taken one after the other. At each step a connected component of the cover is again finite, flat and surjective.

The ramification is the Hurwitz formula of Volume 4: the ramification divisor has coefficient $e - 1$ along a divisor with ramification index $e$, and over $D_j$ the index is exactly $m_j$ because $D'_j$ is reduced. The cover may ramify elsewhere, along $R_0$, and an example below shows that this cannot always be avoided. The corollary is arithmetic: the coefficient of $D'_j$ in $g^*\Delta - R$ is $m_j a_j - (m_j - 1) = 1 - m_j(1 - a_j)$, an integer, and at most $0$ because $m_j(1 - a_j)$ is a positive integer.

Lemma P7.8.9 proves the splitting for a finite flat cover of a smooth variety by duality; here the base is only normal, and the argument is the classical one. The trace of an integral element of the function field of $X'$ is integral over $\mathcal{O}_X$, so it lies in $\mathcal{O}_X$ because $X$ is normal. Dividing by $d$, which is possible in characteristic $0$, splits the inclusion $\mathcal{O}_X \subseteq g_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}$. Tensoring with $\mathcal{F}$ and taking cohomology, which commutes with the finite push-forward, makes $H^i(X, \mathcal{F})$ a direct summand of $H^i(X', g^* \mathcal{F})$.

### Examples

**Example 5.1.6 (the Kummer cover of the plane).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^2$, $D_1, D_2, D_3$ the coordinate lines $\{x = 0\}$, $\{y = 0\}$, $\{z = 0\}$, and $m_1 = m_2 = m_3 = m \geq 2$. Let $g \colon \mathbb{P}^2 \to \mathbb{P}^2$, $[x : y : z] \mapsto [x^m : y^m : z^m]$. It is a morphism, since the three powers have no common zero, and it is finite, since $g^* \mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(m)$ is ample and $g$ contracts no curve. A finite surjective morphism of smooth varieties of the same dimension is flat (Mat86, Theorem 23.1), and the degree is $m^2$, the number of preimages of a point of the open torus. The pullback of $D_1$ is the divisor of $x^m$, which is $m D'_1$ with $D'_1 = \{x = 0\}$, and similarly for $D_2$ and $D_3$. The three lines $D'_j$ are a reduced snc divisor, so $g$ is a Kawamata cover. Proposition 5.1.3 gives $K_{\mathbb{P}^2} = g^* K_{\mathbb{P}^2} + (m - 1)(D'_1 + D'_2 + D'_3) + R_0$, and the degrees are $-3 = -3m + 3(m - 1) + \deg R_0$, so $\deg R_0 = 0$ and $R_0 = 0$. For the pair $\Delta = (1 - \frac1m)(D_1 + D_2 + D_3)$, Corollary 5.1.4 gives $\Delta' = -R_0 = 0$: the klt pair $(\mathbb{P}^2, \Delta)$, with $K + \Delta \equiv -\frac3m H$, is the quotient of $(\mathbb{P}^2, 0)$ by the group $\mu_m^3 / \mu_m$ acting on the coordinates, and $g^*(K + \Delta) = K_{\mathbb{P}^2}$.

**Example 5.1.7 (ramification off the branch divisor).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1$, $D_1 = [0]$, $D_2 = [\infty]$, $m_1 = 2$ and $m_2 = 3$. Let $g \colon C \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be any Kawamata cover, of degree $d$, from a smooth projective curve of genus $g(C)$. Over $0$ every point of $C$ has ramification index $2$, so $D'_1$ has $\frac{d}{2}$ points, and over $\infty$ every point has index $3$, so $D'_2$ has $\frac{d}{3}$ points. Proposition 5.1.3 and the degree give
$$2 g(C) - 2 = -2d + \tfrac{d}{2} + \tfrac{2d}{3} + \deg R_0 = -\tfrac{5d}{6} + \deg R_0.$$
If $R_0 = 0$, then $2 g(C) - 2 < 0$, so $g(C) = 0$ and $d = \frac{12}{5}$, which is not an integer. So every Kawamata cover for these data ramifies off $D_1 + D_2$. The Kummer cover $z \mapsto z^6$ has $g^*[0] = 6[0] = 2 \cdot (3[0])$, and $3[0]$ is not reduced, so it is not a Kawamata cover for $(2, 3)$; it is one for $(6, 6)$.

**Example 5.1.8 (the trace on the double plane).** Let $h \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the double cover branched along a smooth conic, with $Y \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $h^* \mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(1, 1)$, as in Example P7.8.12. Proposition 5.1.5 makes $H^i(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(d))$ a direct summand of $H^i(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(d, d))$. By the Künneth formula $H^1(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(d, d)) = H^0(\mathcal{O}(d)) \otimes H^1(\mathcal{O}(d)) \oplus H^1(\mathcal{O}(d)) \otimes H^0(\mathcal{O}(d))$, which is $0$ for every $d$, since one factor in each product vanishes. So $H^1(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(d)) = 0$ for every $d$, as Theorem P4.2.1 says directly. The summand is proper: for $d = 1$, $h^0(\mathcal{O}(1, 1)) = 4 > 3 = h^0(\mathcal{O}(1))$, and the complement is $H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(1) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-1)) = H^0(\mathcal{O})$, by the decomposition $h_* \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$ of Proposition P7.8.10.

### Proof

*Theorem 5.1.2.* Two remarks are used at every step. First, if $h \colon W \to V$ is a finite flat surjective morphism from a smooth projective scheme of pure dimension to a smooth projective variety and $W_0$ is a connected component of $W$, then $W_0$ is a smooth connected projective scheme, hence a smooth projective variety, and $h|_{W_0}$ is finite and flat, since $W_0$ is open and closed in $W$. Its image is closed, because $h|_{W_0}$ is finite, and open, because a flat morphism of finite type is open, so it is all of $V$. So $h|_{W_0}$ is again finite flat surjective, and pullbacks of divisors to $W_0$ are the restrictions of the pullbacks to $W$, so they keep the properties smooth and reduced snc. Second, a composite of finite flat surjective morphisms is finite flat surjective.

Stage 1. Put $X_0 = X$ and $g_0 = \mathrm{id}$. For $j = 1, \dots, r$, suppose given a finite flat surjective $g_{j-1} \colon X_{j-1} \to X$ from a smooth projective variety, such that $g_{j-1}^* D$ is reduced snc with every $g_{j-1}^* D_i$ a smooth divisor. Apply Proposition P7.8.11 on $X_{j-1}$ to the line bundle $\mathcal{O}(g_{j-1}^* D_j)$, the integer $q = m_j$ and the reduced snc divisor $\sum_i g_{j-1}^* D_i$, and pass to a connected component by the first remark. This gives $h_j \colon X_j \to X_{j-1}$ finite flat surjective, with $X_j$ a smooth projective variety, a line bundle $M_j$ with $h_j^* \mathcal{O}(g_{j-1}^* D_j) \cong M_j^{m_j}$, and $(g_{j-1} h_j)^* D$ reduced snc with every $(g_{j-1} h_j)^* D_i$ smooth. Put $g_j = g_{j-1} h_j$. After $r$ steps, $g_1' = g_r \colon X_1' = X_r \to X$ is finite flat surjective, $g_1'^* D$ is reduced snc, every $B_j = g_1'^* D_j$ is a smooth divisor, and $\mathcal{O}(B_j) \cong L_j^{m_j}$ with $L_j$ the pullback of $M_j$ to $X_1'$, because a pullback of an $m_j$-th power is an $m_j$-th power.

Stage 2. Let $s_j \in H^0(X_1', L_j^{m_j})$ correspond to the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}(B_j)$, so that $\operatorname{div}(s_j) = B_j$ is smooth and reduced. Suppose by induction that for some $c \in \{1, \dots, r\}$ there is a finite flat surjective $p_{c-1} \colon W_{c-1} \to X_1'$ from a smooth projective variety such that $p_{c-1}^* B_j = m_j B'_j$ with $B'_j$ a smooth divisor for $j < c$, $p_{c-1}^* B_j$ is a smooth divisor for $j \geq c$, and $\sum_{j < c} B'_j + \sum_{j \geq c} p_{c-1}^* B_j$ is reduced snc; for $c = 1$ take $p_0 = \mathrm{id}$. If $m_c = 1$, put $p_c = p_{c-1}$ and $B'_c = p_{c-1}^* B_c$. Otherwise the section $p_{c-1}^* s_c$ of $(p_{c-1}^* L_c)^{m_c}$ has divisor $p_{c-1}^* B_c$, smooth and reduced. Apply Proposition P7.8.10 with $L = p_{c-1}^* L_c$, $q = m_c$, $s = p_{c-1}^* s_c$, and with the divisors $\Delta$ of its part 5 the $B'_j$ for $j < c$ and the $p_{c-1}^* B_j$ for $j > c$. Pass to a connected component. This gives $h \colon W_c \to W_{c-1}$ finite flat surjective with $W_c$ a smooth projective variety, $h^* p_{c-1}^* B_c = m_c B'_c$ with $B'_c$ smooth by part 3, and, by part 5, $B'_c$ plus the pullbacks of the other divisors is reduced snc, with every pullback smooth. For $j < c$, $h^* B'_j$ is smooth, so $(p_{c-1} h)^* B_j = m_j h^* B'_j$ is $m_j$ times a smooth divisor, and $h^* B'_j$ replaces $B'_j$. Put $p_c = p_{c-1} h$. After $c = r$, the composite $g = g_1' \circ p_r \colon X' = W_r \to X$ is finite flat surjective, $g^* D_j = p_r^* B_j = m_j B'_j$ with $B'_j$ smooth, and $\sum_j B'_j$ is reduced snc. So $g$ is a Kawamata cover with $D'_j = B'_j$.

*Proposition 5.1.3.* By Lemma 4.2.28 applied to the finite surjective morphism $g$ of smooth varieties, $K_{X'} = g^* K_X + R$ with $R = \sum_{P} (e_P - 1) P$, the sum over prime divisors $P$ of $X'$, where $e_P$ is the ramification index of $g$ along $P$. Let $P$ be a component of $D'_j$. Since $g^* D_j = m_j D'_j$ and $D'_j$ is reduced, the order along $P$ of the pullback of a local equation of $D_j$ is $m_j$, so $e_P = m_j$. The components of different $D'_j$ are different, because $\sum_j D'_j$ is reduced. So $R = \sum_j (m_j - 1) D'_j + R_0$ with $R_0 = \sum (e_P - 1) P$ over the prime divisors $P$ not contained in $\operatorname{Supp} \sum_j D'_j$, and $R_0 \geq 0$ because $e_P \geq 1$.

*Corollary 5.1.4.* With $R = \sum_j (m_j - 1) D'_j + R_0$ from Proposition 5.1.3, $\Delta' = g^* \Delta - R$, and Lemma 4.2.28 gives $K_{X'} + \Delta' = g^*(K_X + \Delta)$. The coefficient of $D'_j$ in $g^* \Delta = \sum_j a_j m_j D'_j$ is $m_j a_j$, so the coefficient of $D'_j$ in $\Delta'$ is $m_j a_j - (m_j - 1) = 1 - m_j(1 - a_j)$. It is an integer since $m_j a_j \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $a_j < 1$, the number $m_j(1 - a_j) = m_j - m_j a_j$ is a positive integer, so it is at least $1$, and the coefficient is at most $0$. The coefficients of $-R_0$ are integers at most $0$. So $\Delta'$ is integral and $\Delta' \leq 0$. Next, $g^*(N - \Delta) = g^* N - \sum_j m_j a_j D'_j$ has integral coefficients, and $X'$ is smooth, so it is Cartier. For $a_j = 1 - \frac{1}{m_j}$, $m_j a_j = m_j - 1$ is an integer and $1 - m_j(1 - a_j) = 0$, so $\Delta' = -R_0$.

*Proposition 5.1.5.* Let $K = k(X) \subseteq K' = k(X')$, a finite extension of degree $d$, and $\operatorname{Tr} \colon K' \to K$ the trace. Let $U \subseteq X$ be an affine open set and $A = \mathcal{O}_X(U)$. Then $A' = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(g^{-1}(U))$ is a finite $A$-algebra, since $g$ is finite, so it is integral over $A$, and it is integrally closed in $K'$, since $X'$ is normal. So $A'$ is the integral closure of $A$ in $K'$. For $b \in A'$, $\operatorname{Tr}(b)$ is the sum of the conjugates of $b$ in a normal closure of $K'/K$, counted with multiplicity, and each conjugate satisfies the same monic equation over $A$ as $b$. So $\operatorname{Tr}(b) \in K$ is integral over $A$ (AM69, Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.3), and $\operatorname{Tr}(b) \in A$ because $A$ is integrally closed. The maps $\operatorname{Tr} \colon A' \to A$ are $A$-linear and compatible with restriction to smaller affine open sets, so they glue to an $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear $\operatorname{Tr} \colon g_* \mathcal{O}_{X'} \to \mathcal{O}_X$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(1) = d$. Since $k$ has characteristic $0$, $\tau = \frac1d \operatorname{Tr}$ satisfies $\tau(1) = 1$. So the inclusion $\iota \colon \mathcal{O}_X \to g_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ has the left inverse $\tau$.

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be locally free. By the projection formula (Proposition P4.4.10), $g_* g^* \mathcal{F} = \mathcal{F} \otimes g_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}$, and $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}} \otimes \iota$ has the left inverse $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}} \otimes \tau$. Since $g$ is affine, $H^i(X', g^* \mathcal{F}) = H^i(X, g_* g^* \mathcal{F})$ (Har77, Exercise III.4.1), and under this identification the pullback map $H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) \to H^i(X', g^* \mathcal{F})$ is induced by $\mathrm{id} \otimes \iota$. Its composite with the map induced by $\mathrm{id} \otimes \tau$ is the identity, so it is injective with a left inverse.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $g \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$, $z \mapsto z^m$. Show that $g$ is a Kawamata cover for $D_1 = [0]$, $D_2 = [\infty]$ and $m_1 = m_2 = m$, that $R_0 = 0$, and that for $\Delta = a_1 [0] + a_2 [\infty]$ with $m a_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $a_j < 1$, the divisor $\Delta'$ of Corollary 5.1.4 has degree $2 - m(2 - a_1 - a_2)$. Check that $\deg(K_{\mathbb{P}^1} + \Delta') = m \deg(K_{\mathbb{P}^1} + \Delta)$.
2. (A) Let $C$ be the elliptic curve with an automorphism $\rho$ of order $6$ fixing a point, and $g \colon C \to C / \langle \rho \rangle \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ the quotient, of degree $6$. Show that $g$ has three branch points, with ramification indices $2$, $3$ and $6$, and that after a coordinate change on $\mathbb{P}^1$ it is a Kawamata cover for $D_1 = [0]$, $D_2 = [\infty]$ and $(m_1, m_2) = (2, 3)$. Check that $\deg R_0 = 5$, as Example 5.1.7 predicts for $d = 6$ and $g(C) = 1$.
3. (B) Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety, $D$ a smooth divisor and $m \geq 2$. Suppose that $\mathcal{O}(D) \cong L^m$ for a line bundle $L$. Show that the cyclic cover of Proposition P7.8.10 is a Kawamata cover for $(D, m)$ with $R_0 = 0$, and that the pair $(X, (1 - \frac1m) D)$ pulls back to $(Y, 0)$.
4. (B) Let $g \colon X' \to X$ be a Kawamata cover for $(D_j, m_j)$ and $\Delta = \sum_j a_j D_j$ with $m_j a_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $0 \leq a_j < 1$. Use Theorem 4.2.29 to show that $(X', \Delta')$ is sub-klt. Show that $\Delta' + R_0 \geq 0$ holds if and only if $a_j = 1 - \frac{1}{m_j}$ for every $j$.
5. (B) Let $g \colon X' \to X$ be finite surjective between normal varieties. Show that $\tau \circ \iota = \mathrm{id}$ and that $\ker \tau$ is a direct summand of $g_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}$. For the Kummer cover of Example 5.1.6 with $m = 2$, decompose $g_* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}$ into the four eigensheaves of the group $\mu_2^3 / \mu_2$ and identify them as $\mathcal{O}$, $\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 3}$.
6. (C) Show that Proposition 5.1.5 fails in characteristic $p$: for the Frobenius morphism $F \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over a field of characteristic $p$, which has degree $p$, the field trace $\operatorname{Tr} \colon k(\mathbb{P}^1)^{1/p} \to k(\mathbb{P}^1)$ is zero. Explain why the splitting can still exist, by exhibiting an $\mathcal{O}$-linear map $F_* \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}$ that sends $1$ to $1$, and relate this to Chapter 6.

### Notes and history

Kawamata proved the covering lemma (Kaw82) to deduce his vanishing theorem from Kodaira vanishing on the cover; Viehweg proved the same vanishing independently (Vie82) by cyclic covers and the degeneration of the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence. The lemma is Theorem 1-1-1 of KMM87 and Proposition 4.1.12 of Laz04 in the form used here, and Lecture 3 of EV92 develops cyclic covers in general, with the eigensheaf decomposition that Exercise 5 computes. Volume P7 uses the cyclic and Bloch-Gieseker covers in its proof of the ample case, Proposition P7.8.17, and leaves the covering lemma itself as Exercise 6 of Section 8.2; the proof above settles that exercise. The relative vanishing of Section 2 is deduced from Theorem P7.9.3 by compactification rather than by covers, so in this chapter the covers are a tool for reducing pairs with fractional coefficients to integral divisors, as in Corollary 5.1.4. Example 5.1.7 shows that the extra ramification $R_0$ is unavoidable, which is why Kawamata's proof works with $g^*(N - \Delta)$ and Kodaira vanishing on $X'$ rather than with a crepant pullback.
