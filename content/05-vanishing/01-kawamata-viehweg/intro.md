# Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for pairs

Volume P7 proves Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for a smooth projective variety and a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with snc fractional part (Theorem P7.8.18), and the relative form for a morphism of projective varieties with smooth source (Theorem P7.9.3). The minimal model program needs more. Its varieties are singular, its morphisms are contractions over a quasi-projective base, and its divisors are Weil divisors that are only $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier. Volume P7 states the form it needs, Theorem P7.9.5, as a black box. This chapter proves it for projective morphisms.

Section 1 proves Kawamata's covering lemma: for smooth divisors $D_1, \dots, D_r$ with snc sum and integers $m_j \geq 1$, there is a finite flat cover by a smooth projective variety on which every $D_j$ becomes $m_j$ times a smooth divisor. The section computes the ramification of such a cover and the pullback of a pair with coefficients in $\frac{1}{m_j}\mathbb{Z}$, which becomes integral, and proves that the cohomology of a locally free sheaf on a normal variety is a direct summand of the cohomology of its pullback to any finite cover. Section 2 proves the main theorem, Theorem 5.1.13: for a klt pair $(X, \Delta)$, a projective morphism $f \colon X \to Z$ to a quasi-projective variety, and a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor $N$ with $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ nef and big over $Z$, the sheaves $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)$ vanish for $j > 0$. The proof perturbs the pair by Kodaira's lemma until the positive part is ample over $Z$, passes to a log resolution, where the round-up of the pullback has the same push-forward, compactifies the resolution over a projective closure of $Z$, and applies Theorem P7.9.3 there. Section 3 proves local vanishing on a log resolution of an arbitrary $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor on a normal variety, and works the two required examples: the complete quadrangle of six lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$ with a fractional coefficient, and the blow-up of a smooth centre of codimension $r$, where the theorem predicts $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(mE) = 0$ for $m \leq r - 1$, which the direct computation confirms and shows to be sharp.

The examples of failure are a log canonical pair on the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at a point, where the conclusion fails because the boundary has a coefficient 1, and the weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$, where the theorem applies to Weil divisors that are not Cartier. The problems compute the cohomology of the quadrangle with other coefficients, the vanishing on blow-ups of points of $\mathbb{P}^3$, and covers of curves.
