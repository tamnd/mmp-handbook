## Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for klt pairs over a base

### Statement

The setting is that of Volume 1: $f \colon X \to Z$ is a projective morphism from a normal quasi-projective variety to a quasi-projective variety, and $N^1(X/Z)$, $\operatorname{Amp}(X/Z)$ and $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$ are the space and cones of Section 1.2 and Section 1.3. A $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor is *$f$-nef* or *$f$-ample* if its class lies in $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$ or $\operatorname{Amp}(X/Z)$, and *$f$-big*, or big over $Z$, if its class lies in $\operatorname{Big}(X/Z) = \operatorname{Amp}(X/Z) + \operatorname{Eff}(X/Z)$ (Definition 1.3.14). Volume P7 calls a divisor $f$-big if it is big on the generic fibre (Definition P7.9.1). For an $f$-nef divisor the two conditions agree, by the relative form of Kodaira's lemma (KM98, Section 2.5); this section uses Definition 1.3.14 throughout, and its Lemma 5.1.9 needs nothing more. For a projective morphism $g$ and a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$, the sheaves $R^j g_* \mathcal{F}$ restrict to open sets of the target (Har77, Proposition III.8.2), so every vanishing statement below is local on the base.

**Lemma 5.1.9 (Kodaira's lemma over a base).** Let $B$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $X$ that is $f$-nef and $f$-big. There is an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $E$ such that $B - \varepsilon E$ is $f$-ample for every rational $0 < \varepsilon \leq 1$.

**Lemma 5.1.10 (perturbing a klt pair).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a klt pair and $E$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor. There is $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ such that $(X, \Delta + \varepsilon E)$ is klt for every rational $0 \leq \varepsilon \leq \varepsilon_0$.

**Lemma 5.1.11 (the push-forward of a round-up).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a klt pair, $N$ a Weil divisor on $X$ that is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, and $\mu \colon Y \to X$ a proper birational morphism from a smooth variety, with $K_Y + \Delta_Y = \mu^*(K_X + \Delta)$ and $\mu_* K_Y = K_X$. Then
$$\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y\big(\lceil \mu^* N - \Delta_Y \rceil\big) = \mathcal{O}_X(N).$$

**Theorem 5.1.12 (vanishing on a smooth model over a base).** Let $X$ be a quasi-projective variety, $f \colon X \to Z$ a projective morphism to a quasi-projective variety, $A$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $X$ that is $f$-ample, and $h \colon Y \to X$ a projective, generically finite, surjective morphism from a smooth variety. Let $N$ be a Cartier divisor on $Y$ and $\Theta$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$ such that $N - \Theta \sim_\mathbb{Q} h^* A$, meaning that for some $m \geq 1$ the divisors $mA$ and $m(N - \Theta)$ are integral, $mA$ is Cartier, and $\mathcal{O}_Y(m(N - \Theta)) \cong h^* \mathcal{O}_X(mA)$. Then
$$R^j (f h)_* \mathcal{O}_Y(K_Y + N) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad R^j h_* \mathcal{O}_Y(K_Y + N) = 0 \quad \text{for } j > 0.$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Theorem 5.1.13 (Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for klt pairs over a base).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a klt pair and $N$ a Weil divisor on $X$ that is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, such that $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ is $f$-nef and $f$-big. Then
$$R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) = 0 \quad \text{for } j > 0.$$

**Corollary 5.1.14 (two special forms).**

1. Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a klt pair with $X$ projective and $N$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor with $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ nef and big. Then $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) = 0$ for $i > 0$.
2. Let $Y$ be a smooth quasi-projective variety, $g \colon Y \to Z$ a projective morphism, and $D$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with snc support that is $g$-nef and $g$-big. Then $R^j g_* \mathcal{O}_Y(K_Y + \lceil D \rceil) = 0$ for $j > 0$.

**Corollary 5.1.15 (the birational case).** If $f$ is birational, the conclusion of Theorem 5.1.13 holds when $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ is only $f$-nef.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.1.16 (the resolution of a Weil divisor).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a klt pair, $N$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor, $\lambda$ a rational number with $\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta + \lambda N) = \operatorname{Supp} \Delta \cup \operatorname{Supp} N$, and $\mu \colon Y \to X$ a projective log resolution of $(X, \Delta + \lambda N)$. Put
$$N_Y = \lceil \mu^* N - \Delta_Y \rceil, \qquad \Theta = N_Y - \mu^* N + \Delta_Y.$$

1. $\Theta$ has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$, so $(Y, \Theta)$ is klt.
2. $N_Y - (K_Y + \Theta) = \mu^*(N - (K_X + \Delta))$.
3. $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = \mathcal{O}_X(N)$ and $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = 0$ for $j > 0$.
4. For every Cartier divisor $B$ on $X$, $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y + \mu^* B) = \mathcal{O}_X(N + B)$ and $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y + \mu^* B) = 0$ for $j > 0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

### Proof in words

Theorem P7.9.3 is the relative theorem for a smooth projective source and a projective base. To reach a klt pair over a quasi-projective base, three gaps are closed. The positivity is improved first: Kodaira's lemma over a base writes an $f$-nef and $f$-big class as an $f$-ample class plus a small effective divisor, and adding the small divisor to the boundary keeps the pair klt, so $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ may be taken $f$-ample. The singularities are removed next. On a log resolution, $N_Y = \lceil \mu^* N - \Delta_Y \rceil$ is a Cartier divisor whose push-forward is $\mathcal{O}_X(N)$, because the round-up changes the pullback by less than $1$ along each divisor and the klt condition says that $-\Delta_Y$ has every coefficient greater than $-1$. The difference $\Theta = N_Y - \mu^* N + \Delta_Y$ is a fractional snc divisor, and $N_Y - K_Y - \Theta$ is the pullback of the $f$-ample class. The base is compactified last. An $f$-ample divisor embeds $X$ into a projective bundle over $Z$, and closures, a blow-up and a resolution give a smooth projective $\bar Y$ over a projective closure $\bar Z$, containing the resolution as the preimage of $Z$. The divisors extend, after a correction supported on the boundary that is split into its integral and fractional parts, and the extended positive part is the pullback of an ample divisor plus the pullback of an ample divisor from $\bar Z$, which is nef and big. Theorem P7.9.3 on $\bar Y$ then restricts to the vanishing over $Z$.

The same argument with $Z = X$ gives the vanishing of $R^j \mu_*$, and the Leray spectral sequence turns the vanishing upstairs into the vanishing of $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)$. In the birational case every divisor is big over the base, so only nefness is needed, and every pullback $\mu^* D$ is $\mu$-nef. That gives the corollary on resolutions of Weil divisors, which the later chapters use to move statements from smooth models down to klt pairs.

### Examples

**Example 5.1.17 (failure for a log canonical pair).** Let $\mu \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up of a point, $E$ the exceptional curve and $H = \mu^* \ell$ the pullback of a line. The pair $(X, E)$ is snc with coefficient $1$, so it is lc and not klt (Corollary 4.2.13). Let $N = K_X + E + H$, a Cartier divisor with $N - (K_X + E) = H$ nef and big. Serre duality gives $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-E - H))^\vee$. In the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(-E - H) \to \mathcal{O}_X(-H) \to \mathcal{O}_E(-H) \to 0$, the sheaf $\mathcal{O}_E(-H)$ is $\mathcal{O}_E$, since $H \cdot E = 0$ and $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$. By the projection formula and $R^1 \mu_* \mathcal{O}_X = 0$ (Proposition P4.4.16), $H^q(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-H)) = H^q(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(-1)) = 0$ for $q = 0, 1$. So $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-E - H)) \cong H^0(E, \mathcal{O}_E) = k$, and $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) = k \neq 0$. The klt pairs $(X, (1 - \varepsilon) E)$ do not help: $N - (K_X + (1 - \varepsilon) E) = H + \varepsilon E$ has degree $-\varepsilon$ on $E$, so it is not nef.

**Example 5.1.18 (the weighted projective plane).** Let $n \geq 2$ and $X = \mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$, with the torus invariant prime divisors $D_1$, $D_3$, $D_4$ of Example P8.7.16: $\operatorname{Cl}(X) = \mathbb{Z} [D_1]$, $D_3 \sim D_1$, $D_4 \sim n D_1$, and $a D_1$ is Cartier if and only if $n \mid a$. The surface $X$ is the quotient of $\mathbb{P}^2$ by $\mu_n$ acting on the last coordinate, so it is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial and klt by Theorem 4.2.30. By Theorem P8.7.17, $K_X = -D_1 - D_3 - D_4 \sim -(n + 2) D_1$. The Cartier divisor $n D_1 \sim D_4$ is ample, so $D_1$ is ample. For $N = a D_1$, $N - K_X \sim (a + n + 2) D_1$ is ample if and only if $a \geq -n - 1$, and Corollary 5.1.14 with $\Delta = 0$ gives
$$H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1)) = 0 \quad \text{for } i > 0 \text{ and } a \geq -n - 1,$$
including the values of $a$ not divisible by $n$, where $\mathcal{O}_X(a D_1)$ is not invertible and neither Theorem P7.8.18 nor Kodaira vanishing applies to $X$. The bound is sharp: for $a = -n - 2$, $\mathcal{O}_X(a D_1) = \mathcal{O}_X(K_X)$, and $H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X)) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X)^\vee = k$ by Serre duality on the normal, hence Cohen-Macaulay, surface $X$. The toric computation of cohomology (CLS11, Chapter 9) gives the same answer: $H^2(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1))$ has a basis of the Laurent monomials $x^{-i} y^{-j} z^{-l}$ with $i, j, l \geq 1$ and $i + j + n l = -a$, which exist if and only if $a \leq -n - 2$, and $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1)) = 0$ for every $a$.

### Proof

*Lemma 5.1.9.* By Definition 1.3.14, $[B] = \alpha + [E_\mathbb{R}]$ with $\alpha \in \operatorname{Amp}(X/Z)$ and $E_\mathbb{R}$ an effective $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor. Write $E_\mathbb{R} = \sum_{i=1}^s t_i G_i$ with $G_i$ Cartier divisors and $t_i \in \mathbb{R}$. The set $C$ of $t' \in \mathbb{R}^s$ with $\sum_i t'_i G_i \geq 0$ is defined by the finitely many inequalities $\sum_i t'_i \operatorname{mult}_P G_i \geq 0$, one for each prime divisor $P$ in $\bigcup_i \operatorname{Supp} G_i$, with integer coefficients. So $C$ is a rational polyhedral cone, it contains $t$, and its rational points are dense in it. The map $t' \mapsto [\sum_i t'_i G_i]$ from $\mathbb{R}^s$ to $N^1(X/Z)$ is linear, hence continuous, and $\operatorname{Amp}(X/Z)$ is open (Theorem 1.3.4, part 1). So there is a rational $t' \in C$ with $[B] - [\sum_i t'_i G_i]$ still in $\operatorname{Amp}(X/Z)$, since it is close to $[B] - [E_\mathbb{R}] = \alpha$. Put $E = \sum_i t'_i G_i$, an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with $B - E$ $f$-ample. For rational $0 < \varepsilon \leq 1$, $B - \varepsilon E = (1 - \varepsilon) B + \varepsilon (B - E)$ is the sum of an $f$-nef and an $f$-ample class, so it is $f$-ample by Corollary 1.3.5.

*Lemma 5.1.10.* For rational $\varepsilon \geq 0$, $K_X + \Delta + \varepsilon E$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, so $(X, \Delta + \varepsilon E)$ is a pair. If $\dim X = 1$, $X$ is a smooth curve, a pair is klt if and only if its coefficients are less than $1$, and the finitely many coefficients of $\Delta$ are less than $1$; take $\varepsilon_0$ with $\operatorname{mult}_P \Delta + \varepsilon_0 \operatorname{mult}_P E < 1$ for every $P$. Let $\dim X \geq 2$, and let $\mu \colon Y \to X$ be a log resolution of $(X, \Delta + E)$ (Theorem 4.2.10), with $\Delta_Y = \sum_i b_i F_i$ and $\mu^* E = \sum_i e_i F_i$. Here $e_i \geq 0$ because $E$ is effective and $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, and $b_i < 1$ by Theorem 4.2.14, part 1, because $(X, \Delta)$ is klt. For $\varepsilon > 0$, $\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta + \varepsilon E) = \operatorname{Supp}(\Delta + E)$, so $\mu$ is a log resolution of $(X, \Delta + \varepsilon E)$, and the crepant pullback is $\sum_i (b_i + \varepsilon e_i) F_i$. Choose $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ with $b_i + \varepsilon_0 e_i < 1$ for every $i$. For $0 \leq \varepsilon \leq \varepsilon_0$ every coefficient is less than $1$, and Theorem 4.2.14, part 1, shows that $(X, \Delta + \varepsilon E)$ is sub-klt, hence klt since $\Delta + \varepsilon E \geq 0$.

*Lemma 5.1.11.* Write $G = \lceil \mu^* N - \Delta_Y \rceil$. Let $U \subseteq X$ be open and $\varphi$ a nonzero rational function. Then $\varphi \in \mathcal{O}_X(N)(U)$ means $\operatorname{div} \varphi + N \geq 0$ on $U$, and $\varphi \in \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(G)(U)$ means $\operatorname{div} \mu^* \varphi + G \geq 0$ on $\mu^{-1}(U)$.

Suppose $\operatorname{div} \mu^* \varphi + G \geq 0$ on $\mu^{-1}(U)$. Push forward along $\mu$, which takes effective divisors to effective divisors and $\operatorname{div} \mu^* \varphi$ to $\operatorname{div} \varphi$. The non-exceptional components of $\Delta_Y$ are the strict transforms of the components of $\Delta$, with the same coefficients $\delta \in [0, 1)$, and $\mu^* N$ has the coefficient $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ of $N$ along the strict transform of each prime divisor of $X$. Since $\lceil n - \delta \rceil = n$, $\mu_* G = N$. So $\operatorname{div} \varphi + N \geq 0$ on $U$.

Conversely suppose $\operatorname{div} \varphi + N \geq 0$ on $U$. This is an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor on $U$, so its pullback $\operatorname{div} \mu^* \varphi + \mu^* N$ is effective on $\mu^{-1}(U)$. Let $P$ be a prime divisor on $\mu^{-1}(U)$, $o = \operatorname{ord}_P \mu^* \varphi \in \mathbb{Z}$, $x$ the coefficient of $P$ in $\mu^* N$ and $b$ that in $\Delta_Y$. Then $o + x \geq 0$, and $b < 1$ because $(X, \Delta)$ is klt. So $o + \lceil x - b \rceil = \lceil o + x - b \rceil \geq \lceil -b \rceil \geq 0$, as $-b > -1$. So $\operatorname{div} \mu^* \varphi + G \geq 0$. The two inclusions give the equality of subsheaves of the constant sheaf $k(X) = k(Y)$.

*Theorem 5.1.12.* Step 1: reductions. Let $V \subseteq Z$ be an affine open set. The restrictions of $R^j (f h)_*$ and $R^j h_*$ over $V$ and over $f^{-1}(V)$ are the higher direct images of the restricted morphisms (Har77, Proposition III.8.2), and the hypotheses restrict: $A|_{f^{-1}(V)}$ is ample over $V$ by Definition 1.2.25. So $Z$ may be taken affine, and then $A$ is ample on $X$. The second statement is the first for $Z = X$ and $f = \mathrm{id}_X$, which is projective, with $A$ ample over $X$ because every line bundle on an affine scheme is ample. So it is enough to prove the first statement, with $Z$ affine.

Step 2: compactification. Replacing $m$ by a multiple, $mA$ is a Cartier divisor whose line bundle is very ample over $Z$ (Har77, Theorem II.7.6), and the immersion $X \to \mathbb{P}^M_Z$ it defines is closed because $X$ is proper over $Z$; the isomorphism $\mathcal{O}_Y(m(N - \Theta)) \cong h^* \mathcal{O}_X(mA)$ survives taking powers. Let $\bar Z$ be the closure of $Z$ in a projective space containing it, $\bar X$ the closure of $X$ in $\mathbb{P}^M \times \bar Z$, and $\bar f \colon \bar X \to \bar Z$ the projection. Then $\bar X$ is a projective variety, $\bar f^{-1}(Z) = X$ because $X$ is closed in $\mathbb{P}^M \times Z$, and $\mathcal{O}_{\bar X}(1)$ restricts to $\mathcal{O}_X(mA)$. By Definition P3.4.29, $h$ factors as a closed immersion $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{M'} \times X$ and the projection. Let $\bar Y_0$ be the closure of $Y$ in $\mathbb{P}^{M'} \times \bar X$, a projective variety with a morphism $\bar h_0 \colon \bar Y_0 \to \bar X$ and $\bar h_0^{-1}(X) = Y$. So $Y$ is open in $\bar Y_0$.

Step 3: resolution. Let $W_0 = \bar Y_0 \setminus Y$ with its reduced structure. The blow-up of $\bar Y_0$ along $W_0$ is projective and an isomorphism over $Y$, and the preimage of $W_0$ is an effective Cartier divisor (Har77, Proposition II.7.13). A resolution of singularities of the blow-up that is an isomorphism over its smooth locus, which contains $Y$, is projective (Hir64; KM98, Theorem 0.2), and pulls the Cartier divisor back to a Cartier divisor. On the resulting smooth projective variety $\bar Y_1$, let $B_1$ be the reduced preimage of $W_0$ and $\bar\Theta_1$ the closure of $\Theta$. Near every point of $Y$, $(\bar Y_1, B_1 + \bar \Theta_1)$ is $(Y, \Theta)$, which has snc support. Theorem 4.2.10, with a resolution that is a composite of blow-ups, gives a projective log resolution $\bar Y \to \bar Y_1$ of $(\bar Y_1, B_1 + \bar \Theta_1)$ that is an isomorphism over $Y$. Let $\pi \colon \bar Y \to \bar Y_0$ be the composite, $\bar h = \bar h_0 \pi$ and $\bar g = \bar f \bar h \colon \bar Y \to \bar Z$. Then $\bar Y$ is a smooth projective variety, $\pi^{-1}(Y) = Y$, so $\bar h^{-1}(X) = Y$ and $\bar g^{-1}(Z) = Y$, and the complement $B = \bar Y \setminus Y$ is the support of the preimage of the Cartier divisor $B_1$, so it is a union of components of the snc divisor $\operatorname{Exc} + (\text{strict transform of } B_1 + \bar \Theta_1)$. The closure $\bar \Theta$ of $\Theta$ in $\bar Y$ is the strict transform of $\bar \Theta_1$, so $B + \operatorname{Supp} \bar \Theta$ is an snc divisor. The morphism $\bar h$ is surjective, because its image is closed and contains $X$, and generically finite, because its generic fibre is that of $h$.

Step 4: extending the divisors. The line bundle $\bar h^* \mathcal{O}_{\bar X}(1)$ is $\mathcal{O}_{\bar Y}(D_0)$ for a divisor $D_0$ on the smooth variety $\bar Y$, and its restriction to $Y$ is $h^* \mathcal{O}_X(mA) \cong \mathcal{O}_Y(m(N - \Theta))$. So $D_0|_Y - m(N - \Theta) = \operatorname{div}(\psi)|_Y$ for a rational function $\psi$, and $\bar A_Y = D_0 - \operatorname{div}(\psi)$ satisfies $\mathcal{O}_{\bar Y}(\bar A_Y) \cong \bar h^* \mathcal{O}_{\bar X}(1)$ and $\bar A_Y|_Y = m(N - \Theta)$. Let $\bar N$ be the closure of $N$, component by component. The integral divisor $G = m(\bar N - \bar \Theta) - \bar A_Y$ restricts to $0$ on $Y$, so $\operatorname{Supp} G \subseteq B$. Put
$$N' = \bar N - \lfloor G/m \rfloor, \qquad \Theta' = \bar \Theta + \{G/m\}.$$
Then $N'$ is a Cartier divisor with $N'|_Y = N$, and $N' - \Theta' = \bar N - \bar \Theta - G/m = \frac1m \bar A_Y$. The divisors $\bar \Theta$ and $\{G/m\}$ have no common component, since every component of $\bar \Theta$ meets $Y$ and every component of $G$ lies in $B$. So $\Theta'$ has coefficients in $[0, 1)$ and support in the snc divisor $B + \operatorname{Supp} \bar \Theta$, and $\Theta'|_Y = \Theta$.

Step 5: positivity and the vanishing. Let $\bar H$ be a very ample divisor on $\bar Z$. The line bundle $\mathcal{O}(1) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}_{\bar Z}(\bar H)$ on $\mathbb{P}^M \times \bar Z$ is very ample, by the Segre embedding, so its restriction $\mathcal{O}_{\bar X}(1) \otimes \bar f^* \mathcal{O}_{\bar Z}(\bar H)$ is ample. Since $\bar h$ is generically finite and surjective, Lemma P7.9.2 shows that its pullback $\mathcal{O}_{\bar Y}(\bar A_Y + \bar g^* \bar H)$ is nef and big. So
$$N' - \Theta' + \bar g^*\big(\tfrac1m \bar H\big) = \tfrac1m \big(\bar A_Y + \bar g^* \bar H\big)$$
is nef and big, and Theorem P7.9.3 for the morphism $\bar g \colon \bar Y \to \bar Z$ of projective varieties, with the smooth source $\bar Y$, the Cartier divisor $N'$, the snc divisor $\Theta'$ and the ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $\frac1m \bar H$, gives $R^j \bar g_* \mathcal{O}_{\bar Y}(K_{\bar Y} + N') = 0$ for $j > 0$. Restrict to $Z$: $\bar g^{-1}(Z) = Y$, $\bar g|_Y = f h$, and $(K_{\bar Y} + N')|_Y = K_Y + N$, so Har77, Proposition III.8.2, gives $R^j (f h)_* \mathcal{O}_Y(K_Y + N) = 0$ for $j > 0$.

*Theorem 5.1.13.* Put $B = N - (K_X + \Delta)$, a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor that is $f$-nef and $f$-big. Lemma 5.1.9 gives an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $E$ with $B - \varepsilon E$ $f$-ample for $0 < \varepsilon \leq 1$, and Lemma 5.1.10 gives a rational $\varepsilon \in (0, 1]$ with $\Delta' = \Delta + \varepsilon E$ klt. Then $A = N - (K_X + \Delta') = B - \varepsilon E$ is $f$-ample. Choose a rational $\lambda$ with $\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta' + \lambda N) = \operatorname{Supp} \Delta' \cup \operatorname{Supp} N$, which excludes finitely many values, and let $\mu \colon Y \to X$ be a projective log resolution of $(X, \Delta' + \lambda N)$ (Theorem 4.2.10, with a resolution that is a composite of blow-ups). Write $K_Y + \Delta'_Y = \mu^*(K_X + \Delta')$ with $\mu_* K_Y = K_X$. The supports of $\Delta'_Y$ and $\mu^* N$ lie in $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu) \cup \mu^{-1}_* \operatorname{Supp}(\Delta' + \lambda N)$, an snc divisor. Put
$$N_Y = \lceil \mu^* N - \Delta'_Y \rceil, \qquad \Theta = N_Y - \mu^* N + \Delta'_Y.$$
Then $N_Y$ is a Cartier divisor on the smooth variety $Y$, $\Theta$ has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$, being the differences $\lceil x \rceil - x$, and
$$(N_Y - K_Y) - \Theta = \mu^* N - \mu^*(K_X + \Delta') = \mu^* A.$$
Theorem 5.1.12, for $h = \mu$, the Cartier divisor $N_Y - K_Y$, the divisor $\Theta$ and the $f$-ample $A$, gives $R^j (f \mu)_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = 0$ and $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = 0$ for $j > 0$. Lemma 5.1.11 for the klt pair $(X, \Delta')$ gives $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = \mathcal{O}_X(N)$. The spectral sequence $R^p f_* R^q \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) \Rightarrow R^{p+q}(f \mu)_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y)$ of Corollary P2.4.26 has $E_2^{p,q} = 0$ for $q > 0$, so $R^p f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \cong R^p (f \mu)_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = 0$ for $p > 0$.

*Corollary 5.1.14.* Part 1 is Theorem 5.1.13 for $Z = \operatorname{Spec} k$, where $R^j f_*$ is $H^j$ and big over $Z$ is big. For part 2, put $\Delta = \lceil D \rceil - D$. It has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$, so $(Y, \Delta)$ is klt by Corollary 4.2.13, the identity being a log resolution. For $N = K_Y + \lceil D \rceil$, Cartier since $Y$ is smooth, $N - (K_Y + \Delta) = D$ is $g$-nef and $g$-big, and Theorem 5.1.13 applies.

*Corollary 5.1.15.* The statement is local on $Z$: over an affine open $V \subseteq Z$, the pair $(f^{-1}(V), \Delta|_{f^{-1}(V)})$ is klt and the restriction of $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ is nef over $V$, since every curve contracted over $V$ is contracted over $Z$. So let $Z$ be affine. It is enough to show that every $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ on $X$ is $f$-big; then Theorem 5.1.13 applies. Let $H$ be an $f$-ample Cartier divisor (Example 1.2.26) and $m \geq 1$ with $mD$ Cartier. The sheaf $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD - H)$ is coherent (Theorem P4.4.8), and it is invertible over the dense open set over which $f$ is an isomorphism, so it is nonzero. On the affine variety $Z$ a nonzero coherent sheaf has a nonzero global section, and such a section is a nonzero $s \in H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - H))$. With $G = \operatorname{div}(s) \geq 0$, $mD \sim H + G$, so $[D] = \frac1m [H] + \frac1m [G] \in \operatorname{Amp}(X/Z) + \operatorname{Eff}(X/Z)$.

*Corollary 5.1.16.* Part 1. The supports of $\Delta_Y$ and $\mu^* N$ lie in the snc divisor $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu) + \mu^{-1}_* \operatorname{Supp}(\Delta + \lambda N)$, and every coefficient of $\Theta$ has the form $\lceil x \rceil - x \in [0, 1)$. So $(Y, \Theta)$ is klt by Corollary 4.2.13. Part 2 is $N_Y - \Theta = \mu^* N - \Delta_Y$ and $K_Y + \Delta_Y = \mu^*(K_X + \Delta)$. Part 3. The push-forward is Lemma 5.1.11. By part 2, $N_Y - (K_Y + \Theta)$ is the pullback of a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor from $X$, so it has degree $0$ on every curve contracted by $\mu$ and is $\mu$-nef. Corollary 5.1.15 for the birational projective morphism $\mu$, the klt pair $(Y, \Theta)$ and the Cartier divisor $N_Y$ gives $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = 0$ for $j > 0$. Part 4. Since $\mu^* B$ is integral, $\lceil \mu^*(N + B) - \Delta_Y \rceil = N_Y + \mu^* B$, and $N + B$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor. Lemma 5.1.11 for $N + B$ gives the push-forward, and the argument of part 3 with $N_Y + \mu^* B - (K_Y + \Theta) = \mu^*(N + B - (K_X + \Delta))$ gives the vanishing.

### Lean

The statement of Theorem 5.1.13 is `MinimalModel.KawamataViehweg` in the project library, stated without proof: a projective morphism to a scheme of finite type over $k$, a klt pair with rational boundary, and $N \equiv_\pi K_X + \Delta + M$ with $M$ nef and big over the base, where big means an ample divisor over the base plus an effective one, as in Definition 1.3.14. Theorem 5.1.13 proves it when $X$ and the base are quasi-projective varieties. The library has no proof of Theorem P7.9.3, no compactification and no resolution, so none of the proofs of this section is formalized. `MinimalModelBridge.KawamataViehwegSNC` is the absolute snc form, which is Serre dual to Corollary 5.1.14, part 2, for $Z$ a point. Lemma 5.1.9, Lemma 5.1.10, Lemma 5.1.11, Theorem 5.1.12 and Corollaries 5.1.14 to 5.1.16 are not yet in Lean.

### Notes and history

Kawamata (Kaw82) and Viehweg (Vie82) proved the vanishing for nef and big divisors with snc fractional part on smooth projective varieties. The relative form for klt pairs is in KMM87, Section 1-2, and KM98, Theorem 2.70, which treats a proper morphism and reduces to the projective case by Chow's lemma; the proof above is the projective case, and it follows KM98 in replacing the klt pair by a smooth model and the base by a compactification. KM98 deduces the smooth relative statement from the absolute one through Theorem 2.64 and a direct compactification argument, while here the smooth relative statement is Theorem P7.9.3. Corollary 5.1.16 is the form used in the proof of the base point free theorem in Volume 7: the push-forward from a log resolution recovers the reflexive sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(N)$ and has no higher direct images. Example 5.1.17 is the standard reason for the klt hypothesis; for lc pairs the right statements are Ambro's and Fujino's vanishing theorems for quasi-log structures (Fuj11), which need the lc centres in their hypotheses. Lazarsfeld, Chapter 9 (Laz04), proves the same theorems in the language of multiplier ideals, which is the subject of Chapter 3.
