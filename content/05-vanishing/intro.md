# Vanishing theorems and multiplier ideals

The minimal model program runs on vanishing theorems. The base point free theorem, the cone theorem, the existence of flips and the extension of sections all reduce, at some step, to the vanishing of a cohomology group of an adjoint divisor $K_X + \Delta + M$ with $M$ positive. Volume P7, Chapters 8 to 10, proves the absolute and smooth forms: Kodaira and Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing on a smooth projective variety, the relative form for a smooth source, and Nadel vanishing for multiplier ideals on a smooth projective variety. This volume proves the forms that the program uses: for klt pairs on singular varieties, relative to a quasi-projective base, and for the multiplier ideals of pairs. It cites Volume P7 by label and does not repeat its proofs. Volume P6 proves Kodaira vanishing by Hodge theory (Corollaries P6.6.13, P6.7.17 and P6.7.27), and this volume uses it only through Volume P7.

> **The discrepancy box.** For a projective birational $f \colon Y \to X$ and a pair $(X, \Delta)$, write $K_Y + \Delta_Y = f^*(K_X + \Delta)$ with $f_*\Delta_Y = \Delta$. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the *discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) = -\operatorname{mult}_E \Delta_Y$, and the *log discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) + 1$. The pair is terminal or canonical if $a > 0$ or $a \ge 0$ for every exceptional $E$ over $X$. It is klt or lc if $a > -1$ or $a \ge -1$ for every prime divisor $E$ over $X$, exceptional or not. For a divisor $E$ on $X$ itself, $a(E, X, \Delta)$ is minus the coefficient of $E$ in $\Delta$, so klt asks for coefficients less than 1 and lc for coefficients at most 1. The Lean code states the same conditions with the log discrepancy, over all divisorial valuations.

The box is the one of Volume 0, Chapter 3, and of Volume 4. In this volume it enters through the round-up: on a log resolution of a klt pair the divisor $\lceil -\Delta_Y \rceil$ is effective and exceptional, because every coefficient of $\Delta_Y$ is less than 1. This one fact moves vanishing theorems from a smooth model down to a singular pair, and it is also the reason why the multiplier ideal of a klt pair is trivial.

Chapter 1 proves Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for klt pairs relative to a quasi-projective base, in the form of Kollár-Mori, Theorem 2.70: if $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ is nef and big over $Z$, then $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) = 0$ for $j > 0$. The proof compactifies a smooth model and applies the relative theorem of Volume P7, Chapter 9. The chapter also proves Kawamata's covering lemma, local vanishing on a log resolution of a singular variety, and works two examples by hand: a fractional arrangement of six lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$, and the higher direct images on the blow-up of a smooth centre. Chapter 2 proves Kollár's injectivity theorem and torsion freeness for klt pairs from the injectivity theorem of Esnault and Viehweg, then the vanishing of $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)$ above the relative dimension and Kollár vanishing, and collects the relative package that Volumes 7 to 9 use. Chapter 3 defines multiplier ideals of pairs on normal varieties and of ideals, proves that they do not depend on the resolution, proves Nadel vanishing for pairs and over a base, and computes the mixed multiplier ideals of monomial ideals with a toric boundary.

Chapter 4 treats the local properties of multiplier ideals: the restriction theorem, subadditivity, Skoda's theorem, jumping numbers, and the log canonical threshold as the first jumping number. Chapter 5 outlines the extension theorems of Siu, Takayama and Hacon-McKernan and the invariance of plurigenera, with the precise statements that the existence of flips needs. Chapter 6 shows what fails in characteristic $p$: Raynaud's surface on which Kodaira vanishing fails, the failure of Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, Frobenius splitting as a partial substitute, and a preview of Volume 11.

The spine is Kollár-Mori, Chapter 2 (KM98), Lazarsfeld's Positivity II, Chapter 9 (Laz04), and the lectures of Esnault and Viehweg (EV92). Kollár's papers on higher direct images of dualizing sheaves (Kol86a, Kol86b) and Fujino's account of the vanishing theorems for the program (Fuj11) are the sources of Chapter 2.
