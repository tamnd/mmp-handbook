## Asymptotic multiplier ideals

### Statement

The multiplier ideal of one linear system $|pL|$ sees the base locus of $|pL|$ only. The extension theorems of Section 2 and the proof of invariance of plurigenera in Section 3 need an ideal that sees all multiples of $L$ at once. This section defines it, after Lazarsfeld (Laz04, Chapter 11), and proves its basic properties: it contains the base ideals, it satisfies Nadel vanishing with no positivity beyond bigness, and it is trivial for a nef and big divisor. Throughout, $X$ is a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$ over $k$ of characteristic $0$, and $L$ is a line bundle on $X$ of nonnegative Iitaka dimension, so that $H^0(X, L^m) \neq 0$ for some $m \geq 1$.

**Definition 5.5.1 (base ideals).** For $m \geq 1$ the *base ideal* $\mathfrak{b}_m = \mathfrak{b}(|mL|) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ is the image of the evaluation map $H^0(X, L^m) \otimes_k L^{-m} \to \mathcal{O}_X$. It is zero if $H^0(X, L^m) = 0$, and its zero set is $\operatorname{Bs}|mL|$. The set $\mathbf{N}(L) = \{ m \geq 1 : \mathfrak{b}_m \neq 0 \}$ is closed under addition, and $\mathfrak{b}_p \cdot \mathfrak{b}_q \subseteq \mathfrak{b}_{p + q}$ for all $p, q$, since the product of two sections is a section.

**Lemma 5.5.2 (a directed family).** Let $c > 0$ be rational, $p \in \mathbf{N}(L)$ and $k \geq 1$. Then $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_{kp}^{c/kp})$.

**Definition 5.5.3 (asymptotic multiplier ideals).** Let $c > 0$ be rational. By Lemma 5.5.2, for $p, q \in \mathbf{N}(L)$ the ideals $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p})$ and $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_q^{c/q})$ are both contained in $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_{pq}^{c/pq})$. Since $\mathcal{O}_X$ is Noetherian, the family $\{ \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p}) : p \in \mathbf{N}(L) \}$ has a unique maximal member, the *asymptotic multiplier ideal*
$$\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p}) \quad \text{for every sufficiently divisible } p \in \mathbf{N}(L).$$
Put $\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|) = \mathcal{J}(m \cdot \| L \|)$.

The phrase "every sufficiently divisible $p$" is justified by Lemma 5.5.2: once $p_0$ computes the maximum, so does every multiple of $p_0$.

**Proposition 5.5.4 (first properties).** Let $c, c' > 0$ be rational and $m \geq 1$.

1. $\mathfrak{b}_m \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$, and the inclusion $H^0(X, L^m \otimes \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)) \to H^0(X, L^m)$ is an isomorphism.
2. If $c \leq c'$, then $\mathcal{J}(c' \cdot \| L \|) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|)$.
3. $\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\| L \|)^m$.
4. The zero set of $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|)$ is contained in the stable base locus $\mathbf{B}(L)$ of Definition P7.7.12.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Theorem 5.5.5 (asymptotic Nadel vanishing).** Let $L$ be big and $A$ a nef Cartier divisor. Then for every $m \geq 1$,
$$H^i\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + A) \otimes L^m \otimes \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)\big) = 0 \quad \text{for } i > 0.$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.5.6 (global generation).** Let $L$ be big and $H$ a very ample divisor. Then for every $m \geq 1$ the sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + (n+1) H) \otimes L^m \otimes \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$ is generated by its global sections. In particular
$$\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|) \subseteq \mathfrak{b}\big(|K_X + (n + 1) H + mL|\big).$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Theorem 5.5.7 (nef and big divisors).** If $L$ is nef and big, then $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|) = \mathcal{O}_X$ for every rational $c > 0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

Corollary 5.5.6 is the reason the asymptotic ideal is useful: the base loci of $|mL|$ for all $m$ are controlled, up to a fixed twist, by one family of ideals. For a big $L$ the union of the zero sets is a numerical invariant.

**Remark 5.5.8 (the restricted base locus).** Let $L$ be big. Ein, Lazarsfeld, Mustaţă, Nakamaye and Popa (ELMNP06, Section 2) prove that $\mathbf{B}_-(L) = \bigcup_{m \geq 1} Z(\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|))$, where $\mathbf{B}_-$ is the restricted base locus of Definition P7.7.14 and $Z$ denotes the zero set. The proof combines part 4 of Proposition 5.5.4, applied to small ample perturbations of $L$, with Corollary 5.5.6. In particular the union depends only on the numerical class of $L$, while $\mathbf{B}(L)$ does not (Example P7.7.19). This is used here only as orientation.

### Proof in words

A $k$-th power of the base ideal of $|pL|$ lies in the base ideal of $|kpL|$, so the family of ideals $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p})$ is directed, and Noetherianity gives a maximum. For vanishing, choose $p$ computing the maximum and a log resolution of $\mathfrak{b}_p$, on which $|pL|$ becomes a free linear system plus a fixed divisor. The free part is the pullback of a hyperplane by the map of $|pL|$, which is generically finite because $L$ is big, so it is nef and big. Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing on the resolution, local vanishing and the Leray spectral sequence finish the proof, as for Nadel vanishing, except that the positivity comes from the free part of $|pL|$ and not from $L - cM$. Global generation is Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity. For a nef and big $L$, adding a fixed adjoint bundle makes every $kL$ free, and Kodaira's lemma absorbs the adjoint bundle into a fixed effective divisor $E_1$, so the base ideals contain $\mathcal{O}_X(-E_1)$ for all large $p$, and dividing by $p$ makes the singularity disappear.

### Examples

**Example 5.5.9 (a fixed component).** Let $\pi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up of a point with exceptional curve $E$, and $H = \pi^* \mathcal{O}(1)$. Let $L = H + E$, which is big. Since $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_X(mE) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}$, the projection formula gives $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mH + mE)) = H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(m))$, so every member of $|mL|$ contains $mE$. So $|mL| = |mH| + mE$ and $\mathfrak{b}_m = \mathcal{O}_X(-mE)$, since $|mH|$ is free. Then $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p}) = \mathcal{J}(cE) = \mathcal{O}_X(-\lfloor c \rfloor E)$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.10.4, and $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|) = \mathcal{O}_X(-\lfloor c \rfloor E)$, so $\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|) = \mathcal{O}_X(-mE) = \mathfrak{b}_m$. Theorem 5.5.5 says $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + A + mH)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and $A$ nef, which is also Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for the nef and big divisor $mH + A$. Corollary 5.5.6 says the base locus of $|K_X + 3H' + mL|$, for $H'$ very ample, lies in $E$: here $\mathbf{B}(L) = E$.

**Example 5.5.10 (curves).** Let $X = C$ be a smooth projective curve and $L$ a line bundle of degree $d$. If $d > 0$, then $L$ is ample, $\mathfrak{b}_m = \mathcal{O}_C$ for $m \geq 2g / d$, and $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|) = \mathcal{O}_C$, as Theorem 5.5.7 predicts. If $d = 0$ and $L$ is torsion of order $r$, then $\mathbf{N}(L) = r \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ and $\mathfrak{b}_{lr} = \mathcal{O}_C$, so again $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|) = \mathcal{O}_C$, although $L$ is not big and Theorem 5.5.5 does not apply: for $L = \mathcal{O}_C$, $A = 0$ and $g \geq 1$, $H^1(C, K_C) \neq 0$. If $d = 0$ and $L$ is not torsion, $\mathbf{N}(L)$ is empty and the definition does not apply.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 5.5.2.* By Definition 5.5.1, $\mathfrak{b}_p^k \subseteq \mathfrak{b}_{kp}$. Let $\mu$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak{b}_p$ with $\mathfrak{b}_p \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$. Then $\mathfrak{b}_p^k \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-kF)$, so $\mathcal{J}((\mathfrak{b}_p^k)^{c/kp}) = \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \frac{c}{kp} kF \rfloor) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p})$. Part 3 of Proposition P7.10.4 gives $\mathcal{J}((\mathfrak{b}_p^k)^{c/kp}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_{kp}^{c/kp})$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 5.5.4.* Part 1. For a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ with log resolution $\mu$, $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F) \subseteq \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - F) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a})$, because $K_{X'/X} \geq 0$. So $\mathfrak{b}_m \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_m^{m/m}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$, where the second inclusion holds by maximality, through Lemma 5.5.2. Every section of $L^m$ is a section of $L^m \otimes \mathfrak{b}_m$ by the definition of $\mathfrak{b}_m$, hence of $L^m \otimes \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$, which gives the isomorphism; if $\mathfrak{b}_m = 0$ both sides are $0$.

Part 2. For a common sufficiently divisible $p$, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c'/p}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p})$ by part 3 of Proposition P7.10.4.

Part 3. Choose $p$ that computes both $\mathcal{J}(m \cdot \| L \|)$ and $\mathcal{J}(1 \cdot \| L \|)$; a common multiple of two such integers works. By Corollary 5.4.8, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{m/p}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{1/p})^m$.

Part 4. Let $x \notin \mathbf{B}(L)$. By Definition P7.7.12, $\mathbf{B}(L) = \operatorname{Bs}|pL|$ for every sufficiently divisible $p$, so there is $p$ that computes $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|)$ with $x \notin \operatorname{Bs}|pL|$. Then $\mathfrak{b}_p = \mathcal{O}_X$ on a neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, and since multiplier ideals are local, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p})|_U = \mathcal{J}(\mathcal{O}_U^{c/p}) = \mathcal{O}_U$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 5.5.5.* Choose $p$ that computes $\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$. Replacing $p$ by a multiple, which does not change the ideal, assume also that the rational map $\varphi_p$ of $|pL|$ is birational onto its image; this holds for all sufficiently divisible $p$ because $L$ is big (Section P7.6, Iitaka dimension $n$). Let $\mu \colon X' \to X$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak{b}_p$ with $\mathfrak{b}_p \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F_p)$. Then
$$\mu^*(pL) = W_p + F_p,$$
where $W_p$ is a Cartier divisor whose linear system contains $\mu^* |pL| - F_p$ and is free: indeed $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(W_p) = \mu^* L^p \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F_p)$ is generated by the pullbacks of the sections of $L^p$. The morphism $X' \to \mathbb{P}^N$ given by these sections resolves $\varphi_p$ and is generically finite onto its image, and $W_p$ is the pullback of a hyperplane. By Lemma P7.9.2, $W_p$ is nef and big, and so is $\frac{m}{p} W_p + \mu^* A$. Put
$$N = \mu^*(mL + A) - \Big\lfloor \frac{m}{p} F_p \Big\rfloor, \qquad \Delta = \Big\{ \frac{m}{p} F_p \Big\}.$$
Then $\Delta$ has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$, and $N - \Delta = \mu^*(mL + A) - \frac{m}{p} F_p \equiv \frac{m}{p} W_p + \mu^* A$ is nef and big. Theorem P7.8.18 gives $H^i(X', \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'} + N)) = 0$ for $i > 0$. Now
$$K_{X'} + N = \mu^*(K_X + mL + A) + K_{X'/X} - \Big\lfloor \frac{m}{p} F_p \Big\rfloor.$$
By Lemma 5.4.1, $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \frac{m}{p} F_p \rfloor) = 0$ for $j > 0$, and $\mu_*$ of this sheaf is $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{m/p}) = \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$. By the projection formula and the Leray spectral sequence,
$$H^i\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + mL + A) \otimes \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)\big) = H^i\big(X', \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'} + N)\big) = 0 \quad (i > 0). \qquad \square$$

*Proof of Corollary 5.5.6.* Embed $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r$ by $|H|$, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be the push-forward to $\mathbb{P}^r$ of $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + (n+1)H) \otimes L^m \otimes \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$. For $1 \leq i \leq n$, $H^i(\mathbb{P}^r, \mathcal{F}(-i)) = H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + (n + 1 - i) H) \otimes L^m \otimes \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)) = 0$ by Theorem 5.5.5 with the nef divisor $A = (n + 1 - i) H$. For $i > n$ the group vanishes because $\dim X = n$. So $\mathcal{F}$ is $0$-regular, and part 3 of Theorem P4.2.16 says $\mathcal{F}$ is generated by global sections. A global section of $\mathcal{F}$ is a section of $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + (n+1)H + mL)$ that lies in the subsheaf, so the subsheaf $\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|) \cdot \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + (n+1)H + mL)$ is generated by sections of $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + (n+1)H + mL)$, which is the displayed inclusion. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 5.5.7.* Let $H$ be very ample and $B = K_X + (n + 1) H$. For every $k \geq 0$ the divisor $kL + B$ is globally generated: for $1 \leq i \leq n$, $kL + (n + 1 - i) H$ is ample, as the sum of a nef and an ample divisor, so $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + kL + (n + 1 - i) H)) = 0$ by Theorem P7.8.2, and the regularity argument of Corollary 5.5.6 applies. Choose $a \geq 1$ such that $aH - B$ is linearly equivalent to an effective divisor $G$, which is possible because $H$ is very ample. Since $L$ is big, Theorem P7.5.3 applied to a member of $|aH|$ gives $m_1 \geq 1$ and an effective divisor $G'$ with $m_1 L - aH \sim G'$. So $m_1 L - B \sim E_1 = G + G'$, effective. For every $p \geq m_1$,
$$pL \sim \big((p - m_1) L + B\big) + E_1,$$
and the first summand is globally generated. Adding $E_1$ to the members of the free linear system $|(p - m_1)L + B|$ gives members of $|pL|$, so $\mathfrak{b}_p \supseteq \mathcal{O}_X(-E_1)$. By part 3 and part 4 of Proposition P7.10.4, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{c/p}) \supseteq \mathcal{J}(\frac{c}{p} E_1)$. By Proposition P7.10.5 the last ideal is $\mathcal{O}_X$ as soon as $\frac{c}{p} < \operatorname{lct}(X; E_1)$, which holds for $p$ large. So the maximal member of the family is $\mathcal{O}_X$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|) = \mathcal{J}(\frac{c}{r} \cdot \| L^r \|)$ for every $r \geq 1$.
2. (A) Let $L$ be semiample. Show that $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|) = \mathcal{O}_X$ for every $c > 0$ directly from the definition, without Theorem 5.5.7.
3. (B) On the blow-up $X$ of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at a point, let $L = aH + bE$ with $a > 0$ and $b \in \mathbb{Z}$. Compute $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \| L \|)$ for all $c > 0$.
4. (B) Show that $\mathcal{J}(\| (m + l) L \|) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\| mL \|) \cdot \mathcal{J}(\| lL \|)$ for all $m, l \geq 1$.
5. (B) Let $L$ be big and $H$ very ample. Deduce from Corollary 5.5.6 that $\operatorname{Bs}|K_X + (n+1) H + mL| \subseteq Z(\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|))$ for every $m \geq 1$, and compute both sides for $L = H_0 + E$ in Example 5.5.9, where $H_0$ is the pullback of a line.
6. (C) Let $L$ be big. Show that $L$ is nef if and only if $\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|) = \mathcal{O}_X$ for every $m \geq 1$. Hint: for the converse, use Corollary 5.5.6 to bound the base locus of $|mL + B|$ for a fixed $B$, and deduce that $L \cdot C \geq 0$ for every curve $C$.

### Notes and history

Asymptotic multiplier ideals were introduced analytically by Siu, through the singular metric $\sum |s_j|^{2/m}$ built from all sections of $L^m$, and algebraically by Ein, Lazarsfeld and Smith in their work on symbolic powers; the systematic treatment is Laz04, Chapter 11. The analytic version of Theorem 5.5.5 is Nadel vanishing for the metric of minimal singularities. Theorem 5.5.7 and the converse in Exercise 6 are in Laz04, Section 11.2. Asymptotic multiplier ideals of graded families of ideals, not only of base ideals, are treated by the same method, and are the tool in Section 3.
