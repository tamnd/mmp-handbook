# Extension theorems and invariance of plurigenera

A vanishing theorem kills an obstruction group, and the first use of a killed $H^1$ is to extend sections from a divisor. This chapter studies extension of sections from a smooth divisor $S \subset X$, the asymptotic multiplier ideals that make extension work for all multiples of a line bundle at once, and the application that made the method famous: Siu's theorem that the plurigenera $P_m = h^0(\omega^{\otimes m})$ are constant in a smooth projective family. Throughout, $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$, and the analytic theorems are over $\mathbb{C}$.

Section 1 defines the asymptotic multiplier ideal $\mathcal{J}(\| mL \|)$ as the largest of the ideals $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}_p^{m/p})$ built from the base ideals of $|pL|$. It proves that it contains the base ideal of $|mL|$, that it satisfies Nadel vanishing for every big $L$ with no further positivity, that it is cut out by an adjoint linear system up to a fixed twist, and that it is trivial when $L$ is nef and big. Section 2 proves the extension theorem with a multiplier ideal: if $L - D$ is nef and big, then every section of $K_S + L|_S$ in $\mathcal{J}(S, D|_S)$ extends to a section of $K_X + S + L$. It then states precisely, with outlines of their proofs, the extension theorems of Siu, Kawamata, Takayama and Hacon-McKernan. Section 3 proves that invariance of plurigenera over an affine curve is equivalent to extension of pluricanonical sections from a fibre, states Siu's theorem, and outlines its proof for fibres of general type.

The examples are a fixed component on the blow-up of the plane, line bundles on curves, a product $\mathbb{P}^1 \times C$ on which the bigness hypothesis is needed, the Poincaré family on an elliptic curve, whose sections jump, and two degenerations of quintic surfaces: to an ordinary triple point, where the geometric genus drops from $4$ to $3$, and to an ordinary double point, where it does not. The chapter ends with problems.
