# Vanishing in characteristic p

The vanishing theorems of Chapters 1 to 5 are theorems about characteristic $0$, and this chapter explains what happens to them over a field of characteristic $p > 0$. Chapter 11 of Part P7 already proves the basic facts: Tango and Raynaud's counterexample to Kodaira vanishing, Deligne-Illusie vanishing for varieties that lift to $W_2$, and vanishing on Frobenius split varieties. This chapter builds on that chapter without repeating it, and turns to the local theory that replaces multiplier ideals.

Section 1 explains why the counterexamples exist: a failure of Kodaira vanishing for an ample $L$ is the same as a failure of injectivity of Frobenius on $H^1(X, L^{-1})$, and Raynaud's surface is an outline of how to build one. It shows that Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing fails, by the cone over a Raynaud surface, that vanishing holds for the reduction of a characteristic $0$ variety modulo all large primes but with no uniform bound, and that even rational surfaces can violate Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing. Section 2 turns to what survives: it proves Fedder's criterion for a hypersurface to be F-split, decides which cones are F-split, defines global F-regularity, and states the theorems of Smith, of Cascini, Tanaka and Witaszek, and of Arvidsson, Bernasconi and Lacini, which give Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing on klt del Pezzo surfaces for $p > 5$. Section 3 defines test ideals and F-pure thresholds by Frobenius roots, proves that monomial test ideals are given by Howald's formula in every characteristic, computes the F-pure threshold of the cusp, which equals $\frac56$ for $p \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ and is smaller for $p \equiv 5 \pmod 6$, and previews the minimal model program of Volume 11.

The examples are curves, on which vanishing always holds, the node, the cusp, the cones over the Fermat cubic and over a conic, a coordinate hyperplane, and the monomial ideal $(x^2, y^3)$ compared with the cusp. The chapter ends with problems.
