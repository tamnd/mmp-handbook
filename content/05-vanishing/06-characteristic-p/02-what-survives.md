## What survives in characteristic p

### Statement

Kodaira vanishing holds on F-split varieties (Theorem P7.11.17), and Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing holds on a stronger class, the globally F-regular varieties of Smith. Whether a singularity is F-split is decided by a computation with one polynomial, Fedder's criterion, which this section proves for hypersurfaces. The section then states the results on surfaces that Volume 11 uses: klt del Pezzo surfaces are globally F-regular in large characteristic, and satisfy Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for $p > 5$. Throughout, $k$ is a perfect field of characteristic $p > 0$, $S = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$, $q = p^e$, and $F_* M$ denotes an $S$-module $M$ viewed as an $S$-module through Frobenius, $s \cdot F_* m = F_*(s^p m)$.

**Definition 5.6.9 (F-split at a point).** Let $R$ be a ring of characteristic $p$ that is a localization of a finitely generated $k$-algebra, local or graded. Then $R$ is *F-split* if the Frobenius map $R \to F_* R$, $r \mapsto F_* r^p$, has an $R$-linear left inverse. A variety $X$ is *F-split at* $x$ if $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is F-split, and *F-pure* if it is F-split at every point. For an ideal $I \subseteq S$, the *Frobenius power* $I^{[q]}$ is the ideal generated by the $q$-th powers of the elements of $I$.

An F-split projective variety in the sense of Definition P7.11.15 is F-split at every point. The converse fails: an elliptic curve is F-split at every point, being smooth, and is not F-split when it is supersingular (Proposition P7.11.19).

**Lemma 5.6.10 (the structure of $F_* S$).** Let $B_q = \{ \beta \in \mathbb{Z}^n : 0 \leq \beta_i \leq q - 1 \}$.

1. $F^e_* S$ is a free $S$-module with basis $\{ F^e_* x^\beta : \beta \in B_q \}$: every $g \in S$ is uniquely $g = \sum_{\beta \in B_q} g_\beta^q x^\beta$ with $g_\beta \in S$.
2. For an ideal $I$, $g \in I^{[q]}$ if and only if $g_\beta \in I$ for every $\beta \in B_q$. In particular $I^{[q]}$ is generated by the $q$-th powers of generators of $I$, and $g^q \in I^{[q]}$ if and only if $g \in I$.
3. Let $\Phi \colon F_* S \to S$ be the $S$-linear map with $\Phi(F_* x^\beta) = 1$ for $\beta = (p - 1, \dots, p - 1)$ and $\Phi(F_* x^\beta) = 0$ for the other $\beta \in B_p$. Every $S$-linear map $F_* S \to S$ is $F_* g \mapsto \Phi(F_*(h g))$ for a unique $h \in S$.
4. For $h \in S$ and an ideal $I$, $\Phi(F_*(h S)) \subseteq I$ if and only if $h \in I^{[p]}$.

**Theorem 5.6.11 (Fedder's criterion for hypersurfaces).** Let $f \in \mathfrak{m}$ be nonzero and $R = S_{\mathfrak{m}} / f S_{\mathfrak{m}}$. Then $R$ is F-split if and only if $f^{p-1} \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[p]}$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

The general form (Fed83, Theorem 1.12) says that $S_{\mathfrak{m}} / I$ is F-split if and only if $(I^{[p]} : I) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}^{[p]}$; for $I = (f)$, $(f^p : f) = (f^{p-1})$.

**Definition 5.6.12 (globally F-regular varieties).** A normal projective variety $X$ over $k$ is *globally F-regular* if for every effective Weil divisor $D$ there is $e \geq 1$ such that the composite $\mathcal{O}_X \to F^e_* \mathcal{O}_X \to F^e_* \mathcal{O}_X(D)$ splits as a map of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules.

Taking $D = 0$ shows that a globally F-regular variety is F-split.

**Theorem 5.6.13 (Smith, black box).** Let $X$ be a globally F-regular projective variety over $k$. Then (Smi00):

1. $X$ is F-split, and its local rings are strongly F-regular, in particular Cohen-Macaulay with rational singularities;
2. $H^i(X, L) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and every nef line bundle $L$;
3. $X$ satisfies Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing in the form $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and every nef and big line bundle $L$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Theorem 5.6.14 (Cascini, Tanaka and Witaszek, black box).** There is a prime $p_0$ such that every klt del Pezzo surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p > p_0$ is globally F-regular (CTW17). Here a klt del Pezzo surface is a normal projective surface $X$ with klt singularities and $-K_X$ ample.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Theorem 5.6.15 (Arvidsson, Bernasconi and Lacini, black box).** Let $X$ be a klt del Pezzo surface over a perfect field of characteristic $p > 5$, and $D$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor on $X$ such that $D - K_X$ is nef and big. Then $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ (ABL22). The bound $p > 5$ is optimal: there are counterexamples in characteristic $2$, $3$ and $5$.

### Proof in words

Frobenius makes $S$ into a free module over itself with the monomials of the box $B_q$ as basis, because $k$ is perfect. A map $F_* S \to S$ is determined by its values on the basis, and the trace map $\Phi$, which picks the coefficient of $(x_1 \cdots x_n)^{p-1}$, generates all of them as an $F_* S$-module. A splitting of $R = S/f$ lifts to a map $F_* S \to S$ that preserves $(f)$, which is $\Phi(F_*(h \cdot))$ with $h$ a multiple of $f^{p-1}$, and it is surjective at $\mathfrak{m}$ exactly when $f^{p-1}$ has a monomial outside $\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}$. So F-splitting of a hypersurface is decided by the monomials of one power of $f$.

### Examples

**Example 5.6.16 (the node).** Let $f = xy$ in $k[x, y]$. Then $f^{p-1} = x^{p-1} y^{p-1} \notin (x^p, y^p)$, so $xy = 0$ is F-split at the origin in every characteristic. More generally a normal crossing divisor $x_1 \cdots x_r = 0$ is F-split, as is every toric variety (Proposition P7.11.18).

**Example 5.6.17 (the cusp).** Let $f = y^2 - x^3$. Every monomial of $f^{p-1} = \sum_j \binom{p-1}{j} y^{2j} (-x^3)^{p-1-j}$ has $2j \leq p - 1$ and $3(p - 1 - j) \leq p - 1$ only if $\frac{2(p-1)}{3} \leq j \leq \frac{p-1}{2}$, which is impossible. So every monomial lies in $(x^p, y^p)$, and the cusp is not F-split at the origin in any characteristic. In characteristic $0$ the pair $(\mathbb{A}^2, C)$ is not log canonical, since $\operatorname{lct}_0(C) = \frac56 < 1$ (Example 5.4.31). F-purity of $S / f$ is the analogue of log canonicity of $(\mathbb{A}^n, \operatorname{div} f)$, and here neither holds.

**Example 5.6.18 (the cone over a plane cubic).** Let $f = x^3 + y^3 + z^3$ and $p \neq 3$. A monomial $x^a y^b z^c$ of $f^{p-1}$ has $a + b + c = 3(p - 1)$, so it lies outside $\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}$ only if $a = b = c = p - 1$. That monomial occurs only if $3 \mid p - 1$, with coefficient the multinomial coefficient $\frac{(p-1)!}{((p-1)/3)!^3}$, which is nonzero modulo $p$. So the cone $f = 0$ is F-split at the vertex if and only if $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$. The projective curve $E = \{ f = 0 \} \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ is the Fermat cubic, which is ordinary exactly for $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, and the cone is F-split exactly when $E$ is F-split, matching part 3 of Proposition P7.11.19. In characteristic $0$ the cone is log canonical and not klt (Volume 4, Chapter 2), and lies on the boundary of the class where vanishing holds.

**Example 5.6.19 (the quadric cone).** Let $f = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ and $p$ odd. The monomial $x^{p-1} y^{p-1} = (x^2)^{(p-1)/2} (y^2)^{(p-1)/2}$ occurs in $f^{p-1}$ with coefficient $\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2} \neq 0$ modulo $p$, and lies outside $\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}$. So the $A_1$ surface singularity is F-split in every odd characteristic, as a quotient singularity of order prime to $p$ should be.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 5.6.10.* Part 1. Since $k$ is perfect, every $c \in k$ is a $q$-th power, so every monomial $c x^\gamma$ is $(c^{1/q} x^{u})^q x^\beta$ with $\gamma = q u + \beta$, $\beta \in B_q$. This gives the existence of the decomposition, and uniqueness follows by comparing monomials, since the map $(u, \beta) \mapsto qu + \beta$ is injective.

Part 2. If $g = \sum_j a_j f_j^q$ with $f_j \in I$, then writing $a_j = \sum_\beta a_{j\beta}^q x^\beta$ gives $g_\beta = \sum_j a_{j \beta} f_j \in I$. The converse is clear from $g = \sum g_\beta^q x^\beta$. The same computation shows $I^{[q]} = (f_1^q, \dots, f_r^q)$ for generators $f_j$ of $I$, using $(a + b)^q = a^q + b^q$. For $g^q$ the only coefficient is $g_0 = g$.

Part 3. Let $\psi \colon F_* S \to S$ be $S$-linear and put $h = \sum_{\beta \in B_p} \psi(F_* x^{\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{1} - \beta})^p x^\beta$, where $\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{1} = (p - 1, \dots, p - 1)$. For $\gamma \in B_p$, $\Phi(F_*(h x^\gamma)) = \sum_\beta \psi(F_* x^{\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{1} - \beta}) \Phi(F_* x^{\beta + \gamma})$. The exponent $\beta + \gamma$ has coordinates in $[0, 2p - 2]$, and $\Phi(F_* x^{\beta + \gamma}) \neq 0$ only if every coordinate is $\equiv p - 1$ modulo $p$, that is $\beta = \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{1} - \gamma$, and then $\Phi(F_* x^{\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{1}}) = 1$. So $\Phi(F_*(h x^\gamma)) = \psi(F_* x^\gamma)$ on the basis, and the two maps agree. Uniqueness: if $\Phi(F_*(h g)) = 0$ for all $g$, then, writing $h = \sum h_\beta^p x^\beta$, the choice $g = x^{\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{1} - \beta}$ gives $h_\beta = 0$ by the same computation.

Part 4. If $h \in I^{[p]}$, write $h = \sum_j a_j f_j^p$ with $f_j \in I$; then $\Phi(F_*(h g)) = \sum_j f_j \Phi(F_*(a_j g)) \in I$. Conversely, with $h = \sum_\beta h_\beta^p x^\beta$, the computation of part 3 gives $\Phi(F_*(h x^{\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{1} - \beta})) = h_\beta$, so $h_\beta \in I$ for all $\beta$ and $h \in I^{[p]}$ by part 2. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 5.6.11.* All four parts of Lemma 5.6.10 localize at $\mathfrak{m}$, since $F_*$ commutes with localization ($F_*(S_\mathfrak{m}) = (F_* S)_\mathfrak{m}$, because $s^p$ is a unit at $\mathfrak{m}$ when $s$ is); write $S$ for $S_\mathfrak{m}$. First, $R$ is F-split if and only if there is an $R$-linear $\varphi \colon F_* R \to R$ with $\varphi(F_* R) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m} R$: a splitting has $\varphi(F_* 1) = 1$, and conversely, if $\varphi(F_* r) = u$ is a unit, then $\varphi' = u^{-1} \varphi(F_*(r \cdot))$ is a splitting. Let $\varphi$ be such a map. Composing with $F_* S \to F_* R$ gives an $S$-linear map $F_* S \to R$, which lifts to an $S$-linear $\psi \colon F_* S \to S$ because $F_* S$ is free. Then $\psi(F_*(f S)) \subseteq f S$, and by part 3 of Lemma 5.6.10, $\psi = \Phi(F_*(h \cdot))$. The condition $\Phi(F_*(h f S)) \subseteq f S$ means $h f \in (f)^{[p]} = (f^p)$ by part 4, that is $h \in (f^{p-1})$, since $S$ is a domain. Conversely every $h \in (f^{p-1})$ defines a map $\Phi(F_*(h \cdot))$ that preserves $(f)$ and so descends to $R$. The image of the descended map is not in $\mathfrak{m} R$ if and only if $\Phi(F_*(h S)) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$, since $f \in \mathfrak{m}$. So $R$ is F-split if and only if $\Phi(F_*(f^{p-1} s S)) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ for some $s$, that is $\Phi(F_*(f^{p-1} S)) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$, which by part 4 is $f^{p-1} \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[p]}$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Decide for which $p$ the curves $y^2 = x^3 + x^2$ and $x^3 + y^3 = 0$ are F-split at the origin, and compare with their log canonical thresholds in characteristic $0$.
2. (A) Show that $f = x_1^2 + \cdots + x_n^2$ is F-split at the origin for every odd $p$ and $n \geq 2$.
3. (B) Show that the $E_8$ surface singularity $x^2 + y^3 + z^5 = 0$ is F-split at the origin for $p > 5$, and not F-split for $p = 2$.
4. (B) Let $f$ be homogeneous of degree $d$ in $n$ variables with $d > n$. Show that $f^{p-1} \in \mathfrak{m}^{[p]}$ for every $p$, so the cone is never F-split, and compare with the fact that the cone over a smooth hypersurface of degree $d > n$ is not log canonical (Volume 4, Chapter 2).
5. (B) Show that the cone over a smooth plane cubic $E$ is F-split at the vertex if and only if $E$ is ordinary, using Theorem 5.6.11 and the Hasse invariant: $E$ is ordinary if and only if the coefficient of $(xyz)^{p-1}$ in $f^{p-1}$ is nonzero (Har77, Section IV.4).
6. (C) Use Theorem 5.6.13 and Theorem 5.6.14 to show that for $p > p_0$ every klt del Pezzo surface $X$ satisfies $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-K_X)^{\otimes m}) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and $m \geq 0$, and that $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = 0$.

### Notes and history

Frobenius splitting of rings goes back to Hochster and Roberts, and the criterion of Theorem 5.6.11 is Fedder's (Fed83), who also proved that F-pure Gorenstein singularities are related to rational and log canonical singularities in large characteristic. Global F-regularity was introduced by Smith (Smi00), who proved that the reductions of Fano varieties with mild singularities are globally F-regular for large $p$ in some cases, and Schwede and Smith later proved that varieties of globally F-regular type are of log Fano type. The theorem of Cascini, Tanaka and Witaszek (CTW17) gives the surface case uniformly in $p$, which is what the minimal model program in positive characteristic needs, and the theorem of Arvidsson, Bernasconi and Lacini (ABL22) gives the optimal bound for Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing on klt del Pezzo surfaces.
