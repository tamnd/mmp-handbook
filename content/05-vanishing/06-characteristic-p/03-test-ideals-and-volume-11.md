## Test ideals and a preview of Volume 11

### Statement

In characteristic $p$ there are no resolutions to compute multiplier ideals with, in general, and the vanishing theorem that makes them useful fails. The test ideal replaces the multiplier ideal: it is defined by Frobenius alone, it is computed by the same formula as the multiplier ideal for monomial ideals, and it agrees with the reduction of the multiplier ideal for $p \gg 0$. The F-pure threshold replaces the log canonical threshold, and for a fixed $p$ it can be strictly smaller. This section develops the definitions in the polynomial ring, following Blickle, Mustață and Smith (BMS08), proves the monomial formula, computes the cusp, and ends with a preview of the minimal model program in positive characteristic. The notation is that of the previous section: $k$ is perfect of characteristic $p$, $S = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and $q = p^e$.

**Definition 5.6.20 (Frobenius roots).** For an ideal $J \subseteq S$, the ideal $J^{[1/q]}$ is the smallest ideal $I$ of $S$ with $J \subseteq I^{[q]}$.

**Lemma 5.6.21 (computing Frobenius roots).** Let $J$ be an ideal of $S$ and write $g = \sum_{\beta \in B_q} g_\beta^q x^\beta$ as in Lemma 5.6.10.

1. $J^{[1/q]}$ exists, and it is the ideal generated by the $g_\beta$ for $g$ in a set of generators of $J$ and $\beta \in B_q$. If $J \subseteq J'$, then $J^{[1/q]} \subseteq J'^{[1/q]}$.
2. For a monomial ideal $J$, $J^{[1/q]}$ is the monomial ideal generated by the $x^{\lfloor w / q \rfloor}$ for $x^w \in J$, where $\lfloor w / q \rfloor$ is taken coordinatewise.
3. $(J^{[p]})^{[1/pq]} = J^{[1/q]}$.
4. $J^{[1/q]} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ if and only if $J \subseteq \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$.

**Definition 5.6.22 (test ideals).** Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq S$ be a nonzero ideal and $c \geq 0$ real. The *test ideal* of $\mathfrak{a}^c$ is
$$\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \bigcup_{e \geq 0} \big( \mathfrak{a}^{\lceil c p^e \rceil} \big)^{[1/p^e]}.$$
For $f \in S$ nonzero write $\tau(f^c) = \tau((f)^c)$.

**Proposition 5.6.23 (the union stabilizes).** The ideals $(\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil c q \rceil})^{[1/q]}$ form an ascending chain as $e$ grows, so $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c) = (\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil c q \rceil})^{[1/q]}$ for all $q = p^e$ large enough. If $c \leq c'$, then $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^{c'}) \subseteq \tau(\mathfrak{a}^c)$.

**Definition 5.6.24 (the F-pure threshold).** Let $f \in \mathfrak{m}$ be nonzero. For $q = p^e$ let $\nu_f(q) = \max \{ N \geq 0 : f^N \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[q]} \}$. The *F-pure threshold* of $f$ at the origin is $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) = \lim_{e \to \infty} \nu_f(p^e) / p^e$.

**Proposition 5.6.25 (the F-pure threshold).** Let $f \in \mathfrak{m}$ be nonzero.

1. $\nu_f(q) \leq q - 1$, and $p \, \nu_f(q) \leq \nu_f(pq) \leq p \, \nu_f(q) + p - 1$.
2. The limit defining $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f)$ exists, and $\frac{\nu_f(q)}{q} \leq \operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \leq \frac{\nu_f(q) + 1}{q}$ for every $q$. In particular $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \leq 1$.
3. $\tau(f^c) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ for $c < \operatorname{fpt}_0(f)$, and $\tau(f^c) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ for $c > \operatorname{fpt}_0(f)$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

Part 3 is the analogue of Proposition 5.4.21: the F-pure threshold is the first jumping number of the test ideals at the origin, as the log canonical threshold is the first jumping number of the multiplier ideals.

**Theorem 5.6.26 (monomial test ideals).** Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq S$ be a nonzero monomial ideal with Newton polyhedron $P(\mathfrak{a})$, and $c > 0$. Then, in every characteristic $p$,
$$\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \big( x^v : v \in \mathbb{Z}^n_{\geq 0}, \ v + \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int}(c P(\mathfrak{a})) \big),$$
the ideal given by Howald's formula for $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ in characteristic $0$ (Theorem P7.10.17).

**Theorem 5.6.27 (reduction modulo p, black box).** Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra, $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq A[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ an ideal, and for a closed point $s$ of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ with residue field $k(s)$ of characteristic $p(s)$ let $\mathfrak{a}_s \subseteq k(s)[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be the reduction.

1. For a fixed $c > 0$ there is a dense open $U \subseteq \operatorname{Spec} A$ such that $\tau(\mathfrak{a}_s^c) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^c)_s$, the reduction of the multiplier ideal, for every closed point $s \in U$ (Hara and Yoshida, HY03).
2. For $f \in A[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ vanishing at the origin, $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f_s) \leq \operatorname{lct}_0(f)$ for $s$ in a dense open set, and $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f_s) \to \operatorname{lct}_0(f)$ as $p(s) \to \infty$ (HY03 and MTW05).

Lean: not yet in Lean.

The open set in part 1 depends on $c$, and it cannot in general be chosen uniformly for all $c$; whether $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f_s) = \operatorname{lct}_0(f)$ for infinitely many $s$ is an open problem for general $f$.

### Proof in words

The Frobenius root of an ideal is computed by expanding its generators in the basis of Lemma 5.6.10 and taking coefficients, so for monomials it divides the exponents by $q$ and rounds down. The test ideal takes the root of $\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil}$: the exponent vectors of $\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil}$ fill out $cq P(\mathfrak{a})$, dividing by $q$ brings them to $c P(\mathfrak{a})$, and rounding down and adding $\mathbf{1}$ moves them strictly inside. That is Howald's formula, with the rounding playing the role of the discrepancies of a toric resolution. The F-pure threshold is read off from the highest power of $f$ that survives modulo $\mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$, and for the cusp this is a binomial coefficient computation, decided by Lucas's theorem.

### Examples

**Example 5.6.28 (a coordinate hyperplane).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x_1)$. Then $(x_1^{\lceil cq \rceil})^{[1/q]} = (x_1^{\lfloor \lceil cq \rceil / q \rfloor})$ by part 2 of Lemma 5.6.21, and $\lceil cq \rceil / q$ lies in $[c, c + \frac1q)$, so for $q$ large its floor is $\lfloor c \rfloor$. So $\tau(x_1^c) = (x_1^{\lfloor c \rfloor})$, as $\mathcal{J}(c H) = \mathcal{O}(-\lfloor c \rfloor H)$ in characteristic $0$, and $\operatorname{fpt}_0(x_1) = 1$.

**Example 5.6.29 (the cusp).** Let $f = x^2 + y^3$ in $k[x, y]$. A monomial of $f^N = \sum_j \binom{N}{j} x^{2j} y^{3(N - j)}$ lies outside $\mathfrak{m}^{[q]} = (x^q, y^q)$ only if $2j \leq q - 1$ and $3(N - j) \leq q - 1$, so
$$\nu_f(q) \leq \Big\lfloor \frac{q - 1}{2} \Big\rfloor + \Big\lfloor \frac{q - 1}{3} \Big\rfloor,$$
with equality when $q = p$, since then $N \leq p - 1$ and $\binom{N}{j} \neq 0$ modulo $p$.

1. If $p \equiv 1 \pmod 6$, then $q \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ for every $e$ and the bound is $N = \frac{5(q-1)}{6}$, attained with $j = \frac{q-1}{2}$. The base $p$ digits of $N$ and of $j$ are all $\frac{5(p-1)}{6}$ and $\frac{p-1}{2}$, so by Lucas's theorem $\binom{N}{j} \equiv \binom{5(p-1)/6}{(p-1)/2}^e \not\equiv 0$ modulo $p$. So $\nu_f(q) = \frac{5(q-1)}{6}$ and $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) = \frac56 = \operatorname{lct}_0(f)$.
2. If $p \equiv 5 \pmod 6$, write $p = 6k + 5$. Then $\nu_f(p) = (3k + 2) + (2k + 1) = 5k + 3$, and part 2 of Proposition 5.6.25 gives $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \leq \frac{5k + 4}{6k + 5} = \frac56 - \frac{1}{6p} < \frac56$. In fact equality holds (MTW05), so $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) = \frac45$ for $p = 5$.
3. For $p = 2$, $f \in (x^2, y^2) = \mathfrak{m}^{[2]}$, so $\nu_f(2) = 0$ and $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \leq \frac12$. For $p = 3$, $f \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[3]}$ and $f^2 = x^4 + 2x^2 y^3 + y^6 \in \mathfrak{m}^{[3]}$, so $\nu_f(3) = 1$ and $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \leq \frac23$. The values are $\frac12$ and $\frac23$ (MTW05; Exercise 3).

So the F-pure threshold of the cusp equals $\frac56$ for half of the primes and is strictly smaller for the others, and tends to $\frac56$, as part 2 of Theorem 5.6.27 predicts. The cusp is not F-split in any characteristic (Example 5.6.17), which is part 3 of Proposition 5.6.25 at $c = 1$.

**Example 5.6.30 (the monomial ideal of the cusp).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, y^3)$, with $P(\mathfrak{a}) = \{ (s, t) \in \mathbb{R}^2_{\geq 0} : \frac{s}{2} + \frac{t}{3} \geq 1 \}$. By Theorem 5.6.26, $x^i y^j \in \tau(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ if and only if $\frac{i+1}{2} + \frac{j+1}{3} > c$, so $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c) = S$ exactly for $c < \frac56$, in every characteristic. In characteristic $0$, $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \operatorname{div}(x^2 + y^3)) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ for $c < 1$ (Theorem 5.4.29). In characteristic $p \equiv 5 \pmod 6$ the test ideals of $f = x^2 + y^3$ and of $\mathfrak{a}$ differ for $c \in (\frac56 - \frac{1}{6p}, \frac56)$, by Example 5.6.29. A general element of a monomial ideal need not have the test ideals of the ideal.

**Remark 5.6.31 (a preview of Volume 11).** Volume 11 runs the minimal model program in positive and mixed characteristic, and its proofs avoid Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing in three ways.

1. Surfaces. For log surfaces over any field of characteristic $p$, the cone theorem, contraction theorem and abundance hold without vanishing, by a direct analysis of curves on surfaces (Tanaka, Tan14). This is the base of the induction.
2. Threefolds for $p > 5$. Hacon and Xu (HX15) construct flips of threefolds in characteristic $p > 5$, and Birkar (Bir16) builds minimal models from them. Where the characteristic $0$ proof uses vanishing to lift sections from a divisor, these proofs use the subspace $S^0 \subseteq H^0$ of sections that survive the trace of Frobenius, and F-adjunction, which replaces the restriction theorems of Chapter 4 by their test ideal analogues. The bound $p > 5$ enters through the surface results of Theorems 5.6.14 and 5.6.15 and the behaviour of klt surface singularities, which are strongly F-regular only for $p > 5$. Hacon and Witaszek extend parts of the program to $p = 5$.
3. Mixed characteristic. Bhatt, Ma, Patakfalvi, Schwede, Tucker, Waldron and Witaszek (BMP23) replace Frobenius by the absolute integral closure $R^+$ and prove the minimal model program for threefolds over excellent bases of mixed characteristic with residue characteristic $p > 5$, using Bhatt's vanishing theorem for $R^+$ as the substitute for Kawamata-Viehweg.

The test ideals of this section are the local invariants of all three approaches, as the multiplier ideals are in characteristic $0$.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 5.6.21.* Part 1. By part 2 of Lemma 5.6.10, $J \subseteq I^{[q]}$ if and only if $g_\beta \in I$ for every generator $g$ of $J$ and every $\beta$, since $I^{[q]}$ is an ideal and each $g \in J$ is an $S$-combination of the generators. So the ideal $I_0$ generated by these $g_\beta$ satisfies $J \subseteq I_0^{[q]}$ and is contained in every $I$ with $J \subseteq I^{[q]}$. If $J \subseteq J'$, then $J \subseteq J' \subseteq (J'^{[1/q]})^{[q]}$, so $J^{[1/q]} \subseteq J'^{[1/q]}$.

Part 2. For a monomial $x^w$, write $w = q \lfloor w / q \rfloor + \beta$ with $\beta \in B_q$; then $x^w = (x^{\lfloor w / q \rfloor})^q x^\beta$ is its expansion, with a single coefficient. Apply part 1 to the monomials of $J$.

Part 3. For an ideal $I$, $(I^{[q]})^{[p]} = I^{[pq]}$, and by part 2 of Lemma 5.6.10, $J^{[p]} \subseteq K^{[p]}$ if and only if $J \subseteq K$, since $J^{[p]}$ is generated by the $p$-th powers of generators of $J$. So $J^{[p]} \subseteq I^{[pq]}$ if and only if $J \subseteq I^{[q]}$, and the smallest such $I$ are the same.

Part 4. If $J^{[1/q]} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$, then $J \subseteq (J^{[1/q]})^{[q]} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$; conversely $J \subseteq \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$ gives $J^{[1/q]} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ by minimality. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 5.6.23.* Since $\lceil cpq \rceil \leq p \lceil cq \rceil$, $\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cpq \rceil} \supseteq \mathfrak{a}^{p \lceil cq \rceil} \supseteq (\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil})^{[p]}$. By parts 1 and 3 of Lemma 5.6.21,
$$(\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cpq \rceil})^{[1/pq]} \supseteq \big( (\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil})^{[p]} \big)^{[1/pq]} = (\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil})^{[1/q]}.$$
An ascending chain of ideals in the Noetherian ring $S$ stabilizes. If $c \leq c'$, then $\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil c'q \rceil} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil}$ for every $q$, and part 1 of Lemma 5.6.21 gives the inclusion of test ideals. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 5.6.25.* Part 1. Since $f \in \mathfrak{m}$, $f^q \in \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$, so $\nu_f(q) \leq q - 1$, and $\nu_f(q)$ is finite. If $f^N \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$, then $f^{pN} = (f^N)^p \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[pq]}$ by part 2 of Lemma 5.6.10, so $\nu_f(pq) \geq p \nu_f(q)$. If $f^{N+1} \in \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$ with $N = \nu_f(q)$, then $f^{p(N+1)} \in \mathfrak{m}^{[pq]}$, so $\nu_f(pq) \leq p(N + 1) - 1$.

Part 2. By part 1, $\nu_f(q)/q$ is nondecreasing and $(\nu_f(q) + 1)/q$ is nonincreasing in $e$, since $\frac{\nu_f(pq) + 1}{pq} \leq \frac{p(\nu_f(q) + 1)}{pq}$. The two sequences differ by $\frac1q \to 0$, so they have a common limit, which lies between them. The bound $\nu_f(q) \leq q - 1$ gives $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \leq 1$.

Part 3. By part 4 of Lemma 5.6.21, $(f^{\lceil cq \rceil})^{[1/q]} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ if and only if $f^{\lceil cq \rceil} \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$, that is $\lceil cq \rceil \leq \nu_f(q)$, since $f^N \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$ implies $f^{N'} \notin \mathfrak{m}^{[q]}$ for $N' \leq N$. If $c < \operatorname{fpt}_0(f)$, then $\nu_f(q) / q > c$ for $q$ large, so $\nu_f(q) \geq \lceil cq \rceil$ and $\tau(f^c) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$. If $c > \operatorname{fpt}_0(f)$, then $\lceil cq \rceil \geq cq > \operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \, q \geq \nu_f(q)$ for every $q$, so every ideal of the chain lies in $\mathfrak{m}$, and so does their union. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 5.6.26.* Write $P = P(\mathfrak{a})$, the convex hull of the exponents of the monomials of $\mathfrak{a}$, and let $e_1, \dots, e_r$ be the exponents of monomial generators of $\mathfrak{a}$, so $P = \operatorname{conv}(e_1, \dots, e_r) + \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0}$. Two facts about $P$ are used. First, $P + \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0} = P$, so for $t \geq s > 0$, $t P = s P + (t - s) P \subseteq s P + \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0} = sP$. Second, if $y \in cP$ and $\delta \in \mathbb{R}^n$ has all coordinates positive, then $y + \delta \in \operatorname{Int}(cP)$, since the open set $y + \mathbb{R}^n_{> 0}$ contains $y + \delta$ and lies in $cP$. Let $T$ be the right hand side of the theorem; by the second fact it is spanned by an upward closed set of monomials. By Proposition 5.6.23 and part 2 of Lemma 5.6.21, for $q$ large $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ is generated by the $x^{\lfloor w / q \rfloor}$ with $x^w \in \mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil}$, and every monomial of $\mathfrak{a}^N$ has its exponent in $N P$.

$\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c) \subseteq T$. Let $x^w \in \mathfrak{a}^{\lceil cq \rceil}$ and $u = \lfloor w / q \rfloor$. Then $w \in \lceil cq \rceil P \subseteq cq P$ by the first fact, so $w / q \in cP$. Each coordinate satisfies $u_i + 1 > w_i / q$, so $u + \mathbf{1} = w/q + \delta$ with $\delta$ positive, and $u + \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int}(cP)$ by the second fact. So $x^u \in T$.

$T \subseteq \tau(\mathfrak{a}^c)$. Let $v \in \mathbb{Z}^n_{\geq 0}$ with $v + \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int}(cP)$. Choose $\varepsilon > 0$ with $v + \mathbf{1} - \varepsilon \mathbf{1} \in cP$. Then there are $\lambda_j \geq 0$ with $\sum_j \lambda_j = 1$ and $c \sum_j \lambda_j e_j \leq v + \mathbf{1} - \varepsilon \mathbf{1}$ coordinatewise. For $q$ large let $N = \lceil cq \rceil$, $n_j = \lceil \lambda_j N \rceil$ and $w = \sum_j n_j e_j$. Then $\sum_j n_j \geq N$, so $x^w = \prod_j (x^{e_j})^{n_j} \in \mathfrak{a}^N$. Using $n_j \leq \lambda_j N + 1$ and $N \leq cq + 1$,
$$\frac{w}{q} \leq \frac{N}{q} \sum_j \lambda_j e_j + \frac1q \sum_j e_j \leq c \sum_j \lambda_j e_j + \frac2q \sum_j e_j \leq v + \mathbf{1} - \varepsilon \mathbf{1} + \frac2q \sum_j e_j,$$
using $\sum_j \lambda_j e_j \leq \sum_j e_j$. For $q$ large the right hand side is coordinatewise less than $v + \mathbf{1}$, so $w_i < q(v_i + 1)$ and $\lfloor w / q \rfloor \leq v$. So $x^v$ is a multiple of the generator $x^{\lfloor w / q \rfloor}$ of $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c)$, and $x^v \in \tau(\mathfrak{a}^c)$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $\tau(\mathfrak{m}^c)$ for $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ from Theorem 5.6.26, and show that $\tau(\mathfrak{m}^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}$ for every $c > 0$, with $\mathfrak{m}^j = S$ for $j \leq 0$, as in Example 5.4.26.
2. (A) Show that $\operatorname{fpt}_0(xy) = 1$ in every characteristic, by computing $\nu_{xy}(q)$.
3. (B) Show that for $f = x^2 + y^3$, $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) = \frac12$ in characteristic $2$ and $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) = \frac23$ in characteristic $3$, by computing $\nu_f(p^e)$ for all $e$.
4. (B) Let $f = x^3 + y^3 + z^3$ and $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$. Show that $\nu_f(q) = q - 1$ for all $q$, so $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) = 1 = \operatorname{lct}_0(f)$, and that for $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f) \leq 1 - \frac1p$.
5. (B) Show that $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \tau(\mathfrak{a}^{c + \varepsilon})$ for $\varepsilon > 0$ small, for a monomial ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, so the jumping numbers of monomial test ideals are discrete and rational. Compare with Proposition 5.4.20.
6. (C) Prove the analogue of Skoda's theorem for monomial test ideals: if $\mathfrak{a}$ is a monomial ideal generated by $r$ monomials and $c \geq r$, then $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{a} \, \tau(\mathfrak{a}^{c - 1})$. Compare with Theorem 5.4.12 and the general statement of BMS08.

### Notes and history

Test ideals come from the theory of tight closure of Hochster and Huneke. The test ideal of a pair $\mathfrak{a}^c$ is due to Hara and Yoshida (HY03), who proved Theorem 5.6.26 and the reduction theorem 5.6.27 part 1; the description of $\tau(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ in a regular ring by Frobenius roots, Definition 5.6.22, is that of Blickle, Mustață and Smith (BMS08), who proved that the F-jumping numbers are discrete and rational. The F-pure threshold was introduced by Takagi and Watanabe, and its relation with the log canonical threshold and Bernstein-Sato polynomials is studied by Mustață, Takagi and Watanabe (MTW05), from whom the values in Example 5.6.29 are taken. The question whether $\operatorname{fpt}_0(f_p) = \operatorname{lct}_0(f)$ for infinitely many $p$ is part of the conjectured correspondence between F-purity and log canonicity, and Volume 11 returns to it.
