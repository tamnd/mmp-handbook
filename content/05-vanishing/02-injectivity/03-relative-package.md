## The relative vanishing package

### Statement

The proofs of the cone, contraction, base point free and flip theorems in Volumes 7 to 9 use the vanishing theorems of Chapters 1 and 2 in a small number of fixed forms. This section states them together and adds two consequences: the lifting of sections from a Cartier divisor, and the global generation of higher direct images.

**Theorem 5.2.14 (the relative vanishing package).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a klt pair with $X$ normal and projective, $f \colon X \to Z$ a surjective morphism onto a projective variety, $d = \dim X - \dim Z$, and $N$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor on $X$.

1. If $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ is $f$-nef and $f$-big, then $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) = 0$ for $j > 0$.
2. If $N - (K_X + \Delta) \sim_\mathbb{Q} f^* A$ for a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $A$ on $Z$, then $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)$ is torsion free for every $j$ and zero for $j > d$.
3. If in part 2 the divisor $A$ is ample, then $H^i(Z, R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and all $j$.

Lean: not yet in Lean. Part 1 is a case of the statement `MinimalModel.KawamataViehweg`.

**Theorem 5.2.15 (lifting sections from a divisor).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective morphism from a normal quasi-projective variety to a quasi-projective variety, $(X, \Delta)$ a klt pair, $S \subseteq X$ a prime divisor that is Cartier, and $N$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor such that $N - S - (K_X + \Delta)$ is $f$-nef and $f$-big. Then the restriction map
$$f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \to f_* \big(\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S\big)$$
is surjective, and $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \cong R^j f_* (\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S)$ for $j > 0$. For $Z$ a point, $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) \to H^0(S, \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S)$ is surjective.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.2.16 (degeneration and global generation).** In the situation of Theorem 5.2.14, part 2, let $H$ be a very ample divisor on $Z$ and $n = \dim Z$.

1. If $A$ is ample, the Leray spectral sequence degenerates to isomorphisms $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) \cong H^0(Z, R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N))$ for every $i$, and $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) = 0$ for $i > d$.
2. If $A$ is nef, $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_Z(mH)$ is generated by global sections for every $j$ and every $m \geq n + 1$; if $A$ is ample, for every $m \geq n$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

### Proof in words

The package is Theorem 5.1.13 and Theorems 5.2.8 to 5.2.10, restated over a projective base. Lifting from a divisor is the long exact sequence of $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(N - S) \to \mathcal{O}_X(N) \to \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S \to 0$ and the vanishing of $R^1 f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N - S)$. When $A$ is ample, Kollár vanishing kills every term $E_2^{p,q}$ with $p > 0$ of the Leray spectral sequence. When $A$ is nef, Kollár vanishing applies to every twist of $N$ by a positive multiple of $f^* H$, which says that the higher direct images are $(n + 1)$-regular for $H$, and Mumford's theorem gives global generation.

### Examples

**Example 5.2.17 (a ruled surface).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, $f = p_2 \colon X \to Z = \mathbb{P}^1$, and $\mathcal{O}(a, b) = p_1^* \mathcal{O}(a) \otimes p_2^* \mathcal{O}(b)$, so $K_X = (-2, -2)$ and $d = 1$. The divisor $N = (a, b)$ satisfies $N - K_X \sim f^* A$ if and only if $a = -2$, with $A = \mathcal{O}(b + 2)$. Then $f_* \mathcal{O}(-2, b) = 0$ and $R^1 f_* \mathcal{O}(-2, b) = H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-2)) \otimes \mathcal{O}(b) \cong \mathcal{O}(b)$, which is torsion free, as part 2 of Theorem 5.2.14 says. For $b \geq -1$, $A$ is ample and $H^1(Z, \mathcal{O}(b)) = 0$, as part 3 says, and Corollary 5.2.16 gives $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}(-2, b)) \cong H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(b))$, of dimension $b + 1$, which is the Künneth formula. For $b = -2$, $A$ is trivial and $H^1(Z, R^1 f_* \mathcal{O}(-2, -2)) = H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-2)) \cong k$; it is the group $H^2(X, \omega_X) \cong k$, and the Leray spectral sequence does not collapse to its $p = 0$ column. For $A = \mathcal{O}$ nef, the sheaf $R^1 f_* \mathcal{O}(-2, -2) \otimes \mathcal{O}(m) = \mathcal{O}(m - 2)$ is generated by global sections exactly for $m \geq 2 = n + 1$, so the bound in part 2 of Corollary 5.2.16 is sharp.

**Example 5.2.18 (lifting on the quadric cone).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ with coordinates $x, y, z$ of weights $1, 1, 2$, and $D_1$, $D_4$ as in Example 5.1.18 with $n = 2$, so $K_X \sim -4 D_1$ and $D_4 \sim 2 D_1$ is Cartier. Let $S \in |2 D_1|$ be the curve $z = q(x, y)$ for a general quadratic form $q$, a Cartier prime divisor not through the vertex, isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ by the coordinates $x, y$. For $N = a D_1$, $N - S - K_X \sim (a + 2) D_1$ is ample if and only if $a \geq -1$, so Theorem 5.2.15 with $\Delta = 0$ and $Z$ a point gives surjectivity of
$$H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1)) \to H^0(S, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S) = H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(a)) \quad \text{for } a \geq -1.$$
Directly, $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1))$ is spanned by the monomials of weighted degree $a$, and restricting $z^c x^i y^j$ gives $q^c x^i y^j$, a form of degree $a$; already the monomials with $c = 0$ give all forms of degree $a$. The map has kernel spanned by multiples of $z - q$, of dimension $h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X((a - 2) D_1))$. The same computation on $\mathbb{P}^2$ with $S$ a line gives the surjectivity of $H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(a)) \to H^0(S, \mathcal{O}_S(a))$ for $a \geq -1$, from $N - S - K = (a + 2) \ell$.

### Proof

*Theorem 5.2.14.* Part 1 is Theorem 5.1.13. Part 2 is Theorems 5.2.8 and 5.2.9, and part 3 is Theorem 5.2.10.

*Theorem 5.2.15.* Since $S$ is Cartier, $\mathcal{O}_X(N - S) = \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(-S)$, and tensoring $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(-S) \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_S \to 0$ with $\mathcal{O}_X(N)$ gives an exact sequence
$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(N - S) \to \mathcal{O}_X(N) \to \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S \to 0,$$
exact on the left because $\mathcal{O}_X(-S)$ is locally free and $\mathcal{O}_X(N)$ is torsion free. The divisor $N - S$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor with $N - S - (K_X + \Delta)$ $f$-nef and $f$-big, so $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N - S) = 0$ for $j > 0$ by Theorem 5.1.13. The long exact sequence of higher direct images then gives the surjectivity of $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \to f_*(\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S)$ and the isomorphisms for $j > 0$. For $Z$ a point, $f_*$ is $H^0$.

*Corollary 5.2.16.* Part 1: by Theorem 5.2.14, part 3, the Leray spectral sequence $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Z, R^q f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N))$ of Theorem P2.4.26 has $E_2^{p,q} = 0$ for $p > 0$, so it degenerates and the edge maps $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) \to H^0(Z, R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N))$ are isomorphisms. For $i > d$ the target is zero by part 2.

Part 2: let $\iota \colon Z \to \mathbb{P}^r$ be the embedding given by $H$, and $\mathcal{F} = \iota_* R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)$, a coherent sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^r$ with $H^p(\mathbb{P}^r, \mathcal{F}(l)) = H^p(Z, R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_Z(lH))$ for every $l$, since $\iota$ is a closed immersion. By the projection formula, Proposition P4.4.10, $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_Z(lH) = R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N + l f^* H)$, and $N + l f^* H - (K_X + \Delta) \sim_\mathbb{Q} f^*(A + lH)$. If $A$ is nef and $l \geq 1$, then $A + lH$ is ample, since a nef class plus an ample class is ample (Laz04, Corollary 1.4.10), and Theorem 5.2.10 gives $H^p(Z, R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N + l f^* H)) = 0$ for $p > 0$. For $m \geq n + 1$ and $1 \leq p \leq n$, $l = m - p \geq 1$, so $H^p(\mathbb{P}^r, \mathcal{F}(m - p)) = 0$; for $p > n$ the group vanishes because $\mathcal{F}$ is supported on $Z$, of dimension $n$ (Har77, Theorem III.2.7). So $\mathcal{F}$ is $m$-regular in the sense of Definition P4.2.15, and $\mathcal{F}(m)$ is generated by global sections by Theorem P4.2.16, part 3. Since $\iota$ is a closed immersion, the same holds for $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_Z(mH)$. If $A$ is ample the same argument works with $l = m - p \geq 0$, so for $m \geq n$.

### Exercises

1. (A) In Example 5.2.17, compute $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}(-2, b))$ for every $i$ and $b$ by the Künneth formula and compare with the Leray spectral sequence for $f$, including the case $b \leq -2$ where part 3 of Theorem 5.2.14 does not apply.
2. (A) Use Theorem 5.2.15 to show that for a smooth plane curve $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ of degree $e$, the restriction $H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(a)) \to H^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C(a))$ is surjective for $a \geq e - 2$, and show from the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}(a - e) \to \mathcal{O}(a) \to \mathcal{O}_C(a) \to 0$ and Theorem P4.2.1 that it is in fact surjective for every $a$.
3. (B) Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective birational morphism onto a normal variety, $(X, \Delta)$ klt, and $S$ a Cartier prime divisor on $X$ with $-S - (K_X + \Delta)$ $f$-nef. Using Corollary 5.1.15 in place of Theorem 5.1.13, show that $\mathcal{O}_Z = f_* \mathcal{O}_X \to f_* \mathcal{O}_S$ is surjective, deduce that $f_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_{f(S)}$, and conclude that $f|_S \colon S \to f(S)$ has connected fibres.
4. (B) In Example 5.2.18, use the exact sequence in the proof of Theorem 5.2.15 and Example 5.1.18 to show that $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1)) \cong H^1(S, \mathcal{O}_S(a))$ for $a \geq -1$. Find the largest $a$ with $H^1(S, \mathcal{O}_S(a)) \neq 0$ and compute $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(a D_1))$ for that $a$.
5. (B) Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a surjective morphism of smooth projective varieties with $\dim Z = n$, and $H$ very ample on $Z$. Show that $f_* \omega_X \otimes \mathcal{O}_Z((n + 1) H)$ is generated by global sections, and that the bound $n + 1$ cannot be lowered in general by taking $f$ the identity of $\mathbb{P}^n$.
6. (C) Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a klt pair, $f \colon X \to Z$ a projective morphism of quasi-projective varieties, and $S_1, \dots, S_r$ Cartier prime divisors whose sum $S$ is reduced, with $N - S - (K_X + \Delta)$ $f$-nef and $f$-big. Show that $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \to f_*(\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S)$ is surjective. On $X = E \times \mathbb{P}^1$ as in Example 5.2.5, with $S$ the sum of two fibres of $p_2$, $N = K_X + S$ and $Z$ a point, show that $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N)) \cong k$ and $H^0(S, \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S) \cong k^2$, so bigness cannot be dropped.

### Lean

Part 1 of Theorem 5.2.14 is a case of `MinimalModel.KawamataViehweg`, stated for a projective morphism to a scheme of finite type, and part 2 for $X$ smooth, $\Delta$ an snc divisor with $\lfloor \Delta \rfloor = 0$ and $A = 0$ is the torsion freeness half of `MinimalModel.KollarTorsionFree`. Theorem 5.2.15 and Corollary 5.2.16 are not in the library.

### Notes and history

The lifting statement of Theorem 5.2.15 is the first step of the proof of the base point free theorem: sections of $N$ on a divisor $S$ lift because $N - S - (K_X + \Delta)$ is nef and big (KM98, Theorem 3.3). In the proofs of the existence of flips and the extension theorems later in this volume, $S$ is the reduced part of a plt boundary rather than a Cartier divisor, and the sequence above is replaced by the adjunction sequence of Volume 4. Corollary 5.2.16 is Kollár's form of global generation (Kol86a, Section 2) and is the reason why the higher direct images of adjoint sheaves behave like vector bundles of nonnegative degree on curves. Fujino (Fuj11) states the whole package for quasi-log schemes.
