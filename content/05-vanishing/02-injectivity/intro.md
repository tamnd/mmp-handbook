# Kollár's injectivity and torsion freeness

Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing needs a nef and big divisor. Many arguments of the minimal model program meet adjoint divisors that are only semiample, for instance the pullback of an ample divisor along a fibration, and for these the cohomology does not vanish. Kollár's theorems say what survives: multiplication by a section of a multiple of the semiample divisor is injective on cohomology, the higher direct images of adjoint sheaves along a fibration have no torsion and vanish above the relative dimension, and their cohomology against an ample divisor on the base vanishes. Volume P7 states these theorems for a smooth projective variety and the dualizing sheaf, Theorems P7.9.13 and P7.9.14, as black boxes. This chapter proves them for klt pairs and $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisors from one input, the injectivity theorem of Esnault and Viehweg for snc divisors, which comes from Hodge theory and is cited.

Section 1 proves the injectivity theorem for snc pairs on a smooth projective variety and then, through the resolution of a Weil divisor in Corollary 5.1.16, for klt pairs. It recovers Theorem P7.9.14. The examples show injectivity without vanishing on a product with an elliptic curve, the failure without the proportionality of the divisor to the semiample class, and the failure for a log canonical pair. Section 2 proves torsion freeness, by the argument that a torsion section would be killed by a section pulled back from the base, against injectivity; then the vanishing of $R^j f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N)$ above the relative dimension, and Kollár vanishing, by induction on the dimension of the base with a general hyperplane section. Section 3 collects the relative Kawamata-Viehweg-Kollár package in the form that Volumes 7 to 9 use, and adds two consequences: the surjectivity of restriction to a divisor, and the global generation of higher direct images after a twist.
