## Problems

Each problem computes a multiplier ideal, a threshold or a vanishing statement of the chapter on an explicit pair, ideal or set of points.

1. (A) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, y^5) \subseteq k[x, y]$. Use Theorem 5.3.15 with $d = 0$ to show that $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2; \mathfrak{a}^c) = (x^i y^j : \frac{i + 1}{2} + \frac{j + 1}{5} > c)$, and show that the values of $c$ in $(0, 1]$ at which the ideal changes are exactly $\frac{7}{10}$ and $\frac{9}{10}$.
2. (A) Let $C = \{x^2 = y^3\} \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2$ and $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, y^3)$. Show that the general element $x^2 + \lambda y^3$ of the span of $x^2$ and $y^3$ defines a curve isomorphic to $C$ by an automorphism of $\mathbb{A}^2$ fixing the origin, and use Lemma 5.3.4 with $m = 1$ and Example P7.10.19 to show that $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2, cC) = \mathcal{O}$ for $c < \frac56$ and $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2, cC) = (x, y)$ for $\frac56 \leq c < 1$. Compare with Example P7.10.7.
3. (A) Let $\Delta = \frac12 H_1 + \frac12 H_2$ on $\mathbb{A}^2$ and $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$. Use Theorem 5.3.15 to show that $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2, \Delta; \mathfrak{m}^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor}$, and check the answer on the blow-up of the origin, where the coefficient of the exceptional curve in $\Delta_Y + cF$ is $c$.
4. (A) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n)$ and $d \in \mathbb{Q}^n_{\geq 0}$. Show that $P(\mathfrak{a}) = \mathbf{1} + \sigma$ and that Theorem 5.3.15 gives $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^n, \Delta_d; \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{O}\big(-\sum_j \lfloor d_j + c \rfloor H_j\big)$. Derive the same answer from part 4 of Proposition 5.3.3 and part 2 of Proposition P7.10.4.
5. (B) Let $d \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ with $0 \leq d_j < 1$ and $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$. Use Corollary 5.3.16 to show that $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n, \Delta_d; \mathfrak{m}) = n - (d_1 + \dots + d_n)$, and check the case $n = 3$, $d = (t, t, t)$ against Example 5.3.19.
6. (B) Let $\mathfrak{a}_1 = (x)$ and $\mathfrak{a}_2 = (x, y)$ in $k[x, y]$. Show that the fan with rays $e_1$, $(1, 1)$ and $e_2$ makes both support functions linear, and that $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2; \mathfrak{a}_1^{c_1} \mathfrak{a}_2^{c_2}) = \mathcal{O}$ if and only if $c_1 < 1$ and $c_1 + c_2 < 2$. Check the answer on the blow-up of the origin, and compare the shape of this region with the region of Example 5.3.18.
7. (B) Let $\Sigma \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be a nonempty finite set of reduced points and $e \geq 1$ an integer such that $\mathcal{I}_\Sigma(e)$ is generated by global sections. Use Example P7.10.8 to show that $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{P}^2; \mathcal{I}_\Sigma^2) = \mathcal{I}_\Sigma$, then Lemma 5.3.4 and Corollary 5.3.9 to show that $H^i(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{I}_\Sigma(k)) = 0$ for $i \geq 1$ and $k \geq 2e - 2$. Check that four general points with $e = 2$ give the bound $k \geq 2$ of Example 5.3.11.
8. (C) Show that the set $\{ \operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^2; (x^a, y^b)) : a, b \geq 1 \}$ equals $\{ \frac1a + \frac1b : a, b \geq 1 \}$, that it contains no strictly increasing infinite sequence, and that its accumulation points are $0$ and the numbers $\frac1a$ for $a \geq 1$.
9. (C) Let $\mathfrak{a}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{a}_r$ be nonzero monomial ideals in $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ and $d \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ with $0 \leq d_j < 1$. Use the proof of Theorem 5.3.15 to show that the set of $c \in \mathbb{Q}^r_{\geq 0}$ with $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^n, \Delta_d; \mathfrak{a}_1^{c_1} \cdots \mathfrak{a}_r^{c_r}) = \mathcal{O}$ is cut out by finitely many strict linear inequalities $\sum_i c_i \varphi_i(u_\rho) < \langle \mathbf{1} - d, u_\rho \rangle$, so it is convex, and that for every $c$ there is $\varepsilon > 0$ with the same mixed ideal at every $c'$ satisfying $c_i \leq c'_i < c_i + \varepsilon$ for all $i$.
