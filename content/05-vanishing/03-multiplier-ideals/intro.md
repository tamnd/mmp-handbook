# Multiplier ideals of pairs

A klt pair has no higher direct images on a log resolution and satisfies Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing. A pair that is not klt fails both, and the multiplier ideal measures the failure: it is the push-forward of the round-down of the crepant pullback, an ideal sheaf that is the whole structure sheaf exactly where the pair is klt. Volume P7, Chapter 10, defines multiplier ideals of divisors and ideals on a smooth variety, proves local vanishing and Nadel vanishing on a smooth projective variety, and computes the multiplier ideals of monomial ideals by Howald's theorem. This chapter makes the three extensions that the minimal model program uses: the ambient variety is normal and carries a boundary $\Delta$ with $K_X + \Delta$ only $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, the vanishing is relative to a projective morphism, and several ideals appear at once with independent exponents.

Section 1 defines $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)$ on a normal variety, proves that it is an ideal independent of the log resolution, lists its first properties, replaces an ideal by general members of a linear system that generates it, and proves local vanishing for pairs from Corollary 5.1.15. The examples are the complete quadrangle of Chapter 1, whose multiplier ideal is the ideal of the four triple points between the thresholds $\frac23$ and $1$, and a line through the vertex of the quadric cone, a Weil divisor that is not Cartier. Section 2 proves Nadel vanishing for pairs over a base and recovers Theorem P7.10.10; the examples show that four general points impose independent conditions on conics and not on lines, recover the vanishing on $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ for Weil divisors of odd degree, and give the classical bound for fat points in $\mathbb{P}^n$. Section 3 defines mixed multiplier ideals and computes them for monomial ideals on affine space with a boundary supported on the coordinate hyperplanes, by the toric argument of Theorem P7.10.17; the examples compute thresholds with a boundary and the klt region of a product of two monomial ideals.
