## Nadel vanishing for pairs

### Statement

Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective morphism from a normal quasi-projective variety to a quasi-projective variety, $(X, \Delta)$ a pair, $\mathfrak{a}$ a nonzero ideal and $c \geq 0$ rational. A coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ is *$f$-generated* if $f^* f_* \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}$ is surjective. Relative positivity is as in Chapter 1, Section 2.

**Theorem 5.3.8 (Nadel vanishing over a base).** Let $N$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$.

1. If $N - (K_X + \Delta)$ is $f$-nef and $f$-big, then $R^j f_* \big(\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta)\big) = 0$ for $j > 0$.
2. Let $M$ be a Cartier divisor with $\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(M)$ $f$-generated. If $N - (K_X + \Delta) - cM$ is $f$-nef and $f$-big, then $R^j f_* \big(\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)\big) = 0$ for $j > 0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.3.9 (the absolute case).** Let $X$ be projective, $N$ a Cartier divisor and $M$ a Cartier divisor with $\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(M)$ generated by global sections. If $N - (K_X + \Delta) - cM$ is nef and big, then $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)) = 0$ for $i > 0$. For $X$ smooth, $N = K_X + L$ and either $\mathfrak{a} = \mathcal{O}_X$, $c = 0$, $\Delta = D$, or $\Delta = 0$, this is Theorem P7.10.10.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.3.10 (sections through the non-klt locus).** In the situation of Theorem 5.3.8, part 1, the restriction map
$$f_* \mathcal{O}_X(N) \to f_* \big(\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X / \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta)\big)$$
is surjective. In particular, if $X$ is projective, $Z$ a point and the non-klt locus of $(X, \Delta)$ is a finite set of reduced points, then $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N))$ separates them.

### Proof in words

On a projective log resolution, $\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{J}$ is the push-forward of the Cartier divisor $\mu^* N - \lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor$, whose higher direct images to $X$ vanish by local vanishing, Theorem 5.3.5. So the Leray spectral sequence identifies the higher direct images to $Z$ from $X$ and from $Y$. On $Y$, the fractional part of $\Delta_Y + cF$ is a klt boundary, and the divisor minus $K_Y$ minus this boundary is the pullback of $N - (K_X + \Delta) - cM$ plus $c$ times the $\mu$-generated class $\mu^* M - F$. This is nef and big over $Z$, and Theorem 5.1.13 on $Y$ gives the vanishing. Surjectivity onto the non-klt locus is the long exact sequence of $0 \to \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X / \mathcal{J} \to 0$ twisted by $N$.

### Examples

**Example 5.3.11 (four points and conics).** In Example 5.3.6, for $\frac23 \leq c < 1$, $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{P}^2, cA) = \mathcal{I}_\Sigma$ for the four points $\Sigma$, no three collinear. For $N = d \ell$, $N - (K_{\mathbb{P}^2} + cA) = (d + 3 - 6c) \ell$ is ample if and only if $d > 6c - 3$. At $c = \frac23$, Corollary 5.3.9 gives $H^i(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{I}_\Sigma(d)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and $d \geq 2$, and Corollary 5.3.10 says that $H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(2)) \to H^0(\mathcal{O}_\Sigma) = k^4$ is onto: four points in general position impose independent conditions on conics. Directly, the conics through $p_1, \dots, p_4$ form the pencil spanned by $L_{12} + L_{34}$ and $L_{13} + L_{24}$, so $h^0(\mathcal{I}_\Sigma(2)) = 2 = 6 - 4$. The bound is sharp: for $d = 1$ no line contains $\Sigma$, so $H^0(\mathcal{O}(1)) = k^3 \to k^4$ is not onto and $h^1(\mathcal{I}_\Sigma(1)) = 1$; and $d = 1$ fails $d > 6c - 3$ for every $c \geq \frac23$.

**Example 5.3.12 (Weil divisors on $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ with $D_1$ and $D_4$ as in Example 5.1.18, $n = 2$: $K_X \sim -4 D_1$, $a D_1$ is Cartier if and only if $a$ is even, and $X$ is locally the quadric cone at its vertex, the image of $[0 : 0 : 1]$. The divisor $D_1$ is a line through the vertex, smooth away from it. By Example 5.3.7 near the vertex and part 2 of Proposition P7.10.4 elsewhere, $\mathcal{J}(X, cD_1) = \mathcal{O}_X(-D_1)$ for $1 \leq c < 2$. Let $N = 2b D_1$, Cartier. Then $N - (K_X + cD_1) \sim (2b + 4 - c) D_1$ is ample for $b \geq -1$ and $c < 2$, and Corollary 5.3.9 gives, with $\mathcal{O}_X(2b D_1) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(-D_1) = \mathcal{O}_X((2b - 1) D_1)$,
$$H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X((2b - 1) D_1)) = 0 \quad \text{for } i > 0, \ b \geq -1.$$
This is the vanishing of Example 5.1.18 for the odd multiples $a = 2b - 1 \geq -3$ of $D_1$, Weil divisors that are not Cartier, obtained here from Cartier divisors by twisting with a multiplier ideal.

**Example 5.3.13 (fat points).** Let $p \in \mathbb{P}^n$, $n \geq 2$, $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}_p$ and $m \geq 1$. The ideal $\mathfrak{m}_p \otimes \mathcal{O}(1)$ is generated by global sections, the linear forms vanishing at $p$, so $M = H$, a hyperplane, satisfies the hypothesis of Corollary 5.3.9. By Example P7.10.8, $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{P}^n, 0; \mathfrak{m}_p^c) = \mathfrak{m}_p^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}$; take $c = m + n - 1$, so that the multiplier ideal is $\mathfrak{m}_p^m$. For $N = dH$, $N - K - cM = (d + n + 1 - m - n + 1) H = (d - m + 2) H$ is ample if and only if $d \geq m - 1$. So
$$H^i(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathfrak{m}_p^m(d)) = 0 \quad \text{for } i > 0 \text{ and } d \geq m - 1,$$
and Corollary 5.3.10 in the form of the exact sequence says that a point of multiplicity $m$ imposes $\binom{m - 1 + n}{n}$ independent conditions on forms of degree $d \geq m - 1$. The bound is sharp: forms of degree $m - 2$ number $\binom{m - 2 + n}{n} < \binom{m - 1 + n}{n}$, so they cannot satisfy that many independent conditions.

### Proof

*Theorem 5.3.8.* Part 1 is part 2 with $\mathfrak{a} = \mathcal{O}_X$, $M = 0$ and $c = 0$; it suffices to prove part 2. Let $\mu \colon Y \to X$ be a projective log resolution of $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$, with $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F)$, and put
$$G = -\lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor, \qquad N_Y = \mu^* N + G, \qquad \Theta = G + \Delta_Y + cF.$$
By Theorem 5.3.5 and the projection formula, Proposition P4.4.10, $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)$ and $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = 0$ for $j > 0$. The Leray spectral sequence for $f \circ \mu$, Theorem P2.4.26, then gives $R^j f_*(\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)) = R^j (f \circ \mu)_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y)$. The pair $(Y, \Theta)$ is klt, with snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$, and
$$N_Y - (K_Y + \Theta) = \mu^* N - K_Y - \Delta_Y - cF = \mu^*\big(N - (K_X + \Delta) - cM\big) + c(\mu^* M - F).$$
The first term is the pullback of an $f$-nef and $f$-big divisor under a birational morphism, so it is $(f \circ \mu)$-nef and big on the generic fibre of $f \circ \mu$, hence $(f \circ \mu)$-big by the comparison of the two notions of relative bigness at the start of Chapter 1, Section 2. For the second term, the surjection $f^* f_*(\mathfrak{a}(M)) \to \mathfrak{a}(M)$ pulls back to a surjection $(f \circ \mu)^* f_*(\mathfrak{a}(M)) \to \mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_Y(\mu^* M) = \mathcal{O}_Y(\mu^* M - F)$, so $\mathcal{O}_Y(\mu^* M - F)$ is $(f \circ \mu)$-generated and $\mu^* M - F$ is $(f \circ \mu)$-nef. The sum of an $(f \circ \mu)$-nef and $(f \circ \mu)$-big divisor and an $(f \circ \mu)$-nef divisor is $(f \circ \mu)$-nef and $(f \circ \mu)$-big, since by Definition 1.3.14 the big term is an $(f \circ \mu)$-ample class plus an effective class, and an ample class plus a nef class is ample, the ample cone being the interior of the nef cone (Section 1.3). The morphism $f \circ \mu$ is projective, and Theorem 5.1.13 for the klt pair $(Y, \Theta)$ and the Cartier divisor $N_Y$ gives $R^j (f \circ \mu)_* \mathcal{O}_Y(N_Y) = 0$ for $j > 0$.

*Corollary 5.3.9.* Take $Z$ a point, so that $f$-generated means generated by global sections and $R^j f_*$ is $H^j$. For $X$ smooth and $N = K_X + L$, the hypotheses become those of Theorem P7.10.10, parts 1 and 2, by Proposition 5.3.2.

*Corollary 5.3.10.* The sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{O}_X(N) \to \mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X / \mathcal{J} \to 0$ is exact, since $\mathcal{O}_X(N)$ is locally free, and $R^1 f_*$ of the left term vanishes by Theorem 5.3.8. If the non-klt locus is a reduced finite set $\Sigma$ and $Z$ is a point, $\mathcal{O}_X(N) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X / \mathcal{J} \cong \mathcal{O}_\Sigma$, and surjectivity onto $H^0(\mathcal{O}_\Sigma) = k^\Sigma$ means that for each $p \in \Sigma$ some section vanishes at the other points of $\Sigma$ and not at $p$.

### Exercises

1. (A) In Example 5.3.11, take $\frac23 < c < \frac56$ and find the $d$ for which Corollary 5.3.9 applies. Check that the conclusion for $d = 2$ survives, and compute $h^0(\mathcal{I}_\Sigma(d))$ for every $d \geq 2$.
2. (A) Deduce Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for a klt pair, Corollary 5.1.14 with $N$ Cartier, from Corollary 5.3.9.
3. (B) Let $\Sigma \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be $r$ general points and $A$ the sum of the $\binom{r}{2}$ lines through pairs of them. Find the threshold $c_0$ at which $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{P}^2, cA)$ first becomes $\mathcal{I}_\Sigma$, and the degrees $d$ for which Corollary 5.3.10 shows that $\Sigma$ imposes independent conditions on curves of degree $d$. Compare with the true bound for $r = 5$ and $r = 6$.
4. (B) In Example 5.3.12, take $\Delta = c D_1$ with $0 \leq c < 1$ and $N = 2b D_1$, and show that Corollary 5.3.9 gives only Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for even multiples. Explain why odd multiples need $c \geq 1$.
5. (B) Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety, $L$ a Cartier divisor, $p \in X$ and $D$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with $L - D$ ample, $\mathcal{J}(D) = \mathfrak{m}_p$ near $p$ and $\mathcal{J}(D) = \mathcal{O}_X$ away from $p$. Use Corollary 5.3.10 to show that $K_X + L$ has a section that does not vanish at $p$. Check the hypotheses for $X = \mathbb{P}^2$, $L = 3\ell$ and $D = \frac23 (\ell_1 + \ell_2 + \ell_3)$ with $\ell_i$ three general lines through $p$.
6. (C) Prove part 2 of Theorem 5.3.8 from part 1 and Lemma 5.3.4, by replacing $\mathfrak{a}^c$ with $\frac{c}{m} \sum D_i$ locally over $Z$ and checking that $N - (K_X + \Delta + \frac{c}{m} \sum D_i)$ is $f$-nef and $f$-big when $N - (K_X + \Delta) - cM$ is.

### Lean

The library has no multiplier ideals, so Nadel vanishing is not stated. `MinimalModel.KawamataViehweg` is the statement of Theorem 5.3.8, part 1, in the klt case, where the multiplier ideal is $\mathcal{O}_X$.

### Notes and history

Nadel proved the vanishing for multiplier ideal sheaves of plurisubharmonic weights on compact Kähler manifolds (Nad90), and the algebraic form for divisors and ideals on smooth projective varieties is Lazarsfeld, Theorem 9.4.8 and Corollary 9.4.15 (Laz04). The relative form for pairs on singular varieties is Laz04, Theorem 9.4.17 for smooth $X$ over a base, and Kollár's survey (Kol97, Section 2) for the singular case; the proof above is the reduction to Theorem 5.1.13 on a log resolution. Corollary 5.3.10 is the form in which multiplier ideals produce sections in the proof of the base point free theorem and in effective global generation: a divisor with an isolated non-klt point at $p$ gives a section of the adjoint bundle that does not vanish at $p$.
