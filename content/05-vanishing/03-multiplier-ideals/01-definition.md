## Multiplier ideals on normal varieties

### Statement

Throughout the chapter $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$, $X$ is a normal quasi-projective variety and $(X, \Delta)$ is a pair: $\Delta$ is an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $K_X + \Delta$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier. Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ be a nonzero ideal sheaf and $c \geq 0$ rational. A *log resolution* of $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$ is a proper birational morphism $\mu \colon Y \to X$ from a smooth variety such that $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ is a divisor, $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F)$ for an effective divisor $F$, and $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu) + \mu^{-1}_* \Delta + F$ has snc support. It exists and can be chosen projective, by Hironaka (Hir64; Theorem 4.2.10 applied after principalizing $\mathfrak{a}$, as in Definition P7.10.1). Write $K_Y + \Delta_Y = \mu^*(K_X + \Delta)$ with $\mu_* \Delta_Y = \Delta$, as in Definition 4.2.9; $\Delta_Y$ may have negative coefficients.

**Definition 5.3.1 (multiplier ideals of pairs).** With a log resolution $\mu$ of $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$, put
$$\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y\big(-\lfloor \Delta_Y + c F \rfloor\big).$$
For $\mathfrak{a} = \mathcal{O}_X$ write $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta)$. The triple $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)$ is *klt* if every coefficient of $\Delta_Y + cF$ is less than $1$. The closed subscheme defined by $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)$ is the *non-klt locus*.

**Proposition 5.3.2 (well defined).** $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)$ is an ideal sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$, and it does not depend on the log resolution. If $X$ is smooth, $\mathcal{J}(X, D) = \mathcal{J}(D)$ and $\mathcal{J}(X, 0; \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ are the multiplier ideals of Definition P7.10.2.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Proposition 5.3.3 (first properties).** Let $(X, \Delta)$ and $(X, \Delta')$ be pairs and $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathfrak{b}$ nonzero ideals.

1. $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{O}_X$ if and only if $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)$ is klt. In particular $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta) = \mathcal{O}_X$ if and only if $(X, \Delta)$ is klt, and this does not depend on the log resolution.
2. If $\Delta \leq \Delta'$, $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathfrak{b}$ and $c' \geq c$, then $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta'; \mathfrak{a}^{c'}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{b}^c)$.
3. If $B$ is an effective Cartier divisor, $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta + B; \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(-B)$.
4. If $\mathfrak{a} = \mathcal{O}_X(-B)$ for an effective Cartier divisor $B$, then $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{J}(X, \Delta + cB)$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Lemma 5.3.4 (general members).** Let $L$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$ and $V \subseteq H^0(X, \mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(L))$ a finite dimensional subspace that generates $\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(L)$. Let $m > c$ be an integer and $D_1, \dots, D_m \in |L|$ the divisors of general elements of $V$. Then
$$\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{J}\Big(X, \Delta + \frac{c}{m}(D_1 + \dots + D_m)\Big).$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Theorem 5.3.5 (local vanishing for pairs).** Let $\mu \colon Y \to X$ be a projective log resolution of $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$. Then
$$R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y\big(-\lfloor \Delta_Y + c F \rfloor\big) = 0 \quad \text{for } j > 0.$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

### Proof in words

The push-forward is an ideal because $\Delta$ is effective: a rational function whose divisor on $Y$ is at least the round-down has, on $X$, a divisor at least $\lfloor \Delta \rfloor \geq 0$, and $X$ is normal. Two log resolutions are dominated by a third, and on the third the round-down differs from the pullback of the round-down by the relative canonical divisor, which is the situation of Lemma P7.10.3. The function $1$ lies in the push-forward exactly when the round-down is not positive, which is the klt condition. General members of a linear system that generates $\mathfrak{a}$ pull back to $F$ plus smooth divisors in general position, and with coefficient $\frac{c}{m} < 1$ they do not change the round-down. Local vanishing is Corollary 5.1.15 on $Y$: the round-down is the round-up of $-\Delta_Y - cF$, the fractional part is a klt boundary, and the remaining class $-\mu^*(K_X + \Delta) - cF$ is $\mu$-nef, because $-F$ is relatively generated by the pullback of $\mathfrak{a}$.

### Examples

**Example 5.3.6 (the complete quadrangle).** Let $A = \sum_{i < j} L_{ij} \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be the six lines through four points $p_1, \dots, p_4$, no three collinear, and $\mu \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^2$ the blow-up of the four points, as in Example 5.1.20. There $\mu$ is a log resolution of $(\mathbb{P}^2, cA)$, and
$$\Delta_Y = c \sum L'_{ij} + (3c - 1) E, \qquad \mu^* A = \sum L'_{ij} + 3E.$$
For $0 \leq c < 1$ the round-down is $\lfloor 3c - 1 \rfloor E$, and $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(-k E) = \mathcal{I}_\Sigma^{(k)}$, the ideal of functions vanishing to order $k$ at the four points, for $k \geq 0$, and $\mathcal{O}$ for $k \leq 0$, since $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(-kE_i) = \mathfrak{m}_{p_i}^k$ near $p_i$. So
$$\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{P}^2, cA) = \begin{cases} \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2} & 0 \leq c < \frac23, \\ \mathcal{I}_\Sigma & \frac23 \leq c < 1, \\ \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-A) & 1 \leq c < \frac53, \end{cases}$$
where $\Sigma = \{p_1, \dots, p_4\}$ is reduced. For $1 \leq c < \frac43$ the round-down is $\sum L'_{ij} + 2E = \mu^* A - E$, and $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(-\mu^* A + E) = \mathcal{O}(-A) \otimes \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(E) = \mathcal{O}(-A)$ by the projection formula. For $\frac43 \leq c < \frac53$ the round-down is $\sum L'_{ij} + 3E = \mu^* A$, with the same push-forward, so $c = \frac43$ is not a jump although the round-down changes there. The pair is klt exactly for $c < \frac23$, as found in Example 5.1.20, and between $\frac23$ and $1$ the non-klt locus is the four triple points, with the diagonal points invisible. At $c = \frac23$ the ideal $\mathcal{I}_\Sigma$ is the defect found there.

**Example 5.3.7 (a Weil divisor on the quadric cone).** Let $X = \{xy = z^2\} \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3$, $L = \{x = z = 0\}$ a line through the vertex, and $\mu \colon Y \to X$ the blow-up of the vertex, the minimal resolution, with exceptional curve $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, $E^2 = -2$. As in Example 4.5.20, $\mu$ is crepant, $L$ is not Cartier and $2L = \operatorname{div}(x)$ is, and $\mu^* L = L' + \frac12 E$, with $L'$ a fibre of $Y \to E$ meeting $E$ transversally. So $\mu$ is a log resolution of $(X, cL)$, with
$$\Delta_Y = c L' + \frac{c}{2} E.$$
For $0 \leq c < 1$ the round-down is $0$ and $\mathcal{J}(X, cL) = \mathcal{O}_X$, so $(X, cL)$ is klt exactly for $c < 1$. For $1 \leq c < 2$ the round-down is $L'$, and $\mathcal{J}(X, cL) = \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(-L')$ consists of the regular functions $\varphi$ with $\operatorname{div}_X \varphi \geq L$, that is, $\mathcal{J}(X, cL) = \mathcal{O}_X(-L) = (x, z)$, the ideal of the line, which is not principal. At $c = 2$ the round-down is $2L' + E = \mu^*(2L)$, and $\mathcal{J}(X, 2L) = \mathcal{O}_X(-2L) = (x)$, as part 3 of Proposition 5.3.3 says for the Cartier divisor $2L$.

### Proof

*Proposition 5.3.2.* Ideal: let $U \subseteq X$ be open and $\varphi$ a section of $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(-\lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor)$ over $U$, a rational function with $\operatorname{div}_Y \varphi \geq \lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor$ on $\mu^{-1}(U)$. Push forward the divisors: $\mu_*$ keeps the non-exceptional components with their coefficients, so $\operatorname{div}_X \varphi \geq \lfloor \Delta + c \mu_* F \rfloor \geq 0$ on $U$, since $\mu_* \Delta_Y = \Delta$ and $\Delta$ and $F$ are effective. As $X$ is normal, $\varphi$ is regular on $U$. The sheaf is coherent by Theorem P4.4.8 when $\mu$ is projective, and in general because it agrees with the push-forward from a projective log resolution, by the next step.

Independence: let $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ be log resolutions. A log resolution of $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$ that factors through both exists: resolve the closure of the graph of $Y_1 \dashrightarrow Y_2$, which is proper and birational over $X$, by Theorem 4.2.10 applied with the preimages of $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu_1)$, $\mu^{-1}_{1*} \Delta$ and $F_1$. So it suffices to compare $\mu$ with $\mu' = \mu \circ \nu$ for a proper birational $\nu \colon Y' \to Y$ from a smooth variety. Put $B = \Delta_Y + cF$, a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with snc support on $Y$. Then $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{Y'} = \mathcal{O}_{Y'}(-\nu^* F)$, and $K_{Y'} + \Delta_{Y'} = \nu^*(K_Y + \Delta_Y)$ gives $\Delta_{Y'} + c \nu^* F = \nu^* B - K_{Y'/Y}$. Since $K_{Y'/Y}$ is integral,
$$-\lfloor \Delta_{Y'} + c \nu^* F \rfloor = K_{Y'/Y} - \lfloor \nu^* B \rfloor,$$
and Lemma P7.10.3 gives $\nu_* \mathcal{O}_{Y'}(K_{Y'/Y} - \lfloor \nu^* B \rfloor) = \mathcal{O}_Y(-\lfloor B \rfloor)$. Apply $\mu_*$.

Comparison: if $X$ is smooth and $\Delta = D$, then $K_Y = \mu^* K_X + K_{Y/X}$ and $\Delta_Y = \mu^* D - K_{Y/X}$, so $-\lfloor \Delta_Y \rfloor = K_{Y/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor$, the divisor of Definition P7.10.2; the same computation with $\Delta = 0$ gives $K_{Y/X} - \lfloor cF \rfloor$.

*Proposition 5.3.3.* Part 1: the ideal is $\mathcal{O}_X$ if and only if $1$ is a section, that is, $\lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor \leq 0$, that is, every coefficient of $\Delta_Y + cF$ is less than $1$. Since the ideal does not depend on $\mu$, neither does this condition, and for $\mathfrak{a} = \mathcal{O}_X$ it is the klt condition on one log resolution, which decides by Theorem 4.2.14 when $\dim X \geq 2$; for $\dim X = 1$, $\mu$ is the identity and both conditions say that the coefficients of $\Delta$ are less than $1$.

Part 2: take a log resolution of $(X, \Delta + \Delta'; \mathfrak{a} \mathfrak{b})$, which is a log resolution of both triples. The divisor $\Delta' - \Delta = (K_X + \Delta') - (K_X + \Delta)$ is effective and $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, so $\Delta'_Y - \Delta_Y = \mu^*(\Delta' - \Delta) \geq 0$. With $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F_{\mathfrak{a}})$ and $\mathfrak{b} \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F_{\mathfrak{b}})$, $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathfrak{b}$ gives $F_{\mathfrak{a}} \geq F_{\mathfrak{b}}$. So $\Delta'_Y + c' F_{\mathfrak{a}} \geq \Delta_Y + c F_{\mathfrak{b}}$, and the round-downs and push-forwards compare.

Part 3: $(\Delta + B)_Y = \Delta_Y + \mu^* B$ with $\mu^* B$ an integral Cartier divisor, so $\lfloor (\Delta + B)_Y + cF \rfloor = \lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor + \mu^* B$, and the projection formula gives the claim. A log resolution of $(X, \Delta + B; \mathfrak{a})$ is one of $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$.

Part 4: on a log resolution of $(X, \Delta + B; \mathfrak{a})$, $F = \mu^* B$ and $(\Delta + cB)_Y = \Delta_Y + c \mu^* B$.

*Lemma 5.3.4.* Let $\mu$ be a log resolution of $(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$ and $S$ the snc divisor $\operatorname{Supp}(\operatorname{Exc}(\mu) + \mu^{-1}_* \Delta + F)$. The images of $V$ generate $\mu^*(\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(L)) \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(\mu^* L - F)$, so for $s \in V$, $\mu^* \operatorname{div}(s) = F + M_s$ with $M_s$ a member of a free linear system on $Y$. By Bertini's theorem in the form of Har77, Corollary III.10.9, applied to the morphism to projective space given by this free system on $Y$ and on each stratum of $S$, general $M_1, \dots, M_m$ are smooth, have no common components with $S$ or with each other, and $S + M_1 + \dots + M_m$ is snc. Then $\mu$ is a log resolution of $(X, \Delta + \frac{c}{m} \sum D_i)$, the strict transform of $D_i$ is $M_i$ since $F$ is supported on $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu) \cup \mu^{-1}(V(\mathfrak{a}))$ and $D_i$ has no component in $V(\mathfrak{a})$, and
$$\Big(\Delta + \frac{c}{m} \sum_i D_i\Big)_Y = \Delta_Y + cF + \frac{c}{m} \sum_i M_i.$$
The $M_i$ are new components with coefficient $\frac{c}{m} < 1$, so the round-down equals $\lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor$.

*Theorem 5.3.5.* Put $G = -\lfloor \Delta_Y + cF \rfloor$, an integral divisor on the smooth variety $Y$, and $\Theta = G + \Delta_Y + cF$, the fractional part of $\Delta_Y + cF$. Then $\Theta$ has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$, so $(Y, \Theta)$ is klt, and
$$G - (K_Y + \Theta) = -K_Y - \Delta_Y - cF = -\mu^*(K_X + \Delta) - cF.$$
The pullback $\mu^*(K_X + \Delta)$ has degree $0$ on every curve contracted by $\mu$. The surjection $\mu^* \mathfrak{a} \to \mathcal{O}_Y(-F)$ shows that $\mathcal{O}_Y(-F)$ is generated by sections over every affine open subset of $X$, so $-F$ is $\mu$-nef. So $G - (K_Y + \Theta)$ is $\mu$-nef, and $\mu$ is a projective birational morphism from the normal quasi-projective variety $Y$. Corollary 5.1.15 gives $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(G) = 0$ for $j > 0$.

### Exercises

1. (A) In Example 5.3.6, compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{P}^2, cA)$ for $\frac53 \leq c < 2$, and show that the values of $c$ in $[0, 2)$ at which $c \mapsto \mathcal{J}(\mathbb{P}^2, cA)$ jumps are $\frac23$, $1$ and $\frac53$.
2. (A) Let $X$ be the quadric cone of Example 5.3.7 and $\mathfrak{m}$ the ideal of the vertex. Using $\mathfrak{m} \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-E)$, $K_Y = \mu^* K_X$ and $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(-kE) = \mathfrak{m}^k$ for $k \geq 0$, show that $\mathcal{J}(X, 0; \mathfrak{m}^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor}$ and that $\operatorname{lct}(X; \mathfrak{m}) = 1$. Compare with $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2, 0; \mathfrak{m}^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - 1}$ from Example P7.10.8.
3. (B) Compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2, 0; \mathfrak{m}^c)$ for $\mathfrak{m}$ the ideal of the origin on the blow-up of the origin and on the composite with the blow-up of a point of the exceptional curve, and check that the two answers agree, as Proposition 5.3.2 says. Identify the term $K_{Y'/Y}$ that makes them agree.
4. (B) Let $X$ be the quadric cone of Example 5.3.7 and $L_1$, $L_2$ two distinct lines through the vertex. Compute $\mathcal{J}(X, c(L_1 + L_2))$ on the minimal resolution, show that $L_1 + L_2 = \operatorname{div}(z)$ is Cartier, and show that $(X, c(L_1 + L_2))$ is klt if and only if $c < 1$.
5. (B) Prove that Lemma 5.3.4 fails for $m \leq c$: on $\mathbb{A}^2$ with $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}$ and $c = 2$, compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2, 0; \mathfrak{m}^2)$ and $\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{A}^2, D_1 + D_2)$ for two general lines through the origin.
6. (C) Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a pair and $\mathfrak{a}$ an ideal. Show that $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^c)$ depends only on the integral closure of $\mathfrak{a}$, using that $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\bar{\mathfrak{a}}$ have the same pullback $\mathcal{O}_Y(-F)$ on a log resolution of $\bar{\mathfrak{a}}$ that principalizes $\mathfrak{a}$.

### Lean

The library has no multiplier ideals, no ideal sheaves on schemes in this generality, and no log resolutions. Definition 5.3.1 is not yet in Lean, and neither are the results of this section.

### Notes and history

Multiplier ideals of pairs on normal varieties are in Lazarsfeld, Section 9.3.G (Laz04), and in de Fernex-Hacon for the case where $K_X + \Delta$ is not assumed $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier; the minimal model program needs only the case of a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $K_X + \Delta$. The klt condition of Definition 5.3.1 is the condition of Definition 4.6.2 restricted to the divisors on one log resolution, and Part 1 of Proposition 5.3.3 says that one log resolution decides, as Theorem 4.2.14 says for pairs. Lemma 5.3.4 is Laz04, Proposition 9.2.26, in the relative form; it is how the multiplier ideal of an ideal is reduced to that of a divisor. Local vanishing for pairs is Laz04, Theorem 9.4.1, for smooth $X$; for singular $X$ the proof above uses the relative Kawamata-Viehweg theorem of Chapter 1 in place of Theorem P7.10.9. The quadric cone example is the standard example of a klt surface singularity on which a non-Cartier Weil divisor has non-principal multiplier ideal.
