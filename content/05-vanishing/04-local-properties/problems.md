## Problems

Each problem computes a multiplier ideal or a jumping number of an explicit divisor or ideal, by a log resolution, by Howald's theorem P7.10.17, or by Theorem 5.4.29, or tests one of the inclusions of Sections 1 and 2 on an example. Say which result each step uses.

1. (A) Let $D = \frac23 \{x = 0\} + \frac23 \{y = 0\} + \frac23 \{x = y\}$ on $\mathbb{A}^2$. Compute $\mathcal{J}(cD)$ for all $c \in [0, 2]$ by the blow-up of the origin, and list the jumping numbers at the origin in this range.
2. (A) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^3, y^3)$ and $\mathfrak{b} = (x, y^2)$. Compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a})$, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b})$ and $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a} \mathfrak{b})$ by Howald's theorem, and check the subadditivity inclusion of Theorem 5.4.7.
3. (A) Let $C = \{y^3 = x^4\}$ and $H = \{x = 0\}$. Compute both sides of the restriction inclusion of Theorem 5.4.4 for every $c \in [0, 1)$, and find the values of $c$ at which it is strict.
4. (A) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^3, y^4) \subset k[x, y]$. Compute $\overline{\mathfrak{a}}$ and $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^2}$ by Newton polyhedra, and check the Briançon-Skoda inclusion $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^2} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$ of Corollary 5.4.15.
5. (B) Show by an example in $k[x, y]$ that the inclusion $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - 1}$ of Corollary 5.4.13 can be strict for every $c \geq 2$, and by another that it can be an equality.
6. (B) Let $C = \{x^2 + y^4 = 0\}$, two smooth branches with contact order $2$. Compute its jumping numbers in $(0, 2]$ at the origin, and compare with the tacnode of Example 5.4.35.
7. (B) Let $f = x^a + y^b$ and $C = \operatorname{div}(f)$. Show that for $\varepsilon > 0$ small $\mathcal{J}((1 - \varepsilon) C) = (x^i y^j : \frac{i+1}{a} + \frac{j+1}{b} \geq 1)$, and that its colength in $k[x, y]$ is the number of pairs $(i, j)$ with $i, j \geq 0$ and $\frac{i+1}{a} + \frac{j+1}{b} < 1$.
8. (B) Count the jumping numbers of $x^a + y^b$ in $(0, 1)$ with their multiplicities $\dim_k \mathcal{J}((\xi - \varepsilon) C) / \mathcal{J}(\xi C)$, and show that the total is $\delta$, the delta invariant $\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}{2}$, when $\gcd(a, b) = 1$.
9. (B) Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a nonzero ideal and $\xi$ a jumping number at the origin with $\xi > n - 1$. Show that $\xi - 1$ is a jumping number unless $\xi - 1 \leq n - 1$, and give an example with $n = 2$ where $\xi = 2$ is a jumping number and $1$ is not.
10. (C) Let $D$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $X$ and $x \in X$ with $\operatorname{mult}_x D < 1$. Choose general hyperplanes through $x$ in local coordinates, so that their successive intersections are smooth near $x$ and not contained in $\operatorname{Supp} D$, and apply Corollary 5.4.5 inductively down to a smooth curve germ $\Gamma$ through $x$. Show that $\operatorname{ord}_x (D|_\Gamma) = \operatorname{mult}_x D$ for general choices, and deduce that $\mathcal{J}(D)_x = \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$.
11. (C) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, y^3)$. Compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{5/3})$ and $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{5/6})^2$ by Howald's theorem, check the inclusion of Corollary 5.4.8, and show that it is strict. Then show that for every nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ with $\operatorname{lct}_0(\mathfrak{a}) = c_0$, the stalk $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{m c_0})_0$ is contained in $\mathfrak{m}_0^m$ for every $m \geq 1$.
