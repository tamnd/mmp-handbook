# Local properties of multiplier ideals

Volume P7, Chapter 10, defines multiplier ideals, proves Nadel vanishing and Howald's theorem, and states restriction, subadditivity and Skoda's theorem as black boxes. This chapter proves those three theorems and studies the local structure of the family $c \mapsto \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$. Throughout, $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$ and $X$ is a smooth variety, not necessarily projective. Chapter 3 proved Nadel vanishing for pairs; here only local vanishing on a log resolution is used, so the arguments are local on $X$.

Section 1 proves that a smooth divisor $H$ gives $\mathcal{J}(H, D|_H) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(X, D) \cdot \mathcal{O}_H$, with equality for a general member of a free linear system, and deduces the subadditivity theorem of Demailly, Ein and Lazarsfeld, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c \mathfrak{b}^d) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}^d)$, by restricting to the diagonal of $X \times X$. Section 2 proves Skoda's theorem $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1})$ for $c \geq n$ by a Koszul complex on a log resolution, and deduces the Briançon-Skoda theorem $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{l + n - 1}} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^l$. Section 3 defines the jumping numbers of Ein, Lazarsfeld, Smith and Varolin, and proves that they are discrete and rational, that the first one is the log canonical threshold, and that they are periodic: with period $1$ for a divisor, and above $n - 1$ for an ideal. Section 4 computes the multiplier ideals of the curves $x^a + y^b = 0$ below exponent $1$ by Howald's theorem and a torus action, and lists their jumping numbers.

The worked examples are the cusp, restricted to the two coordinate axes, the ideal $(x^2, y^3)$ and its integral closure $(x^2, xy^2, y^3)$, the reduction $(x^2, y^2)$ of $\mathfrak{m}^2$, the snc divisor $\frac12 \{x = 0\} + \frac43 \{y = 0\}$, and the plane curve singularities $A_2$, $A_4$, $E_6$, the ordinary $d$-fold point and the tacnode, with every multiplier ideal below exponent $1$. The chapter ends with problems that compute multiplier ideals and jumping numbers by hand.
