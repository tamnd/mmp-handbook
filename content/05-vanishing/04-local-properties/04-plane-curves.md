## Multiplier ideals of plane curves

### Statement

This section computes the multiplier ideals and the jumping numbers of the curves $x^a + y^b = 0$ in $\mathbb{A}^2$, and of the singularities that are isomorphic to them. The method combines three earlier results: a general member of an ideal has the same multiplier ideals as the ideal below exponent $1$ (Lemma 5.4.3), the torus acts on $\mathbb{A}^2$ and carries every curve $\lambda x^a + \mu y^b = 0$ with $\lambda \mu \neq 0$ to the curve $x^a + y^b = 0$, and Howald's theorem P7.10.17 computes the multiplier ideals of the monomial ideal $(x^a, y^b)$. The field $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$.

**Lemma 5.4.28 (automorphisms).** Let $\varphi \colon X \to X$ be an automorphism of a smooth variety and $D$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor. Then $\mathcal{J}(\varphi^* D) = \varphi^{-1} \mathcal{J}(D) \cdot \mathcal{O}_X$, the ideal generated by the functions $g \circ \varphi$ with $g$ a local section of $\mathcal{J}(D)$. The same holds for an ideal: $\mathcal{J}((\varphi^{-1} \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{O}_X)^c) = \varphi^{-1} \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \cdot \mathcal{O}_X$.

**Theorem 5.4.29 (the curves $x^a + y^b$).** Let $a, b \geq 2$ be integers, $f = x^a + y^b$ and $C = \operatorname{div}(f) \subset \mathbb{A}^2$. For every rational $c$ with $0 \leq c < 1$,
$$\mathcal{J}(cC) = \Big( x^i y^j : i, j \geq 0, \ \frac{i+1}{a} + \frac{j+1}{b} > c \Big).$$
In particular $\operatorname{lct}_0(C) = \min\{1, \frac1a + \frac1b\}$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.4.30 (jumping numbers).** In the setting of Theorem 5.4.29, the jumping numbers of $C$ at the origin are the numbers $m + \xi$ with $m \geq 0$ an integer and $\xi$ in the set
$$\Xi(a, b) = \Big\{ \frac{i+1}{a} + \frac{j+1}{b} : i, j \geq 0 \Big\} \cap (0, 1) \ \cup \ \{1\}.$$
At every other point of $C$ the jumping numbers are the positive integers.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

The formula of Theorem 5.4.29 is the formula of Howald for $\mathcal{J}((x^a, y^b)^c)$, and it is false for $c \geq 1$: $\mathcal{J}(C) = (f)$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.10.4, while $x^{a-1} \in \mathcal{J}((x^a, y^b))$ by Theorem P7.10.17, since $\frac{a}{a} + \frac1b > 1$, and $x^{a-1} \notin (f)$.

### Proof in words

Choose a log resolution of the monomial ideal $\mathfrak{a} = (x^a, y^b)$. A general linear combination $h = \lambda x^a + \mu y^b$ of its generators defines a curve whose multiplier ideals agree with those of $\mathfrak{a}$ for every exponent below $1$. General means in a dense open set of coefficients, so it can be taken with $\lambda \mu \neq 0$. Rescaling $x$ by an $a$-th root of $\lambda$ and $y$ by a $b$-th root of $\mu$ carries $h$ to $f$ and carries every monomial ideal to itself. So $f$ has the multiplier ideals of $\mathfrak{a}$ below exponent $1$, and Howald's theorem computes them from the Newton polygon, whose interior is $\{ \frac{u}{a} + \frac{w}{b} > 1 \}$. Above $1$ periodicity takes over.

### Examples

**Example 5.4.31 (the cusp).** For $a = 2$, $b = 3$ the numbers $\frac{i+1}{2} + \frac{j+1}{3}$ are $\frac56$ for $(i, j) = (0, 0)$, and at least $\frac76$ otherwise. So $\mathcal{J}(cC) = \mathcal{O}$ for $c < \frac56$ and $\mathcal{J}(cC) = (x, y) = \mathfrak{m}$ for $\frac56 \leq c < 1$, which is Example P7.10.7, now computed without a resolution of $C$. The jumping numbers at the origin are $\frac56 + m$ and $1 + m$ for $m \geq 0$, as in Example 5.4.27.

**Example 5.4.32 (the $E_6$ singularity $x^3 + y^4$).** The values of $\frac{i+1}{3} + \frac{j+1}{4}$ below $1$ are
$$(0,0) \mapsto \tfrac{7}{12}, \qquad (0,1) \mapsto \tfrac{5}{6}, \qquad (1,0) \mapsto \tfrac{11}{12},$$
while $(0,2) \mapsto \frac{13}{12}$, $(1,1) \mapsto \frac{7}{6}$ and $(2,0) \mapsto \frac54$. A monomial $x^i y^j$ lies in $\mathcal{J}(cC)$ when its value exceeds $c$. So
$$\mathcal{J}(cC) = \begin{cases} \mathcal{O} & 0 \leq c < \tfrac{7}{12}, \\ (x, y) & \tfrac{7}{12} \leq c < \tfrac56, \\ (x, y^2) & \tfrac56 \leq c < \tfrac{11}{12}, \\ (x^2, xy, y^2) & \tfrac{11}{12} \leq c < 1, \end{cases}$$
and $\Xi(3, 4) = \{\frac{7}{12}, \frac56, \frac{11}{12}, 1\}$. The threshold $\frac{7}{12}$ is the log canonical threshold of the $E_6$ curve singularity.

**Example 5.4.33 (the $A_4$ singularity $x^2 + y^5$).** The values below $1$ are $\frac{7}{10}$ at $(0, 0)$ and $\frac{9}{10}$ at $(0, 1)$, while $(0, 2) \mapsto \frac{11}{10}$ and $(1, 0) \mapsto \frac65$. So $\mathcal{J}(cC) = \mathcal{O}$ for $c < \frac{7}{10}$, $\mathfrak{m}$ for $\frac{7}{10} \leq c < \frac{9}{10}$, and $(x, y^2)$ for $\frac{9}{10} \leq c < 1$. The jumping numbers in $(0, 1]$ are $\frac{7}{10}$, $\frac{9}{10}$ and $1$. More generally for $A_{2l}$, $x^2 + y^{2l+1}$, they are $\frac12 + \frac{j+1}{2l+1}$ for $0 \leq j < l$, and $1$.

**Example 5.4.34 (an ordinary $d$-fold point).** Let $a = b = d \geq 2$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed, $x^d + y^d$ is a product of $d$ distinct linear forms, and $C$ is $d$ lines through the origin. The condition $\frac{i + j + 2}{d} > c$ is $i + j \geq \lfloor cd \rfloor - 1$, so
$$\mathcal{J}(cC) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor cd \rfloor - 1} \quad (0 \leq c < 1),$$
read as $\mathcal{O}$ when $\lfloor cd \rfloor \leq 1$. The jumping numbers in $(0, 1]$ are $\frac2d, \frac3d, \dots, \frac{d-1}{d}$ and $1$, and $\operatorname{lct}_0(C) = \frac2d$. The same answer holds for any $d$ distinct lines through the origin, by the blow-up of the origin: it is a log resolution with $\mu^* C = \widetilde{C} + dE$ and $K_{X'/X} = E$, and $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}((1 - \lfloor cd \rfloor) E) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor cd \rfloor - 1}$ as in Example P7.10.8. For $d = 3$ this is Example P7.10.15.

**Example 5.4.35 (the tacnode).** Let $C = \operatorname{div}(y(y - x^2))$, two smooth branches tangent at the origin. The automorphism $\psi(x, y) = (x, y + \frac12 x^2)$ of $\mathbb{A}^2$ fixes the origin and gives
$$\psi^*\big(y^2 - x^2 y\big) = \big(y + \tfrac12 x^2\big)^2 - x^2\big(y + \tfrac12 x^2\big) = y^2 - \tfrac14 x^4.$$
A further diagonal scaling carries $y^2 - \frac14 x^4$ to a nonzero multiple of $x^4 + y^2$. By Lemma 5.4.28 the multiplier ideals of $C$ are the images of those of $x^4 + y^2$ under automorphisms fixing the origin, and both $\mathcal{O}$ and $\mathfrak{m}$ are preserved. For $(a, b) = (4, 2)$ the values below $1$ are only $\frac34$ at $(0, 0)$, as $(1, 0) \mapsto 1$ and $(0, 1) \mapsto \frac54$. So $\mathcal{J}(cC) = \mathfrak{m}$ for $\frac34 \leq c < 1$, $\operatorname{lct}_0(C) = \frac34$, and $\Xi = \{\frac34, 1\}$. A resolution check: blowing up the origin gives $E_1$ with $\operatorname{ord}_{E_1} C = 2$ and $k_1 = 1$, and the strict transforms still meet at one point of $E_1$; blowing up that point gives $E_2$ with $\operatorname{ord}_{E_2} C = 4$ and $k_2 = 2$, and the divisor is now snc. The threshold is $\min\{\frac{1+1}{2}, \frac{2+1}{4}\} = \frac34$ by Proposition 5.4.21.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 5.4.28.* Let $\mu \colon X' \to X$ be a log resolution of $D$. Then $\mu' = \varphi^{-1} \circ \mu$ is a log resolution of $\varphi^* D$, since $\mu'^* \varphi^* D = \mu^* D$ and $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu') = \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$. Because $\varphi$ is an isomorphism, $K_{X'/X}$ computed for $\mu'$ equals $K_{X'/X}$ computed for $\mu$: a top form $\omega$ on $X$ gives $\mu'^*(\varphi^* \omega) = \mu^* \omega$, and $\operatorname{div}(\varphi^* \omega) = \varphi^* \operatorname{div}(\omega)$. So the two multiplier ideals are push-forwards of the same sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor)$ along $\mu' = \varphi^{-1} \circ \mu$ and $\mu$, and $\mu'_* = (\varphi^{-1})_* \mu_*$, which on ideals of $\mathcal{O}_X$ is $g \mapsto g \circ \varphi$. The case of an ideal is the same, with $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ in place of $\mu^* D$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 5.4.29.* Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^a, y^b) \subset k[x, y]$, and let $\mu \colon X' \to \mathbb{A}^2$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak{a}$ with $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$. Apply Lemma 5.4.3 with $r = k = 1$ and the generators $x^a$, $y^b$. There is a dense open set $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2$ of coefficients $(\lambda, \mu_0)$ such that $h = \lambda x^a + \mu_0 y^b$ satisfies
$$\mathcal{J}(c \operatorname{div}(h)) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \qquad \text{for all rational } 0 \leq c < 1.$$
The set $\Omega$ meets the dense open set $\{\lambda \mu_0 \neq 0\}$; fix such a point. Choose $\alpha, \beta \in k$ with $\alpha^a = \lambda$ and $\beta^b = \mu_0$, which exist since $k$ is algebraically closed, and let $\varphi(x, y) = (\alpha^{-1} x, \beta^{-1} y)$. Then $h \circ \varphi = f$, so $\varphi^* \operatorname{div}(h) = C$, and $\varphi$ maps every monomial ideal to itself, since $x^i y^j \circ \varphi = \alpha^{-i} \beta^{-j} x^i y^j$. Lemma 5.4.28 gives, for $0 \leq c < 1$,
$$\mathcal{J}(cC) = \varphi^{-1} \mathcal{J}(c \operatorname{div}(h)) \cdot \mathcal{O} = \varphi^{-1} \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \cdot \mathcal{O} = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c),$$
where the last step uses that $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ is a monomial ideal, by Theorem P7.10.17. The Newton polyhedron of $\mathfrak{a}$ is $P(\mathfrak{a}) = \{ (u, w) \in \mathbb{R}^2_{\geq 0} : \frac{u}{a} + \frac{w}{b} \geq 1 \}$, the convex hull of $(a, 0) + \mathbb{R}^2_{\geq 0}$ and $(0, b) + \mathbb{R}^2_{\geq 0}$. For $c > 0$ its interior scaled by $c$ is $\{ u > 0, w > 0, \frac{u}{a} + \frac{w}{b} > c \}$. A point $(i + 1, j + 1)$ with $i, j \geq 0$ has positive coordinates, so Theorem P7.10.17 gives the displayed formula; for $c = 0$ both sides are $\mathcal{O}$. The monomial $1$ lies in the ideal if and only if $\frac1a + \frac1b > c$, which with Proposition 5.4.21 and part 1 of Proposition 5.4.24 gives the threshold. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 5.4.30.* Away from the origin $C$ is smooth, since $\partial f / \partial x = a x^{a-1}$ and $\partial f / \partial y = b y^{b-1}$ vanish together only at the origin in characteristic $0$, and $C$ is reduced. Near such a point $C$ is a smooth divisor with coefficient $1$, so $\mathcal{J}(cC) = \mathcal{O}(-\lfloor c \rfloor C)$ there by part 2 of Proposition P7.10.4, and the jumping numbers are the positive integers. At the origin, let $\xi \in (0, 1)$. By Theorem 5.4.29, $\mathcal{J}(cC)$ for $c < 1$ is spanned by the monomials of value $v(i, j) = \frac{i+1}{a} + \frac{j+1}{b} > c$. If $\xi = v(i, j)$, then $x^i y^j \in \mathcal{J}((\xi - \varepsilon) C)$ and $x^i y^j \notin \mathcal{J}(\xi C)$, so $\xi$ is a jumping number. If $\xi$ is not a value, then the finitely many values below $1$ leave an interval $(\xi - \varepsilon, \xi]$ that contains none of them, and the set of monomials of value greater than $c$ is the same for all $c$ in that interval, so $\xi$ is not a jumping number. The number $1$ is a jumping number at the origin by part 1 of Proposition 5.4.24, which also gives the numbers $m + \xi$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $\mathcal{J}(cC)$ for $0 \leq c < 1$ and the jumping numbers in $(0, 1]$ for $C = \{x^2 + y^7 = 0\}$ and for $C = \{x^3 + y^5 = 0\}$ (the $E_8$ singularity).
2. (A) Show that the multiset of values $v(i, j) = \frac{i+1}{a} + \frac{j+1}{b}$ with $0 \leq i \leq a - 2$ and $0 \leq j \leq b - 2$ is invariant under $\xi \mapsto 2 - \xi$, and list it for $(a, b) = (3, 4)$. Compare with Example 5.4.32.
3. (B) Show that the number of pairs $(i, j)$ with $i, j \geq 0$ and $v(i, j) < 1$ is the number of lattice points in the interior of the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(a, 0)$, $(0, b)$, and that it equals $\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}{2}$ when $\gcd(a, b) = 1$.
4. (B) Using Lemma 5.4.28 and Theorem 5.4.29, compute the multiplier ideals and the jumping numbers in $(0, 1]$ of the curves $(y - x^2)^2 = x^3$ and $(y - x^3)^3 = x^4$.
5. (B) Let $a, b \geq 2$, $\mathfrak{a} = (x^a, y^b)$ and $C = \operatorname{div}(x^a + y^b)$. Show that $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \neq \mathcal{J}(cC)$ for every rational $c \geq 1$. Hint: a nonzero monomial ideal is not contained in $(f)$.
6. (C) Let $f = x^a + y^b + z^c$ with $a, b, c \geq 2$. Adapt the proof of Theorem 5.4.29 to show that $\mathcal{J}(t \operatorname{div}(f)) = (x^i y^j z^l : \frac{i+1}{a} + \frac{j+1}{b} + \frac{l+1}{c} > t)$ for $0 \leq t < 1$, and deduce that the surface singularities $x^2 + y^3 + z^4$ ($E_6$) and $x^2 + y^3 + z^5$ ($E_8$) have log canonical threshold $1$, being canonical, while $x^3 + y^3 + z^3$ has threshold $1$ and $x^4 + y^4 + z^4$ has threshold $\frac34$.

### Notes and history

The formula of Theorem 5.4.29 is a special case of Howald's later computation of the multiplier ideals of a polynomial that is nondegenerate with respect to its Newton polyhedron, below exponent $1$; the argument by general members and the torus action is the one given here. The jumping numbers of $x^a + y^b$ in $(0, 1)$ are related to the Hodge spectrum of Steenbrink, as Budur showed through the Hodge filtration. For an irreducible plane curve the jumping numbers are determined by the Puiseux characteristic and were computed by Järvilehto and by Naie; the examples above are the case of one Puiseux pair.
