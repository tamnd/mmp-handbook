## Skoda's theorem and Briançon-Skoda

### Statement

Theorem P7.10.13 states Skoda's theorem for integer exponents $m \geq n$, and Proposition P7.10.14 proves the easy inclusion $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c - 1}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$. This section proves the theorem for every rational exponent $c \geq n$, with a sharper form in terms of the number of generators of a reduction, and deduces the Briançon-Skoda theorem on integral closures. As in Section 4.1, $X$ is a smooth variety of dimension $n$ over $k$ of characteristic $0$.

An ideal $\mathfrak{b} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$ is a *reduction* of $\mathfrak{a}$ on a log resolution $\mu$ of $\mathfrak{a}$ if $\mathfrak{b} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'}$. The *integral closure* $\overline{\mathfrak{a}}$ of an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ is the set of local functions $\varphi$ that satisfy an equation $\varphi^d + a_1 \varphi^{d-1} + \cdots + a_d = 0$ with $a_i \in \mathfrak{a}^i$; it is an ideal (Laz04, Section 9.6.A).

**Lemma 5.4.11 (reductions with $n$ generators).** Let $X$ be affine, $\mathfrak{a}$ a nonzero ideal, $\mu$ a log resolution of $\mathfrak{a}$ and $x \in X$. Then there are $b_1, \dots, b_n \in \mathfrak{a}$ and an affine open $U \ni x$ such that $\mathfrak{b} = (b_1, \dots, b_n)$ has $\mathfrak{b} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ over $U$.

**Theorem 5.4.12 (Skoda).** Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nonzero ideal with a log resolution $\mu$, and $\mathfrak{b} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$ an ideal generated by $r$ elements with $\mathfrak{b} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'}$. Then for every rational $c \geq r$,
$$\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{b} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c - 1}) = \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1}).$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.4.13 (Skoda for rational exponents).** For every nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ and rational $c \geq n$, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1})$. In particular $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}$ for $c \geq n$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

For integer $c$ this is Theorem P7.10.13.

**Lemma 5.4.14 (integral closure).** Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nonzero ideal with a log resolution $\mu$ and $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$.

1. $\overline{\mathfrak{a}} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$, so $\mathcal{J}(\overline{\mathfrak{a}}^c) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ for every rational $c \geq 0$.
2. $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^l} \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^l)$ for every integer $l \geq 1$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 5.4.15 (Briançon-Skoda).** For every nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ and integer $m \geq 1$, $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{m + n - 1}} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^m$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

### Proof in words

The fixed part $F$ of $\mathfrak{a}$ on $X'$ is the zero divisor of the sections $\mu^* b_1, \dots, \mu^* b_r$ of $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$, and these sections generate it. So the Koszul complex of $(\mu^* b_i)$ is an exact resolution of $\mathcal{O}_{X'}$ by sums of the line bundles $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(pF)$. Twisted by $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor c F \rfloor)$, its terms are the sheaves $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor (c - p) F \rfloor)$, whose higher direct images vanish by local vanishing as long as $c - p \geq 0$. A long exact sequence chase then shows that push-forward keeps the last map onto, and the last map is multiplication by $b_1, \dots, b_r$. So every element of $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ is a combination of the $b_i$ with coefficients in $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1})$. Locally $n$ general elements of $\mathfrak{a}$ suffice, by a dimension count on the fibre of $\mu$, which gives the bound $c \geq n$.

Briançon-Skoda follows: an element integral over $\mathfrak{a}^{m+n-1}$ has large order along every divisor of $X'$, so it lies in $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{m+n-1})$, and Skoda peels off $m$ factors of $\mathfrak{a}$.

### Examples

**Example 5.4.16 (the ideal $(x^2, y^3)$).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, y^3) \subset k[x, y]$. By Theorem P7.10.17 and Example P7.10.19, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ is spanned by the monomials $x^i y^j$ with $\frac{i+1}{2} + \frac{j+1}{3} > c$, that is, $3i + 2j > 6c - 5$. Here $\mathfrak{a}$ has $r = 2$ generators, so Theorem 5.4.12 with $\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{a}$ applies for $c \geq 2$.

At $c = \frac52$: $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{5/2})$ is given by $3i + 2j > 10$, with minimal monomials $y^6$, $x y^4$, $x^2 y^3$, $x^3 y$, $x^4$. The ideal $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{3/2})$ is given by $3i + 2j > 4$, with minimal monomials $y^3$, $xy$, $x^2$. Then
$$\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{3/2}) = (x^2, y^3)(x^2, xy, y^3) = (x^4, x^3 y, x^2 y^3, x y^4, y^6) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{5/2}),$$
where the six products of generators are $x^4$, $x^3 y$, $x^2 y^3$ (twice), $x y^4$ and $y^6$. The theorem holds.

At $c = \frac32 < r$: $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{1/2})$ is given by $3i + 2j > -2$, so it is $\mathcal{O}$, and $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{1/2}) = (x^2, y^3)$. This misses $xy \in \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{3/2})$. So Skoda's equality fails below the bound, and the inclusion of Proposition P7.10.14 is strict.

The integral closure of a monomial ideal is spanned by the monomials whose exponents lie in the Newton polyhedron (Laz04, Section 9.6.A). So $\overline{\mathfrak{a}} = (x^2, x y^2, y^3)$: the monomial $x y^2$ has $\frac12 + \frac23 \geq 1$, and it satisfies $(xy^2)^2 - x^2 \cdot y^3 \cdot y = 0$ with $x^2 y^4 \in \mathfrak{a}^2$. It is not in $\mathfrak{a}$, so $\overline{\mathfrak{a}} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{a}$, and the exponent $m + n - 1$ in Corollary 5.4.15 cannot be replaced by $m$. With $m = 1$ the corollary says $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^2} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$. Directly: $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^2}$ is spanned by the $x^i y^j$ with $3i + 2j \geq 12$, and a monomial outside $\mathfrak{a}$ has $i \leq 1$, $j \leq 2$, so $3i + 2j \leq 7$.

**Example 5.4.17 (a reduction with fewer generators).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}^2 = (x^2, xy, y^2)$ and $\mathfrak{b} = (x^2, y^2)$. On the blow-up of the origin, in the chart $y = xv$, $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O} = x^2 (1, v, v^2) = (x^2)$ and $\mathfrak{b} \mathcal{O} = x^2 (1, v^2) = (x^2)$, and the other chart is symmetric. So $\mathfrak{b}$ is a reduction of $\mathfrak{a}$ with $r = 2$, while $\mathfrak{a}$ needs $3$ generators. By Example P7.10.8, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^{2c}) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor 2c \rfloor - 1}$. At $c = 2$, Theorem 5.4.12 says $\mathfrak{m}^3 = \mathfrak{b} \cdot \mathfrak{m}$, and indeed $(x^2, y^2)(x, y) = (x^3, x^2 y, x y^2, y^3) = \mathfrak{m}^3$. The element $xy$ lies in $\overline{\mathfrak{b}} = \mathfrak{m}^2$, since $(xy)^2 = x^2 \cdot y^2$, but not in $\mathfrak{b}$. Corollary 5.4.15 for $\mathfrak{b}$ and $m = 1$ says $\overline{\mathfrak{b}^2} = \mathfrak{m}^4 \subseteq \mathfrak{b}$, which holds because a monomial of degree $4$ has $x$-degree or $y$-degree at least $2$.

**Example 5.4.18 (the maximal ideal in all dimensions).** Let $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$. By Example P7.10.8, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}$. For rational $c \geq n$, $\lfloor c - 1 \rfloor = \lfloor c \rfloor - 1$, so $\mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^{c-1}) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}$, as Corollary 5.4.13 says. For $n - 1 \leq c < n$ the left side is $\mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1} = \mathcal{O}$ and the right side is $\mathfrak{m}$: the bound $c \geq n$ is sharp for $\mathfrak{m}$, which has no reduction with fewer than $n$ generators, since the zero locus of $n - 1$ functions through the origin has positive dimension.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 5.4.11.* Let $g_1, \dots, g_s$ generate $\mathfrak{a}$ and let $V \subseteq H^0(X', \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F))$ be the span of the $\mu^* g_l$, a base point free linear system. The fibre $Z = \mu^{-1}(x)$ is a proper scheme over $k$ and a proper closed subset of the irreducible $n$-dimensional $X'$, so its irreducible components have dimension at most $n - 1$. A general member of $V$ vanishes identically on no component of $Z$: at a point of each component some section of $V$ is nonzero, and the sections that vanish on a given component form a proper subspace. So for general $b_1 \in V$, the components of $Z \cap V(\mu^* b_1)$ have dimension at most $n - 2$, and by induction, for general $b_1, \dots, b_n \in V$ the common zero locus $B$ of the $\mu^* b_i$ misses $Z$. The set $B$ is closed in $X'$ and $\mu$ is proper, so $\mu(B)$ is closed and does not contain $x$. Let $U \ni x$ be an affine open missing $\mu(B)$. Over $U$ the sections $\mu^* b_i$ have no common zero, so they generate $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$, that is, $\mathfrak{b} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$ over $U$. The $b_i$ lie in $V$, so in $\mathfrak{a}$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 5.4.12.* Both sides are ideals, so the question is local, and $X$ may be taken affine, with generators $b_1, \dots, b_r$ of $\mathfrak{b}$. Write $s_i = \mu^* b_i \in H^0(X', \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F))$. These sections generate $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$, so $\sigma = (s_1, \dots, s_r) \colon W = \mathcal{O}_{X'}^{\oplus r} \to \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$ is onto. Its Koszul complex
$$0 \to \Lambda^r W \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X'}(rF) \to \cdots \to \Lambda^2 W \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X'}(2F) \to W \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X'}(F) \to \mathcal{O}_{X'} \to 0,$$
with the differentials given by contraction with $\sigma$, is exact: locally some $s_i$ generates $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$, and the Koszul complex of a sequence that contains a unit is exact. Put $\mathcal{M}_t = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor t F \rfloor)$ for rational $t \geq 0$. For an integer $p$, $\lfloor cF \rfloor - pF = \lfloor (c - p) F \rfloor$, so $\mathcal{M}_c \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X'}(pF) = \mathcal{M}_{c - p}$. Tensoring with the line bundle $\mathcal{M}_c$ gives an exact complex
$$0 \to K_r \to \cdots \to K_2 \to K_1 \to \mathcal{M}_c \to 0, \qquad K_p = \Lambda^p W \otimes \mathcal{M}_{c - p},$$
whose last map $K_1 = \mathcal{M}_{c-1}^{\oplus r} \to \mathcal{M}_c$ is $(m_i) \mapsto \sum_i s_i m_i$. Since $c \geq r$, every $c - p$ with $1 \leq p \leq r$ is at least $0$, and Lemma 5.4.1 gives $R^j \mu_* K_p = 0$ for $j > 0$. Let $Z_p$ be the kernel of $K_p \to K_{p-1}$, with $K_0 = \mathcal{M}_c$, so there are exact sequences $0 \to Z_p \to K_p \to Z_{p-1} \to 0$ for $1 \leq p \leq r$, with $Z_0 = \mathcal{M}_c$ and $Z_r = 0$. Their long exact sequences give injections $R^j \mu_* Z_{p-1} \hookrightarrow R^{j+1} \mu_* Z_p$ for $j \geq 1$ and $p \geq 2$, so
$$R^1 \mu_* Z_1 \hookrightarrow R^2 \mu_* Z_2 \hookrightarrow \cdots \hookrightarrow R^{r} \mu_* Z_r = 0.$$
For $r = 1$ the same holds trivially, since $Z_1 = 0$. So $\mu_* K_1 \to \mu_* \mathcal{M}_c$ is onto. Now $\mu_* \mathcal{M}_t = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^t)$, and the map is $(\varphi_i) \mapsto \sum_i b_i \varphi_i$, because $s_i$ is the pull-back of $b_i$. Hence $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{b} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1})$. Finally $\mathfrak{b} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1}) \subseteq \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ by Proposition P7.10.14, and the three ideals are equal. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 5.4.13.* Equality of ideals is local. Cover $X$ by affine opens $U$ as in Lemma 5.4.11. On each $U$, Theorem 5.4.12 with $r = n$ gives $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1})$ for $c \geq n$, using that the restriction of $\mu$ to $\mu^{-1}(U)$ is a log resolution of $\mathfrak{a}|_U$. For the inclusion, put $l = \lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1 \geq 1$. Applying the equality $l$ times, at the exponents $c, c - 1, \dots, c - l + 1$, all at least $n$, gives $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{a}^l \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c - l}) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^l$. $\square$

*Proof of Lemma 5.4.14.* Let $\varphi \in \overline{\mathfrak{a}}$ near a point, with $\varphi^d + a_1 \varphi^{d-1} + \cdots + a_d = 0$ and $a_i \in \mathfrak{a}^i$. Let $E$ be a prime divisor on $X'$, $v = \operatorname{ord}_E$ and $e = \operatorname{ord}_E(F)$. Then $v(\mu^* a_i) \geq i e$. If $v(\varphi) < e$, then $v(a_i \varphi^{d-i}) \geq ie + (d - i) v(\varphi) > d \, v(\varphi) = v(\varphi^d)$ for every $i \geq 1$, which contradicts the equation. So $v(\mu^* \varphi) \geq \operatorname{ord}_E(F)$ for every prime divisor $E$, and since $X'$ is normal, $\mu^* \varphi$ is a local section of $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$. Hence $\overline{\mathfrak{a}} \mathcal{O}_{X'} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F) = \mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'} \subseteq \overline{\mathfrak{a}} \mathcal{O}_{X'}$. Then $\mu$ is a log resolution of $\overline{\mathfrak{a}}$ with the same $F$, and the multiplier ideals agree. This is part 1. For part 2, $\mathfrak{a}^l \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-lF)$, and part 1 for $\mathfrak{a}^l$ gives $\mu^* \varphi \in \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-lF)$ for $\varphi \in \overline{\mathfrak{a}^l}$. Since $K_{X'/X} \geq 0$, $\varphi \in \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-lF) \subseteq \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - lF) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^l)$. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 5.4.15.* By Lemma 5.4.14, $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{m+n-1}} \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{m+n-1})$, and by Corollary 5.4.13 with $c = m + n - 1 \geq n$, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{m + n - 1}) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^{m}$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^3, y^3) \subset k[x, y]$. Compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ for $c = \frac43$, $\frac73$ and $\frac{10}{3}$ by Theorem P7.10.17, and check Theorem 5.4.12 at $c = \frac73$ and $c = \frac{10}{3}$.
2. (A) For $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, y^3)$, find $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^2}$ and $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^3}$ as monomial ideals, and check Corollary 5.4.15 with $m = 2$.
3. (B) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, xy^2, y^4) \subset k[x, y]$. Show that $\mathfrak{b} = (x^2, y^4)$ is a reduction of $\mathfrak{a}$: show that $\overline{\mathfrak{b}} = \mathfrak{a}$, and use part 1 of Lemma 5.4.14 on a log resolution of $\mathfrak{b}$. Then verify $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{2}) = \mathfrak{b} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a})$.
4. (B) Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be an ideal generated by $r < n$ elements. Show that $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1})$ already for $c \geq r$, and deduce $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^m) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^{m - r + 1}$ for integers $m \geq r$. For $\mathfrak{a} = (x_1)$ compare with part 2 of Proposition P7.10.4.
5. (B) Show that Lemma 5.4.11 is sharp for $\mathfrak{m} \subset k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$: if $\mathfrak{b} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ is generated by $n - 1$ elements, then $\mathfrak{b} \mathcal{O}_{X'} \neq \mathfrak{m} \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ on the blow-up of the origin. Hint: the strict transform of a curve in $V(\mathfrak{b})$ through the origin.
6. (C) Prove the Briançon-Skoda inclusion $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{m+n-1}} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^m$ by hand for a monomial ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq k[x, y]$ generated by $x^a$ and $y^b$: describe $\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{m+1}}$ by the Newton polygon, and show that a monomial $x^i y^j$ with $\frac{i}{a} + \frac{j}{b} \geq m + 1$ is divisible by some $x^{a p} y^{b q}$ with $p + q = m$.

### Notes and history

Skoda proved the analytic division theorem behind Theorem 5.4.12 by $L^2$ methods (Sko72). Briançon and Skoda deduced Corollary 5.4.15 for the ring of convergent power series in 1974, answering a question of Mather, and Lipman and Teissier, and later Lipman and Sathaye, gave algebraic proofs, the latter for all regular rings. The algebraic proof by the Koszul complex and local vanishing is Ein and Lazarsfeld's, in Lazarsfeld, Section 9.6.C (Laz04). Example 5.4.16 shows that the exponent $n$ cannot be lowered for a general ideal with two generators in the plane. Tight closure gives another proof of Briançon-Skoda in characteristic $p$, by the method of Hochster and Huneke; Chapter 6 meets the Frobenius side of this story.
