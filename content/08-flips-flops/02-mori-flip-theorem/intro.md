# Mori's flip theorem

This chapter is about the flips of 3-folds with terminal singularities. Mori proved in 1988 that they exist (Mor88), which completed the minimal model program for 3-folds. His proof is a long classification of the analytic germs of a 3-fold along a flipping curve, and it is cited here, not reproved. What is proved in full is the structure that every such germ has, the reduction of the existence of the flip to a local problem at finitely many points, and the properties of the flip that follow from Chapter 1 once it exists.

Section 1 studies flipping curves. By Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, the fibres of a flipping contraction of a klt pair have no first cohomology, so a flipping curve is a tree of smooth rational curves. For a terminal 3-fold, the dimension estimate for the space of rational curves on a local complete intersection shows that a flipping curve must pass through a point of index at least $2$: a smooth or Gorenstein terminal 3-fold has no flipping contractions, which extends Corollary 6.3.4. Finite generation of the relative canonical algebra is a property of the completed local rings at the finitely many points of $Z$ over which $f$ is not an isomorphism, so the flip can be constructed one germ at a time. The Francia flip and its family are the examples.

Section 2 states Mori's theorem and the theorem of Kollár and Mori on the general elephant: on the germ along a flipping curve, a general member of $|-K_X|$ is a surface with Du Val singularities. It outlines the classification of extremal neighbourhoods into the semistable types and the exceptional types, and it ends with an account of what is proved in this volume and what is quoted. Shokurov's later proof, which avoids the classification, is the subject of Chapter 3.
