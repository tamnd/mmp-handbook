# Flips, flops and the 3-fold MMP

This volume constructs the minimal model program in dimension 3. Volume 7 gives the cone and contraction theorems for klt pairs, so a $(K_X + \Delta)$-negative extremal ray $R$ of a projective $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial klt pair has a contraction $\varphi_R \colon X \to Z$. If $\varphi_R$ is a divisorial contraction or a fibre space, the program continues on $Z$ or stops. If $\varphi_R$ is small, $Z$ is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial and $K_Z + \varphi_{R*} \Delta$ is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, and the program needs a new step, the flip. Two problems then remain: the flip must exist, and a sequence of flips must stop. In dimension 3 both were solved between 1985 and 1992, by Mori for terminal 3-folds and by Shokurov for klt pairs, and this volume follows those solutions, in the forms of Kollár and Mori (KM98), the Utah seminar (Kol92) and Corti's account of Shokurov's method (Cor07).

> **The flip box.** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a small contraction of normal varieties and $D$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor on $X$ with $-D$ ample over $Z$. The *$D$-flip* is a small contraction $f^+ \colon X^+ \to Z$ such that the strict transform $D^+$ of $D$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $Z$. It exists if and only if the algebra $\bigoplus_{m \ge 0} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mrD)$ is finitely generated, for $r$ with $rD$ integral, and then $X^+$ is its relative Proj; in particular it is unique. For $D = K_X + \Delta$ it is the *flip*, and for $K_X + \Delta \equiv_f 0$ it is a *flop*. Flips raise discrepancies, so they preserve the classes of singularities of Volume 4.

The box is used throughout. The finite generation turns the existence of a flip into a question about a graded ring on the base $Z$. The negativity lemma of Volume 1 turns the comparison of $X$ and $X^+$ into a comparison of discrepancies, and termination is proved by counting discrepancies.

Chapter 1 sets up flips and flops: small and flipping contractions, $D$-flips, the existence of the flip as the finite generation of the relative algebra, its uniqueness, the $\mathbb{Q}$-factoriality and Picard number of the flip of an extremal ray, and the increase of discrepancies. The examples are the Atiyah flop of the cone $xy = zw$, the weighted blow-up of a $\frac12(1,1,1)$ point, and the Francia flip, with $K \cdot C^- = -\frac12$ and $K \cdot C^+ = 1$; the toric flips of Volume 3 are the model case.

Chapter 2 is Mori's flip theorem: the flip of a flipping contraction of a terminal $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial 3-fold exists. The chapter proves what the handbook can prove in full: the flipping locus is a tree of smooth rational curves, it passes through a point of index at least $2$, and the existence of the flip is a local question that can be checked on completions. The extremal neighbourhoods, the general elephant theorem and the classification of Kollár and Mori are outlined and cited.

Chapter 3 is Shokurov's theorem that 3-fold log flips exist. Flips of klt pairs reduce to pl flips, where a component $S$ of the boundary is negative on the flipping curves, and the finite generation of the flipping algebra reduces to the finite generation of its restriction to $S$, a question on surfaces. The chapter proves the restriction lemma and outlines the reduction, special termination and the use of complements on surfaces.

Chapter 4 proves the termination of 3-fold terminal flips by Shokurov's difficulty, the number of exceptional divisors of discrepancy less than $1$, and treats the canonical and klt cases. Termination in dimension 4, due to Kawamata, Matsuda and Matsuki, is cited.

Chapter 5 assembles the 3-fold minimal model program: every projective 3-fold with canonical singularities has a terminal $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial model that is a minimal model or a Mori fibre space, and Kollár's theorem that two minimal models are connected by a sequence of flops, with Reid's pagoda as the worked example.

Chapter 6 outlines the semistable minimal model program of Kawamata and of Kollár and Mori, for a semistable family of surfaces over a curve, summarizes the volume and points to Volumes 9 and 10.

The spine is Kollár and Mori, Chapters 4 to 7 (KM98), with the Utah seminar, Astérisque 211 (Kol92), and Corti's Chapter 2 of Flips for 3-folds and 4-folds (Cor07). The original sources are Mori (Mor88), Kollár and Mori (KM92), Shokurov (Sho92), Kawamata (Kaw92), Reid (Rei83b) and Francia (Fra80).
