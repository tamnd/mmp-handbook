## The Atiyah flop, the weighted blow-up and the Francia flip

### Statement

The three examples of this section are the models for every flip and flop in the volume. Each is toric, so each statement can be read off a fan as in Volume 3, but the statements below are made and proved in coordinates, and the fans serve as a check. Intersection numbers of a Weil divisor $D$ with a curve $C$ on a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial variety are $\frac1r (rD \cdot C)$ for $rD$ Cartier.

**Proposition 8.1.11 (the Atiyah flop).** Let $Z = \{xy = zw\} \subset \mathbb{A}^4$, the cone over a smooth quadric surface, with vertex $0$. Let $\Pi_1 = \{x = z = 0\}$ and $\Pi_2 = \{x = w = 0\}$, two planes in $Z$ through $0$ from the two rulings, and for $i = 1, 2$ let $f_i \colon X_i \to Z$ be the blow-up of $Z$ along $\Pi_i$.

1. $X_i$ is smooth, $f_i$ is small with exceptional locus a curve $C_i \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ over $0$, and $N_{C_i/X_i} \cong \mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$. In particular $K_{X_i} \cdot C_i = 0$.
2. On $X_1$, the strict transform $\Pi_1'$ of $\Pi_1$ has $\Pi_1' \cdot C_1 = -1$, and the strict transform of $\Pi_2$ has $\Pi_2' \cdot C_1 = 1$.
3. $\mathcal{O}_Z(\Pi_1)$ is generated by $1$ and $u = w/x = y/z$, and $X_2 = \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Z R(X_1/Z, \Pi_1')$. So $X_1 \dashrightarrow X_2$ is the $\Pi_1'$-flop of $f_1$, the *Atiyah flop*.
4. The blow-up $Y$ of $X_1$ along $C_1$ has exceptional divisor $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with $\mathcal{O}_E(E) \cong \mathcal{O}(-1, -1)$, and the two rulings of $E$ contract to $X_1$ and to $X_2$.

**Proposition 8.1.12 (the weighted blow-up of a $\frac12(1,1,1)$ point).** Let $X = \frac12(1,1,1) = \mathbb{A}^3 / \mu_2$, a terminal quotient singularity of index $2$ (Example 3.3.24), and let $g \colon Y \to X$ be the weighted blow-up with weights $\frac12(1,1,1)$, the star subdivision of the cone of $X$ at $v = \frac12(u_1 + u_2 + u_3)$ (Example 3.5.18). Then $Y$ is smooth, the exceptional divisor is $F \cong \mathbb{P}^2$ with $\mathcal{O}_F(F) \cong \mathcal{O}(-2)$, and
$$K_Y = g^* K_X + \tfrac12 F.$$
For a line $\ell \subset F$, $F \cdot \ell = -2$ and $K_Y \cdot \ell = -1$.

**Proposition 8.1.13 (the Francia flip).** Let $N = \mathbb{Z}^3$ with basis $u_2, u_3, u_4$ and $u_1 = -u_2 + u_3 + 2u_4$, so that $u_1 + u_2 - u_3 - 2u_4 = 0$, the circuit with $q = (1, 1, -1, -2)$ of Example 3.5.24. Let $X^-$ be the toric variety of the cones $(u_1, u_2, u_3)$ and $(u_1, u_2, u_4)$, $X^+$ that of the cones $(u_1, u_3, u_4)$ and $(u_2, u_3, u_4)$, and $Z$ that of the cone $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4)$, with $f^\pm \colon X^\pm \to Z$ the toric morphisms. Let $C^- = V(\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2))$ and $C^+ = V(\operatorname{Cone}(u_3, u_4))$.

1. $X^+$ is smooth, $N_{C^+/X^+} \cong \mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-2)$ and $K_{X^+} \cdot C^+ = 1$.
2. $X^-$ has one singular point, of type $\frac12(1,1,1)$, on $C^-$, it is smooth elsewhere and $K_{X^-} \cdot C^- = -\frac12$.
3. $f^-$ and $f^+$ are small with exceptional curves $C^-$ and $C^+$, so $X^- \dashrightarrow X^+$ is the flip of the flipping contraction $f^-$ of the terminal 3-fold $X^-$.
4. Let $v = u_3 + u_4 = \frac12(u_1 + u_2 + u_3)$. The star subdivisions $Y_1$ of $X^+$ and $Y$ of $X^-$ at $v$ are smooth; $Y_1 \to X^+$ is the blow-up of $C^+$, $Y \to X^-$ is the weighted blow-up of the $\frac12(1,1,1)$ point, and $Y_1 \dashrightarrow Y$ is an Atiyah flop. So the Francia flip factors as a blow-up, an Atiyah flop and a weighted blow-down.

**Example 8.1.14 (the family $q = (1,1,-1,-n)$).** For $n \ge 2$ the circuit $u_1 + u_2 - u_3 - n u_4 = 0$, with $u_1 = -u_2 + u_3 + n u_4$ in the basis $u_2, u_3, u_4$, gives in the same way a smooth $X^+$ with $N_{C^+/X^+} \cong \mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-n)$ and $K_{X^+} \cdot C^+ = n - 1$, and an $X^-$ with one singular point, of type $\frac1n(1, 1, -1)$, and $K_{X^-} \cdot C^- = -\frac{n-1}{n}$, which tends to $-1$. The point $\frac1n(1, -1, 1)$ is terminal by the terminal lemma (Theorem 4.4.6), so $X^- \dashrightarrow X^+$ is a flip of a terminal 3-fold. For $n = 1$ the circuit is that of the Atiyah flop, with $K \cdot C^\pm = 0$.

### Proof in words

The Atiyah flop is a computation with the two families of planes in the quadric cone. Blowing up a plane of one family makes the plane a Cartier divisor, which is negative on the new curve, while the planes of the other family become positive on it; the relative algebra of the first plane has two generators and gives the blow-up of a plane of the other family.

For the toric examples the intersection numbers come from the wall relation of Theorem 3.4.5: if $\tau$ is a two-dimensional cone shared by two three-dimensional cones, with the relation $\sum_\rho b_\rho u_\rho = 0$ normalised so that the coefficients of the two vertices off $\tau$ are $1$ and the vertex of index one is used for the normalisation, then $D_\rho \cdot V(\tau) = b_\rho$ up to the multiplicity of the cone. In the Francia example the flipped curve $C^+$ has a smooth neighbourhood whose normal bundle is read off from the relation, and the flipping curve $C^-$ passes through the $\frac12(1,1,1)$ point. The factorisation through the common star subdivision at $v$ computes $K_{X^-} \cdot C^-$ from $K_Y$ and the discrepancy $\frac12$.

### Examples

**Example 8.1.15 (the Atiyah flop on fans).** In Corollary 3.5.22 the Atiyah flop is the circuit $u_1 + u_2 - u_3 - u_4 = 0$ in $N = \mathbb{Z}^3$: $X_1$ and $X_2$ are the two triangulations of the square cone, by the diagonals $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2)$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(u_3, u_4)$, as in Example 3.5.23. The four planes are the divisors $D_1, \dots, D_4$; with $\Pi_1 = D_3$ and $\Pi_2 = D_1$ the relation gives $D_3 \cdot C_1 = D_4 \cdot C_1 = -1$ and $D_1 \cdot C_1 = D_2 \cdot C_1 = 1$ on the side where $C_1 = V(\operatorname{Cone}(u_3, u_4))$, which is part 2 of Proposition 8.1.11.

**Example 8.1.16 (discrepancies in the Francia flip).** The divisor $F$ over the $\frac12(1,1,1)$ point of $X^-$ has discrepancy $a(F, X^-) = \frac12$ by Proposition 8.1.12. On the other side, $F$ is the strict transform on $Y$ of the exceptional divisor $E_1$ of the blow-up $Y_1 \to X^+$ of the smooth curve $C^+$, so $a(F, X^+) = 1$. The discrepancy goes up across the flip, which is Example 3.6.5 and a case of Lemma 8.1.19.

### Proof

*Proposition 8.1.11.* Parts 1 and 2. The blow-up of $Z$ along $\Pi_1 = V(x, z)$ is the closure of the graph of $[x : z]$. On $Z \setminus \Pi_1$, $xy = zw$ gives $[x : z] = [w : y]$ where both are defined, so $X_1 = \{([s : t], p) : sz = tx, \ sy = tw\} \subset \mathbb{P}^1 \times Z$, and one checks that this closed set is irreducible of dimension $3$ by the two charts. In the chart $t = 1$, the equations give $x = sz$ and $w = sy$, so the chart is $\mathbb{A}^3$ with coordinates $(s, y, z)$, mapping to $(sz, y, z, sy)$. In the chart $s = 1$, $z = tx$ and $y = tw$, and the chart is $\mathbb{A}^3$ with coordinates $(t, x, w)$. So $X_1$ is smooth. The fibre over $p \ne 0$ is one point, and the fibre over $0$ is $C_1 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \{0\}$, given by $y = z = 0$ and $x = w = 0$ in the two charts; so $f_1$ is small with exceptional locus $C_1$. On the overlap $t = 1/s$, the normal coordinates $(y, z)$ and $(x, w)$ are related by $x = sz$ and $w = sy$. So the conormal bundle of $C_1$ has transition function multiplication by $s$ in both coordinates, $N^\vee \cong \mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 2}$ and $N \cong \mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}$. By adjunction, $\deg K_{C_1} = K_{X_1} \cdot C_1 + \deg \det N$, that is $-2 = K_{X_1} \cdot C_1 - 2$, so $K_{X_1} \cdot C_1 = 0$.

The preimage of $\Pi_1$ is computed in the charts. In the chart $(s, y, z)$ the preimage of $\Pi_1$ is $\{sz = 0, z = 0\} = \{z = 0\}$, a smooth divisor containing $C_1 \cap \{t = 1\}$; in the chart $(t, x, w)$ it is $\{x = 0, tx = 0\} = \{x = 0\}$. So $\Pi_1' = f_1^{-1} \Pi_1$ is the exceptional divisor of the blow-up, a Cartier divisor whose ideal is $\mathcal{O}_{X_1}(1)$ for the $\mathbb{P}^1$ coordinate, and $\mathcal{O}_{X_1}(-\Pi_1') \cong \mathcal{O}(1)$ restricts to $\mathcal{O}_{C_1}(1)$. Hence $\Pi_1' \cdot C_1 = -1$. The plane $\Pi_2 = \{x = w = 0\}$ has preimage $\{s = 0\} \cup C_1$ in the chart $(s, y, z)$ and preimage the curve $C_1 \cap \{s = 1\}$ in the chart $(t, x, w)$. So its strict transform is the closure of $\{s = 0\}$, which meets $C_1$ transversally in the one point $s = 0$, and $\Pi_2' \cdot C_1 = 1$.

Part 3. The divisors of the coordinate functions on $Z$ are $\operatorname{div} x = \Pi_1 + \Pi_2$, $\operatorname{div} z = \Pi_1 + \Sigma$, $\operatorname{div} w = \Pi_2 + \Sigma'$ and $\operatorname{div} y = \Sigma + \Sigma'$, where $\Sigma = \{z = y = 0\}$ and $\Sigma' = \{w = y = 0\}$, each with multiplicity one, as one checks on the smooth locus. So $u = w/x = y/z$ has $\operatorname{div} u = \Sigma' - \Pi_1$, and $u \in \mathcal{O}_Z(\Pi_1)$. The ring $k[Z]$ is graded by the torus of Example 8.1.15, and $\mathcal{O}_Z(\Pi_1)$ is spanned by the characters $\chi^m$ with $\langle m, u_\rho \rangle \ge -\delta_\rho$ where $\delta$ is the coefficient vector of $\Pi_1$; the lattice points of this polyhedron are those of the cone of $k[Z]$ together with those of the cone translated by the character of $u$, so $\mathcal{O}_Z(\Pi_1) = k[Z] \cdot 1 + k[Z] \cdot u$. The module $\mathcal{O}_Z(m \Pi_1)$ is generated by $1, u, \dots, u^m$ in the same way, so $R(X_1/Z, \Pi_1') = k[Z][T_0, T_1]/I$ with $T_0 = t$ and $T_1 = ut$, by Lemma 8.1.4. The relations are $x T_1 = w T_0$ and $z T_1 = y T_0$, so $\operatorname{Proj} R$ is the closure of the graph of $[1 : u] = [x : w] = [z : y]$, which is the blow-up of $Z$ along $V(x, w) = \Pi_2$, that is $X_2$. By part 1 for $X_2$, the strict transform of $\Pi_1$ is positive on $C_2$, so it is ample over $Z$, as Theorem 8.1.6 predicts. Since $K_{X_1} \cdot C_1 = 0$ and $\rho(X_1/Z) = 1$, $K_{X_1} \equiv_{f_1} 0$ and the flip is a flop.

Part 4. Since $C_1$ is a smooth curve in a smooth 3-fold, $E = \mathbb{P}(N_{C_1/X_1}^\vee) \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $\mathcal{O}_E(E) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(N^\vee)}(-1)$, which is $\mathcal{O}(-1, -1)$ for $N \cong \mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}$. The projection $E \to C_1$ is one ruling. The fibres $\ell$ of the other ruling have $E \cdot \ell = -1$. On the fan of Example 8.1.15, $Y$ is the star subdivision of the square at $u_1 + u_2 = u_3 + u_4$, which refines both triangulations and is the blow-up of $C_1$ in $X_1$ and of $C_2$ in $X_2$; the morphism $Y \to X_2$ contracts $E$ along the second ruling.

*Proposition 8.1.12.* On the fan, $v = \frac12(u_1 + u_2 + u_3)$ with $u_1, u_2, u_3$ spanning the cone of $X$ and $N = \mathbb{Z}^3 + \mathbb{Z} v$. The cones $(v, u_2, u_3)$, $(u_1, v, u_3)$ and $(u_1, u_2, v)$ each have determinant $\frac12 \cdot 2 = 1$ with respect to $N$, so $Y$ is smooth. The divisor $F = D_v$ is the toric variety of the star of $v$, the fan of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in $N / \mathbb{Z} v$. For the line $\ell = V(\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, v))$, the wall between $(u_1, v, u_2)$ and $(u_1, v, u_3)$ has relation $u_2 + u_3 + u_1 - 2v = 0$, so by Theorem 3.4.5 $D_2 \cdot \ell = D_3 \cdot \ell = D_1 \cdot \ell = 1$ and $F \cdot \ell = -2$. Since all lines of $F$ are algebraically equivalent in $F \cong \mathbb{P}^2$, $\mathcal{O}_F(F) \cong \mathcal{O}(-2)$. With $K_Y = -D_1 - D_2 - D_3 - F$, $K_Y \cdot \ell = -3 + 2 = -1$. The pullback $g^* K_X = -(D_1 + D_2 + D_3) - cF$ is the linear function on the fan that takes the value $1$ at $u_1, u_2, u_3$, so its value at $v$ is $\frac32$ and $c = \frac32$; hence $K_Y = g^* K_X + \frac12 F$, by Proposition 3.3.35. As a check, $g^* K_X \cdot \ell = 0$ and $K_Y \cdot \ell = \frac12 F \cdot \ell = -1$.

*Proposition 8.1.13.* Part 1. With respect to the basis $u_2, u_3, u_4$, the cone $(u_2, u_3, u_4)$ is unimodular, and $\det(u_1, u_3, u_4) = \det(-u_2, u_3, u_4) = -1$, so $(u_1, u_3, u_4)$ is unimodular too and $X^+$ is smooth. The wall $\operatorname{Cone}(u_3, u_4)$ between them has the relation $u_1 + u_2 - u_3 - 2u_4 = 0$ with coefficient $1$ at the two vertices off the wall, so $D_1 \cdot C^+ = D_2 \cdot C^+ = 1$, $D_3 \cdot C^+ = -1$ and $D_4 \cdot C^+ = -2$ (Theorem 3.4.5). Then $K_{X^+} \cdot C^+ = -(1 + 1 - 1 - 2) = 1$. The normal bundle of $C^+$ is $\mathcal{O}_{C^+}(D_3) \oplus \mathcal{O}_{C^+}(D_4) = \mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-2)$, since $C^+ = D_3 \cap D_4$ transversally on a smooth toric variety.

Part 2. $\det(u_1, u_2, u_4) = \det(u_3, u_2, u_4) = -1$, so $(u_1, u_2, u_4)$ is unimodular, while $\det(u_1, u_2, u_3) = \det(2u_4, u_2, u_3) = 2$. The vector $v = u_3 + u_4$ satisfies $2v = 2u_3 + 2u_4 = u_1 + u_2 + u_3$, so the cone $(u_1, u_2, u_3)$ is of type $\frac12(1,1,1)$, terminal by Example 3.3.24, and $X^-$ is smooth elsewhere. The wall $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2)$ has multiplicity $1$, since $u_1, u_2$ extend to the basis $u_1, u_2, u_4$. Normalising the circuit at the vertex $u_4$ of the unimodular cone gives $\frac12 u_1 + \frac12 u_2 - \frac12 u_3 - u_4 = 0$, so $D_3 \cdot C^- = \frac12$, $D_4 \cdot C^- = 1$, $D_1 \cdot C^- = D_2 \cdot C^- = -\frac12$ by Theorem 3.4.5, and $K_{X^-} \cdot C^- = -(-\frac12 - \frac12 + \frac12 + 1) = -\frac12$.

Part 3. Both $f^\pm$ are the toric morphisms of the two triangulations of the circuit cone, which have no new rays, so they are small with exceptional curves $C^\mp$ over the torus fixed point of $Z$ (Theorem 3.5.20). The curve $C^-$ is $K$-negative and $\rho(X^-/Z) = 1$, so $f^-$ is a flipping contraction, and $K_{X^+}$ is positive on $C^+$ and so ample over $Z$. By Theorem 8.1.6, $X^+$ is the flip.

Part 4. $Y$ has the cones $(u_1, u_2, u_4)$, $(u_1, u_2, v)$, $(u_1, u_3, v)$ and $(u_2, u_3, v)$, which are unimodular since the last three subdivide the $\frac12(1,1,1)$ cone as in Proposition 8.1.12; it is the weighted blow-up of the singular point. $Y_1$ has the cones $(u_1, u_3, v)$, $(u_1, u_4, v)$, $(u_2, u_3, v)$ and $(u_2, u_4, v)$, all unimodular, for instance $\det(u_2, u_4, v) = \det(u_2, u_4, u_3) = -1$. Since $v = u_3 + u_4$, $Y_1$ is the star subdivision of $X^+$ at the sum of the two generators of the smooth wall $\operatorname{Cone}(u_3, u_4)$, which is the blow-up of $C^+$. The fans of $Y$ and $Y_1$ share the cones $(u_1, u_3, v)$ and $(u_2, u_3, v)$, and differ in the square $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2, u_4, v)$ with the relation $u_1 + u_2 - u_4 - v = 0$, which follows from $u_1 + u_2 = u_3 + 2u_4 = u_4 + v$. In $Y$ the square is cut by $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2)$, in $Y_1$ by $\operatorname{Cone}(u_4, v)$; by Example 8.1.15 the map $Y_1 \dashrightarrow Y$ is an Atiyah flop. As a check of part 2 by this factorisation, in $Y$ the curve $\Gamma = V(\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2))$ is the strict transform of $C^-$, and the relation $u_4 + v - u_1 - u_2 = 0$ gives $F \cdot \Gamma = D_v \cdot \Gamma = 1$ and $K_Y \cdot \Gamma = -(1 + 1 - 1 - 1) = 0$. From $K_Y = g^* K_{X^-} + \frac12 F$ and the projection formula, $K_{X^-} \cdot C^- = g^* K_{X^-} \cdot \Gamma = 0 - \frac12 = -\frac12$.

*Example 8.1.14.* The determinants are $\det(u_1, u_3, u_4) = -1$ and $\det(u_1, u_2, u_4) = -1$ as before, and $\det(u_1, u_2, u_3) = n$, with $\frac1n(u_1 + u_2 + (n - 1) u_3) = u_3 + u_4$, so the singular cone is of type $\frac1n(1, 1, n-1) = \frac1n(1,1,-1)$. The relation gives $D_3 \cdot C^+ = -1$, $D_4 \cdot C^+ = -n$, hence the normal bundle and $K \cdot C^+ = -(2 - 1 - n) = n - 1$; normalised at $u_4$ it is $-\frac1n u_1 - \frac1n u_2 + \frac1n u_3 + u_4 = 0$, which gives $K \cdot C^- = -(-\frac1n - \frac1n + \frac1n + 1) = -\frac{n-1}{n}$.

### Exercises

1. (A) In Proposition 8.1.11, compute the strict transforms of the planes $A_\lambda = \{x = \lambda z, \ w = \lambda y\}$ in $X_1$ and show that $A_\lambda' \cdot C_1 = 1$ for every $\lambda$.
2. (A) Verify that the four divisors of coordinate functions in the proof of part 3 of Proposition 8.1.11 are correct by computing them in the two charts of $X_1$ and pushing forward.
3. (A) Find an automorphism of $Z$ that exchanges $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$, and deduce that $X_1$ and $X_2$ are isomorphic as varieties, although $X_1 \dashrightarrow X_2$ does not extend to an isomorphism over $Z$.
4. (B) In the Francia flip, compute all the intersection numbers $D_i \cdot C^\pm$ for $i = 1, \dots, 4$ and check that $\sum_i D_i \cdot C^+$ and $\sum_i D_i \cdot C^-$ have opposite signs.
5. (B) For the family of Example 8.1.14 with $n = 3$, compute the discrepancy of the divisor $D_v$, $v = u_3 + u_4$, over $X^-$ and over $X^+$, and check that it goes up across the flip.
6. (C) Show that the blow-up of $C^+$ in Proposition 8.1.13 has exceptional divisor the Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb{F}_1$, identify the section $\sigma = V(\operatorname{Cone}(u_4, v))$, and show that $N_{\sigma / Y_1} \cong \mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}$.

### Notes and history

The flop of Proposition 8.1.11 appears in Atiyah's work on the families of surfaces with ordinary double points (Ati58), where the two small resolutions explain the non-separated moduli. Francia's example (Fra80) showed that on a terminal 3-fold a $K$-negative curve can be contracted by a small contraction, so that the minimal model program in dimension three must leave the category of Gorenstein varieties; Reid's account of the example and of its factorisation (Rei83b) is the source of the computation in part 4 of Proposition 8.1.13. The toric description is that of Volume 3, and Example 8.1.14 is its simplest generalisation; Cor07, Chapter 2 has further examples.
