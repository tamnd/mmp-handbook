# Flips and flops

This chapter defines flips and flops and proves the basic theorem about them: the $D$-flip of a small contraction $f \colon X \to Z$ exists if and only if the relative algebra $R(X/Z, D) = \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ is finitely generated, and then it is the relative Proj of this algebra, so it is unique. The ground field $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$.

Section 1 proves this theorem, following KM98, Section 6.1, and Corti's account in Cor07, Chapter 2. A small morphism does not change sections of divisorial sheaves, so $R(X/Z, D)$ depends only on $Z$ and the Weil divisor $f_* D$. The relative Proj of a finitely generated algebra of this kind is small over $Z$, by a computation with the open sets where a section does not vanish, and the strict transform of $f_* D$ on it is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample. The examples are an ample divisor, which gives back $X$, and a divisorial contraction, where the construction gives the target.

Section 2 computes the three examples of the chapter. The Atiyah flop exchanges the two small resolutions of the cone $xy = zw$, each with an exceptional curve with normal bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}$. The weighted blow-up of a $\frac12(1,1,1)$ point has exceptional divisor $\mathbb{P}^2$ with normal bundle $\mathcal{O}(-2)$ and discrepancy $\frac12$. The Francia flip goes from a 3-fold with a $\frac12(1,1,1)$ point and a curve with $K \cdot C^- = -\frac12$ to a smooth 3-fold with a curve with normal bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-2)$ and $K \cdot C^+ = 1$, and the two sides are joined by a blow-up, an Atiyah flop and the weighted blow-up. The toric flips of Volume 3 are the model, and every computation is checked on fans.

Section 3 treats the flip of an extremal ray of a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial pair. The flip is again $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial with the same Picard number, it does not depend on the divisor chosen negative on the ray, and its discrepancies are at least those of $X$, with strict inequality over the flipping and the flipped loci. So a flip of a terminal, canonical, klt or lc pair is of the same kind. This is the inequality on which the termination of Chapter 4 is built.
