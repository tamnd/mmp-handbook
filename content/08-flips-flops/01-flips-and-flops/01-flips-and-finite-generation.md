## Flips and the finite generation of the relative algebra

### Statement

The ground field $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$. A *contraction* is as in Definition 1.5.1, and for a projective birational morphism $f \colon X \to Z$ onto a normal variety the exceptional locus $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ is as in Definition 1.5.5. For a Weil divisor $D$ on a normal variety $X$, $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ is the divisorial sheaf whose sections over an open set $V$ are $0$ and the rational functions $\varphi$ with $\operatorname{div} \varphi + D \ge 0$ on $V$.

**Definition 8.1.1 (small and flipping contractions).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective birational morphism of normal varieties. It is *small* if $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ has codimension at least $2$ in $X$, so that $f$ has no exceptional divisors (Proposition 1.5.6, part 4). Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a pair, with $K_X + \Delta$ an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor.

1. A *flipping contraction* of $(X, \Delta)$ is a small contraction $f$ with $\operatorname{Exc}(f) \neq \emptyset$ and $-(K_X + \Delta)$ ample over $Z$.
2. A *flopping contraction* of $(X, \Delta)$ is a small contraction $f$ with $\operatorname{Exc}(f) \ne \emptyset$ and $K_X + \Delta \equiv_f 0$.
3. A flipping contraction is *extremal* if $X$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, $X$ is projective and $f$ is the contraction $\varphi_R$ of a $(K_X + \Delta)$-negative extremal ray $R$ of $\overline{NE}(X)$, as in Theorem 1.1.12.

**Definition 8.1.2 (flips and flops).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a small contraction and $D$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $X$ with $-D$ ample over $Z$. A *$D$-flip* of $f$ is a small contraction $f^+ \colon X^+ \to Z$ such that the strict transform $D^+ = f^{+\,-1}_* f_* D$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $Z$. This is Definition 3.5.19.

1. If $f$ is a flipping contraction of $(X, \Delta)$, the $(K_X + \Delta)$-flip is the *flip* of $f$, and $(X^+, \Delta^+)$ with $\Delta^+ = f^{+\,-1}_* f_* \Delta$ is the *flipped pair*. The curves contracted by $f$ are the *flipping curves*, and those contracted by $f^+$ the *flipped curves*.
2. If $f$ is a flopping contraction of $(X, \Delta)$, a $D$-flip is called a *$D$-flop*, or a *flop*.

The birational map $X \dashrightarrow X^+$ is also called the flip or the flop. It is an isomorphism in codimension $1$, so strict transform identifies the Weil divisors of $X$ and of $X^+$.

**Definition 8.1.3 (the relative algebra).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective birational morphism of normal varieties and $D$ a Weil divisor on $X$. The *relative algebra* of $D$ is the sheaf of graded $\mathcal{O}_Z$-algebras
$$R(X/Z, D) = \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \, t^m \subseteq k(X)[t],$$
with the multiplication of $k(X)[t]$. It is *finitely generated* if $Z$ is covered by affine open sets $U$ such that $R(X/Z, D)(U)$ is a finitely generated $\mathcal{O}(U)$-algebra. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ one uses $R(X/Z, rD)$ for an integer $r \ge 1$ with $rD$ integral; by Lemma 8.1.5 its finite generation does not depend on $r$.

**Lemma 8.1.4 (sections over a small morphism).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a small projective birational morphism of normal varieties and $D$ a Weil divisor on $X$. Then $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(D) = \mathcal{O}_Z(f_* D)$ as subsheaves of the constant sheaf $k(X) = k(Z)$. So $R(X/Z, D) = \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} \mathcal{O}_Z(m f_* D) \, t^m$ depends only on $Z$ and $f_* D$.

**Lemma 8.1.5 (the relative algebra is normal, and Veronese subalgebras).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective birational morphism of normal varieties with $Z = \operatorname{Spec} A$ affine, $D$ a Weil divisor on $X$, $R = R(X/Z, D)(Z) = \bigoplus_m R_m t^m$ with $R_m = H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))$, and for $d \ge 1$ let $R^{(d)} = \bigoplus_m R_{md} t^{md}$.

1. $R$ is a normal domain with $R_0 = A$.
2. $R$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra if and only if $R^{(d)}$ is.
3. If $R$ is finitely generated, there is $d \ge 1$ with $R_{kd} t^{kd} = (R_d t^d)^k$ for every $k \ge 1$, so that $R^{(d)}$ is generated by its degree $d$ part.

**Theorem 8.1.6 (flips and finite generation).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a small projective birational morphism of normal varieties, $D$ a Weil divisor on $X$ and $D_Z = f_* D$.

1. If $R(X/Z, D)$ is finitely generated, then $X^+ = \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Z R(X/Z, D)$ is a normal variety, $f^+ \colon X^+ \to Z$ is a small projective birational morphism, and the strict transform $D^+ = f^{+\,-1}_* D_Z$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $Z$. For $d$ as in part 3 of Lemma 8.1.5, $dD^+$ is Cartier and $\mathcal{O}_{X^+}(dD^+) \cong \mathcal{O}(1)$ for the Proj of $R^{(d)}$.
2. Conversely, let $g \colon X' \to Z$ be a small projective birational morphism from a normal variety such that $D' = g^{-1}_* D_Z$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $Z$. Then $R(X/Z, D)$ is finitely generated, and there is a unique isomorphism $X' \cong X^+$ over $Z$.
3. In particular, if $D$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier with $-D$ ample over $Z$, the $D$-flip of $f$ exists if and only if $R(X/Z, D)$ is finitely generated, it is $X^+$, and it is unique up to a unique isomorphism over $Z$. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ the same holds with $rD$ in place of $D$.

Lean: `MinimalModel.IsFlip`, in the project library, defines a flip as a step of the program with a small contraction; this theorem is not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 8.1.7 (locality and the choice of the divisor).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a small contraction.

1. Let $D$ be $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier with $-D$ ample over $Z$, and $Z = \bigcup_i U_i$ an open cover. The $D$-flip of $f$ exists if and only if the $D$-flip of $f^{-1}(U_i) \to U_i$ exists for every $i$, and then it is glued from these.
2. Let $D_1, D_2$ be $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier with $-D_1, -D_2$ ample over $Z$, and suppose that $D_1 \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} c D_2 + f^* L$ for a rational $c > 0$ and a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $L$ on $Z$. Then the $D_1$-flip exists if and only if the $D_2$-flip exists, and they are the same.

So the flip of a flipping contraction of $(X, \Delta)$, with $r(K_X + \Delta)$ Cartier, exists if and only if the *relative canonical algebra* $\bigoplus_{m \ge 0} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mr(K_X + \Delta))$ is finitely generated, and then $X^+$ is its relative Proj. Proposition 3.5.21 is the uniqueness statement of Theorem 8.1.6 in the form used for toric flips.

### Proof in words

A small morphism changes neither the prime divisors nor their valuations, so the sections of $\mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ over $f^{-1}(U)$ are the sections of $\mathcal{O}_Z(mD_Z)$ over $U$. The relative algebra is then an intersection of valuation rings with a polynomial ring, hence normal, and finite generation passes between $R$ and its Veronese subalgebras by finiteness of the integral closure.

For part 1 of Theorem 8.1.6, take a Veronese subalgebra generated in degree $1$, so that $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is invertible and ample over $Z$. A section $s \in R_1$ is a rational function $\varphi$ with $B_s = \operatorname{div} \varphi + D_Z \ge 0$. The chart of $X^+$ where $s$ does not vanish has coordinate ring $R_{(s)}$, and this ring is exactly the ring of functions on the open set $Z_s = Z \setminus \operatorname{Supp} B_s$. The quasi-affine $Z_s$ is an open subset of $\operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}(Z_s)$ with complement of codimension at least $2$, by the approximation theorem for Krull domains. So every prime divisor of $X^+$ meets an open set that maps isomorphically onto an open subset of $Z$, and $f^+$ is small. On that open set the divisor of the generator of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is $D_Z$, so $\mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_{X^+}(D^+)$.

For part 2, Lemma 8.1.4 applied to $g$ shows that the relative algebra of $D'$ on $X'$ is the relative algebra of $D$ on $X$, and the section ring of an ample line bundle is finitely generated with Proj the variety.

### Examples

**Example 8.1.8 (an ample divisor gives back $X$).** If $D$ is itself $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $Z$, part 2 of Theorem 8.1.6 with $X' = X$ and $g = f$ gives $X \cong \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Z R(X/Z, D)$. So a small contraction is recovered from $Z$ and the Weil divisor $f_* D$ of any relatively ample divisor $D$. For a flip this says: $X$ is the $(-D)$-flip of $f^+$, and the flip of the flip is the original variety, which is the remark after Theorem 3.5.20.

**Example 8.1.9 (a divisorial contraction gives the target).** Smallness is needed in Lemma 8.1.4. Let $f \colon X \to Z = \mathbb{A}^2$ be the blow-up of the origin, with exceptional curve $E$, so that $E^2 = -1$ and $-E$ is ample over $Z$. For $m \ge 0$, $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mE) = \mathcal{O}_Z$, since a rational function with poles only along $E$ is regular on $X \setminus E = Z \setminus \{0\}$, hence on $Z$, which is normal. So $R(X/Z, E) = k[x, y][t]$ and its Proj is $Z$ itself. In the other direction $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(-mE) = \mathfrak{m}^m$ for the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$, and $\operatorname{Proj} \bigoplus_m \mathfrak{m}^m$ is the blow-up $X$, as Example 8.1.8 predicts. The "flip" of $E$ is $Z$: for a divisorial contraction the relative algebra of an anti-ample divisor contracts the exceptional divisor instead of flipping it, and $f_* E = 0$ is not a divisor on $Z$ whose strict transform could be ample.

**Example 8.1.10 (generation in degree one can fail).** Let $Z = \frac12(1,1,1) = \mathbb{A}^3/\mu_2$, with $\mu_2$ acting by $-1$ on the coordinates $x_1, x_2, x_3$, and $f = \mathrm{id}_Z$, which is small. Let $D_Z$ be the image of the plane $x_1 = 0$, a Weil divisor with $2D_Z = \operatorname{div}(x_1^2)$ Cartier. Then $\mathcal{O}_Z(mD_Z)$ is the module of semi-invariants $\{h \in k[x_1, x_2, x_3] : h(-x) = (-1)^m h(x)\} \cdot x_1^{-m}$, and $R(Z/Z, D_Z)$ is the algebra $\bigoplus_m k[x]_{(m)} \, t^m$ of semi-invariants of weight $m \bmod 2$, generated by $x_1 t, x_2 t, x_3 t$ over $k[x]^{\mu_2}$. Its Proj is $Z$ again, as Example 8.1.8 predicts, since $D_Z$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier. The degree $1$ part is generated by three elements, while $\mathcal{O}_Z(D_Z)$ is not invertible; only $R^{(2)}$ has an invertible $\mathcal{O}(1)$, matching $d = 2$ in part 1 of Theorem 8.1.6.

### Proof

*Lemma 8.1.4.* Let $U \subseteq Z$ be open and $V = f^{-1}(U)$. A rational function $\varphi$ is a section of $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(D)$ over $U$ if and only if $\operatorname{mult}_P(\operatorname{div} \varphi + D) \ge 0$ for every prime divisor $P$ of $X$ meeting $V$. By part 3 of Proposition 1.5.6, applied to $f$ and restricted over $U$, $P \mapsto f_* P$ is a bijection from the non-exceptional prime divisors of $X$ meeting $V$ to the prime divisors of $Z$ meeting $U$, and since $f$ is small every prime divisor is non-exceptional. The morphism $f$ is an isomorphism near the generic point of $P$, so $\operatorname{mult}_P(\operatorname{div} \varphi + D) = \operatorname{mult}_{f_* P}(\operatorname{div} \varphi + f_* D)$. So the two conditions are the same, and $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(D)(U) = \mathcal{O}_Z(f_* D)(U)$.

*Lemma 8.1.5.* Part 1: $R_0 = H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = A$, since $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z$ by Zariski's main theorem for a projective birational morphism onto a normal variety. For a prime divisor $P$ of $X$ let $v_P$ be its valuation and $d_P = \operatorname{mult}_P D$, and define $w_P$ on $k(X)[t] \setminus \{0\}$ by $w_P(\sum_m \varphi_m t^m) = \min_m (v_P(\varphi_m) + m d_P)$. This is the Gauss extension of $v_P$ twisted by $d_P$: it is a valuation of $k(X)(t)$, and its valuation ring $\mathcal{V}_P$ is integrally closed. Since $X$ is normal, $\varphi \in H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))$ if and only if $v_P(\varphi) + m d_P \ge 0$ for every $P$. So an element $\sum_m \varphi_m t^m$ of $k(X)[t]$ lies in $R$ if and only if $w_P \ge 0$ for every $P$, that is
$$R = k(X)[t] \cap \bigcap_P \mathcal{V}_P.$$
An intersection of integrally closed subrings of the field $k(X)(t)$ is integrally closed in $k(X)(t)$, hence in the fraction field of $R$. So $R$ is a normal domain.

Part 2: suppose $R$ is generated over $A$ by homogeneous elements $x_1, \dots, x_r$ of positive degree. Then $R$ is a finite module over the Noetherian ring $A[x_1^d, \dots, x_r^d]$, generated by the monomials with all exponents less than $d$, and $A[x_1^d, \dots, x_r^d] \subseteq R^{(d)} \subseteq R$. A submodule of a finite module over a Noetherian ring is finite, so $R^{(d)}$ is a finite module over a finitely generated $A$-algebra, hence a finitely generated $A$-algebra. Conversely, suppose $R^{(d)}$ is finitely generated. Every homogeneous $x \in R$ has $x^d \in R^{(d)}$, so $R$ is integral over $R^{(d)}$. Let $L$ and $K$ be the fraction fields of $R$ and $R^{(d)}$. Then $L$ is algebraic over $K$, and $L$ is a finitely generated field extension of $k$, as a subfield of the finitely generated extension $k(X)(t)$; so $L$ is a finite extension of $K$. By part 1, $R$ is the integral closure of $R^{(d)}$ in $L$. The integral closure of a finitely generated domain over a field in a finite extension of its fraction field is a finite module (Eis95, Corollary 13.13), so $R$ is a finite $R^{(d)}$-module and a finitely generated $A$-algebra.

Part 3: let $x_1, \dots, x_r$ be homogeneous generators of positive degrees $d_1, \dots, d_r$, let $e = \operatorname{lcm}(d_1, \dots, d_r)$ and $d = re$. Each $x_i^{e/d_i}$ has degree $e$. A monomial $x^a = \prod_i x_i^{a_i}$ of degree $kd$, $k \ge 1$, can be written with $a_i = q_i e/d_i + c_i$ and $0 \le c_i < e/d_i$. The factor $\prod_i x_i^{c_i}$ has degree $\sum_i c_i d_i < re = d$, and this degree is a multiple of $e$, since $kd$ and the degree of $\prod_i (x_i^{e/d_i})^{q_i}$ are; write it $je$ with $0 \le j < r$. The other factor is a product of $N = kr - j \ge r - j$ elements of degree $e$. Group $\prod_i x_i^{c_i}$ with $r - j$ of these elements, and the remaining $(k-1) r$ elements into $k - 1$ groups of $r$. Each group has degree $d$, so $x^a \in (R_d t^d)^k$. As $R_{kd} t^{kd}$ is spanned over $A$ by such monomials, $R_{kd} t^{kd} = (R_d t^d)^k$.

*Theorem 8.1.6, part 1.* All statements are local on $Z$: the relative Proj is glued from the Proj over affine open sets (Definition P3.6.31 and Proposition P3.6.32), and normality, smallness and relative ampleness are local on $Z$. So let $Z = \operatorname{Spec} A$. By Lemma 8.1.5 choose $d$ with $R^{(d)}$ generated by its degree $d$ part. The Proj of a graded ring and of its Veronese subring are canonically isomorphic, and $R^{(d)} = R(X/Z, dD)$, so after replacing $D$ by $dD$ assume that $R$ is generated by $R_1 t$ over $A$. Then $X^+ = \operatorname{Proj} R$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}(R_1)$ over $Z$, projective over $Z$, with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ invertible and very ample over $Z$. It is integral since $R$ is a domain. It is covered by the charts $D_+(s) = \operatorname{Spec} R_{(s)}$ for $0 \neq s \in R_1 t$, where $R_{(s)}$ is the degree $0$ part of $R[s^{-1}]$. Since $R[s^{-1}] = R_{(s)}[s, s^{-1}]$ and $R[s^{-1}]$ is normal by part 1 of Lemma 8.1.5, $R_{(s)}$ is normal: an element of its fraction field integral over it lies in $R[s^{-1}] \cap k(X) = R_{(s)}$. So $X^+$ is normal.

By Lemma 8.1.4, $R_m = H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z(mD_Z))$. Fix $0 \ne s = \varphi t \in R_1 t$. Then $B_s = \operatorname{div} \varphi + D_Z$ is an effective Weil divisor on $Z$; let $Z_s = Z \setminus \operatorname{Supp} B_s$.

Step 1: $R_{(s)} = \mathcal{O}(Z_s)$ inside $k(Z)$. An element of $R_{(s)}$ is $\psi t^m / s^m = \psi / \varphi^m$ with $\operatorname{div} \psi + m D_Z \ge 0$. On $Z$, $\operatorname{div}(\psi/\varphi^m) = (\operatorname{div} \psi + mD_Z) - m B_s \ge -m B_s$, which is effective on $Z_s$; since $Z_s$ is normal, $\psi / \varphi^m \in \mathcal{O}(Z_s)$. Conversely let $g \in \mathcal{O}(Z_s)$. Its poles lie in the finitely many components of $B_s$, each of positive coefficient in $B_s$, so $\operatorname{div} g + k B_s \ge 0$ on $Z$ for $k \gg 0$. Then $\psi = g \varphi^k$ has $\operatorname{div} \psi + k D_Z = \operatorname{div} g + k B_s \ge 0$, so $\psi t^k \in R_k t^k$ and $g = \psi t^k / s^k \in R_{(s)}$.

Step 2: the open set $Z_s$ of the affine variety $Z$ is quasi-affine, so the canonical morphism $j \colon Z_s \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}(Z_s) = D_+(s)$ is an open immersion (Stacks, the chapter on properties of schemes, quasi-affine schemes), and the composite of $j$ with $f^+|_{D_+(s)} \colon D_+(s) \to Z$, induced by $A \subseteq R_{(s)}$, is the inclusion $Z_s \subseteq Z$. So $f^+$ maps $j(Z_s)$ isomorphically onto the open set $Z_s$.

Step 3: the complement of $j(Z_s)$ in $V = D_+(s)$ has codimension at least $2$. Suppose a prime divisor $P$ of $V$ is disjoint from $j(Z_s)$. The ring $\mathcal{O}(V)$ is a normal Noetherian domain, hence a Krull domain, and by the approximation theorem for Krull domains (Mat86, Section 12) there is $h \in k(V)$ with $v_P(h) = -1$ and $v_Q(h) \ge 0$ for every other prime divisor $Q$ of $V$. Then $h$ is regular on $V \setminus P \supseteq j(Z_s)$, so $h \in \mathcal{O}(Z_s) = \mathcal{O}(V)$, which contradicts $v_P(h) < 0$.

Step 4: smallness. The morphism $f^+$ is birational, as it is an isomorphism on the dense open set $j(Z_s)$. Let $E$ be a prime divisor of $X^+$ and choose $s$ with $E \cap D_+(s) \neq \emptyset$. By Step 3, $E$ meets $j(Z_s)$, where $f^+$ is an isomorphism onto an open set of $Z$, so $f^+(E)$ is a divisor and $E$ is not exceptional. By part 4 of Proposition 1.5.6, $\operatorname{Exc}(f^+)$ has no component of codimension $1$, and $f^+$ is small.

Step 5: the divisor. On $D_+(s)$ the sheaf $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is $R_{(s)} \cdot s = \mathcal{O}_V \cdot \varphi t$, so $\mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_{X^+}(G)$ for the Cartier divisor $G$ with $G|_{D_+(s)} = -\operatorname{div}_V \varphi$, and these local descriptions agree on overlaps since they all come from the inclusion of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ in the constant sheaf $k(X) \, t$. On $j(Z_s)$, $-\operatorname{div} \varphi = D_Z - B_s = D_Z$. For a prime divisor $E$ of $X^+$, choose $s$ as in Step 4; at the generic point of $E$, which lies in $j(Z_s)$, $\operatorname{mult}_E G = \operatorname{mult}_{f^+(E)} D_Z = \operatorname{mult}_E D^+$. So $G = D^+$ as Weil divisors, $D^+$ is Cartier and $\mathcal{O}_{X^+}(D^+) \cong \mathcal{O}(1)$ is ample over $Z$. For the original $D$ this says that $dD^+$ is Cartier and ample over $Z$.

*Theorem 8.1.6, part 2.* Locally on $Z$, so let $Z$ be affine. Let $m \ge 1$ with $mD'$ Cartier. Since $g$ is small and $g_* D' = D_Z$, Lemma 8.1.4 for $g$ and for $f$ gives $g_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(kmD') = \mathcal{O}_Z(kmD_Z) = f_* \mathcal{O}_X(kmD)$ for every $k \ge 0$, so $R(X'/Z, mD') = R^{(m)}$. The line bundle $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(mD')$ is ample on $X'$, which is projective over the affine $Z$, so its section ring is finitely generated and its Proj is $X'$ (Lemma P7.6.20 and its proof, over the base $A$ in place of $k$, or Laz04, Section 2.1). So $R^{(m)}$ is finitely generated, $R$ is finitely generated by part 2 of Lemma 8.1.5, and $X' \cong \operatorname{Proj} R^{(m)} = \operatorname{Proj} R = X^+$ over $Z$. Two isomorphisms $X' \to X^+$ over $Z$ agree on the dense open set over which $g$ and $f^+$ are isomorphisms; since $X'$ is reduced and $X^+$ is separated, they are equal.

*Theorem 8.1.6, part 3.* If $R(X/Z, D)$ is finitely generated, part 1 gives a small $f^+$ with $D^+$ $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $Z$: a $D$-flip. If a $D$-flip $X'$ exists, part 2 shows that $R(X/Z, D)$ is finitely generated and $X' \cong X^+$ by a unique isomorphism over $Z$. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ apply this to $rD$; by part 2 of Lemma 8.1.5 the choice of $r$ does not matter.

*Corollary 8.1.7.* Part 1: finite generation is local on $Z$ by Definition 8.1.3, and the flips over $U_i$ and $U_j$ restrict to $D$-flips over $U_i \cap U_j$, which are identified by the unique isomorphism of part 3 of Theorem 8.1.6. These identifications satisfy the cocycle condition by uniqueness, so the flips glue to a $D$-flip over $Z$; the converse is restriction. Part 2: suppose $X^+$ is the $D_2$-flip. Let $r \ge 1$ with $r D_1 - rc D_2 - f^* (rL) = \operatorname{div} \psi$ for a rational function $\psi$. Push forward to $Z$ and take strict transforms to $X^+$; both operations commute with $\operatorname{div} \psi$, since $f$ and $f^+$ are small, and the strict transform of $L$ is $f^{+*} L$, since $f^{+*} L$ and $f^{+\,-1}_* L$ differ by an $f^+$-exceptional divisor, which is $0$. So $D_1^+ \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} c D_2^+ + f^{+*} L$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $Z$, and $X^+$ is a $D_1$-flip. The argument is symmetric.

### Exercises

1. (A) In Example 8.1.10, show that the elements $x_1 t, x_2 t, x_3 t$ generate $R(Z/Z, D_Z)$ over $k[x]^{\mu_2}$, and that $\operatorname{Proj}$ of this algebra is $Z$ by computing the chart $D_+(x_1 t)$.
2. (A) Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a small contraction and $D$ a Cartier divisor on $X$ with $D = f^* D_Z$ for a Cartier divisor $D_Z$ on $Z$. Compute $R(X/Z, D)$ and its Proj, and explain why this does not contradict Theorem 8.1.6.
3. (A) Show that the relative algebra of $D$ and of $D + \operatorname{div} \varphi$ are isomorphic as graded $\mathcal{O}_Z$-algebras for every rational function $\varphi$, and that the isomorphism induces the identity on Proj.
4. (B) Let $R = A[x, y]$ with $\deg x = 2$ and $\deg y = 3$. Find the least $d$ with $R_{kd} = (R_d)^k$ for every $k \ge 1$, and compare it with the bound $d = re$ of the proof of part 3 of Lemma 8.1.5.
5. (B) Let $Z$ be the cone $xy = zw$ in $\mathbb{A}^4$ and $D_Z$ the plane $x = z = 0$. Show that $\mathcal{O}_Z(D_Z)$ is generated by $1$ and $w/x = y/z$, compute $R(Z/Z, D_Z)$, and identify its Proj with the blow-up of $Z$ along the plane $x = w = 0$.
6. (C) Give an example of a normal affine variety $Z$ and a Weil divisor $D_Z$ for which $\bigoplus_m \mathcal{O}_Z(mD_Z)$ is not finitely generated. (Hint: take the cone over an elliptic curve $E$ embedded by a divisor of degree $3$, and $D_Z$ the cone over a divisor of degree $0$ that is not torsion.)

### Notes and history

The description of the flip as the relative Proj of the canonical algebra goes back to Reid's work on canonical 3-folds (Rei83b) and is KM98, Lemma 6.2, and Cor07, Chapter 2. It turns the existence of flips into a finite generation problem, which is how both Shokurov's method in Chapter 3 and the proof of the existence of flips in all dimensions by Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan approach it. Exercise 6 is the standard example of a non finitely generated divisorial algebra; on a klt variety the algebra of a Weil divisor is finitely generated, by the existence of $\mathbb{Q}$-factorializations, which needs the full minimal model program.
