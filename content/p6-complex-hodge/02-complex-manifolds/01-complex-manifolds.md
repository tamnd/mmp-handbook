## Complex manifolds

### Statement

Chapter 1 studied holomorphic functions on open sets of $\mathbb{C}^n$. A complex manifold is a space glued from such open sets by holomorphic maps, so that every local notion of Chapter 1 that is invariant under biholomorphisms makes sense on it. Throughout, $z_j = x_j + i y_j$ are the coordinates of $\mathbb{C}^n$, and a manifold is Hausdorff and second countable.

**Definition P6.2.1 (complex manifolds and holomorphic maps).** Let $X$ be a Hausdorff, second countable topological space and $n \geq 0$. A *holomorphic atlas* of dimension $n$ on $X$ is a family of homeomorphisms $\varphi_i \colon U_i \to \varphi_i(U_i)$ from open sets $U_i$ covering $X$ onto open sets of $\mathbb{C}^n$ such that every transition map
$$\varphi_j \circ \varphi_i^{-1} \colon \varphi_i(U_i \cap U_j) \to \varphi_j(U_i \cap U_j)$$
is holomorphic in the sense of Definition P6.1.9. Two atlases are equivalent if their union is an atlas; a *complex structure* is an equivalence class, and a *complex manifold of dimension $n$* is a space with a complex structure. Its charts are the members of the maximal atlas, and the components of a chart are *local holomorphic coordinates*. A function $f$ on an open set $U \subseteq X$ is *holomorphic* if $f \circ \varphi_i^{-1}$ is holomorphic on $\varphi_i(U \cap U_i)$ for every chart; the holomorphic functions form a sheaf of rings $\mathcal{O}_X$, the *structure sheaf*. A continuous map $f \colon X \to Y$ of complex manifolds is *holomorphic* if $\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is holomorphic, where defined, for all charts $\varphi$ of $X$ and $\psi$ of $Y$; a *biholomorphism* is a holomorphic bijection whose inverse is holomorphic. A complex manifold of dimension 1 is a *Riemann surface*. The manifold need not be connected.

Since holomorphic maps are smooth (Corollary P6.1.5), a complex manifold of dimension $n$ is in particular a smooth manifold of real dimension $2n$, and holomorphic maps are smooth. The transition maps of a holomorphic atlas have $\mathbb{C}$-linear derivatives, so their real Jacobian determinants $|\det_{\mathbb{C}} D(\varphi_j \circ \varphi_i^{-1})|^2$ are positive: a complex manifold carries a canonical orientation.

**Example P6.2.2 (projective space).** Let $\mathbb{P}^n$ be the set of complex lines through $0$ in $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$, with the quotient topology from $\pi \colon \mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{P}^n$, $Z \mapsto [Z] = [Z_0 : \dots : Z_n]$. For $0 \leq i \leq n$ put $U_i = \{Z_i \neq 0\}$ and
$$\varphi_i \colon U_i \to \mathbb{C}^n, \qquad [Z] \mapsto \Big( \frac{Z_0}{Z_i}, \dots, \widehat{\frac{Z_i}{Z_i}}, \dots, \frac{Z_n}{Z_i} \Big),$$
the hat meaning omission. Then $\mathbb{P}^n$ is a compact connected complex manifold of dimension $n$ with the atlas $(U_i, \varphi_i)$, and $\pi$ is holomorphic. For $n = 1$, with $t = Z_1/Z_0$ on $U_0$ and $s = Z_0/Z_1$ on $U_1$, the transition map is $s = 1/t$ on $\mathbb{C}^*$, and $\mathbb{P}^1$ is the Riemann sphere $\mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$.

The verification is short. The map $\pi$ is open, because the saturation $\bigcup_{c \in \mathbb{C}^*} cW$ of an open set $W$ is open. The map $\varphi_i$ is a bijection onto $\mathbb{C}^n$ with continuous inverse $w \mapsto \pi(w_0, \dots, 1, \dots, w_n)$ (the $1$ in place $i$), and $\varphi_i$ is continuous because $\varphi_i \circ \pi$ is continuous on the open set $\pi^{-1}(U_i)$ and $\pi$ is an open surjection. On $\varphi_i(U_i \cap U_j) = \{w_j \neq 0\}$ the transition map $\varphi_j \circ \varphi_i^{-1}$ divides all coordinates by $w_j$ and replaces the coordinate $w_j$ by $1/w_j$, so it is holomorphic. For Hausdorffness, take two points $p \neq q$ of $\mathbb{P}^n$; the linear forms on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ vanishing on the line $p$ form a hyperplane of the dual space, and so do those vanishing on $q$, so some linear form $\ell$ vanishes on neither. After a linear change of coordinates $\ell = Z_0$, which induces a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{P}^n$ carrying charts to charts, both points lie in $U_0 \cong \mathbb{C}^n$, which is Hausdorff and open. The space is second countable as a finite union of open sets homeomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^n$, and compact and connected as the image of the unit sphere $S^{2n+1} \subseteq \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$. Finally, $\varphi_i \circ \pi$ is the map $Z \mapsto (Z_a/Z_i)_{a \neq i}$ on $\{Z_i \neq 0\}$, which is holomorphic.

**Example P6.2.3 (complex tori).** Let $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ be a *lattice*, the subgroup $\mathbb{Z}\lambda_1 + \dots + \mathbb{Z}\lambda_{2n}$ generated by an $\mathbb{R}$-basis $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_{2n}$ of $\mathbb{C}^n = \mathbb{R}^{2n}$. The quotient group $X = \mathbb{C}^n/\Lambda$ with the quotient topology is a compact connected complex manifold of dimension $n$, a *complex torus*, and the projection $\pi \colon \mathbb{C}^n \to X$ is holomorphic and a local biholomorphism. As a smooth manifold $X \cong \mathbb{R}^{2n}/\mathbb{Z}^{2n} = (S^1)^{2n}$. For $n = 1$ and $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z}\tau$ with $\operatorname{Im} \tau > 0$ the torus $E_\tau = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ is an *elliptic curve*.

Here is the verification. Let $\delta > 0$ be the minimum of $|\lambda|$ over $\lambda \in \Lambda \setminus \{0\}$, which exists because $\Lambda$ is discrete. The map $\pi$ is open, as the saturation of $W$ is $\bigcup_{\lambda} (W + \lambda)$. On every ball $B$ of radius $\delta/2$ the map $\pi$ is injective, so $\pi|_B$ is a homeomorphism onto an open set; take its inverses as charts. If two such charts $B$, $B'$ overlap, then on each connected component of $\pi(B) \cap \pi(B')$ the transition map is $z \mapsto z + \lambda$ for a fixed $\lambda \in \Lambda$, because it is continuous with values in the discrete set $z + \Lambda$; so transitions are translations, hence holomorphic. If $x - y \notin \Lambda$, the distance $\epsilon$ from $x$ to the closed set $y + \Lambda$ is positive, and the balls of radius $\epsilon/2$ about $x$ and $y$ have disjoint images: $X$ is Hausdorff. The image of the parallelotope $\{\sum t_k \lambda_k : 0 \leq t_k \leq 1\}$ is all of $X$, so $X$ is compact and connected, and the real linear isomorphism $\mathbb{R}^{2n} \to \mathbb{C}^n$, $t \mapsto \sum t_k \lambda_k$, induces the diffeomorphism with $\mathbb{R}^{2n}/\mathbb{Z}^{2n}$.

**Example P6.2.4 (Hopf surfaces).** Let $\Gamma \cong \mathbb{Z}$ be the group of biholomorphisms $z \mapsto 2^m z$ of $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. The quotient $X = (\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\})/\Gamma$ is a compact connected complex manifold of dimension $2$, the *Hopf surface*, diffeomorphic to $S^1 \times S^3$. Its Betti numbers $b_k = \dim H^k_{dR}(X, \mathbb{C})$ (Definition P6.2.22) are
$$b_0 = 1, \quad b_1 = 1, \quad b_2 = 0, \quad b_3 = 1, \quad b_4 = 1.$$

The map $\Phi \colon \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{R} \times S^3$, $z \mapsto (\log_2 |z|, z/|z|)$, is a diffeomorphism with inverse $(t, u) \mapsto 2^t u$, and it carries $z \mapsto 2^m z$ to $(t, u) \mapsto (t + m, u)$. So $X \cong (\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}) \times S^3$, which is compact, connected, Hausdorff and second countable, and the projection $\pi \colon \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\} \to X$ is a covering map. Take as charts the inverses of $\pi|_W$ for open sets $W$ on which $\pi$ is injective, for example open subsets of shells $\{r < |z| < 2r\}$. As for tori, on each component of an overlap the transition map is $z \mapsto 2^m z$ for one $m$, which is holomorphic, and the smooth structure underlying this complex structure is the one of $S^1 \times S^3$ because $\Phi$ is a diffeomorphism. The de Rham cohomology of $S^1$ is $\mathbb{C}$ in degrees $0$ and $1$, that of $S^3$ is $\mathbb{C}$ in degrees $0$ and $3$, and the Künneth formula for de Rham cohomology gives $H^k_{dR}(S^1 \times S^3) = \bigoplus_{a + b = k} H^a_{dR}(S^1) \otimes H^b_{dR}(S^3)$; both facts are cited as black boxes from BT82, Chapter I (the cohomology of spheres from the Mayer-Vietoris sequence, and the Künneth formula). This gives the Betti numbers above. Example P6.4.19 shows that $b_1 = 1$ prevents $X$ from carrying a Kähler metric.

**Definition P6.2.5 (complex submanifolds).** Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$ and $0 \leq k \leq n$. A subset $Y \subseteq X$ is a *complex submanifold* of dimension $k$, or of codimension $n - k$, if every $y \in Y$ lies in a chart $(U, \varphi)$ of $X$ with
$$\varphi(U \cap Y) = \varphi(U) \cap (\mathbb{C}^k \times \{0\}).$$
Such charts are *adapted* to $Y$. The restrictions of the first $k$ coordinates of adapted charts form a holomorphic atlas on $Y$ with the subspace topology, so $Y$ is a complex manifold and the inclusion is holomorphic. A *closed complex submanifold* is one that is a closed subset. A complex submanifold of codimension $1$ is a *smooth hypersurface*.

**Proposition P6.2.6 (submanifolds as zero sets).** Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$.

1. Let $U \subseteq X$ be open, $f = (f_1, \dots, f_c) \colon U \to \mathbb{C}^c$ holomorphic and $Y = f^{-1}(0)$. Suppose that $0$ is a *regular value*: at every $y \in Y$ the holomorphic Jacobian $(\partial f_a/\partial z_b)(y)$ in local coordinates has rank $c$. Then $Y$ is a closed complex submanifold of $U$ of dimension $n - c$, and $T_y Y = \ker df_y$ for $y \in Y$.
2. Let $F \in \mathbb{C}[Z_0, \dots, Z_n]$ be homogeneous of degree $d \geq 1$ such that $\partial F/\partial Z_0, \dots, \partial F/\partial Z_n$ have no common zero in $\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$. Then $V(F) = \{[Z] : F(Z) = 0\}$ is a closed complex submanifold of $\mathbb{P}^n$ of dimension $n - 1$, a *smooth projective hypersurface of degree $d$*. In the chart $U_i$ it is the zero set of $f_i = F(Z)/Z_i^d$, whose differential vanishes nowhere on $V(F) \cap U_i$.

**Proposition P6.2.7 (holomorphic functions on compact manifolds are constant).** Let $X$ be a compact connected complex manifold. Then every holomorphic function on $X$ is constant: $\mathcal{O}_X(X) = \mathbb{C}$.

**Corollary P6.2.8 (compact submanifolds of $\mathbb{C}^m$).** Every holomorphic map from a compact connected complex manifold to $\mathbb{C}^m$ is constant. A compact complex submanifold of $\mathbb{C}^m$ is a finite set of points. In particular no compact complex manifold of positive dimension, such as $\mathbb{P}^n$, a complex torus or the Hopf surface, is biholomorphic to a submanifold of some $\mathbb{C}^m$.

**Definition P6.2.9 (tangent bundles and almost complex structure).** Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$ and $T^{\mathbb{R}}_X$ the tangent bundle of the underlying smooth manifold. In local holomorphic coordinates $z_j = x_j + i y_j$ define $J \colon T^{\mathbb{R}}_X \to T^{\mathbb{R}}_X$ by
$$J \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y_j}, \qquad J \frac{\partial}{\partial y_j} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}.$$
This is independent of the coordinates, satisfies $J^2 = -1$, and is the *almost complex structure* of $X$. Extend $J$ complex linearly to the complexified tangent bundle $T^{\mathbb{R}}_X \otimes \mathbb{C}$, and let $T^{1,0}_X$ and $T^{0,1}_X$ be its eigenbundles for the eigenvalues $i$ and $-i$, so that
$$T^{\mathbb{R}}_X \otimes \mathbb{C} = T^{1,0}_X \oplus T^{0,1}_X, \qquad T^{0,1}_X = \overline{T^{1,0}_X},$$
with local frames $\partial/\partial z_j = \frac{1}{2}(\partial/\partial x_j - i \partial/\partial y_j)$ and $\partial/\partial \bar z_j = \frac{1}{2}(\partial/\partial x_j + i \partial/\partial y_j)$. The *holomorphic tangent bundle* $T_X$ is $T^{1,0}_X$ with the local frames $\partial/\partial z_1, \dots, \partial/\partial z_n$; under a holomorphic change of coordinates $w = w(z)$ these frames change by the holomorphic Jacobian matrix $(\partial w_a/\partial z_b)$, so $T_X$ is a holomorphic vector bundle in the sense of Definition P6.2.11. The map $v \mapsto \frac{1}{2}(v - iJv)$ is an isomorphism of real vector bundles $(T^{\mathbb{R}}_X, J) \to T^{1,0}_X$ that carries $J$ to multiplication by $i$ and $\partial/\partial x_j$ to $\partial/\partial z_j$.

**Proposition P6.2.10 (forms of type $(p,q)$ on a complex manifold).** Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$ and $\mathcal{A}^k_X$ the sheaf of smooth complex valued $k$-forms.

1. There is a decomposition $\mathcal{A}^k_X = \bigoplus_{p + q = k} \mathcal{A}^{p,q}_X$, where a local section of $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}_X$ is a form that, in some and then in every system of local holomorphic coordinates, is $\sum_{|I| = p, |J| = q} \alpha_{IJ} \, dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J$ with smooth $\alpha_{IJ}$. Pointwise, $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}_X$ is the bundle $\Lambda^p (T^{1,0}_X)^* \otimes \Lambda^q (T^{0,1}_X)^*$.
2. Let $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$ be the components of $d$ of type $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Then $d = \partial + \bar\partial$, $\partial^2 = 0$, $\bar\partial^2 = 0$, $\partial \bar\partial = -\bar\partial \partial$, both satisfy the Leibniz rule, and in local coordinates they are the operators of Proposition P6.1.33.
3. Holomorphic maps pull forms of type $(p,q)$ back to forms of type $(p,q)$ and commute with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$. Complex conjugation maps $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}_X$ to $\mathcal{A}^{q,p}_X$, and $\overline{\partial \alpha} = \bar\partial \bar\alpha$.
4. A $(p,0)$-form with $\bar\partial \alpha = 0$ is a form $\sum_{|I| = p} \alpha_I \, dz_I$ with holomorphic coefficients, a *holomorphic $p$-form*. These form the sheaf $\Omega^p_X$, with $\Omega^0_X = \mathcal{O}_X$, and on holomorphic $p$-forms $d = \partial$.

### Proof in words

The examples are verified by writing down charts and observing that their transition maps are rational maps or translations. Proposition P6.2.6 is the implicit function theorem of Chapter 1 transported by charts, and the Euler identity reduces the projective case to the affine one. Proposition P6.2.7 is the maximum principle: $|f|$ attains its maximum on a compact space, and the set where $f$ equals its value at a maximum is open by the local maximum principle and closed by continuity. Applied to coordinate functions this gives Corollary P6.2.8. Definition P6.2.9 and Proposition P6.2.10 rest on one fact: the derivative of a holomorphic map is complex linear, so it commutes with $J$, and it sends $dz_j$ to combinations of $dw_a$ with no $d\bar w_a$.

### Examples

Two lines through the origin. The zero set $Y = \{z_1 z_2 = 0\} \subseteq \mathbb{C}^2$ fails the hypothesis of part 1 of Proposition P6.2.6 at $0$, where $d(z_1 z_2) = z_2 \, dz_1 + z_1 \, dz_2$ vanishes, and it is not a complex submanifold near $0$. Indeed, a complex submanifold of dimension $1$ near $0$ has arbitrarily small neighbourhoods of $0$ homeomorphic to a disc, which stay connected when $0$ is removed, and $Y$ is not a submanifold of dimension $0$ or $2$ near $0$ since it is neither discrete nor open. But every neighbourhood $W$ of $0$ in $Y$ meets both lines $L_1 = \{z_2 = 0\}$ and $L_2 = \{z_1 = 0\}$ away from $0$, and $W \setminus \{0\}$ is the disjoint union of the two relatively open sets $W \cap (L_1 \setminus 0)$ and $W \cap (L_2 \setminus 0)$, so it is disconnected. The same argument applies to the conic $V(Z_1 Z_2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ at $[1:0:0]$, where all three partial derivatives $0$, $Z_2$, $Z_1$ of $Z_1 Z_2$ vanish.

The Fermat hypersurfaces. For $F = Z_0^d + \dots + Z_n^d$ with $d \geq 1$ the partial derivatives are $d Z_i^{d-1}$, which vanish simultaneously only at $Z = 0$. So $V(F) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ is a smooth hypersurface by part 2 of Proposition P6.2.6. For $n = 2$, $d = 3$ this is a smooth plane cubic, and for $n = 3$, $d = 4$ a smooth quartic surface. In the chart $U_0$ the hypersurface is $\{1 + w_1^d + \dots + w_n^d = 0\}$, whose differential $d \sum_j w_j^{d-1} dw_j$ is zero only at $w = 0$, which is not on it.

Compactness is needed in Proposition P6.2.7. The coordinate functions on $\mathbb{C}^n$ are nonconstant, and so is $z_1$ on $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, the universal cover of the Hopf surface; it does not descend to the quotient because $z_1(2z) = 2 z_1(z)$. The function $1/z$ on the punctured sphere $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0\}$ is holomorphic and nonconstant, so the conclusion fails as soon as one point is removed from a compact Riemann surface. Connectedness is also needed: on the disjoint union of two copies of $\mathbb{P}^1$ the function equal to $0$ on one copy and $1$ on the other is holomorphic.

A noncompact quotient. If $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{C}$, a discrete subgroup of rank $1$ only, the construction of Example P6.2.3 still gives a complex manifold, and $e^{2\pi i z}$ identifies $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}$ biholomorphically with $\mathbb{C}^*$. It is not compact, and $e^{2\pi i z}$ itself is a nonconstant holomorphic function on it.

Types of forms on $\mathbb{P}^1$. On $U_0 \cap U_1 = \mathbb{C}^*$ the coordinates are related by $s = 1/t$, so $ds = -t^{-2} \, dt$ and $d\bar s = -\bar t^{-2} \, d\bar t$: the change of coordinates preserves the type, as part 1 of Proposition P6.2.10 asserts. With the convention $dt \wedge d\bar t = -2i \, dx \wedge dy$ for $t = x + iy$, the form $\frac{i}{2} \, dt \wedge d\bar t = dx \wedge dy$ is the area form of the plane, and $\frac{i}{2} \, ds \wedge d\bar s = \frac{i}{2} |t|^{-4} \, dt \wedge d\bar t$, so $\frac{i}{2} \, dt \wedge d\bar t = \frac{i}{2} |s|^{-4} \, ds \wedge d\bar s$ does not extend over $s = 0$, while $\frac{i}{2\pi} (1 + |t|^2)^{-2} \, dt \wedge d\bar t$ is invariant under $t \mapsto 1/t$ and extends.

### Proof

Proof of Proposition P6.2.6. For part 1, let $y \in Y$ and let $(V, \psi)$ be a chart of $X$ around $y$ with $V \subseteq U$ and $\psi(y) = 0$; write $g = f \circ \psi^{-1}$, a holomorphic map on the open set $\psi(V) \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$. After renumbering the coordinates the $c \times c$ matrix $(\partial g_a/\partial z_{n - c + b}(0))_{a, b}$ is invertible. The holomorphic map $h(z) = (z_1, \dots, z_{n-c}, g_1(z), \dots, g_c(z))$ has invertible holomorphic Jacobian at $0$, so by the holomorphic inverse function theorem (Theorem P6.1.10) it restricts to a biholomorphism from an open neighbourhood $W$ of $0$ onto an open set $h(W)$. Then $\varphi = h \circ \psi$ is a chart of $X$ on $\psi^{-1}(W)$ (its transition maps with other charts are composites of holomorphic maps), and $\varphi(Y \cap \psi^{-1}(W)) = h(W) \cap \{w_{n-c+1} = \dots = w_n = 0\}$, a chart adapted to $Y$ after reordering. So $Y$ is a complex submanifold of dimension $n - c$; it is closed in $U$ as the zero set of a continuous map. In the chart $\varphi$ the last $c$ coordinates are $f_1, \dots, f_c$, so $T_y Y$ is the span of the first $n - c$ coordinate vectors, which is $\ker df_y$. For part 2, the set $V(F)$ is well defined and closed, since $F(cZ) = c^d F(Z)$ and $F$ is continuous. On $U_i$, in the coordinates $w_a = Z_a/Z_i$ for $a \neq i$, the function $f_i([Z]) = F(Z)/Z_i^d$ is the polynomial $F(w)$ obtained by putting $Z_i = 1$, and $V(F) \cap U_i = f_i^{-1}(0)$. Let $[Z] \in V(F) \cap U_i$ with $Z_i = 1$. Then $\partial f_i/\partial w_a = (\partial F/\partial Z_a)(Z)$ for $a \neq i$. If all of these vanished, the Euler identity $\sum_a Z_a \, \partial F/\partial Z_a = d F$, valid for homogeneous $F$ of degree $d$, would give $\partial F/\partial Z_i(Z) = d F(Z) - \sum_{a \neq i} Z_a \partial F/\partial Z_a(Z) = 0$, so all partial derivatives of $F$ would vanish at $Z \neq 0$, against the hypothesis. So $0$ is a regular value of $f_i$ on $U_i$, and part 1 applies on each $U_i$; being a submanifold is a local property and $V(F)$ is closed.

Proof of Proposition P6.2.7. Let $f \in \mathcal{O}_X(X)$. Since $X$ is compact, $|f|$ attains its maximum at some $p \in X$. Let $S = \{x \in X : f(x) = f(p)\}$, a closed set containing $p$. Let $x \in S$ and let $(U, \varphi)$ be a chart around $x$ with $\varphi(U)$ a ball. Then $f \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is holomorphic on the connected open set $\varphi(U)$ and $|f \circ \varphi^{-1}|$ attains its maximum at the interior point $\varphi(x)$, because $|f(x)| = |f(p)|$ is the maximum of $|f|$ on all of $X$. By the maximum principle (Theorem P6.1.6) $f \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is constant, so $f = f(x) = f(p)$ on $U$, and $U \subseteq S$. So $S$ is open, closed and nonempty, and $S = X$ since $X$ is connected.

Proof of Corollary P6.2.8. If $f = (f_1, \dots, f_m) \colon X \to \mathbb{C}^m$ is holomorphic and $X$ is compact and connected, each $f_j$ is a holomorphic function on $X$, constant by Proposition P6.2.7, so $f$ is constant. Let $Y \subseteq \mathbb{C}^m$ be a compact complex submanifold. Its connected components are open in $Y$, because $Y$ is locally connected, hence also closed and so compact. The inclusion of a component $Y'$ is a holomorphic map $Y' \to \mathbb{C}^m$, hence constant, so $Y'$ is a point. Then the points of $Y$ are open and $Y$ is compact, so $Y$ is finite. A biholomorphism of a compact manifold $X$ of positive dimension onto a submanifold of $\mathbb{C}^m$ would make some component of $X$ a point, which is impossible.

Proof of the claims in Definition P6.2.9. Let $w = w(z)$ be a holomorphic change of coordinates with $w_a = u_a + i v_a$. By the Cauchy-Riemann equations (Corollary P6.1.5), $\partial u_a/\partial x_b = \partial v_a/\partial y_b$ and $\partial u_a/\partial y_b = -\partial v_a/\partial x_b$, which say exactly that the real Jacobian matrix of $w$ commutes with the matrix of $J$ in the bases $(\partial/\partial x, \partial/\partial y)$ and $(\partial/\partial u, \partial/\partial v)$. So the endomorphisms $J$ defined in the two coordinate systems agree, and $J^2 = -1$ is clear from the formula. On $T^{\mathbb{R}}_X \otimes \mathbb{C}$ one computes $J(\partial/\partial z_j) = \frac{1}{2}(\partial/\partial y_j + i \partial/\partial x_j) = i \, \partial/\partial z_j$ and $J(\partial/\partial \bar z_j) = -i \, \partial/\partial \bar z_j$; these $2n$ vectors form a basis of each fibre, which gives the decomposition, and conjugation exchanges the two eigenbundles because $J$ is real. The chain rule gives $\partial/\partial z_b = \sum_a (\partial w_a/\partial z_b) \partial/\partial w_a + \sum_a (\partial \bar w_a/\partial z_b) \partial/\partial \bar w_a$, and the second sum vanishes because $\bar w_a$ is antiholomorphic, $\partial \bar w_a/\partial z_b = \overline{\partial w_a/\partial \bar z_b} = 0$. So the frames of $T^{1,0}_X$ change by the holomorphic matrix $(\partial w_a/\partial z_b)$. Finally $v \mapsto \frac{1}{2}(v - iJv)$ is real linear and injective, its image lies in the $i$-eigenspace since $J(v - iJv) = Jv + iv = i(v - iJv)$, the ranks agree, it carries $Jv$ to $\frac{1}{2}(Jv + iv) = i \cdot \frac{1}{2}(v - iJv)$, and it carries $\partial/\partial x_j$ to $\frac{1}{2}(\partial/\partial x_j - i \partial/\partial y_j) = \partial/\partial z_j$.

Proof of Proposition P6.2.10. The complexified cotangent bundle is dual to $T^{\mathbb{R}}_X \otimes \mathbb{C}$, and $dz_j$, $d\bar z_j$ form the basis dual to $\partial/\partial z_j$, $\partial/\partial \bar z_j$. By the proof of the claims in Definition P6.2.9, the annihilator of $T^{0,1}_X$ is $(T^{1,0}_X)^*$, spanned by $dz_1, \dots, dz_n$, and does not depend on coordinates; in another chart $dw_a = \sum_b (\partial w_a/\partial z_b) dz_b$ indeed has no $d\bar z$ terms. So the subbundle of $\Lambda^k (T^{\mathbb{R}}_X \otimes \mathbb{C})^*$ spanned by the $dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J$ with $|I| = p$, $|J| = q$ is $\Lambda^p (T^{1,0}_X)^* \otimes \Lambda^q (T^{0,1}_X)^*$, independent of coordinates, and these subbundles give the direct sum decomposition of part 1. Part 2: the operators $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$ are defined intrinsically as components of $d$, and in a chart they are given by the formulas of Proposition P6.1.33, which shows that $d$ maps $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}$ into $\mathcal{A}^{p+1,q} \oplus \mathcal{A}^{p,q+1}$, so that $d = \partial + \bar\partial$. The identities $\partial^2 = \bar\partial^2 = 0$, $\partial\bar\partial = -\bar\partial\partial$ and the Leibniz rules are local, and they hold in every chart by Proposition P6.1.33. Part 3: if $F$ is holomorphic, $F^* dw_a = d(w_a \circ F)$ is a combination of the $dz_b$ by the same chain rule computation, and $F^* d\bar w_a$ is its conjugate; so $F^*$ preserves types, and since $F^*$ commutes with $d$, comparing components of type $(p+1, q)$ and $(p, q+1)$ shows it commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$. Conjugation exchanges $dz_j$ and $d\bar z_j$ and commutes with $d$ because $d$ is real, which gives the last claims. Part 4: for $\alpha = \sum_I \alpha_I \, dz_I$, $\bar\partial \alpha = \sum_{I, j} (\partial \alpha_I/\partial \bar z_j) \, d\bar z_j \wedge dz_I$, which vanishes if and only if each $\alpha_I$ satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations, that is, is holomorphic by Corollary P6.1.5. For such $\alpha$, $d\alpha = \partial \alpha + \bar\partial \alpha = \partial \alpha$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Write down the transition maps $\varphi_j \circ \varphi_i^{-1}$ of Example P6.2.2 for $\mathbb{P}^2$ and $(i, j) = (0, 1)$, and check the cocycle condition $\varphi_0 \circ \varphi_2^{-1} = (\varphi_0 \circ \varphi_1^{-1}) \circ (\varphi_1 \circ \varphi_2^{-1})$ on $\varphi_2(U_0 \cap U_1 \cap U_2)$.
2. (A) Show that the map $\mathbb{P}^1 \to S^2 \subseteq \mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{R}$, $[Z_0 : Z_1] \mapsto (2 Z_1 \bar Z_0, |Z_0|^2 - |Z_1|^2)/(|Z_0|^2 + |Z_1|^2)$, is a diffeomorphism.
3. (B) Show that $\{(z, w) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : w^2 = z^3 - z\}$ is a closed complex submanifold of $\mathbb{C}^2$ and that its closure in $\mathbb{P}^2$, the curve $V(Z_0 Z_2^2 - Z_1^3 + Z_0^2 Z_1)$, is a smooth projective hypersurface. Show that $V(Z_0 Z_2^2 - Z_1^3)$ is not: find the point where all partial derivatives vanish.
4. (B) Let $X$ be a connected complex manifold and $f \in \mathcal{O}_X(X)$ such that $|f|$ has a local maximum. Show that $f$ is constant. Deduce Proposition P6.2.7 again. Show that every holomorphic map from $\mathbb{P}^1$ to a complex torus is constant, by lifting it to $\mathbb{C}^n$ through the covering map, which is possible because $\mathbb{P}^1$ is simply connected.
5. (B) Let $J$ be the almost complex structure of $\mathbb{C}^n$. Show that a $C^1$ map $f$ between open sets of $\mathbb{C}^n$ and $\mathbb{C}^m$ is holomorphic if and only if $df \circ J = J \circ df$.
6. (C) Let $\Lambda, \Lambda' \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ be lattices. Show that every holomorphic map $\mathbb{C}^n/\Lambda \to \mathbb{C}^n/\Lambda'$ sending $0$ to $0$ lifts to a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $A$ of $\mathbb{C}^n$ with $A\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda'$. (Lift to the universal covers, and show that the partial derivatives of the lift are $\Lambda$-periodic, hence bounded and constant by Liouville's theorem, which follows from Theorem P6.1.4.) Deduce that $E_\tau \cong E_{\tau'}$ if and only if $\tau' = (a\tau + b)/(c\tau + d)$ with $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$.

### Lean

Mathlib has smooth manifolds modelled on normed spaces, which include manifolds modelled on $\mathbb{C}^n$ with holomorphic transition maps, complex differentiability in several variables, and a proof that holomorphic functions on a compact connected complex manifold are constant by the maximum modulus principle. It has projective spaces as algebraic objects but not the complex manifold structure of Example P6.2.2 in this form, and it has no decomposition of forms into types. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Riemann introduced Riemann surfaces in his 1851 thesis as multivalued domains of holomorphic functions, and Weyl gave the first definition of a Riemann surface by charts in 1913. The definition of a complex manifold of higher dimension by holomorphic atlases became standard in the 1940s, in the work of Chern, of Weil and of the Cartan seminar. The Hopf surface appears in Hopf's 1948 paper on complex manifolds as the first example of a compact complex manifold that admits no Kähler metric. The almost complex structure and the decomposition of forms into types go back to Ehresmann's work of 1947 and to Chern, who used forms of type $(p,q)$ on Hermitian manifolds in Che46; Kähler used complex coordinates on forms in Kah33. The exposition follows Huy05, Section 2.1 and Section 1.3, Voi02, Chapter 2, and GH78, Section 0.2.
