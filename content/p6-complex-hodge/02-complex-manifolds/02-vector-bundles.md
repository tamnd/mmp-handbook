## Holomorphic vector bundles

### Statement

A holomorphic vector bundle is a family of complex vector spaces parametrized holomorphically by a complex manifold. Its sheaf of holomorphic sections is a locally free $\mathcal{O}_X$-module, and the two points of view are equivalent; this section uses both, with the conventions for transition functions of Proposition P2.6.28. In this section $X$ is a complex manifold of dimension $n$.

**Definition P6.2.11 (holomorphic vector bundles).** A *holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r$* on $X$ is a complex manifold $E$ with a holomorphic map $\pi \colon E \to X$, a structure of complex vector space of dimension $r$ on each fibre $E_x = \pi^{-1}(x)$, and an open cover $(U_i)$ of $X$ with biholomorphisms $\varphi_i \colon \pi^{-1}(U_i) \to U_i \times \mathbb{C}^r$, the *trivializations*, that commute with the projections to $U_i$ and are $\mathbb{C}$-linear on each fibre. The *transition functions* are the maps $g_{ij} \colon U_i \cap U_j \to \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{C})$ defined by
$$\varphi_i \circ \varphi_j^{-1}(x, v) = (x, g_{ij}(x) v).$$
A *line bundle* is a bundle of rank $1$. A *morphism* $E \to F$ of holomorphic vector bundles on $X$ is a holomorphic map commuting with the projections and linear on fibres. A *holomorphic section* over an open $U \subseteq X$ is a holomorphic map $s \colon U \to E$ with $\pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}_U$; the holomorphic sections form a sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules $\mathcal{E}$, often denoted $E$ again, and $H^q(X, E)$ means $H^q(X, \mathcal{E})$, with $h^q(X, E) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^q(X, E)$. Smooth sections form a sheaf $\mathcal{A}_X(E)$ of $\mathcal{A}_X$-modules, and smooth $E$-valued $(p,q)$-forms form the sheaf $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}_X(E) = \mathcal{A}^{p,q}_X \otimes_{\mathcal{A}_X} \mathcal{A}_X(E)$. For a line bundle the *frame* on $U_i$ is the section $e_i = \varphi_i^{-1}(\cdot, 1)$, and
$$e_j = g_{ij} e_i, \qquad s = f_i e_i = f_j e_j \implies f_i = g_{ij} f_j \quad \text{on } U_i \cap U_j.$$

**Proposition P6.2.12 (cocycles and locally free sheaves).**

1. The transition functions of a holomorphic vector bundle are holomorphic and satisfy $g_{ii} = 1$ and $g_{ij} g_{jk} = g_{ik}$ on $U_i \cap U_j \cap U_k$.
2. Conversely, for an open cover $(U_i)$ of $X$ and holomorphic maps $g_{ij} \colon U_i \cap U_j \to \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{C})$ satisfying these identities, there is a holomorphic vector bundle with trivializations over the $U_i$ whose transition functions are the $g_{ij}$, unique up to isomorphism. Two cocycles $(g_{ij})$, $(g'_{ij})$ on the same cover give isomorphic bundles if and only if $g'_{ij} = h_i g_{ij} h_j^{-1}$ for holomorphic $h_i \colon U_i \to \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{C})$.
3. The functor $E \mapsto \mathcal{E}$ is an equivalence from the category of holomorphic vector bundles of rank $r$ on $X$ to the category of locally free $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules of rank $r$. A trivialization $\varphi_i$ of $E$ induces an isomorphism $\mathcal{E}|_{U_i} \cong \mathcal{O}_{U_i}^r$, and the transition functions of $\mathcal{E}$ for these isomorphisms in the sense of Proposition P2.6.28 are the $g_{ij}$ of Definition P6.2.11.

**Definition P6.2.13 (operations on bundles and the canonical bundle).** Let $E$ and $F$ be holomorphic vector bundles of ranks $r$ and $r'$ with transition functions $g_{ij}$ and $h_{ij}$ on a common cover. The bundles $E^*$, $E \oplus F$, $E \otimes F$, $\Lambda^k E$, $\det E = \Lambda^r E$ and $\mathrm{Hom}(E, F) = E^* \otimes F$ are defined by the cocycles $(g_{ij}^{T})^{-1}$, $g_{ij} \oplus h_{ij}$, $g_{ij} \otimes h_{ij}$, $\Lambda^k g_{ij}$, $\det g_{ij}$ and $h_{ij} \otimes (g_{ij}^T)^{-1}$. For a holomorphic map $f \colon Y \to X$, the *pullback* $f^* E$ has the cocycle $g_{ij} \circ f$ on the cover $(f^{-1} U_i)$; for a submanifold $Y$, $E|_Y$ is the pullback by the inclusion. A sequence $0 \to E' \to E \to E'' \to 0$ of morphisms is *exact* if it is exact on every fibre; then $E'$ is a *subbundle* of $E$ and $E''$ the *quotient bundle* $E/E'$. The *holomorphic cotangent bundle* is $\Omega_X = T_X^*$, with local frames $dz_1, \dots, dz_n$ dual to $\partial/\partial z_1, \dots, \partial/\partial z_n$; the bundle $\Lambda^p \Omega_X$ has as sheaf of sections the sheaf $\Omega^p_X$ of holomorphic $p$-forms of Proposition P6.2.10. The *canonical bundle* is the line bundle
$$K_X = \Lambda^n \Omega_X = \det \Omega_X,$$
with local frames $dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n$. If $z$ are coordinates on $U_i$ and $w$ on $U_j$, then $dw_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dw_n = \det(\partial w_a/\partial z_b) \, dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n$, so for these frames the transition function $g_{ij}$ of $K_X$ is the Jacobian determinant $\det(\partial w/\partial z)$. For a submanifold $Y \subseteq X$ the *normal bundle* is the quotient $N_{Y/X} = T_X|_Y / T_Y$.

**Example P6.2.14 (tori have trivial tangent bundle).** Let $X = \mathbb{C}^n/\Lambda$ be a complex torus. The charts of Example P6.2.3 differ by translations, whose Jacobian is the identity, so the vector fields $\partial/\partial z_1, \dots, \partial/\partial z_n$ are well defined on $X$ and form a global holomorphic frame of $T_X$. So $T_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X^n$, $\Omega_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X^n$ with frame $dz_1, \dots, dz_n$, $\Omega^p_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X^{\binom{n}{p}}$ with frame $(dz_I)_{|I| = p}$, and $K_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X$ with the nowhere vanishing section $dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n$. By Proposition P6.2.7 the holomorphic coefficients of a global holomorphic $p$-form are constant, so $H^0(X, \Omega^p_X) = \bigoplus_{|I| = p} \mathbb{C} \, dz_I$ has dimension $\binom{n}{p}$. On the Hopf surface of Example P6.2.4 the form $dz_1 \wedge dz_2$ does not descend, since its pullback under $z \mapsto 2z$ is $4 \, dz_1 \wedge dz_2$, while the Euler vector field $z_1 \partial/\partial z_1 + z_2 \partial/\partial z_2$ is invariant and descends to a nowhere vanishing holomorphic vector field.

**Theorem P6.2.15 (the Picard group).** The isomorphism classes of holomorphic line bundles on $X$ form an abelian group $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$ under $\otimes$, with unit the trivial bundle and inverse $L^{-1} = L^*$. The map that sends $L$ to the class of its transition functions $(g_{ij})$ is an isomorphism
$$\mathrm{Pic}(X) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*),$$
where $\mathcal{O}_X^*$ is the sheaf of nowhere vanishing holomorphic functions under multiplication. The bundles trivial on the sets of a cover $\mathcal{U}$ correspond to the subgroup $\check{H}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{O}_X^*)$. This is Theorem P2.7.28 for the ringed space $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$, with the same convention $g_{ij} = \varphi_i \circ \varphi_j^{-1}$.

**Definition P6.2.16 (the line bundles $\mathcal{O}(d)$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$).** On $\mathbb{P}^n$ with the cover $U_i = \{Z_i \neq 0\}$ of Example P6.2.2 and $d \in \mathbb{Z}$, the functions $g_{ij} = (Z_j/Z_i)^d$ are holomorphic and nowhere zero on $U_i \cap U_j$, and $g_{ij} g_{jk} = (Z_j/Z_i)^d (Z_k/Z_j)^d = g_{ik}$. The line bundle $\mathcal{O}(d) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(d)$ is the bundle with these transition functions (Proposition P6.2.12). Its frames satisfy $e_j = (Z_j/Z_i)^d e_i$, which the notation $e_i = Z_i^d$ suggests, and a section over $U$ is a family of holomorphic functions $f_i$ on $U \cap U_i$ with $f_i = (Z_j/Z_i)^d f_j$. Multiplying cocycles gives $\mathcal{O}(0) = \mathcal{O}$, $\mathcal{O}(d) \otimes \mathcal{O}(d') \cong \mathcal{O}(d + d')$ and $\mathcal{O}(d)^* \cong \mathcal{O}(-d)$. The *tautological bundle* $\{([Z], v) \in \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^{n+1} : v \in \mathbb{C} Z\}$ is a holomorphic line bundle isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}(-1)$. For a submanifold $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ write $\mathcal{O}_Y(d) = \mathcal{O}(d)|_Y$.

**Proposition P6.2.17 (sections of $\mathcal{O}(d)$).** Let $n \geq 1$ and $d \in \mathbb{Z}$. A homogeneous polynomial $P$ of degree $d \geq 0$ in $Z_0, \dots, Z_n$ defines the section $(P/Z_i^d)_i$ of $\mathcal{O}(d)$, and this gives an isomorphism
$$\mathbb{C}[Z_0, \dots, Z_n]_d \cong H^0(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(d)), \qquad h^0(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(d)) = \binom{n + d}{n}.$$
For $d < 0$, $H^0(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(d)) = 0$.

**Proposition P6.2.18 (the Euler sequence and the canonical bundle of $\mathbb{P}^n$).** There is an exact sequence of holomorphic vector bundles on $\mathbb{P}^n$
$$0 \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus (n+1)} \to T_{\mathbb{P}^n} \to 0.$$
The first map sends $h$ to $(h Z_0, \dots, h Z_n)$. For the second, a section $s_a = (f_{a,i})_i$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ over $U$ defines the function $S_a$ on $\pi^{-1}(U) \subseteq \mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$, homogeneous of degree $1$, equal to $Z_i f_{a,i}([Z])$ where $Z_i \neq 0$; the second map sends $(s_0, \dots, s_n)$ to the vector field $v$ on $U$ with $v(\pi(Z)) = d\pi_Z(\sum_a S_a(Z) \, \partial/\partial Z_a)$. In the chart $U_0$ with $w_j = Z_j/Z_0$ and the frame $e_0$ it is
$$(f_0, \dots, f_n) \mapsto \sum_{j=1}^{n} (f_j - w_j f_0) \frac{\partial}{\partial w_j}.$$
In particular $\det T_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(n+1)$ and
$$K_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(-n-1).$$

**Proposition P6.2.19 (normal bundles and adjunction).** Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle on $X$ and $s \in H^0(X, L)$ with zero set $Y = Z(s)$. Suppose that $s$ *vanishes transversally*: for every $y \in Y$ and a frame $e$ of $L$ near $y$, writing $s = f e$, the differential $df(y)$ is nonzero. This condition does not depend on the frame. Then $Y$ is a closed complex submanifold of dimension $n - 1$, the map $v \mapsto df(v) \, e$ is a well defined surjective morphism $T_X|_Y \to L|_Y$ with kernel $T_Y$, so that $N_{Y/X} \cong L|_Y$, and
$$K_Y \cong (K_X \otimes L)|_Y.$$

### Proof in words

The dictionary between bundles and sheaves is the gluing of trivial pieces by transition functions: for manifolds by gluing products $U_i \times \mathbb{C}^r$, for sheaves by Proposition P2.6.28. The Picard group is then Theorem P2.7.28. A section of $\mathcal{O}(d)$ is the same as a holomorphic function on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$, homogeneous of degree $d$; by Hartogs it extends to $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$, and an entire homogeneous function is a homogeneous polynomial. The Euler sequence says that a vector field on $\mathbb{P}^n$ lifts locally to a vector field on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$ with homogeneous coefficients of degree $1$, unique up to multiples of the radial field $\sum Z_a \partial/\partial Z_a$. Adjunction is linear algebra: the derivative of $s$ along $Y$ identifies the normal direction with $L$, and determinants are multiplicative in exact sequences.

### Examples

**Example P6.2.20 (hypersurfaces in projective space).** Let $X = V(F) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ be a smooth hypersurface of degree $d \geq 1$ and dimension $n \geq 1$, as in part 2 of Proposition P6.2.6. Then $F$ is a section of $\mathcal{O}(d)$ by Proposition P6.2.17, its zero set is $X$, and it vanishes transversally, because in the frame $e_i$ its component is $f_i = F/Z_i^d$, whose differential is nonzero on $X \cap U_i$ by Proposition P6.2.6. So Propositions P6.2.18 and P6.2.19 give
$$N_{X/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}} \cong \mathcal{O}_X(d), \qquad K_X \cong (\mathcal{O}(-n-2) \otimes \mathcal{O}(d))|_X = \mathcal{O}_X(d - n - 2).$$
For a hyperplane, $d = 1$ and $X \cong \mathbb{P}^n$, this is $K_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(-n-1)$ again, since the cocycle $(Z_j/Z_i)^e$ of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(e)$ restricts on $\{Z_0 = 0\}$, covered by the $U_i$ with $i \geq 1$, to the cocycle of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(e)$. A smooth plane cubic ($n = 1$, $d = 3$) and a smooth quartic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ ($n = 2$, $d = 4$) have trivial canonical bundle. For the smooth conic $C = V(Z_0 Z_2 - Z_1^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ the formula gives $K_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C(-1)$. The map $\nu \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to C$, $[S : T] \mapsto [S^2 : ST : T^2]$, is a biholomorphism, with inverse $[Z] \mapsto [Z_0 : Z_1]$ on $\{Z_0 \neq 0\}$ and $[Z_1 : Z_2]$ on $\{Z_2 \neq 0\}$. Since $\nu^{-1}(U_0) = \{S \neq 0\}$ and $\nu^{-1}(U_2) = \{T \neq 0\}$ cover $\mathbb{P}^1$ and $g_{02} \circ \nu = (T^2/S^2)^{e}$, the pullback $\nu^* \mathcal{O}_C(e)$ is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(2e)$. So $\nu^* K_C \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-2) \cong K_{\mathbb{P}^1}$, as it must be.

The canonical bundle of $\mathbb{P}^1$ directly. On $U_0 \cap U_1$, with $t = Z_1/Z_0$ and $s = 1/t$, the frames $dt$ and $ds$ of $K_{\mathbb{P}^1}$ satisfy $ds = -t^{-2} \, dt$, so $g_{01} = -t^{-2} = -(Z_1/Z_0)^{-2}$. With $h_0 = 1$ and $h_1 = -1$ this is $h_0 \, (Z_1/Z_0)^{-2} h_1^{-1}$, so $K_{\mathbb{P}^1} \cong \mathcal{O}(-2)$ by Proposition P6.2.12, in accordance with Proposition P6.2.18. Since $T_{\mathbb{P}^1} = K_{\mathbb{P}^1}^*$, the tangent bundle is $\mathcal{O}(2)$, and by Proposition P6.2.17 it has the $3$-dimensional space of sections spanned by $\partial/\partial t$, $t \, \partial/\partial t$, $t^2 \, \partial/\partial t$; in the coordinate $s$ these are $-s^2 \partial/\partial s$, $-s \, \partial/\partial s$, $-\partial/\partial s$.

**Example P6.2.21 (the blow-up of the origin in $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$).** Let
$$B = \{(z, \ell) \in \mathbb{C}^{n+1} \times \mathbb{P}^n : z \in \ell\}, \qquad p \colon B \to \mathbb{C}^{n+1}, \quad q \colon B \to \mathbb{P}^n$$
the two projections, $n \geq 1$. With $\ell = [Z]$ the condition is $z_a Z_b = z_b Z_a$ for all $a, b$. Over $U_i$, with affine coordinates $w_a = Z_a/Z_i$ ($w_i = 1$), it says $z_a = z_i w_a$ for all $a \neq i$, so $B \cap (\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \times U_i)$ is the graph of the holomorphic map $(z_i, w) \mapsto (z_i w_a)_{a \neq i}$, and $B$ is a closed complex submanifold of dimension $n + 1$ with coordinates $(u_i, w) = (z_i, w)$ over $U_i$. The map $q$ makes $B$ the total space of the tautological bundle, that is, of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ (Definition P6.2.16), and $u_i$ is the fibre coordinate in the frame $Z/Z_i$. The map $p$ restricts to a biholomorphism $B \setminus E \to \mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$ with inverse $z \mapsto (z, [z])$, where $E = p^{-1}(0) = \{0\} \times \mathbb{P}^n$ is the zero section of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, the *exceptional divisor*. On $B \cap q^{-1}(U_i \cap U_j)$ the coordinates are related by $u_j = z_j = u_i w_j$ and $w$ is a function of $\ell$ only, so on $E = \{u_i = 0\}$ the normal vectors $\nu_i = \partial/\partial u_i \bmod T_E$ satisfy $\nu_i = (\partial u_j/\partial u_i) \nu_j = w_j \nu_j$, that is, $\nu_j = (Z_i/Z_j) \nu_i$. So the normal bundle $N_{E/B}$ has transition functions $(Z_j/Z_i)^{-1}$ and
$$N_{E/B} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(-1).$$
For $n = 1$ this is the blow-up of the origin in $\mathbb{C}^2$: the exceptional curve $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ has normal bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, which has no nonzero sections by Proposition P6.2.17.

A section that does not vanish transversally. Let $s = Z_0^2 \in H^0(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}, \mathcal{O}(2))$, $n \geq 1$. Its zero set is the hyperplane $H = \{Z_0 = 0\} \cong \mathbb{P}^n$, a submanifold, but in the frame $e_i$, $i \geq 1$, the component is $f_i = (Z_0/Z_i)^2$, whose differential $2 (Z_0/Z_i) \, d(Z_0/Z_i)$ vanishes on $H$. The conclusion of Proposition P6.2.19 fails: the section $Z_0$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ does vanish transversally on $H$, so $N_{H/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}} \cong \mathcal{O}_H(1) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1)$, which is not isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}_H(2) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(2)$ because their spaces of sections have dimensions $n + 1$ and $\binom{n+2}{2}$ by Proposition P6.2.17. The formula of Proposition P6.2.19 with $L = \mathcal{O}(2)$ would give $K_H \cong \mathcal{O}_H(-n)$ instead of the correct $\mathcal{O}_H(-n-1)$.

### Proof

Proof of Proposition P6.2.12. Part 1: the map $\varphi_i \circ \varphi_j^{-1}$ is holomorphic, so its second component $g_{ij}(x) v$ is holomorphic in $x$ for each standard basis vector $v$, and the entries of $g_{ij}$ are holomorphic. On $U_i \cap U_j \cap U_k$, $\varphi_i \varphi_k^{-1} = (\varphi_i \varphi_j^{-1})(\varphi_j \varphi_k^{-1})$ gives $g_{ik} = g_{ij} g_{jk}$, and $g_{ii} = 1$ is clear. Part 2: on $\bigsqcup_i U_i \times \mathbb{C}^r \times \{i\}$ put $(x, v, j) \sim (x, g_{ij}(x) v, i)$ for $x \in U_i \cap U_j$. This is an equivalence relation: reflexive since $g_{ii} = 1$, symmetric since $g_{ji} = g_{ij}^{-1}$ by the cocycle identity with $k = i$, transitive by the cocycle identity. Let $E$ be the quotient and $\pi(x, v, i) = x$. The map $\iota_i \colon U_i \times \mathbb{C}^r \to E$ is injective, since $(x, v, i) \sim (x, v', i)$ forces $v = v'$, and open, since the saturation of an open $W \subseteq U_i \times \mathbb{C}^r \times \{i\}$ meets $U_j \times \mathbb{C}^r \times \{j\}$ in $\{(x, v, j) : x \in U_i \cap U_j, (x, g_{ij}(x) v) \in W\}$, which is open by continuity of $g_{ij}$. So the $\iota_i$ are open embeddings covering $E$. Two points of $E$ over distinct points of $X$ are separated by the preimages under the continuous map $\pi$ of disjoint neighbourhoods, and two points over the same $x \in U_i$ lie in the Hausdorff open set $\iota_i(U_i \times \mathbb{C}^r)$; so $E$ is Hausdorff. As $X$ is second countable, countably many $U_i$ cover $X$, and the corresponding $\iota_i(U_i \times \mathbb{C}^r)$ cover $E$, so $E$ is second countable. With $\varphi_i = \iota_i^{-1}$ composed with charts of $U_i$ as charts, the transition maps $(x, v) \mapsto (x, g_{ij}(x) v)$ are holomorphic, the vector space structures on fibres transported by the $\iota_i$ agree because $g_{ij}(x)$ is linear, and $\varphi_i \circ \varphi_j^{-1}(x, v) = (x, g_{ij}(x) v)$. If $E'$ has trivializations $\varphi'_i$ with the same transition functions, then $\varphi_i'^{-1} \circ \varphi_i = \varphi_j'^{-1} \circ \varphi_j$ on $\pi^{-1}(U_i \cap U_j)$, since both sides composed with $\varphi'_i$ give $\varphi_i$ after inserting $\varphi'_i \varphi_j'^{-1} = \varphi_i \varphi_j^{-1}$; so these maps glue to an isomorphism $E \to E'$. For the last claim, an isomorphism $\psi \colon E \to E'$ gives $\varphi'_i \psi \varphi_i^{-1}(x, v) = (x, h_i(x) v)$ with $h_i$ holomorphic and invertible, and
$$g'_{ij} = \varphi'_i \varphi_j'^{-1} = (\varphi'_i \psi \varphi_i^{-1})(\varphi_i \varphi_j^{-1})(\varphi_j \psi^{-1} \varphi_j'^{-1}) = h_i g_{ij} h_j^{-1};$$
conversely, given such $h_i$, the local maps $\varphi_i'^{-1} h_i \varphi_i$ agree on overlaps by the same identity and glue to $\psi$.

Part 3: a section $s$ of $E$ over $V \subseteq U_i$ gives $\varphi_i \circ s = (x, \sigma_i(x))$ with $\sigma_i \in \mathcal{O}(V)^r$, and $s \mapsto \sigma_i$ is an isomorphism $\mathcal{E}|_{U_i} \to \mathcal{O}_{U_i}^r$ of $\mathcal{O}$-modules, with inverse $\sigma \mapsto \varphi_i^{-1}(x, \sigma(x))$. On overlaps $\sigma_i = g_{ij} \sigma_j$, so $\mathcal{E}$ is locally free with transition functions $g_{ij}$ in the sense of Proposition P2.6.28. A morphism $u \colon E \to F$ induces $s \mapsto u \circ s$, which is $\mathcal{O}$-linear. Conversely an $\mathcal{O}$-linear map $\alpha \colon \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{F}$ is given, over a set $U_i$ on which both are trivialized, by the $r' \times r$ matrix $A_i$ of holomorphic functions whose columns are the images of the standard basis, and the maps $\varphi_i^{F,-1} \circ (x, v \mapsto A_i(x) v) \circ \varphi_i^E$ agree on overlaps because $\alpha$ is a global map, so they glue to a morphism $u$ with $u \circ s = \alpha(s)$; $u$ is unique because every point of $E$ lies on a local holomorphic section, for instance a constant one in a trivialization. So the functor is fully faithful. A locally free sheaf of rank $r$ has, by Proposition P2.6.28, a cocycle of holomorphic invertible matrices on a cover; the bundle of part 2 with this cocycle has, by the computation above, a sheaf of sections with the same cocycle, which is isomorphic to the given sheaf by the uniqueness in Proposition P2.6.28. So the functor is essentially surjective.

Proof of Theorem P6.2.15. By part 3 of Proposition P6.2.12, holomorphic line bundles up to isomorphism correspond to invertible $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules up to isomorphism. The sheaf of sections of $E \otimes F$ and the sheaf $\mathcal{E} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{F}$ are both trivialized on the $U_i$ with transition functions $g_{ij} \otimes h_{ij}$, so they are isomorphic by Proposition P2.6.28; in the same way the sheaf of sections of $E^*$ is $\mathcal{E}^\vee$, and similarly for the other operations of Definition P6.2.13. So the group of line bundles under $\otimes$ is the group $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$ of invertible sheaves of Theorem P2.6.29, and Theorem P2.7.28 gives the isomorphism with $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*)$ and the statement about $\check{H}^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{O}_X^*)$; by part 3 of Proposition P6.2.12 the transition functions used there are those of Definition P6.2.11.

Proof of the claims in Definition P6.2.16. Let $T$ be the tautological set. Over $U_i$ the condition $v \in \mathbb{C} Z$ says $v = v_i \, Z/Z_i$, so $T \cap (U_i \times \mathbb{C}^{n+1})$ is the graph of the holomorphic map $([Z], c) \mapsto c Z/Z_i$ from $U_i \times \mathbb{C}$, and $T$ is a closed complex submanifold with the trivialization $\varphi_i([Z], v) = ([Z], v_i)$ over $U_i$. Then $\varphi_i \varphi_j^{-1}([Z], c) = \varphi_i([Z], c Z/Z_j) = ([Z], c Z_i/Z_j)$, so $g_{ij} = Z_i/Z_j = (Z_j/Z_i)^{-1}$, the cocycle of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$.

Proof of Proposition P6.2.17. The section defined by $P$ is $f_i = P(Z)/Z_i^d$, a well defined holomorphic function on $U_i$ because $P$ and $Z_i^d$ are homogeneous of the same degree, and $(Z_j/Z_i)^d f_j = Z_j^d P/(Z_i^d Z_j^d) = f_i$. If this section is zero, then $P = Z_0^d f_0$ vanishes on the dense open set $\{Z_0 \neq 0\}$ of $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$, so $P = 0$: the map is injective. Conversely, let $(f_i)$ be a global section of $\mathcal{O}(d)$, $d \in \mathbb{Z}$. On $\{Z_i \neq 0\} \subseteq \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ put $G(Z) = Z_i^d f_i([Z])$. On $\{Z_i Z_j \neq 0\}$, $Z_i^d f_i = Z_i^d (Z_j/Z_i)^d f_j = Z_j^d f_j$, so $G$ is a well defined holomorphic function on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$ with $G(cZ) = c^d G(Z)$ for $c \in \mathbb{C}^*$. Since $n + 1 \geq 2$, Corollary P6.1.15 extends $G$ to a holomorphic function on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$, with a power series $G = \sum_\alpha a_\alpha Z^\alpha$ converging absolutely on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ (Theorem P6.1.4). If $d < 0$, then for fixed $Z \neq 0$ the function $c \mapsto c^d G(Z) = G(cZ)$ is bounded as $c \to 0$, which forces $G(Z) = 0$; so $G = 0$ and the section is zero. If $d \geq 0$, fix $Z$ and group the series by total degree, which is allowed by absolute convergence: $G(cZ) = \sum_{m \geq 0} c^m G_m(Z)$ with $G_m = \sum_{|\alpha| = m} a_\alpha Z^\alpha$. This power series in $c$ equals $c^d G(Z)$, so by uniqueness of power series coefficients $G_m(Z) = 0$ for $m \neq d$ and $G(Z) = G_d(Z)$. So $G$ is the homogeneous polynomial $P = G_d$ and $f_i = P/Z_i^d$. The dimension is the number $\binom{n+d}{n}$ of monomials of degree $d$ in $n + 1$ variables.

Proof of Proposition P6.2.18. First, a section $s = (f_i)$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ over $U$ defines $S(Z) = Z_i f_i([Z])$, homogeneous of degree $1$ on $\pi^{-1}(U)$, by the computation in the proof of Proposition P6.2.17, and conversely $f_i = S/Z_i$. For $c \in \mathbb{C}^*$ let $m_c(Z) = cZ$, so that $\pi \circ m_c = \pi$ and $dm_c(\partial/\partial Z_a|_Z) = c \, \partial/\partial Z_a|_{cZ}$. For $V = \sum_a S_a \partial/\partial Z_a$ this gives $dm_c(V(Z)) = \sum_a c S_a(Z) \partial/\partial Z_a|_{cZ} = V(cZ)$, hence $d\pi_{cZ}(V(cZ)) = d\pi_{cZ} dm_c (V(Z)) = d\pi_Z(V(Z))$, and $v(\pi(Z)) = d\pi_Z(V(Z))$ is well defined. In the chart $U_0$, $\pi(Z) = (Z_1/Z_0, \dots, Z_n/Z_0)$, and at the point $Z = (1, w)$ one has $d\pi(\partial/\partial Z_0) = -\sum_j w_j \partial/\partial w_j$ and $d\pi(\partial/\partial Z_j) = \partial/\partial w_j$ for $j \geq 1$. With $S_a(1, w) = f_{a,0}(w)$ this gives the displayed formula, which shows that $v$ is holomorphic and that the second map is $\mathcal{O}$-linear; by symmetry of the coordinates the same holds in every chart. In the frame $e_0$ the first map is $h \mapsto (h Z_a/Z_0)_a = (h, h w_1, \dots, h w_n)$, which is injective on every fibre. The composite is $\sum_j (h w_j - w_j h) \partial/\partial w_j = 0$. If $\sum_j (f_j - w_j f_0) \partial/\partial w_j = 0$, then $(f_0, \dots, f_n) = f_0 (1, w_1, \dots, w_n)$ is in the image of the first map. And $(0, \dots, f_j, \dots, 0) \mapsto f_j \partial/\partial w_j$, so the second map is surjective on every fibre. These statements hold pointwise, so the sequence is exact on every fibre, in $U_0$ and by symmetry everywhere.

For the determinant, let $0 \to E' \to E \to E'' \to 0$ be an exact sequence of bundles of ranks $r', r, r''$. Near any point the map $E \to E''$ is, in trivializations, a holomorphic $r'' \times r$ matrix $A(x)$ of rank $r''$; choosing $r''$ columns with nonzero minor at the point gives a holomorphic right inverse $R(x)$ nearby, so local frames of $E''$ lift to local sections of $E$. Define $\det E' \otimes \det E'' \to \det E$ on fibres by
$$(u'_1 \wedge \dots \wedge u'_{r'}) \otimes (u''_1 \wedge \dots \wedge u''_{r''}) \mapsto u'_1 \wedge \dots \wedge u'_{r'} \wedge \tilde u_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \tilde u_{r''},$$
with $\tilde u_k \in E_x$ any lifts of $u''_k$. Changing a lift by an element of $E'_x$ does not change the right side, since $\Lambda^{r'+1} E'_x = 0$, and the right side is multilinear and alternating in each group, so the map is well defined. It is nonzero, hence an isomorphism of lines, because $u'_1, \dots, u'_{r'}, \tilde u_1, \dots, \tilde u_{r''}$ is a basis of $E_x$ when the $u'$ and $u''$ are bases. Applied to local frames and holomorphic lifts it is holomorphic. So $\det E \cong \det E' \otimes \det E''$. The cocycle of $\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus(n+1)}$ is the scalar matrix $(Z_j/Z_i) \cdot 1$, with determinant $(Z_j/Z_i)^{n+1}$, so $\mathcal{O}(n+1) \cong \det(\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus (n+1)}) \cong \mathcal{O} \otimes \det T_{\mathbb{P}^n}$. Finally $\det(E^*)$ has cocycle $\det((g_{ij}^T)^{-1}) = (\det g_{ij})^{-1}$, so $\det(E^*) \cong (\det E)^*$, and $K_{\mathbb{P}^n} = \det \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cong (\det T_{\mathbb{P}^n})^* \cong \mathcal{O}(-n-1)$.

Proof of Proposition P6.2.19. If $e'$ is another frame near $y$, then $e = u e'$ with $u$ holomorphic and nowhere zero, $s = f e = (uf) e'$, and $d(uf) = u \, df + f \, du = u \, df$ at points of $Y$, where $f = 0$. So transversality does not depend on the frame, and $df(v) \, e = u \, df(v) \, e' = d(uf)(v) \, e'$ for $v \in T_y X$, $y \in Y$: the map $v \mapsto df(v) e$ is well defined on $T_X|_Y$. Locally $Y = f^{-1}(0)$ with $df \neq 0$ on $Y$, so by part 1 of Proposition P6.2.6 the set $Y$ is a complex submanifold of dimension $n - 1$, closed since $s$ is continuous, and $T_y Y = \ker df_y$. In local coordinates the map is $\sum_b v_b \partial/\partial z_b \mapsto (\sum_b v_b \partial f/\partial z_b) e$, a morphism of holomorphic bundles, surjective on every fibre since $df(y) \neq 0$, with kernel $T_Y$. So it induces an isomorphism $N_{Y/X} = T_X|_Y/T_Y \cong L|_Y$. The exact sequence $0 \to T_Y \to T_X|_Y \to N_{Y/X} \to 0$ and the multiplicativity of determinants proved for Proposition P6.2.18 give $\det(T_X|_Y) \cong \det T_Y \otimes N_{Y/X}$; dualizing, and using that restriction commutes with $\det$, $\otimes$ and duals (it pulls back cocycles), $K_X|_Y \cong K_Y \otimes N_{Y/X}^*$. Tensoring with $N_{Y/X} \cong L|_Y$ gives $K_Y \cong (K_X \otimes L)|_Y$.

### Exercises

1. (A) On $\mathbb{P}^n$ let $w_j = Z_j/Z_0$ ($1 \leq j \leq n$) on $U_0$ and $w'_0 = Z_0/Z_1$, $w'_j = Z_j/Z_1$ ($2 \leq j \leq n$) on $U_1$. Show that $dw'_0 \wedge dw'_2 \wedge \dots \wedge dw'_n = -(Z_0/Z_1)^{n+1} \, dw_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dw_n$, and deduce $K_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(-n-1)$ without the Euler sequence.
2. (A) Show that $\mathcal{O}(d) \cong \mathcal{O}(d')$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$, $n \geq 1$, only if $d = d'$, by computing $h^0$ of $\mathcal{O}(d - d')$ and of $\mathcal{O}(d' - d)$.
3. (B) Show that a holomorphic line bundle with a nowhere vanishing holomorphic section is trivial, and that a holomorphic line bundle $L$ on a compact connected manifold with nonzero sections of both $L$ and $L^*$ is trivial.
4. (B) Show that for $A \in \mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})$ the vector field $d\pi(\sum_a (AZ)_a \, \partial/\partial Z_a)$ is a well defined global holomorphic vector field on $\mathbb{P}^n$, and that it vanishes identically if and only if $A$ is scalar. Conclude that $h^0(\mathbb{P}^n, T_{\mathbb{P}^n}) \geq (n+1)^2 - 1$.
5. (B) Show that the holomorphic functions on the blow-up $B$ of Example P6.2.21 are the functions $G \circ p$ with $G$ entire on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$. (Use Corollary P6.1.15 on $B \setminus E \cong \mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$.)
6. (C) Let $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be a smooth curve of degree $d$ with affine equation $f(z, w) = F(1, z, w) = 0$ on $U_0$, and suppose that the line $Z_0 = 0$ meets $C$ in $d$ distinct points. For a polynomial $P(z, w)$ of degree at most $d - 3$, show that $P \, dz / (\partial f/\partial w)$, which on $C$ equals $-P \, dw/(\partial f/\partial z)$ where both make sense, is a holomorphic $1$-form on $C \cap U_0$ that extends holomorphically to $C$. Deduce $h^0(C, K_C) \geq (d-1)(d-2)/2$, and compare with $K_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C(d-3)$ from Example P6.2.20.

### Lean

Mathlib has topological and smooth vector bundles, including their construction from a cocycle of transition functions, and the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold. It has no holomorphic vector bundles over complex manifolds as such, no Picard group of a complex manifold, and no line bundles $\mathcal{O}(d)$ on complex projective space. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Fibre bundles with transition functions go back to Whitney and to Steenrod in the 1930s and 1940s. The cohomological description of holomorphic line bundles by $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*)$, and with it the exponential sequence, is due to Kodaira and Spencer in 1953; it is the analytic model for the algebraic Theorem P2.7.28. The Euler sequence and the formula for $K_{\mathbb{P}^n}$ are the analytic forms of Theorem P3.12.20 and Corollary P3.12.22, and the adjunction formula is the analytic form of Theorem P3.12.30; for plane curves it contains the classical genus formula $g = (d-1)(d-2)/2$. The blow-up of a point, the *$\sigma$-process*, was introduced into complex geometry by Hopf in 1951. The exposition follows Huy05, Sections 2.2, 2.4 and 2.5, and GH78, Sections 0.5 and 1.1.
