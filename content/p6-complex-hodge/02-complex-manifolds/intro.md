# Complex manifolds and Dolbeault cohomology

A complex manifold is a space that looks locally like an open set of $\mathbb{C}^n$, glued by holomorphic maps. The local theory of Chapter 1 then becomes global. Holomorphic functions on a compact connected complex manifold are constant, so the interesting global objects are sections of holomorphic vector bundles, and their failure to exist is measured by cohomology. Dolbeault cohomology computes this cohomology with differential forms: by the Dolbeault-Grothendieck lemma of Chapter 1 the sheaf of holomorphic $p$-forms has a resolution by sheaves of smooth $(p, q)$-forms, and these sheaves have no higher cohomology because they admit partitions of unity. Chapter 4 adds Hodge theory to this description and computes the cohomology of a compact Kähler manifold by harmonic forms.

Section 2.1 defines complex manifolds, holomorphic maps and complex submanifolds, and gives the three model examples of the volume: projective space, complex tori and the Hopf surface, a compact complex surface that Chapter 4 shows is not Kähler. It proves that holomorphic functions on compact connected manifolds are constant, so that a compact complex submanifold of $\mathbb{C}^m$ is a finite set, and it splits complex differential forms into types. Section 2.2 treats holomorphic vector bundles through their transition functions and their sheaves of sections, identifies the Picard group with $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*)$, constructs the line bundles $\mathcal{O}(d)$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$, computes their global sections by the Hartogs theorem, and proves the Euler sequence, the formula $K_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(-n-1)$ and the adjunction formula $K_Y \cong (K_X \otimes L)|_Y$ for the zero set of a section. Section 2.3 defines Dolbeault and de Rham cohomology, proves the theorems of Dolbeault and de Rham, sets up the Frölicher spectral sequence, and computes the cohomology of the projective line, of $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, of complex tori by Fourier series, and the Betti numbers of $\mathbb{P}^n$.

The chapter follows Huybrechts, Chapter 2 (Huy05), Voisin, Chapters 2 and 4 (Voi02), and Griffiths and Harris, Sections 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5 (GH78), with Wells, Chapter II (Wel80), for fine sheaves. From P2 it uses sheaves, sheaf cohomology as a derived functor, flasque sheaves, Čech cohomology and Leray's theorem, transition functions of locally free sheaves and the Picard group as $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^\times)$. From topology it uses only homotopy invariance, the Mayer-Vietoris sequence and the Künneth formula for de Rham cohomology, cited from Bott and Tu (BT82).
