## Analytification and Serre's GAGA

### Statement

In this section and the next, a scheme is a scheme of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$. For such a scheme $X$, the set $X(\mathbb{C})$ of closed points carries the topology induced, on each affine open piece $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_N]/(f_1, \dots, f_r)$, by the Euclidean topology of $\mathbb{C}^N$. This is the *classical topology*. The analytic spaces below are the analytic spaces of Serre, possibly with nilpotents in the structure sheaf (Ser56, Section 1; GR84a, Chapter 1).

**Definition P6.7.1 (analytic spaces and coherent analytic sheaves).** Let $\mathcal{H}_N$ be the sheaf of holomorphic functions on an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}^N$.

1. A *local model* is a ringed space $(Z, \mathcal{H}_N / \mathcal{J}|_Z)$, where $\mathcal{J} \subseteq \mathcal{H}_N$ is the ideal generated by finitely many holomorphic functions $f_1, \dots, f_r$ on $U$ and $Z = \{f_1 = \dots = f_r = 0\}$.
2. A *complex analytic space* is a Hausdorff locally $\mathbb{C}$-ringed space that is locally isomorphic to a local model. A morphism is a morphism of locally $\mathbb{C}$-ringed spaces. A *closed analytic subset* of an analytic space $Y$ is a closed set that is, near each of its points, the common zero set of finitely many holomorphic functions.
3. A sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_Y$-modules $\mathcal{M}$ on an analytic space $Y$ is *coherent* if it is locally the cokernel of a map $\mathcal{O}_Y^a \to \mathcal{O}_Y^b$.

A complex manifold of dimension $n$ is an analytic space locally isomorphic to $(U, \mathcal{H}_n)$. By the coherence theorem of Oka, $\mathcal{O}_Y$ is a coherent sheaf of rings, so kernels, cokernels, images and tensor products of coherent analytic sheaves are coherent (GR84a, Chapter 2). The axiom of Hausdorff separation is part of the definition only for convenience; the analytification of a nonseparated scheme is a locally $\mathbb{C}$-ringed space with the same local structure.

**Definition P6.7.2 (analytification).** Let $X$ be a scheme. Its *analytification* is the locally $\mathbb{C}$-ringed space $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ defined as follows. If $X = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_N]/(f_1, \dots, f_r)$, then $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is the local model of the polynomials $f_1, \dots, f_r$ on $U = \mathbb{C}^N$. For general $X$, the analytifications of the affine open pieces glue along the analytifications of their intersections. There is a morphism of locally ringed spaces $h \colon X^{\mathrm{an}} \to X$, the identity on points from $X^{\mathrm{an}} = X(\mathbb{C})$ to the closed points of $X$, and on structure sheaves the inclusion of polynomial functions into holomorphic functions. For an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$, put
$$\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}} = h^* \mathcal{F} = h^{-1} \mathcal{F} \otimes_{h^{-1} \mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}},$$
and for a morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ let $f^{\mathrm{an}} \colon X^{\mathrm{an}} \to Y^{\mathrm{an}}$ be the induced morphism. The *comparison maps* are the composites
$$\varepsilon^q \colon H^q(X, \mathcal{F}) \longrightarrow H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, h^{-1} \mathcal{F}) \longrightarrow H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}),$$
the first induced by the continuous map $h$ and the second by $h^{-1} \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$.

The maps $\varepsilon^q$ form a morphism of $\delta$-functors on coherent sheaves: they commute with the maps of a long exact sequence once $\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is known to be exact, which is part 1 of the next proposition.

**Proposition P6.7.3 (properties of analytification).** Let $X$ be a scheme and $x \in X^{\mathrm{an}}$ a point.

1. The local homomorphism $\mathcal{O}_{X, x} \to \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}, x}$ is faithfully flat and induces an isomorphism of completions. Hence $\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is exact and faithful on coherent sheaves, $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is coherent, a coherent $\mathcal{F}$ is zero if and only if $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is zero, and $\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}})$ for coherent $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{G}$.
2. $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is Hausdorff if and only if $X$ is separated, and compact and Hausdorff if and only if $X$ is proper. $X$ is smooth of pure dimension $n$ if and only if $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a complex manifold of dimension $n$, and then $(\Omega^p_X)^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \Omega^p_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$, the sheaf of holomorphic $p$-forms. If $X$ is reduced, so is $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, and if $X$ is connected, so is $X^{\mathrm{an}}$.
3. If $i \colon X \to P$ is a closed immersion, then $i^{\mathrm{an}}$ identifies $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ with a closed analytic subspace of $P^{\mathrm{an}}$, and $(i_* \mathcal{F})^{\mathrm{an}} \cong i^{\mathrm{an}}_* (\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}})$ for every coherent $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$.
4. $(\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}})^{\mathrm{an}}$ is the complex projective space $\mathbb{P}^n$ with its structure of complex manifold, and $\mathcal{O}(m)^{\mathrm{an}}$ is the holomorphic line bundle $\mathcal{O}(m)$.

**Lemma P6.7.4 (flatness and completions).** Let $A \to B$ be a local homomorphism of Noetherian local rings that induces an isomorphism $\widehat{A} \cong \widehat{B}$ of completions. Then $B$ is faithfully flat over $A$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P6.7.5 (GAGA, Serre).** Let $X$ be a projective scheme over $\mathbb{C}$.

1. For every coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ and every $q \geq 0$, the comparison map $\varepsilon^q \colon H^q(X, \mathcal{F}) \to H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}})$ is an isomorphism.
2. For coherent sheaves $\mathcal{F}$, $\mathcal{G}$ on $X$, the map $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}) \to \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}}(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}})$ is bijective.
3. For every coherent analytic sheaf $\mathcal{M}$ on $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ there is a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ with $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \mathcal{M}$, unique up to unique isomorphism.

So $\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is an equivalence from the category of coherent sheaves on $X$ to the category of coherent analytic sheaves on $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, and it preserves cohomology.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P6.7.6 (GAGA for proper schemes, black box).** The three statements of Theorem P6.7.5 hold for every proper scheme $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$. If $f \colon X \to Y$ is a proper morphism of schemes of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ is coherent on $X$, then $(R^q f_* \mathcal{F})^{\mathrm{an}} \cong R^q f^{\mathrm{an}}_* (\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}})$ for every $q$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

The proof of Theorem P6.7.5 goes through two lemmas on projective space.

**Lemma P6.7.7 (analytic cohomology of projective space).** Let $n \geq 0$.

1. For every $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $q \geq 0$, the comparison map $H^q(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(m)) \to H^q(\mathbb{P}^{n, \mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{O}(m))$ is an isomorphism.
2. $H^q(\mathbb{P}^{n, \mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{M}) = 0$ for every coherent analytic sheaf $\mathcal{M}$ and every $q > n$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P6.7.8 (Theorems A and B on projective space).** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a coherent analytic sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^{n, \mathrm{an}}$, and write $\mathcal{M}(m) = \mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{O}(m)$. There is $m_0$ such that for every $m \geq m_0$ the sheaf $\mathcal{M}(m)$ is generated by finitely many global sections and $H^q(\mathbb{P}^{n, \mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{M}(m)) = 0$ for every $q > 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Analytification replaces polynomial functions by convergent power series. The local ring of a point in $\mathbb{C}^N$ is a localization of a polynomial ring, the analytic local ring is the ring of convergent power series, and both have the formal power series ring as completion. Two Noetherian local rings with the same completion are faithfully flat over each other in the direction of the map, and this one fact gives the exactness and faithfulness of analytification. The topological statements are local or follow from the valuative criteria; they are cited.

The proof of GAGA has three steps, all on projective space, to which a closed immersion reduces everything. First, the cohomology of $\mathcal{O}(m)$: for $m = 0$ both sides are $\mathbb{C}$ in degree $0$ and zero otherwise, algebraically by Serre's computation and analytically by the Hodge decomposition of Chapter 4. The exact sequence of a hyperplane and induction on $n$ move the isomorphism from $m$ to $m \pm 1$, by the five lemma. Second, every coherent sheaf is a quotient of a sum of line bundles $\mathcal{O}(-a)$, and the comparison map is an isomorphism in degrees above $n$, where both sides vanish; a descending induction on the degree, again with the five lemma, gives the comparison for every coherent sheaf. Applied to $\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})$ in degree $0$, it gives the comparison of morphisms. Third, for essential surjectivity, a coherent analytic sheaf $\mathcal{M}$ on projective space becomes generated by global sections after a twist. This is Serre's analytic Theorem A, proved by induction on the dimension from the finiteness theorem of Cartan and Serre: the dimensions $h^1(\mathcal{M}(m))$ cannot decrease forever, and when they stop decreasing, sections on a hyperplane lift. Then $\mathcal{M}$ is the cokernel of a map between sums of line bundles, that map is algebraic by the second step, and $\mathcal{M}$ is the analytification of its algebraic cokernel.

### Examples

**Example P6.7.9 (sections on projective space).** Lemma P6.7.7 with $q = 0$ and Theorem P4.2.1 give, for $m \geq 0$, that every holomorphic section of $\mathcal{O}(m)$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $m$ in $x_0, \dots, x_n$. Directly for $n = 1$: a section is a pair of entire functions $s_0(z)$ on $\{x_0 \neq 0\}$, with $z = x_1/x_0$, and $s_1(w)$ on $\{x_1 \neq 0\}$, with $w = 1/z$, with $s_0(z) = z^m s_1(1/z)$ on $\mathbb{C}^\times$. Then $|s_0(z)| \leq C |z|^m$ for $|z| \geq 1$, with $C = \max_{|w| \leq 1} |s_1(w)|$, and by the Cauchy estimates the entire function $s_0$ is a polynomial of degree at most $m$. For $m < 0$ the same inequality forces $s_0 \to 0$ at infinity, so $s_0 = 0$ by Liouville's theorem, in agreement with $H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(m)) = 0$.

**Example P6.7.10 (GAGA fails for the affine line).** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$. Then $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = \mathbb{C}[z]$, while $H^0(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$ is the ring of entire functions, which contains $e^z$. So the comparison map in degree $0$ is injective and not surjective, and part 2 of Theorem P6.7.5 fails too: multiplication by $e^z$ is an endomorphism of $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$ that is not the analytification of an endomorphism of $\mathcal{O}_X$, since the endomorphisms of $\mathcal{O}_X$ are multiplications by polynomials. Projectivity, or at least properness as in Theorem P6.7.6, is necessary.

**Example P6.7.11 (a coherent analytic sheaf that is not algebraic).** On $X = \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ let $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$ be the ideal of holomorphic functions that vanish on $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$. Near an integer $k$ it is generated by $z - k$, and near a point outside $\mathbb{Z}$ it is $\mathcal{O}$, so $\mathcal{I}$ and $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}$ are coherent. If $\mathcal{M} \cong \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ with $\mathcal{F}$ coherent on $X$, then by part 1 of Proposition P6.7.3 the support of $\mathcal{M}$ is the set of closed points of $\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}$, a Zariski closed subset of $\mathbb{A}^1$, which is finite or all of $\mathbb{C}$. The support of $\mathcal{M}$ is $\mathbb{Z}$, which is neither. So part 3 of Theorem P6.7.5 fails on the affine line.

### Proof

*Lemma P6.7.4.* Let $M$ be a finitely generated $A$-module. Since $B \to \widehat{B}$ is faithfully flat (completion of a Noetherian local ring) and $A \to \widehat{A}$ is flat,
$$\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M, B) \otimes_B \widehat{B} \cong \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M, \widehat{B}) = \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M, \widehat{A}) = 0,$$
where the first isomorphism uses the flatness of $\widehat{B}$ over $B$ to commute $\otimes_B \widehat{B}$ with the homology of a free resolution of $M$ tensored with $B$. Faithful flatness of $\widehat{B}$ over $B$ gives $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M, B) = 0$. Applied to $M = A/I$ for the ideals $I$ of $A$, part 4 of Theorem P2.3.32 shows that $B$ is flat over $A$, and a flat local homomorphism of local rings is faithfully flat, since $\mathfrak{m}_A B \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_B \neq B$.

*Proposition P6.7.3.* Part 1. Let $x$ be a point of $\mathbb{C}^N$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $R = \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_N]$. The ring $\mathbb{C}\{x - x(x)\}$ of convergent power series is Noetherian (GR84a, Chapter 2), and both $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ and $\mathbb{C}\{x - x(x)\}$ have completion $\mathbb{C}[[x - x(x)]]$. If $X = \operatorname{Spec} R/(f_1, \dots, f_r)$, then $\mathcal{O}_{X, x} = R_{\mathfrak{m}}/(f)$ and $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}, x} = \mathbb{C}\{x - x(x)\}/(f)$, and their completions are both $\mathbb{C}[[x - x(x)]]/(f)$ since completion of finitely generated modules over a Noetherian local ring is exact. Lemma P6.7.4 gives faithful flatness. Since $(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}})_x = \mathcal{F}_x \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X, x}} \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}, x}$, exactness holds on stalks, and $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}_x = 0$ forces $\mathcal{F}_x = 0$ at every closed point, hence $\mathcal{F} = 0$, since the support of a coherent sheaf is closed and a nonempty closed subset of a scheme of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$ has a closed point. Faithfulness follows by applying this to the image of a morphism. A coherent $\mathcal{F}$ is locally a cokernel of $\mathcal{O}_X^a \to \mathcal{O}_X^b$, so $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is locally a cokernel of $\mathcal{O}^a \to \mathcal{O}^b$ and is coherent. For $\mathcal{H}om$, both sides are left exact in $\mathcal{F}$ and agree for $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}^a$, and the comparison follows from a local presentation by the five lemma, using exactness of analytification. Part 2 is SGA1, Exposé XII, Sections 2 and 3, and Ser56, Section 2: Hausdorff separation corresponds to the closedness of the diagonal, and compactness to the valuative criterion; smoothness of dimension $n$ at a closed point means that $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is regular of dimension $n$ (Theorem P3.7.24), which by the equality of completions holds if and only if $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}, x}$ is regular of dimension $n$, that is, isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}\{z_1, \dots, z_n\}$. The sheaf $\Omega_X$ is generated by the $dx_i$ with the relations $df_j$, and so is $\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$ for the local model, which gives $(\Omega^1_X)^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \Omega^1_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$, and exterior powers commute with pullback. The statements on reducedness and connectedness are in SGA1, Exposé XII, Section 2. Part 3 is local, and on an affine piece with $X = V(f_1, \dots, f_r) \subseteq P$ it says that $\mathcal{O}_P^{\mathrm{an}}/(f) = (\mathcal{O}_P/(f))^{\mathrm{an}}$, which is exactness of analytification; for a coherent $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ both sheaves have stalk $\mathcal{F}_x \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P, x}} \mathcal{O}_{P^{\mathrm{an}}, x}$ at points of $X$ and are zero elsewhere. Part 4: on $\{x_i \neq 0\}$ both are $\mathbb{C}^n$ with the coordinates $x_j/x_i$, glued by the same transition maps, and $\mathcal{O}(m)$ has the transition functions $(x_j/x_i)^m$ in both settings.

*Lemma P6.7.7.* Part 2. Let $U_i = \{x_i \neq 0\}$ for $0 \leq i \leq n$. A finite intersection $U_{i_0} \cap \dots \cap U_{i_p}$ is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^p \times \mathbb{C}^{n-p}$, an open subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$ that is a product of domains in $\mathbb{C}$, hence a domain of holomorphy and a Stein manifold. By Cartan's Theorem B (Hor90a, Section 7.4), $H^q(U_{i_0 \cdots i_p}, \mathcal{M}) = 0$ for $q > 0$. By Leray's theorem, Theorem P2.7.21, $H^q(\mathbb{P}^{n,\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{M})$ is the Čech cohomology of the cover by the $n + 1$ sets $U_i$, and the alternating Čech complex of $n + 1$ sets is zero in degrees above $n$.

Part 1, by induction on $n$. For $n = 0$ both sides are $\mathbb{C}$ in degree $0$ and zero otherwise. Let $n \geq 1$. For $m = 0$: algebraically $H^0(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}) = \mathbb{C}$ and $H^q(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}) = 0$ for $q > 0$ (Corollary P4.2.2). Analytically, $H^0(\mathbb{P}^{n,\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{O}) = \mathbb{C}$ since $\mathbb{P}^n$ is a compact connected complex manifold and a holomorphic function on it is constant by the maximum principle (Chapter 2). By the Dolbeault isomorphism of Chapter 2, $H^q(\mathbb{P}^{n,\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{O}) \cong H^{0, q}(\mathbb{P}^n)$, and by the Hodge decomposition of Chapter 4, $\sum_{p + q = k} h^{p, q}(\mathbb{P}^n) = b_k(\mathbb{P}^n)$, which is $1$ for even $k \leq 2n$ and $0$ otherwise (GH78, Chapter 0, Section 4). For $k = 2j$ the class $[\omega_{FS}^j]$ of the $j$-th power of the Fubini-Study form (Proposition P6.5.20) is a nonzero class in $H^{j, j}$, since $\int_{\mathbb{P}^n} \omega_{FS}^n > 0$. So $h^{j, j} = 1 = b_{2j}$ and $h^{0, 2j} = 0$ for $j \geq 1$, and $h^{0, q} = 0$ for odd $q$. In all cases the comparison map for $\mathcal{O}$ is an isomorphism, since in degree $0$ it sends $1$ to $1$.

Let $H = \{x_n = 0\} \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, with the exact sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}(m - 1) \xrightarrow{x_n} \mathcal{O}(m) \to i_* \mathcal{O}_H(m) \to 0$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$. Its analytification is the analytic sequence, by Proposition P6.7.3, and the comparison maps give a map of long exact sequences. By Proposition P2.7.4, both algebraically and analytically, the cohomology of $i_* \mathcal{O}_H(m)$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is the cohomology of $\mathcal{O}_H(m)$ on $H$, and the comparison maps for it are isomorphisms by induction. In the diagram
$$H^{q-1}(\mathcal{O}_H(m)) \to H^q(\mathcal{O}(m-1)) \to H^q(\mathcal{O}(m)) \to H^q(\mathcal{O}_H(m)) \to H^{q+1}(\mathcal{O}(m-1)),$$
if the comparison maps for $\mathcal{O}(m - 1)$ are isomorphisms in all degrees, the five lemma gives them for $\mathcal{O}(m)$; and if they are isomorphisms for $\mathcal{O}(m)$ in all degrees, the five lemma, applied to the same sequence shifted by one place, gives them for $\mathcal{O}(m - 1)$. Starting from $m = 0$, induction upward and downward gives all $m$.

*Theorem P6.7.5, part 1.* Let $i \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^n$ be a closed immersion. By Proposition P2.7.4 and part 3 of Proposition P6.7.3, $H^q(X, \mathcal{F}) = H^q(\mathbb{P}^n, i_* \mathcal{F})$ and $H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}) = H^q(\mathbb{P}^{n,\mathrm{an}}, (i_* \mathcal{F})^{\mathrm{an}})$ compatibly with $\varepsilon^q$, so it is enough to treat $X = \mathbb{P}^n$. If $\mathcal{F}$ is a finite direct sum of sheaves $\mathcal{O}(m)$, then $\varepsilon^q$ is an isomorphism for every $q$ by Lemma P6.7.7. For $q > n$, both sides vanish for every coherent $\mathcal{F}$, by Theorem P4.2.7 and part 2 of Lemma P6.7.7. Assume that $\varepsilon^{q+1}$ is an isomorphism for every coherent sheaf. Given $\mathcal{F}$, Theorem P3.6.23 gives a surjection $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{O}(-a)^b \to \mathcal{F}$, with coherent kernel $\mathcal{K}$. The comparison maps give a map of exact sequences
$$H^q(\mathcal{K}) \to H^q(\mathcal{L}) \to H^q(\mathcal{F}) \to H^{q+1}(\mathcal{K}) \to H^{q+1}(\mathcal{L}).$$
The second and fourth maps are isomorphisms and the fifth is injective, so $\varepsilon^q(\mathcal{F})$ is surjective by the four lemma. This holds for every coherent sheaf, in particular for $\mathcal{K}$, so the first map is surjective, and the four lemma now gives that $\varepsilon^q(\mathcal{F})$ is injective. Descending induction on $q$ ends at $q = 0$.

*Theorem P6.7.5, part 2.* By part 1 of Proposition P6.7.3, $\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}})$, and $\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})$ is coherent. Part 1 in degree $0$ gives
$$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}) = H^0(X, \mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})) \cong H^0(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}})) = \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}}),$$
and the composite is the map $\varphi \mapsto \varphi^{\mathrm{an}}$, as one sees on stalks.

*Lemma P6.7.8.* By the theorem of Cartan and Serre (CS53a), $H^q(\mathbb{P}^{n,\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{M})$ is finite dimensional for every coherent analytic $\mathcal{M}$ and every $q$. The proof is by induction on $n$, the case $n = 0$ being clear. Let $n \geq 1$, let $t = x_n \in H^0(\mathcal{O}(1))$ and $H = \{t = 0\}$, and consider
$$0 \to \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{M}(-1) \xrightarrow{t} \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{B} \to 0,$$
with $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ the kernel and cokernel. They are coherent and annihilated by the ideal of $H$, so they are pushforwards of coherent analytic sheaves on $H \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ (GR84a, Chapter 1), and by induction and Proposition P2.7.4 there is $m_1$ with $H^q(\mathcal{A}(m)) = H^q(\mathcal{B}(m)) = 0$ for $q > 0$ and $m \geq m_1$, and $\mathcal{B}(m)$ generated by global sections for $m \geq m_1$. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the image of $t$, twisted so that $0 \to \mathcal{A}(m) \to \mathcal{M}(m - 1) \to \mathcal{C}_m \to 0$ and $0 \to \mathcal{C}_m \to \mathcal{M}(m) \to \mathcal{B}(m) \to 0$ are exact. For $m \geq m_1 + 1$, the first sequence gives that $H^1(\mathcal{M}(m - 1)) \to H^1(\mathcal{C}_m)$ is surjective, since $H^2(\mathcal{A}(m)) = 0$, and the second gives that $H^1(\mathcal{C}_m) \to H^1(\mathcal{M}(m))$ is surjective, since $H^1(\mathcal{B}(m)) = 0$. So $h^1(\mathcal{M}(m)) \leq h^1(\mathcal{M}(m - 1))$, a nonincreasing sequence of nonnegative integers, which is constant for $m \geq m_2$. For $m > m_2$ both surjections are between spaces of the same finite dimension, so $H^1(\mathcal{C}_m) \to H^1(\mathcal{M}(m))$ is injective, and the second sequence shows that $H^0(\mathcal{M}(m)) \to H^0(\mathcal{B}(m))$ is surjective. At a point $x \in H$, $\mathcal{B}(m)_x = \mathcal{M}(m)_x / t \mathcal{M}(m-1)_x$, which is $\mathcal{M}(m)_x / \tau \mathcal{M}(m)_x$ for a local equation $\tau \in \mathfrak{m}_x$ of $H$. Global sections generate $\mathcal{B}(m)_x$, hence generate $\mathcal{M}(m)_x$ by Nakayama's lemma. The set $V_m$ of points where the global sections generate $\mathcal{M}(m)$ is open, as the complement of the support of a coherent cokernel. For $m' \geq m$, $V_m \subseteq V_{m'}$, since $\mathcal{O}(m' - m)$ is generated by the monomials. So for each hyperplane $H$ there is $m_H$ with $H \subseteq V_{m_H}$. Every point lies on a hyperplane, every hyperplane is of the form $\{t = 0\}$ after a linear change of coordinates, and by compactness finitely many open sets $V_{m_H}$ cover $\mathbb{P}^n$. With $m_0$ the largest of these $m_H$, $\mathcal{M}(m)$ is generated by global sections for $m \geq m_0$, and by finitely many, since $H^0$ is finite dimensional.

Assume now that part 3 of Theorem P6.7.5 is proved for $\mathbb{P}^n$ using only this generation statement, as is done below. Then $\mathcal{M} \cong \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}}$ for a coherent $\mathcal{G}$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$, and $H^q(\mathcal{M}(m)) \cong H^q(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{G}(m))$ by part 1, which is zero for $q > 0$ and $m$ large by Theorem P4.2.7. This is the vanishing statement, and it is the form used in the induction: the induction hypothesis for $H \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ includes both generation and vanishing, and the vanishing on $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is deduced from generation on $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ and parts 1 to 3 of Theorem P6.7.5 on $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. So the induction runs on the joint statement of Lemma P6.7.8 and part 3 of Theorem P6.7.5.

*Theorem P6.7.5, part 3.* First $X = \mathbb{P}^n$. By the generation statement of Lemma P6.7.8 there is a surjection $\mathcal{O}(-m)^b \to \mathcal{M}$, and applied to its coherent kernel, a surjection $\mathcal{O}(-m')^{b'}$ onto the kernel. So $\mathcal{M}$ is the cokernel of a map $\varphi \colon \mathcal{O}(-m')^{b', \mathrm{an}} \to \mathcal{O}(-m)^{b, \mathrm{an}}$. By part 2, $\varphi = \psi^{\mathrm{an}}$ for a map $\psi$ of algebraic sheaves, and by exactness $\mathcal{M} \cong (\operatorname{coker} \psi)^{\mathrm{an}}$. For general $X$, with a closed immersion $i \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^n$ and ideal $\mathcal{I}$, the sheaf $i_* \mathcal{M}$ is coherent on $\mathbb{P}^{n, \mathrm{an}}$ (GR84a, Chapter 1), so $i_* \mathcal{M} \cong \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}}$. The image of $\mathcal{I} \otimes \mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{G}$ is a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{I}\mathcal{G}$ with $(\mathcal{I} \mathcal{G})^{\mathrm{an}} = \mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{an}} \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{an}} = 0$, so $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{G} = 0$ by Proposition P6.7.3, and $\mathcal{G} = i_* \mathcal{F}$ for the coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F} = i^* \mathcal{G}$ on $X$. Then $i_*(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}) \cong i_* \mathcal{M}$, hence $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \mathcal{M}$. Uniqueness up to unique isomorphism is part 2.

*Theorem P6.7.6.* This is SGA1, Exposé XII, Théorème 4.4 for the three statements, proved by reduction to the projective case with Chow's lemma and induction on the dimension of the support, and Théorème 4.2 for the higher direct images, which rests on Grauert's direct image theorem; Hartshorne, Appendix B, Theorem 2.1 (Har77) states both.

### Lean

Mathlib has schemes and has manifolds modelled on $\mathbb{C}^n$, but it has no complex analytic spaces with nilpotents, no coherent analytic sheaves and no analytification functor, so none of the statements of this section is formalized. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Serre proved the three theorems in 1956 (Ser56, Théorèmes 1, 2 and 3), for projective varieties, with the proof given here: the reduction to projective space, the computation for line bundles, and the use of the finiteness theorem of Cartan and Serre (CS53a) for analytic Theorem A. The title, Géométrie algébrique et géométrie analytique, gave the name GAGA. Grothendieck extended the theorems to proper schemes in SGA 1, Exposé XII (SGA1), with Chow's lemma and Grauert's coherence theorem for proper direct images, and Hartshorne, Appendix B (Har77), surveys the statements. The literature state of all three theorems is theorem. The flatness of analytic local rings over algebraic ones, Proposition P6.7.3, is the algebraic heart of the matter; it is proved in Section 2 of Ser56. Example P6.7.11 shows the difference between Zariski closed and analytic: on a noncompact space, analytic sets can have infinitely many components.
