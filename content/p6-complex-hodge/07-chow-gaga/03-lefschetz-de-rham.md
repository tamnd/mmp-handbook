## The Lefschetz principle and algebraic de Rham cohomology

### Statement

In this section $k$ is a field of characteristic $0$, so that $k$ contains $\mathbb{Q}$. A subfield $k_0 \subseteq k$ is *finitely generated* if it is generated as a field over $\mathbb{Q}$ by finitely many elements. For a scheme $X_0$ over a field $k_0$ and a field extension $K \supseteq k_0$, write $X_{0, K} = X_0 \times_{\operatorname{Spec} k_0} \operatorname{Spec} K$, let $g \colon X_{0, K} \to X_0$ be the projection, and write $\mathcal{F}_K = g^* \mathcal{F}$ for a sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_{X_0}$-modules $\mathcal{F}$. A *model* of a scheme $X$ over $k$ is a pair of a subfield $k_0 \subseteq k$ and a scheme $X_0$ over $k_0$ with an isomorphism $X_{0, k} \cong X$ over $k$. For a smooth scheme $X$ over $k$, $\Omega^p_X = \bigwedge^p \Omega_{X/k}$, and $h^{p,q}(X) = \dim_k H^q(X, \Omega^p_X)$ when this is finite.

**Lemma P6.7.21 (finitely generated fields embed in the complex numbers).** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ that is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then there is an injective homomorphism of fields $K \to \mathbb{C}$.

**Proposition P6.7.22 (models over finitely generated fields).** Let $X$ be a projective scheme over $k$.

1. There is a model $(k_0, X_0)$ of $X$ with $k_0$ finitely generated and $X_0$ a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^N_{k_0}$, such that $X_{0,k} = X$ as closed subschemes of $\mathbb{P}^N_k$.
2. Let $k_1 \subseteq k$ be a finitely generated subfield, $X_1$ projective over $k_1$ with $X_{1,k} = X$, and $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent sheaf on $X$. There is a finitely generated subfield $k_0$ with $k_1 \subseteq k_0 \subseteq k$ and a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}_0$ on $X_{1, k_0}$ with $(\mathcal{F}_0)_k \cong \mathcal{F}$.
3. In the situation of part 2, let $\mathcal{G}_1, \mathcal{G}_2$ be coherent sheaves on $X_1$ and $\varphi \colon \mathcal{G}_{1,k} \to \mathcal{G}_{2,k}$ a morphism. There are a finitely generated $k_0$ with $k_1 \subseteq k_0 \subseteq k$ and a morphism $\varphi_0 \colon \mathcal{G}_{1,k_0} \to \mathcal{G}_{2,k_0}$ with $(\varphi_0)_k = \varphi$. If $\varphi$ is an isomorphism, $\varphi_0$ can be chosen to be an isomorphism.
4. Let $X_0$ be a scheme of finite type over a field $k_0$ and $K \supseteq k_0$ a field extension. Then $X_0$ is smooth over $k_0$ of pure dimension $n$ if and only if $X_{0, K}$ is smooth over $K$ of pure dimension $n$, and if $X_0$ is projective, so is $X_{0,K}$.

Every finitely generated field between $k_0$ and $k$ again serves in parts 1 to 3, since the data can be base changed to it.

**Proposition P6.7.23 (cohomology and extension of the field).** Let $X$ be a separated scheme of finite type over a field $k$ of any characteristic and $K \supseteq k$ a field extension.

1. For every quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ and every $q$, $H^q(X_K, \mathcal{F}_K) \cong H^q(X, \mathcal{F}) \otimes_k K$, so the two dimensions agree.
2. $(\Omega^p_{X/k})_K \cong \Omega^p_{X_K/K}$ for every $p$.
3. If $X$ is smooth over $k$, then $H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_K/K) \cong H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) \otimes_k K$ for every $m$, in the sense of Definition P6.7.24 below, and $h^{p,q}(X_K) = h^{p,q}(X)$.

**Definition P6.7.24 (algebraic de Rham cohomology).** Let $X$ be a smooth scheme over a field $k$. The *algebraic de Rham complex* is the complex of sheaves of $k$-vector spaces
$$\Omega^\bullet_{X/k} \colon \ \mathcal{O}_X \xrightarrow{d} \Omega^1_X \xrightarrow{d} \Omega^2_X \xrightarrow{d} \cdots \xrightarrow{d} \Omega^n_X,$$
with $d$ the exterior derivative, which is $k$-linear and not $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear. The *algebraic de Rham cohomology* of $X$ is the hypercohomology $H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) = \mathbb{H}^m(X, \Omega^\bullet_{X/k})$ of Definition P2.4.23. Let $\sigma_{\geq p} \Omega^\bullet_{X/k}$ be the subcomplex of terms of degree at least $p$. The *Hodge filtration* is $F^p H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) = \operatorname{im}\big(\mathbb{H}^m(X, \sigma_{\geq p} \Omega^\bullet) \to H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)\big)$. The *Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence* is the first hypercohomology spectral sequence of Theorem P2.4.24 (Example P2.4.30),
$$E_1^{p,q} = H^q(X, \Omega^p_X) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k),$$
with $d_1$ induced by $d$. It *degenerates at $E_1$* if $d_r = 0$ for all $r \geq 1$.

If $X$ is projective, every $E_1^{p,q}$ is finite dimensional (Corollary P4.2.8), and so is $H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)$, since the spectral sequence has finitely many nonzero terms. As $E_\infty^{p,q}$ is a subquotient of $E_1^{p,q}$, and $\sum_p \dim E_\infty^{p, m-p} = \dim H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)$ by convergence of a filtration of finite length, there is an inequality $\dim H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) \leq \sum_{p+q=m} h^{p,q}(X)$, with equality for all $m$ if and only if the spectral sequence degenerates at $E_1$. If $X$ is affine, $E_1^{p,q} = 0$ for $q > 0$ by Theorem P4.1.8, so the spectral sequence degenerates at $E_2$ and $H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) = H^m(\Gamma(X, \Omega^\bullet_{X/k}))$, the cohomology of the complex of global algebraic forms.

**Theorem P6.7.25 (comparison for smooth projective varieties).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective scheme over $\mathbb{C}$, so that $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a compact complex manifold (Proposition P6.7.3).

1. For all $p$ and $q$ the comparison map $H^q(X, \Omega^p_X) \to H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^p_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$ is an isomorphism. In particular $h^{p,q}(X)$ is the Hodge number $h^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$, the dimension of the Dolbeault cohomology $H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$.
2. The inclusion of algebraic forms into holomorphic forms induces an isomorphism $H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{H}^m(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$, and the holomorphic Poincaré lemma gives $\mathbb{H}^m(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}) \cong H^m(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathbb{C})$. So $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbb{C}) = b_m(X^{\mathrm{an}})$, the Betti number.

**Theorem P6.7.26 (degeneration and Hodge symmetry in characteristic 0).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective scheme over a field $k$ of characteristic $0$. Then the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of $X$ degenerates at $E_1$, so that
$$\dim_k H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) = \sum_{p+q=m} h^{p,q}(X) \quad \text{for every } m,$$
and $h^{p,q}(X) = h^{q,p}(X)$ for all $p, q$. In particular $\dim_k H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)$ is even for odd $m$.

**Corollary P6.7.27 (Kodaira vanishing in characteristic 0).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective scheme of pure dimension $n$ over a field $k$ of characteristic $0$ and $L$ an ample line bundle on $X$. Then
$$H^q(X, \Omega^p_X \otimes L) = 0 \ \text{ for } p + q > n, \qquad H^q(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = 0 \ \text{ for } q > 0, \qquad H^q(X, L^{-1}) = 0 \ \text{ for } q < n.$$

**Theorem P6.7.28 (Grothendieck's comparison theorem, black box).** Let $X$ be a smooth separated scheme of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$, not necessarily proper. The inclusion $\Omega^\bullet_{X/\mathbb{C}} \to \Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$ induces an isomorphism
$$H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{H}^m(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}) \cong H^m(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathbb{C}) \quad \text{for every } m.$$
In particular, if $X$ is affine, the cohomology of the complex $\Gamma(X, \Omega^\bullet_{X/\mathbb{C}})$ of algebraic differential forms is the complex cohomology of $X^{\mathrm{an}}$.

### Proof in words

The Lefschetz principle, in the form used here, has three parts. A projective scheme over $k$, together with finitely many coherent sheaves and morphisms, is cut out by finitely many polynomials with finitely many coefficients, so it is defined over the subfield $k_0$ that these coefficients generate. A finitely generated field of characteristic $0$ has finite transcendence degree, and $\mathbb{C}$ has uncountable transcendence degree and is algebraically closed, so $k_0$ embeds into $\mathbb{C}$. Cohomology of quasi-coherent sheaves commutes with flat base change, and every field extension is flat, so the dimensions of the cohomology groups over $k$, over $k_0$ and over $\mathbb{C}$ agree. A statement about such dimensions that holds over $\mathbb{C}$ therefore holds over $k$.

Over $\mathbb{C}$, GAGA identifies the algebraic and the analytic Hodge groups $H^q(\Omega^p)$. Algebraic and analytic de Rham cohomology are both computed by Čech double complexes on an affine cover, since affine open sets are acyclic for quasi-coherent sheaves and their analytifications are Stein manifolds, acyclic for coherent analytic sheaves. The inclusion of algebraic into holomorphic forms is a map of filtered double complexes that is an isomorphism on the graded pieces, by GAGA, hence on total cohomology. The holomorphic Poincaré lemma identifies analytic de Rham cohomology with the cohomology of the constant sheaf. The Hodge decomposition of Chapter 4 then says that $b_m = \sum_{p+q=m} h^{p,q}$ and $h^{p,q} = h^{q,p}$, which is degeneration and symmetry over $\mathbb{C}$, and the Lefschetz principle carries both to every field of characteristic $0$. Kodaira vanishing over $k$ follows from Corollary P6.7.17 in the same way. Grothendieck's theorem for smooth varieties that are not proper is deeper: GAGA is not available, the comparison maps $H^q(\Omega^p) \to H^q(\Omega^p_{\mathrm{an}})$ are far from isomorphisms, and only the total cohomology agrees.

### Examples

**Example P6.7.29 (projective space).** Let $k$ have characteristic $0$. Then $h^{p,q}(\mathbb{P}^n_k) = 1$ if $p = q \leq n$ and $0$ otherwise, and $H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{P}^n_k/k)$ is $k$ for even $0 \leq m \leq 2n$ and $0$ otherwise. Over $\mathbb{C}$, $b_m(\mathbb{P}^n) = 1$ for even $m \leq 2n$ and $0$ otherwise (GH78, Chapter 0, Section 4), the class $[\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^j]$ is a nonzero element of $H^{j,j}$ for $j \leq n$ (Proposition P6.5.20), and the Hodge decomposition $b_{2j} = \sum_{p+q=2j} h^{p,q}$ forces $h^{j,j} = 1$ and $h^{p,q} = 0$ for $p \neq q$; Theorem P6.7.25 gives the algebraic statement over $\mathbb{C}$. Since $\mathbb{P}^n_k = (\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Q}})_k$ and $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}} = (\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Q}})_{\mathbb{C}}$ (Example P3.3.22), Proposition P6.7.23 gives the same numbers over $k$. Degeneration can also be seen directly: $E_1^{p,q}$ is zero off the diagonal $p = q$, and $d_r$ sends $E_r^{p,p}$ to $E_r^{p+r, p-r+1}$, which lies off the diagonal since $2r \neq 1$. For $n = 1$ the de Rham group $H^2$ can be computed by hand with the charts $U_0 = \operatorname{Spec} k[z]$, $U_1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[w]$, $w = z^{-1}$, $U_{01} = \operatorname{Spec} k[z, z^{-1}]$. The total Čech complex of Step 1 of the proof of Proposition P6.7.23 has degree $2$ part $\Omega^1(U_{01}) = k[z, z^{-1}]\, dz$, since $\Omega^2 = 0$ and there are no triple intersections. The image of the degree $1$ part is spanned by $a(z)\, dz$ with $a \in k[z]$, by $b(w)\, dw = -b(z^{-1}) z^{-2}\, dz$ with $b \in k[w]$, and by $d(z^{j+1}) = (j+1) z^j\, dz$ with $j \in \mathbb{Z}$: together these are the monomials $z^j\, dz$ with $j \neq -1$, since $j + 1 \neq 0$ in $k$ for $j \neq -1$. So $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{P}^1_k/k) = k \cdot [dz/z]$, and $dz/z$ is also the generator of $H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \Omega^1) = E_1^{1,1}$ of Example P4.1.25.

**Example P6.7.30 (a plane cubic).** Let $E = V_+(y^2 z - x^3 + x z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$, with $k$ of characteristic $0$. It is smooth: on $z = 1$ the partial derivatives of $y^2 - x^3 + x$ are $2y$ and $1 - 3x^2$, which vanish together with the equation only if $y = 0$, $x^3 = x$ and $3x^2 = 1$, impossible since $3x^2 \in \{0, 3\}$ for $x \in \{0, \pm 1\}$; and at the only point at infinity, $(0:1:0)$, the chart $y = 1$ gives $z - x^3 + x z^2$, whose derivative in $z$ is $1$ there. By Corollary P3.12.31 with $n = 2$ and $e = 3$, $\omega_E = \Omega^1_E \cong \mathcal{O}_E$. The sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-3) \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2} \to \mathcal{O}_E \to 0$ and Theorem P4.2.1 give $H^0(E, \mathcal{O}_E) = k$ and $H^1(E, \mathcal{O}_E) \cong H^2(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(-3)) = k$, spanned by $(xyz)^{-1}$, the only monomial of degree $-3$ with all exponents at most $-1$. So $h^{0,0} = h^{0,1} = h^{1,0} = h^{1,1} = 1$, and Theorem P6.7.26 gives $\dim H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(E/k) = 2$, which over $\mathbb{C}$ is $b_1$ of the torus $E^{\mathrm{an}}$. The equality $h^{1,0} = h^{0,1}$ is the case $p = 1$, $q = 0$ of Hodge symmetry, here checked by hand. A generator of $H^0(E, \Omega^1_E)$ is $\eta = dx/y$ on the affine part. It is regular where $y \neq 0$, and where $y = 0$ it equals $2\, dy/(3x^2 - 1)$, from $2y\, dy = (3x^2 - 1)\, dx$, with $3x^2 - 1 \neq 0$ at these points. At $O = (0:1:0)$, $x$ is a function of degree $2$ on $E$ with its only pole at $O$, so $\operatorname{ord}_O x = -2$, and $2 \operatorname{ord}_O y = 3 \operatorname{ord}_O x$ gives $\operatorname{ord}_O y = -3$. In characteristic $0$, $\operatorname{ord}_O dx = -3$, so $\operatorname{ord}_O \eta = 0$ and $\eta$ is a nowhere vanishing global form.

**Example P6.7.31 (a quartic surface).** Let $X = V_+(F) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$ be a smooth quartic surface, for instance the Fermat quartic $F = x_0^4 + x_1^4 + x_2^4 + x_3^4$, whose partial derivatives $4x_i^3$ vanish together only at the origin. By Corollary P3.12.31, $\omega_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X$. From $0 \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(m - 4) \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(m) \to \mathcal{O}_X(m) \to 0$ and Theorem P4.2.1: $H^0(\mathcal{O}_X) = k$, $H^1(\mathcal{O}_X) = 0$ since $H^1(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}) = H^2(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(-4)) = 0$, and $H^2(\mathcal{O}_X) \cong H^3(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(-4)) = k$. So $h^{0,0} = 1$, $h^{0,1} = 0$, $h^{0,2} = 1$, and by Serre duality (Corollary P4.7.21) with $\omega_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X$, $h^{2,0} = h^{2,2} = 1$ and $h^{2,1} = 0$. For $\Omega^1_X$, the conormal sequence (Theorem P3.12.14) and the restricted Euler sequence (Theorem P3.12.20) are
$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(-4) \to \Omega^1_{\mathbb{P}^3}|_X \to \Omega^1_X \to 0, \qquad 0 \to \Omega^1_{\mathbb{P}^3}|_X \to \mathcal{O}_X(-1)^4 \to \mathcal{O}_X \to 0.$$
Since $H^0(\mathcal{O}_X(-1)) = 0$, $H^0(\Omega^1_{\mathbb{P}^3}|_X) = 0$; and $H^1(\mathcal{O}_X(-4)) = 0$, since $H^1(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(-4)) = H^2(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(-8)) = 0$. So $h^{1,0} = h^0(\Omega^1_X) = 0$, which agrees with $h^{0,1} = 0$, as Theorem P6.7.26 predicts. On a surface the wedge pairing $\Omega^1 \otimes \Omega^1 \to \omega_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X$ is perfect, so $T_X \cong \Omega^1_X$, and Serre duality gives $h^{1,2} = h^0(T_X) = h^0(\Omega^1_X) = 0$. Euler characteristics, with $\chi(\mathcal{O}_X(m)) = \binom{m+3}{3} - \binom{m-1}{3}$ as polynomials in $m$: $\chi(\mathcal{O}_X) = 1 - (-1) = 2$, $\chi(\mathcal{O}_X(-1)) = 0 - (-4) = 4$ and $\chi(\mathcal{O}_X(-4)) = -1 - (-35) = 34$. Hence $\chi(\Omega^1_{\mathbb{P}^3}|_X) = 4 \cdot 4 - 2 = 14$ and $\chi(\Omega^1_X) = 14 - 34 = -20$, so $h^{1,1} = 20$. The Hodge diamond has rows $1$; $0, 0$; $1, 20, 1$; $0, 0$; $1$, and Theorem P6.7.26 gives $\dim H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) = 1, 0, 22, 0, 1$ for $m = 0, \dots, 4$. Over $\mathbb{C}$ these are the Betti numbers of a K3 surface, with Euler number $24$.

**Example P6.7.32 (the affine and the punctured line).** Let $k$ have characteristic $0$. Both $\mathbb{A}^1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[x]$ and $\mathbb{G}_m = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, x^{-1}]$ are affine, so by Definition P6.7.24 their de Rham cohomology is that of the complex of global forms. For $\mathbb{A}^1$ it is $k[x] \to k[x]\, dx$, $f \mapsto f'\, dx$, with $H^0 = k$ and $H^1 = 0$, since $x^m\, dx = d(x^{m+1}/(m+1))$ (Example P2.3.7). For $\mathbb{G}_m$ the map $d(x^j) = j x^{j-1}\, dx$ hits every $x^i\, dx$ with $i \neq -1$ and kills only the constants, so $H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{G}_m/k) = k$ and $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{G}_m/k) = k \cdot [dx/x]$. Over $\mathbb{C}$ this matches $H^1(\mathbb{C}^\times, \mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}$ and $H^1(\mathbb{C}, \mathbb{C}) = 0$, as Theorem P6.7.28 says, and $\int_{|x| = 1} dx/x = 2\pi i \neq 0$. The Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of $\mathbb{G}_m$ does not degenerate at $E_1$: its only nonzero terms are $E_1^{0,0} = k[x, x^{-1}]$ and $E_1^{1,0} = k[x, x^{-1}]\, dx$, both of infinite dimension, and $d_1 = d$ is nonzero, since $d_1(x) = dx$. The identity $\dim H^1_{\mathrm{dR}} = h^{1,0} + h^{0,1}$ fails, with $1$ on the left and an infinite dimension on the right. What does hold is degeneration at $E_2$, for degree reasons, as for every affine scheme, and $E_2 = E_\infty$ is $k$ in bidegrees $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$. The hypothesis of properness in Theorem P6.7.26 cannot be dropped. The analytic de Rham complex with all holomorphic functions behaves differently term by term and the same in cohomology. On $\mathbb{C}$ the complex $\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}) \to \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\, dz$ is surjective in degree $1$, since a power series has a primitive with the same radius of convergence, so its $H^1$ is $0$, while $\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})$ contains $e^z$ and is much larger than $\mathbb{C}[z]$. On $\mathbb{C}^\times$ a form $f\, dz$ with Laurent series $f = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} a_n z^n$ is exact if and only if $a_{-1} = 0$: if $a_{-1} = 0$, then $\sum_n a_n z^{n+1}/(n+1)$ converges on $\mathbb{C}^\times$, and conversely $\oint_{|z|=1} f\, dz = 2\pi i\, a_{-1}$ vanishes for exact forms. So the analytic $H^1$ is again $\mathbb{C} \cdot [dz/z]$, and the inclusion of algebraic into holomorphic global forms is a quasi-isomorphism for $\mathbb{A}^1$ and $\mathbb{G}_m$ although it is an isomorphism in no degree.

**Example P6.7.33 (characteristic p).** Let $k$ have characteristic $p > 0$. The de Rham complex of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ is again $k[x] \to k[x]\, dx$, but now $d(x^m) = m x^{m-1}\, dx$ is zero exactly when $p \mid m$. So $H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{A}^1_k/k) = k[x^p]$, which is infinite dimensional, and the image of $d$ is spanned by the $x^{i}\, dx$ with $i \not\equiv -1 \pmod p$, so $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{A}^1_k/k)$ has the basis $[x^{mp-1}\, dx]$, $m \geq 1$, and is infinite dimensional too (Example P2.3.7 treats $m = 1$). In characteristic $0$ both groups are $k$ and $0$, as for the contractible space $\mathbb{C}$. Two hypotheses fail here. Lemma P6.7.21 has no analogue, since a field of characteristic $p$ admits no homomorphism to $\mathbb{C}$. And the Poincaré lemma fails for algebraic forms: the closed form $x^{p-1}\, dx$ is not exact on any open neighbourhood of a point, since localization of the computation above at a prime of $k[x]$ still leaves $d(x^p) = 0$ and $x^{p-1}\, dx$ outside the image. So no comparison of the type of Theorem P6.7.28 can hold for algebraic de Rham cohomology in characteristic $p$.

### Proof

*Lemma P6.7.21.* Let $t_1, \dots, t_r$ be a transcendence basis of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$; it is finite since $K$ is finitely generated, and $K$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}(t_1, \dots, t_r)$, being algebraic and finitely generated. Choose $a_1, \dots, a_r \in \mathbb{C}$ inductively with $a_{i+1}$ not algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}(a_1, \dots, a_i)$. This is possible: a countable field has countably many polynomials, each with finitely many roots, so its algebraic closure in $\mathbb{C}$ is countable, while $\mathbb{C}$ is uncountable. Then $a_1, \dots, a_r$ are algebraically independent, and $t_i \mapsto a_i$ defines an embedding $\sigma \colon \mathbb{Q}(t_1, \dots, t_r) \to \mathbb{C}$. Since the characteristic is $0$, the primitive element theorem gives $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)(\alpha)$ with minimal polynomial $f$ over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$. The polynomial $f^\sigma$ has a root $\beta \in \mathbb{C}$, since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, and $K \cong \mathbb{Q}(t)[T]/(f) \to \mathbb{C}$, $T \mapsto \beta$, extends $\sigma$.

*Proposition P6.7.22.* Two facts on a field extension $k_0 \subseteq k$ are used throughout. First, for a vector space $V$ over $k_0$ the map $V \to V \otimes_{k_0} k$ is injective; so a morphism of quasi-coherent sheaves on a scheme over $k_0$ is zero if its base change to $k$ is zero, as one sees on sections over affine open sets. Second, for a projective $X_1$ over $k_1$, coherent $\mathcal{G}_1$, $\mathcal{G}_2$ on $X_1$ and an extension $K \supseteq k_1$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{1,K}, \mathcal{G}_{2,K}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{G}_1, \mathcal{G}_2) \otimes_{k_1} K.$$
Indeed $\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{G}_1, \mathcal{G}_2)_K \cong \mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{G}_{1,K}, \mathcal{G}_{2,K})$, because $\mathcal{G}_1$ is of finite presentation and $K$ is flat over $k_1$ (Mat86, Theorem 7.11, applied on affine open sets), and $H^0$ commutes with flat base change by Theorem P4.1.17.

Part 1. By Corollary P3.6.15, $X = \operatorname{Proj} k[x_0, \dots, x_N]/I$ for a homogeneous ideal $I$, generated by finitely many homogeneous polynomials $G_1, \dots, G_s$ since $k[x]$ is Noetherian. Let $k_0$ be the field generated by their coefficients and $X_0 = \operatorname{Proj} k_0[x]/(G_1, \dots, G_s)$. On the chart $D_+(x_i)$ the coordinate ring of $X_0$ is $A_0 = k_0[x_j/x_i]/(G_l/x_i^{\deg G_l})$, and $A_0 \otimes_{k_0} k$ is the coordinate ring of $X$ on the same chart, since tensor product is right exact. These identifications are compatible with the gluing, so $X_{0,k} = X$ in $\mathbb{P}^N_k = (\mathbb{P}^N_{k_0})_k$ (Example P3.3.22).

Part 2. By Theorem P3.6.23 and Theorem P4.2.7, for $a \gg 0$ there is a surjection $\mathcal{O}_X(-a)^b \to \mathcal{F}$, and applying the same theorem to its coherent kernel gives a presentation $\mathcal{O}_X(-a')^{b'} \xrightarrow{M} \mathcal{O}_X(-a)^b \to \mathcal{F} \to 0$. The sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X(-a)^b$ and $\mathcal{O}_X(-a')^{b'}$ are base changes of the same sheaves on $X_1$, so by the second fact $M = \sum_i c_i \otimes M_i$ with $c_i \in k$ and $M_i$ morphisms on $X_1$, a finite sum. Let $k_0 = k_1(c_1, c_2, \dots)$, the field generated by these finitely many $c_i$, put $M_0 = \sum_i c_i M_i$ on $X_{1,k_0}$ and $\mathcal{F}_0 = \operatorname{coker} M_0$. Base change is right exact, so $(\mathcal{F}_0)_k \cong \operatorname{coker} M = \mathcal{F}$.

Part 3. By the second fact, $\varphi = \sum_i c_i \otimes \psi_i$ with finitely many $c_i \in k$ and $\psi_i \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{G}_1, \mathcal{G}_2)$. Put $k_0 = k_1(c_i)$ and $\varphi_0 = \sum_i c_i \psi_{i, k_0}$; then $(\varphi_0)_k = \varphi$. If $\varphi$ is an isomorphism, apply this also to $\varphi^{-1}$ and take $k_0$ to contain both sets of coefficients, giving $\varphi_0$ and $\chi_0$. The morphisms $\chi_0 \varphi_0 - \mathrm{id}$ and $\varphi_0 \chi_0 - \mathrm{id}$ have zero base change to $k$, so they are zero by the first fact, and $\varphi_0$ is an isomorphism.

Part 4. Projectivity is preserved because a closed immersion into $\mathbb{P}^N_{k_0}$ base changes to a closed immersion into $\mathbb{P}^N_K$ (Proposition P3.3.20, part 2). The projection $g \colon X_{0,K} \to X_0$ is flat, and it is surjective, since the fibre over $x$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(x) \otimes_{k_0} K)$ and the tensor product of two nonzero vector spaces is nonzero; so for $y \mapsto x$ the local map $\mathcal{O}_{X_0, x} \to \mathcal{O}_{X_{0,K}, y}$ is faithfully flat. By Theorem P3.12.2, part 4, $g^* \Omega_{X_0/k_0} \cong \Omega_{X_{0,K}/K}$. By Corollary P3.12.11, smoothness of pure dimension $n$ means pure dimension $n$ together with local freeness of rank $n$ of $\Omega$. If $X_0$ has these properties, so does $X_{0,K}$: purity by Lemma P3.7.23, and local freeness by pullback. Conversely, suppose $X_{0,K}$ is smooth of pure dimension $n$. If $M = \Omega_{X_0, x}$ and $B = \mathcal{O}_{X_{0,K}, y}$ with $y \mapsto x$, then $M \otimes B \cong B^n$ is free, and $\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathcal{O}_{X_0,x}}(N, M) \otimes B \cong \operatorname{Tor}_1^B(N \otimes B, M \otimes B) = 0$ for every module $N$, so $M$ is flat by faithful flatness, hence free of rank $n$, being finitely generated over a Noetherian local ring. For purity, let $Z$ be an irreducible component of $X_0$, of dimension $e$, and $U \subseteq Z$ the nonempty open set of points on no other component. Then $U_K$ is a nonempty open subset of $X_{0,K}$ contained in $Z_K$, and every irreducible component of $U_K$ is open in a component of $X_{0,K}$, of dimension $n$, and open in a component of $Z_K$, of dimension $e$ by Lemma P3.7.23. So $e = n$.

*Proposition P6.7.23.* Part 1 is Example P4.1.20, that is, Theorem P4.1.17 for the flat map $k \to K$. Part 2 is part 4 of Theorem P3.12.2 together with the compatibility of exterior powers with pullback. For part 3, the following step is used here and in the proof of Theorem P6.7.25.

*Step 1 (Čech computation of hypercohomology).* Let $Y$ be a topological space, $\mathcal{U} = (U_0, \dots, U_N)$ a finite open cover, and $K^\bullet$ a nonnegative complex of sheaves of abelian groups with $H^i(U_I, K^m) = 0$ for all $i > 0$, all $m$ and every finite intersection $U_I = U_{i_0 \cdots i_p}$. Let $C^{p,m} = \check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}, K^m)$ be the Čech double complex, with the cochains of Definition P2.7.18 indexed by $i_0 < \cdots < i_p$, so that $0 \leq p \leq N$. Then $\mathbb{H}^n(Y, K^\bullet) \cong H^n(\operatorname{Tot} C)$, naturally in $K^\bullet$. To prove it, let $K^\bullet \to I^{\bullet,\bullet}$ be a Cartan-Eilenberg resolution and $J^\bullet = \operatorname{Tot} I$, a complex of injective sheaves with a quasi-isomorphism $K^\bullet \to J^\bullet$ (Theorem P2.4.15, since each column resolves a term of $K^\bullet$), so that $\mathbb{H}^n(Y, K^\bullet) = H^n(\Gamma(Y, J^\bullet))$ by Definition P2.4.23. Let $D^{p,m} = \check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}, J^m)$, with the map $C \to D$. Filter $\operatorname{Tot} C$ and $\operatorname{Tot} D$ by the Čech degree $p$, a filtration of length $N + 1$. The graded pieces are $\prod_I \Gamma(U_I, K^\bullet)$ and $\prod_I \Gamma(U_I, J^\bullet)$, up to shift. The restriction of $I^{\bullet,\bullet}$ to $U_I$ is a Cartan-Eilenberg resolution of $K^\bullet|_{U_I}$, since restriction to an open set is exact and keeps injectives, so $H^m(\Gamma(U_I, J^\bullet)) = \mathbb{H}^m(U_I, K^\bullet)$. The first spectral sequence of Theorem P2.4.24 on $U_I$ has $E_1^{a,b} = H^b(U_I, K^a) = 0$ for $b > 0$, so its edge map $H^m(\Gamma(U_I, K^\bullet)) \to \mathbb{H}^m(U_I, K^\bullet)$ is an isomorphism. A map of complexes with finite filtrations that is a quasi-isomorphism on each graded piece is a quasi-isomorphism, by induction on the length with the five lemma applied to $0 \to F^{p+1} \to F^p \to \operatorname{gr}^p \to 0$. So $\operatorname{Tot} C \to \operatorname{Tot} D$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Next filter $\operatorname{Tot} D$ and $\Gamma(Y, J^\bullet)$ by the degree $m$ in $J^\bullet$, a filtration that is finite in each total degree. On graded pieces the map $\Gamma(Y, J^m) \to \check{C}^\bullet(\mathcal{U}, J^m)$ is a quasi-isomorphism by Proposition P2.7.19, part 1, and Theorem P2.7.20, since $J^m$ is injective. So $\Gamma(Y, J^\bullet) \to \operatorname{Tot} D$ is a quasi-isomorphism, and $H^n(\operatorname{Tot} C) \cong H^n(\operatorname{Tot} D) \cong \mathbb{H}^n(Y, K^\bullet)$. Naturality follows from the functoriality of Cartan-Eilenberg resolutions up to homotopy (Definition P2.4.23).

Now let $X$ be smooth and separated over $k$, and $\mathcal{U}$ a finite cover by affine open sets $U_i = \operatorname{Spec} A_i$. Every $U_I$ is affine since $X$ is separated, and $H^i(U_I, \Omega^m_X) = 0$ for $i > 0$ by Theorem P4.1.8. By Step 1, $H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) = H^n(\operatorname{Tot} \check{C}^\bullet(\mathcal{U}, \Omega^\bullet_{X/k}))$. The sets $U_{i,K}$ form an affine cover of $X_K$, with $U_{I,K} = (U_I)_K$, and for $U_I = \operatorname{Spec} A_I$, $\Omega^m_{A_I \otimes_k K / K} = \Omega^m_{A_I/k} \otimes_k K$ with exterior derivative $d \otimes \mathrm{id}$, by parts 1 and 4 of Theorem P3.12.2. So the Čech double complex of $X_K$ is that of $X$ tensored over $k$ with $K$, and since $- \otimes_k K$ is exact, $H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_K/K) \cong H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) \otimes_k K$. The equality $h^{p,q}(X_K) = h^{p,q}(X)$ is parts 1 and 2.

*Theorem P6.7.25.* Part 1 is part 1 of Theorem P6.7.5 applied to the coherent sheaf $\Omega^p_X$, with $(\Omega^p_X)^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \Omega^p_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$ by Proposition P6.7.3, together with the Dolbeault isomorphism $H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^p_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}) \cong H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ of Chapter 2.

Part 2. Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a finite cover of $X$ by affine open sets. Each $U_I$ is a smooth affine scheme, a closed subscheme of some $\mathbb{A}^M$, so $U_I^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a closed complex submanifold of $\mathbb{C}^M$ (Proposition P6.7.3), hence a Stein manifold (Hor90a, Chapter 5), and Cartan's Theorem B (Hor90a, Section 7.4) gives $H^i(U_I^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^m_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}) = 0$ for $i > 0$. So Step 1 applies on both sides: $H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbb{C})$ is the cohomology of $\operatorname{Tot} C$ with $C^{p,m} = \check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}, \Omega^m_X)$, and $\mathbb{H}^n(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$ is the cohomology of $\operatorname{Tot} C^{\mathrm{an}}$ with $C^{\mathrm{an}, p, m} = \check{C}^p(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^m_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$. The inclusion of algebraic forms on $U_I$ into holomorphic forms on $U_I^{\mathrm{an}}$ commutes with the Čech differential and with $d$, since the exterior derivative of a polynomial form is its holomorphic exterior derivative, so it gives a map of double complexes $C \to C^{\mathrm{an}}$. Filter both total complexes by the form degree $m$, a filtration of length $\dim X + 1$. The $m$-th graded pieces are the Čech complexes $\check{C}^\bullet(\mathcal{U}, \Omega^m_X)$ and $\check{C}^\bullet(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^m_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$, shifted by $m$, whose cohomology groups are $H^q(X, \Omega^m_X)$ and $H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^m_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$ by Theorem P2.7.21. The map between them is the comparison map $\varepsilon^q$ of Definition P6.7.2, by the naturality of the map from Čech cohomology to sheaf cohomology (Proposition P2.7.19, part 3), and it is an isomorphism by part 1. By the five lemma argument of Step 1, $C \to C^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a quasi-isomorphism of total complexes, which is the first isomorphism. For the second, the holomorphic Poincaré lemma of Chapter 1 says that on a polydisc every closed holomorphic $m$-form with $m \geq 1$ is exact, and a holomorphic function with $df = 0$ is constant; so $\mathbb{C} \to \Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$ is a resolution of the constant sheaf. The second spectral sequence of Theorem P2.4.24, with $E_2^{a,b} = H^a(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{H}^b(\Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}))$, is then concentrated in the row $b = 0$, where it is $H^a(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathbb{C})$, which gives $\mathbb{H}^n(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^\bullet_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}) \cong H^n(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathbb{C})$. On a manifold the cohomology of the constant sheaf $\mathbb{C}$ is de Rham cohomology of smooth forms, by the smooth Poincaré lemma and the fine resolution by smooth forms (GH78, Chapter 0, Section 3), so its dimension is $b_n(X^{\mathrm{an}})$.

*Theorem P6.7.26.* First let $k = \mathbb{C}$. Choose a closed immersion $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$. Then $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a closed complex submanifold of $\mathbb{P}^N$, and the restriction of the Fubini-Study form of Proposition P6.5.20 is a Kähler form on it, being closed and positive on every complex tangent space. So each connected component of $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a compact Kähler manifold, and the Hodge decomposition of Chapter 4 gives $H^m(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathbb{C}) = \bigoplus_{p+q=m} H^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ with $\overline{H^{p,q}} = H^{q,p}$ and $H^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cong H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^p_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})$. With Theorem P6.7.25,
$$\dim H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbb{C}) = b_m(X^{\mathrm{an}}) = \sum_{p+q=m} h^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) = \sum_{p+q=m} h^{p,q}(X),$$
and complex conjugation, a real linear bijection from $H^{p,q}$ onto $H^{q,p}$, gives $h^{p,q}(X) = h^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) = h^{q,p}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) = h^{q,p}(X)$. By the discussion after Definition P6.7.24 the equality of dimensions for all $m$ is degeneration at $E_1$.

For general $k$ of characteristic $0$, part 1 of Proposition P6.7.22 gives a model $X_0 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_{k_0}$ with $k_0$ finitely generated, and by part 4, $X_0$ is smooth and projective over $k_0$. By Lemma P6.7.21 there is an embedding $k_0 \to \mathbb{C}$; let $X_{\mathbb{C}} = X_{0,\mathbb{C}}$, smooth and projective over $\mathbb{C}$ by part 4. By Proposition P6.7.23, applied to $k_0 \subseteq k$ and to $k_0 \subseteq \mathbb{C}$,
$$h^{p,q}(X) = h^{p,q}(X_0) = h^{p,q}(X_{\mathbb{C}}), \qquad \dim_k H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) = \dim_{k_0} H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/k_0) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_{\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{C}).$$
The case $k = \mathbb{C}$ applied to $X_{\mathbb{C}}$ gives the equality of dimensions and the symmetry for $X$, and degeneration follows as before. For odd $m$, $\sum_{p+q=m} h^{p,q} = 2 \sum_{p < q,\, p+q=m} h^{p,q}$ is even.

*Corollary P6.7.27.* By Theorem P3.6.25 there is $m \geq 1$ with $L^m$ very ample, so there are a closed immersion $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_k$ and an isomorphism $u \colon L^{\otimes m} \cong \mathcal{O}_X(1)$. By part 1 of Proposition P6.7.22 there is a model $X_1 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_{k_1}$ of this embedding, by part 2 a finitely generated $k_2 \supseteq k_1$ and a line bundle $L_2$ on $X_{1,k_2}$ with $(L_2)_k \cong L$ (a coherent sheaf whose base change is invertible is invertible, by the faithful flatness argument of part 4), and by part 3, applied over $k_2$ to $L_2^{\otimes m}$ and $\mathcal{O}(1)$, a finitely generated $k_0 \supseteq k_2$ and an isomorphism $u_0 \colon L_0^{\otimes m} \cong \mathcal{O}_{X_0}(1)$, where $X_0 = X_{1,k_0}$ and $L_0 = (L_2)_{k_0}$. Embed $k_0$ into $\mathbb{C}$ by Lemma P6.7.21 and put $X_{\mathbb{C}} = X_{0,\mathbb{C}}$ and $L_{\mathbb{C}} = (L_0)_{\mathbb{C}}$. Then $X_{\mathbb{C}}$ is smooth and projective of pure dimension $n$ (part 4 of Proposition P6.7.22), and $L_{\mathbb{C}}^{\otimes m} \cong \mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathbb{C}}}(1)$ is very ample, so $L_{\mathbb{C}}$ is ample by Proposition P3.6.24. Each connected component $Y$ of $X_{\mathbb{C}}$ is a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$ with the ample line bundle $L_{\mathbb{C}}|_Y$, so Corollary P6.7.17 gives $H^q(Y, \Omega^p_Y \otimes L_{\mathbb{C}}|_Y) = 0$ for $p + q > n$, and cohomology of a finite disjoint union is the direct sum. Since $\Omega^p_X \otimes L = (\Omega^p_{X_0} \otimes L_0)_k$ and $\Omega^p_{X_{\mathbb{C}}} \otimes L_{\mathbb{C}} = (\Omega^p_{X_0} \otimes L_0)_{\mathbb{C}}$ by part 2 of Proposition P6.7.23, part 1 of that proposition gives
$$\dim_k H^q(X, \Omega^p_X \otimes L) = \dim_{k_0} H^q(X_0, \Omega^p_{X_0} \otimes L_0) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^q(X_{\mathbb{C}}, \Omega^p_{X_{\mathbb{C}}} \otimes L_{\mathbb{C}}) = 0$$
for $p + q > n$. The case $p = n$ is the second statement, since $\Omega^n_X = \omega_X$. For the third, Serre duality (Corollary P4.7.21, with $k$ perfect) gives $H^q(X, L^{-1}) \cong H^{n-q}(X, \omega_X \otimes L)'$, which vanishes for $q < n$.

*Theorem P6.7.28.* This is Grothendieck's theorem (Gro66a, Theorem 1 for affine $X$ and Theorem 1' in general). The proof embeds $X$ as an open subset of a smooth proper scheme whose complement is a divisor with normal crossings, by Hironaka's resolution of singularities, and compares the algebraic de Rham complex of $X$ with the complex of meromorphic forms with poles along the divisor; it is cited and not proved here. For smooth projective $X$ it is part 2 of Theorem P6.7.25. The second isomorphism is the holomorphic Poincaré lemma, as in that proof. For affine $X$ the identification of $H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbb{C})$ with the cohomology of global forms is the remark after Definition P6.7.24.

### Notes and history

The Lefschetz principle is named after Lefschetz, who used topology and complex analysis to prove theorems about algebraic varieties and held that the results remain valid over every algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. In the precise form of Lemma P6.7.21 and Propositions P6.7.22 and P6.7.23 it is a statement about descent of finitely presented data to finitely generated fields and flat base change of cohomology. Serre's GAGA (Ser56) supplied the comparison of coherent cohomology that makes it work for Hodge numbers. Algebraic de Rham cohomology and Theorem P6.7.28 are due to Grothendieck (Gro66a), in a paper written as a letter to Atiyah; the statement for smooth projective varieties, Theorem P6.7.25, needs only GAGA and is the case treated in Voisin, Chapter 8 (Voi02a), and Huybrechts, Chapter 3 (Huy05). Hartshorne, Chapter III (Har77), contains the algebraic results on cohomology used here. The degeneration of Theorem P6.7.26 over a field of characteristic $0$ was proved by Hodge theory and the Lefschetz principle, as here, until Deligne and Illusie (DI87) found an algebraic proof by reduction modulo $p$, through smooth projective varieties in characteristic $p > \dim X$ that lift to $W_2(k)$; their method also gives Corollary P6.7.27 without analysis. In characteristic $p$ both statements can fail, and Raynaud (Ray78) constructed smooth projective surfaces in characteristic $p$ with an ample line bundle $L$ and $H^1(X, L^{-1}) \neq 0$. The literature state of all results of this section is theorem. Example P6.7.33 shows the basic obstruction in characteristic $p$: the Poincaré lemma fails for algebraic forms, because $d(x^p) = 0$.
