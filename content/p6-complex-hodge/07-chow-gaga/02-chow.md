## Chow's theorem and its consequences

### Statement

As in Section 7.1, schemes are of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$, and $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is the analytification of Definition P6.7.2. A *projective variety* is an integral projective scheme over $\mathbb{C}$. For a closed analytic subset $Z$ of an analytic space $Y$, the *ideal sheaf* $\mathcal{I}_Z \subseteq \mathcal{O}_Y$ is the sheaf of holomorphic functions that vanish at every point of $Z$.

**Theorem P6.7.12 (Chow).** Let $Z \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{n, \mathrm{an}}$ be a closed analytic subset. There is a unique reduced closed subscheme $W \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ with $W(\mathbb{C}) = Z$. More generally, the same holds with $\mathbb{P}^n$ replaced by any projective scheme $X$ and $Z \subseteq X^{\mathrm{an}}$ a closed analytic subset.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.7.13 (line bundles are algebraic).** Let $X$ be a projective scheme over $\mathbb{C}$. Then $\mathcal{L} \mapsto \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is an isomorphism $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \operatorname{Pic}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ from the group of invertible sheaves to the group of holomorphic line bundles of Definition P6.5.1, and $H^0(X, \mathcal{L}) \cong H^0(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}})$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.7.14 (meromorphic functions are rational).** Let $X$ be a connected smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$. Every meromorphic function on the complex manifold $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a rational function on $X$, so the field of meromorphic functions of $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is the function field $\mathbb{C}(X)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.7.15 (holomorphic maps are algebraic).** Let $X$ and $Y$ be reduced projective schemes over $\mathbb{C}$ and $\varphi \colon X^{\mathrm{an}} \to Y^{\mathrm{an}}$ a holomorphic map. There is a unique morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ with $f^{\mathrm{an}} = \varphi$. In particular, $X$ and $Y$ are isomorphic as schemes if and only if $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ and $Y^{\mathrm{an}}$ are biholomorphic.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.7.16 (compact manifolds with a positive line bundle).** Let $M$ be a compact complex manifold that carries a positive holomorphic line bundle, for instance a compact Kähler manifold whose Kähler class is rational, or a compact Kähler manifold with $h^{2,0} = 0$. Then there is a smooth projective variety $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$ with $X^{\mathrm{an}} \cong M$, unique up to isomorphism, and every holomorphic line bundle, coherent analytic sheaf, meromorphic function and holomorphic map to a projective variety on $M$ is algebraic.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.7.17 (Kodaira vanishing for ample line bundles).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$ of dimension $n$ and $L$ an ample line bundle on $X$. Then
$$H^q(X, \Omega^p_X \otimes L) = 0 \quad \text{for } p + q > n, \qquad \text{in particular} \quad H^q(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = 0 \quad \text{for } q > 0.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Chow's theorem is GAGA applied to one sheaf. The ideal sheaf of a closed analytic subset is coherent by Cartan's theorem, so the structure sheaf of the reduced subspace is a coherent analytic quotient of $\mathcal{O}$. By GAGA this quotient and the quotient map are algebraic, the kernel of the algebraic quotient map is an ideal sheaf, and its zero set is the given analytic set, since analytification does not change supports.

The corollaries follow the same pattern. An invertible analytic sheaf is the analytification of a coherent sheaf, and faithful flatness shows that this sheaf is invertible. A meromorphic function on a manifold has a divisor of poles, its product with the canonical section of the line bundle of that divisor is holomorphic, and so it is a quotient of two holomorphic sections of a line bundle, both algebraic by GAGA. The graph of a holomorphic map is a closed analytic subset of a product of projective varieties, hence algebraic, and its projection to the source is a finite morphism whose analytification is an isomorphism, hence an isomorphism. Kodaira's embedding theorem of Chapter 6 places a compact manifold with a positive line bundle in projective space as a closed submanifold, and Chow's theorem makes it algebraic. Finally, an ample line bundle has a very ample power, which is positive, so Kodaira's vanishing theorem applies on $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, and GAGA carries the vanishing back to $X$.

### Examples

**Example P6.7.18 (the graph of the exponential).** Let $Z = \{(z, w) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : w = e^z\}$, a closed complex submanifold of $\mathbb{C}^2 = \mathbb{A}^{2, \mathrm{an}}$. No nonzero polynomial vanishes on $Z$. Let $P(z, w) = \sum_{j=0}^d a_j(z) w^j$ with $a_d \neq 0$ and suppose $P(z, e^z) = 0$ for all $z$. For real $z = x \to +\infty$, $|a_d(x) e^{dx}| \geq c\, x^{-N} e^{dx}$ for some $c > 0$ and $N$, while $\sum_{j < d} |a_j(x)| e^{jx} \leq C x^{N'} e^{(d-1)x}$, and the quotient of the second by the first tends to $0$. This contradicts $P(x, e^x) = 0$. So the Zariski closure of $Z$ is $\mathbb{A}^2$, and $Z$ is not the set of points of a closed subscheme: Chow's theorem fails in affine space. The closure $\overline{Z}$ of $Z$ in $\mathbb{P}^{2,\mathrm{an}}$ is not analytic. If it were, Theorem P6.7.12 would give a reduced closed subscheme $W \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ with $W(\mathbb{C}) = \overline{Z}$. Since $Z$ is closed in $\mathbb{C}^2$, $\overline{Z} \cap \mathbb{C}^2 = Z \neq \mathbb{C}^2$, so $W \cap \mathbb{A}^2$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{A}^2$ whose set of points $Z$ is not all of $\mathbb{C}^2$. Its ideal is therefore nonzero, and a nonzero polynomial in it vanishes on $Z$, which is impossible.

**Example P6.7.19 (a torus that is not algebraic).** Let $T = \mathbb{C}^2 / \Lambda$ be the complex torus of Example P6.5.17, with Néron-Severi group $0$. Suppose $T \cong X^{\mathrm{an}}$ for a projective scheme $X$. Then $X$ is a smooth connected projective surface by Proposition P6.7.3, and a very ample $\mathcal{L}$ on $X$ gives a holomorphic line bundle $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}$, positive by Proposition P6.5.22. Its first Chern class is represented by a positive form $\alpha$, so $\int_T \alpha \wedge \alpha > 0$ and $c_1(\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}) \neq 0$ in $H^2(T, \mathbb{R})$. This contradicts $\operatorname{NS}(T) = 0$. So $T$ is a compact Kähler manifold that is not the analytification of any projective scheme, and by Corollary P6.7.16 it carries no positive line bundle. Proper schemes do not help either: $T$ is not the analytification of a proper scheme $X$, since $X$ would be a smooth proper surface, and smooth proper surfaces over $\mathbb{C}$ are projective (Har77, Appendix B).

**Example P6.7.20 (meromorphic functions on the sphere).** Let $f$ be meromorphic on $\mathbb{P}^1$. Its poles are isolated in a compact space, hence finite in number; replacing $f$ by $1/(f - c)$ for a suitable $c$ if needed, $\infty$ is not a pole. Let $a_1, \dots, a_r$ be the poles in $\mathbb{C}$ and $p_i(z)$ the principal part of $f$ at $a_i$, a polynomial in $(z - a_i)^{-1}$ without constant term. Then $g = f - \sum_i p_i$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ and bounded near $\infty$, hence constant by Liouville's theorem, and $f = g + \sum_i p_i$ is rational. This is Corollary P6.7.14 for $X = \mathbb{P}^1$. The polar divisor in the proof of the corollary is $D = \sum_i m_i a_i$, with $m_i$ the pole orders, the line bundle is $\mathcal{O}(\deg D)$, and $f = t / s_D$ with $t$ and $s_D$ homogeneous polynomials of degree $\deg D$.

### Proof

*Theorem P6.7.12.* Let $X$ be projective and $Z \subseteq X^{\mathrm{an}}$ closed analytic. By Cartan's coherence theorem, $\mathcal{I}_Z$ is a coherent analytic sheaf of ideals (GR84a, Chapter 4), so $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}} / \mathcal{I}_Z$ is coherent, with support $Z$: at $x \notin Z$ the function $1$ vanishes on $Z$ near $x$, and at $x \in Z$ every element of $\mathcal{I}_{Z, x}$ vanishes at $x$. By part 3 of Theorem P6.7.5, $\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}_Z \cong \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ for a coherent $\mathcal{F}$, and by part 2 the quotient map $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}} \to \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is $u^{\mathrm{an}}$ for a morphism $u \colon \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{F}$. The cokernel of $u$ has zero analytification, so $u$ is surjective by Proposition P6.7.3; its kernel $\mathcal{J}$ is a coherent ideal with $\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{an}} = \mathcal{I}_Z$. Let $W'$ be the closed subscheme of $\mathcal{J}$ (Proposition P3.5.23), so $\mathcal{O}_{W'} = \mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{J}$, and $W = W'_{\mathrm{red}}$. A closed point $x$ lies in $W$ if and only if $(\mathcal{O}_X / \mathcal{J})_x \neq 0$, if and only if, by faithful flatness, $(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}_Z)_x \neq 0$, if and only if $x \in Z$. So $W(\mathbb{C}) = Z$. A reduced closed subscheme of a scheme of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$ is determined by its closed points, since closed points are dense in every closed subset, so $W$ is unique.

*Corollary P6.7.13.* Analytification commutes with tensor products, so $\mathcal{L} \mapsto \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a homomorphism. If $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}} \cong \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}$, the isomorphism and its inverse are analytifications of maps $\mathcal{L} \to \mathcal{O}_X$ and $\mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{L}$ by part 2 of Theorem P6.7.5, whose composites are the identities by faithfulness, so $\mathcal{L} \cong \mathcal{O}_X$. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a holomorphic line bundle, a coherent analytic sheaf, and $\mathcal{M} \cong \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}$ by part 3. At a closed point $x$, put $A = \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ and $B = \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}, x}$. For every $A$-module $N$, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N, \mathcal{F}_x) \otimes_A B \cong \operatorname{Tor}_1^B(N \otimes_A B, \mathcal{F}_x \otimes_A B) = 0$, since $B$ is flat over $A$ and $\mathcal{F}_x \otimes_A B \cong B$ is free. By faithful flatness, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N, \mathcal{F}_x) = 0$, so $\mathcal{F}_x$ is a finitely generated flat module over a Noetherian local ring, hence free, of rank $1$ since $\mathcal{F}_x \otimes_A B \cong B$. So $\mathcal{F}$ is invertible near every closed point, hence everywhere, since the locus where a coherent sheaf is locally free of rank $1$ is open and contains all closed points. The statement on sections is part 1 of Theorem P6.7.5 with $q = 0$.

*Corollary P6.7.14.* A rational function is holomorphic on the dense open set where it is regular, and locally a quotient of regular functions, so it is meromorphic. Conversely, let $f$ be meromorphic on $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, which is a connected compact complex manifold by Proposition P6.7.3. The local rings $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}, x}$ are factorial by Theorem P6.1.28, which rests on the Weierstrass preparation theorem, so near each point $f = g/h$ with $g$, $h$ coprime, unique up to a common unit, and the divisors of $h$ glue to an effective divisor $D$, the divisor of poles. By Proposition P6.5.4, $D$ defines a holomorphic line bundle $\mathcal{O}(D)$ with a section $s_D$, locally $h$ up to a unit, and $t = f s_D$, locally $g$ up to the same unit, is a holomorphic section of $\mathcal{O}(D)$. By Corollary P6.7.13, $\mathcal{O}(D) \cong \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}$ and $s_D$, $t$ are analytifications of sections $s$, $t'$ of $\mathcal{L}$. The variety $X$ is irreducible, being smooth and connected, and $s \neq 0$, so $t'/s$ is a rational function on $X$ whose analytification agrees with $f$ on the dense open set where $s_D \neq 0$, hence everywhere.

*Corollary P6.7.15.* Choose closed immersions $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^a$ and $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^b$. The product $X \times Y$ is projective, by the Segre embedding (Theorem P3.10.20), and $(X \times Y)^{\mathrm{an}} = X^{\mathrm{an}} \times Y^{\mathrm{an}}$, since both represent the same functor on analytic spaces (SGA1, Exposé XII, Section 1). The graph $\Gamma = \{(x, \varphi(x))\}$ is the preimage of the diagonal of $Y^{\mathrm{an}}$, which is closed analytic as the analytification of the diagonal of the separated scheme $Y$, under the holomorphic map $\varphi \times \mathrm{id}$; so $\Gamma$ is closed analytic. By Theorem P6.7.12, $\Gamma = W(\mathbb{C})$ for a reduced closed subscheme $W \subseteq X \times Y$. Let $p \colon W \to X$ be the first projection. The map $\gamma = (\mathrm{id}, \varphi) \colon X^{\mathrm{an}} \to X^{\mathrm{an}} \times Y^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a closed embedding with image $\Gamma$, since it has the left inverse $\mathrm{pr}_1$, and $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is reduced by Proposition P6.7.3; so $\gamma$ is an isomorphism of $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ onto the reduced analytic subspace $W^{\mathrm{an}}$ supported on $\Gamma$, with inverse $p^{\mathrm{an}}$. The morphism $p$ is projective and bijective on closed points, so its fibres are finite, and by Corollary P4.6.17 it is finite. By Theorem P6.7.6, $(p_* \mathcal{O}_W)^{\mathrm{an}} = p^{\mathrm{an}}_* \mathcal{O}_{W^{\mathrm{an}}}$, and $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}} \to p^{\mathrm{an}}_* \mathcal{O}_{W^{\mathrm{an}}}$ is an isomorphism since $p^{\mathrm{an}}$ is. By Proposition P6.7.3 the map $\mathcal{O}_X \to p_* \mathcal{O}_W$ is an isomorphism, and since $p$ is affine, $p$ is an isomorphism. Put $f = \mathrm{pr}_2 \circ p^{-1}$; then $f^{\mathrm{an}} = \mathrm{pr}_2 \circ \gamma = \varphi$. Two morphisms from the reduced scheme $X$ to the separated scheme $Y$ that agree on closed points agree, since the locus where they agree is closed and contains the dense set of closed points, and a reduced scheme maps through it. The last statement follows by applying the first to a biholomorphism and its inverse.

*Corollary P6.7.16.* By Theorem P6.6.21 a positive line bundle $L$ on $M$ has a power that defines a holomorphic embedding $\iota \colon M \to \mathbb{P}^N$ onto a closed complex submanifold. The two examples in the statement carry positive line bundles by Corollaries P6.6.22 and P6.6.23. By Theorem P6.7.12, $\iota(M) = X(\mathbb{C})$ for a reduced closed subscheme $X$, and $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is the reduced analytic subspace supported on $\iota(M)$, which is $\iota(M)$ with its manifold structure. So $X^{\mathrm{an}} \cong M$, and $X$ is smooth by Proposition P6.7.3; it is connected if $M$ is. Uniqueness is Corollary P6.7.15. Line bundles and coherent sheaves are algebraic by Theorem P6.7.5 and Corollary P6.7.13, meromorphic functions by Corollary P6.7.14, and holomorphic maps by Corollary P6.7.15.

*Corollary P6.7.17.* By Theorem P3.6.25, $L^m$ is very ample for some $m \geq 1$. By Proposition P6.5.22, $(L^m)^{\mathrm{an}} = (L^{\mathrm{an}})^m$ carries a Hermitian metric $h_m$ with positive curvature, and $h = h_m^{1/m}$ is a Hermitian metric on $L^{\mathrm{an}}$ with curvature $\frac1m \Theta_{h_m}$, so $L^{\mathrm{an}}$ is positive. By Theorem P6.6.12 on the compact complex manifold $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, $H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, \Omega^p_{X^{\mathrm{an}}} \otimes L^{\mathrm{an}}) = 0$ for $p + q > n$. By Proposition P6.7.3, $(\Omega^p_X \otimes L)^{\mathrm{an}} = \Omega^p_{X^{\mathrm{an}}} \otimes L^{\mathrm{an}}$, and part 1 of Theorem P6.7.5 gives the vanishing on $X$. For $p = n$, $\Omega^n_X = \omega_X$; this is Corollary P6.6.13 transported to $X$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $Z \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{2,\mathrm{an}}$ be the zero set of the holomorphic section $x_0^2 x_2 - x_1^3$ of $\mathcal{O}(3)$. Find the reduced subscheme $W$ of Theorem P6.7.12 and its ideal, and check that $Z$ is the cuspidal cubic.
2. (A) Show that every holomorphic map $\mathbb{P}^{1} \to \mathbb{P}^{1}$ is given by $(x_0 : x_1) \mapsto (P(x_0, x_1) : Q(x_0, x_1))$ for coprime homogeneous polynomials of the same degree, using Corollary P6.7.15, and also directly from Example P6.7.20.
3. (B) Prove that $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^{1,\mathrm{an}} \times \mathbb{P}^{1,\mathrm{an}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, using Corollary P6.7.13 and the algebraic computation of the Picard group of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$.
4. (B) Let $f \colon \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ be an entire function that is not a polynomial. Show that its graph is a closed analytic subset of $\mathbb{C}^2$ whose closure in $\mathbb{P}^2$ is not analytic, by adapting Example P6.7.18 with the Casorati-Weierstrass theorem at infinity.
5. (B) Let $E = \mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z} + \tau \mathbb{Z})$ with $\operatorname{Im} \tau > 0$. Using the Weierstrass function $\wp$ and its derivative, write down a holomorphic embedding of $E$ into $\mathbb{P}^2$, and identify by Chow's theorem the algebraic curve that is its image.
6. (C) Let $M$ be a compact complex manifold of dimension $n$ and $a(M)$ the transcendence degree over $\mathbb{C}$ of its field of meromorphic functions. Show that $a(M) = n$ if $M$ carries a positive line bundle, using Corollaries P6.7.14 and P6.7.16. Then show that the torus $T$ of Example P6.7.19 has $a(T) = 0$: the polar divisor $D$ of a nonconstant meromorphic function is a nonzero effective divisor, and $\int_T c_1(\mathcal{O}(D)) \wedge \omega = \int_D \omega > 0$ for a Kähler form $\omega$, which contradicts $\operatorname{NS}(T) = 0$.

### Lean

Mathlib has no analytic subsets of complex projective space, no coherent analytic sheaves and no comparison between holomorphic and algebraic maps, so neither Chow's theorem nor its corollaries are formalized. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Chow proved the theorem in 1949 (Cho49a) by a geometric argument with projections, before the Remmert-Stein extension theorem for analytic sets was available. Remmert and Stein later gave the proof through extension of the cone over $Z$ to $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ and Riemann's extension theorem, which is the proof in Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 1, Section 3 (GH78). Serre's proof through GAGA (Ser56, Section 19) is the one given here, and it makes the theorem a statement about coherent sheaves. The literature state of all results of this section is theorem. Corollary P6.7.16 combines Kodaira's embedding theorem (Kod54a) with Chow's theorem; it is the reason that the analytic theory of Chapters 4 to 6 applies to projective varieties, and it is the source of the slogan that compact Kähler manifolds with rational Kähler class are algebraic. Corollary P6.7.17 is the analytic proof of Theorem P7.8.2 over $\mathbb{C}$, and the field change in the proof of that theorem, made precise in Section 7.3, gives every algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. Corollary P6.7.15 fails without compactness: the map $z \mapsto e^z$ from $\mathbb{C}$ to $\mathbb{C}^\times$ is holomorphic and not algebraic.
