## The Dolbeault-Grothendieck lemma

### Statement

Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ be open. For finite sets $I = \{ i_1 < \dots < i_p \}$ and $J = \{ j_1 < \dots < j_q \}$ of $\{1, \dots, n\}$ write $dz_I = dz_{i_1} \wedge \dots \wedge dz_{i_p}$ and $d\bar z_J = d\bar z_{j_1} \wedge \dots \wedge d\bar z_{j_q}$. The forms $dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J$ with $|I| + |J| = k$ form a basis of the complexified exterior algebra $\Lambda^k (\mathbb{R}^{2n})^* \otimes \mathbb{C}$, since $dx_j = (dz_j + d\bar z_j)/2$ and $dy_j = (dz_j - d\bar z_j)/(2i)$.

**Definition P6.1.32 (forms of type (p,q)).** $\mathcal{A}^k(U)$ is the space of smooth complex-valued $k$-forms on $U$, and $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(U)$ the subspace of forms $\alpha = \sum_{|I| = p, |J| = q} \alpha_{IJ} \, dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J$ with $\alpha_{IJ} \in C^\infty(U)$, the forms of *type $(p,q)$*. So $\mathcal{A}^k(U) = \bigoplus_{p+q=k} \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(U)$; the component of type $(p,q)$ of $\alpha$ is written $\alpha^{p,q}$. A $(p,0)$-form is *holomorphic* if its coefficients are holomorphic, and the holomorphic $p$-forms form the sheaf $\Omega^p_U$, with $\Omega^0_U = \mathcal{O}_U$. The sheaves $U \mapsto \mathcal{A}^k(U)$ and $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(U)$ are written $\mathcal{A}^k_U$ and $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}_U$. Complex conjugation of coefficients extends to forms and maps $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}$ to $\mathcal{A}^{q,p}$; a form is *real* if it equals its conjugate. For a form $\alpha$ and a coordinate index $j$, $\partial \alpha / \partial \bar z_j$ denotes the form obtained by differentiating each coefficient.

**Proposition P6.1.33 (the operators d, ∂ and ∂̄).** Define $\partial, \bar\partial$ on $\alpha = \sum \alpha_{IJ} dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(U)$ by
$$\partial \alpha = \sum_{I,J} \sum_j \frac{\partial \alpha_{IJ}}{\partial z_j} dz_j \wedge dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J \in \mathcal{A}^{p+1,q}(U), \qquad \bar\partial \alpha = \sum_{I,J} \sum_j \frac{\partial \alpha_{IJ}}{\partial \bar z_j} d\bar z_j \wedge dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q+1}(U),$$
and extend them additively to $\mathcal{A}^k(U)$. Then:

1. $d = \partial + \bar\partial$.
2. $\partial^2 = 0$, $\bar\partial^2 = 0$ and $\partial \bar\partial = - \bar\partial \partial$.
3. (Leibniz rule.) $\bar\partial (\alpha \wedge \beta) = \bar\partial \alpha \wedge \beta + (-1)^k \alpha \wedge \bar\partial \beta$ for $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}^k(U)$, and the same for $\partial$.
4. If $F \colon V \to U$ is a holomorphic map of open sets, $F^*$ maps $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(U)$ to $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(V)$ and commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$.
5. $\overline{\partial \alpha} = \bar\partial \bar\alpha$.
6. A $(p,0)$-form $\alpha$ is holomorphic if and only if $\bar\partial \alpha = 0$. So $\Omega^p(U) = \ker (\bar\partial \colon \mathcal{A}^{p,0}(U) \to \mathcal{A}^{p,1}(U))$.

**Theorem P6.1.34 (Poincaré lemma).** Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ be open and star-shaped with respect to $0$, and let $\alpha$ be a smooth closed complex-valued $k$-form on $U$ with $k \geq 1$. Then $\alpha = d\beta$ for a smooth $(k-1)$-form $\beta$ on $U$. A closed $0$-form on $U$ is constant.

**Proposition P6.1.35 (Laurent expansion on products of discs and annuli).** Let $D = D_1 \times \dots \times D_n$, where each $D_j \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is either a disc $\{ |z| < R_j \}$ or an annulus $\{ r_j < |z| < R_j \}$ with $0 \leq r_j < R_j \leq \infty$. Every $f \in \mathcal{O}(D)$ has a unique expansion $f = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}^n} c_\alpha z^\alpha$, converging absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of $D$, with $c_\alpha = 0$ whenever $\alpha_j < 0$ for some $j$ such that $D_j$ is a disc. In particular $f$ is a limit, uniformly on compact subsets of $D$, of Laurent polynomials that are holomorphic on $D$.

**Theorem P6.1.36 (Dolbeault-Grothendieck lemma).** Let $D = D_1 \times \dots \times D_n$ be a product of discs and annuli as in Proposition P6.1.35, with radii in $[0, \infty]$. Let $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(D)$ with $q \geq 1$ and $\bar\partial \alpha = 0$. There is $\beta \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(D)$ with $\bar\partial \beta = \alpha$. By conjugation, a form $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(D)$ with $p \geq 1$ and $\partial \alpha = 0$ is $\partial \gamma$ for some $\gamma \in \mathcal{A}^{p-1,q}(D)$.

**Corollary P6.1.37 (the Dolbeault resolution).** For every open $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ and every $p$, the sequence of sheaves on $U$
$$0 \to \Omega^p_U \to \mathcal{A}^{p,0}_U \xrightarrow{\bar\partial} \mathcal{A}^{p,1}_U \xrightarrow{\bar\partial} \cdots \xrightarrow{\bar\partial} \mathcal{A}^{p,n}_U \to 0$$
is exact. By part 4 of Proposition P6.1.33 the same holds on every complex manifold, with the sheaves of Chapter 2.

**Corollary P6.1.38 (the local ddbar-lemma).** Let $\Delta = \Delta(a, r)$ be a polydisc with $r \in (0, \infty]^n$, and let $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(\Delta)$ with $p, q \geq 1$ and $d\alpha = 0$. There is $\gamma \in \mathcal{A}^{p-1,q-1}(\Delta)$ with $\alpha = \partial \bar\partial \gamma$. If $\alpha$ is a real closed $(1,1)$-form, then $\alpha = i \partial \bar\partial \varphi$ for a real function $\varphi \in C^\infty(\Delta)$.

### Proof in words

The operators $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$ are the two type components of $d$, and their formal properties follow from those of $d$ by sorting types. The Poincaré lemma comes from a homotopy operator for the contraction to $0$. The Dolbeault-Grothendieck lemma is proved in two steps. Locally, on a slightly smaller product, the form is reduced one variable at a time: if $\alpha$ involves only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_k$, then $\bar\partial \alpha = 0$ makes its coefficients holomorphic in $z_{k+1}, \dots, z_n$, and the Cauchy integral of Theorem P6.1.8 in the variable $z_k$, applied to the cut-off coefficients of $d\bar z_k$, removes $d\bar z_k$ without destroying holomorphy in the later variables. Globally, $D$ is exhausted by an increasing sequence of smaller products, and the local solutions are corrected so that they stabilize. For $q \geq 2$ the corrections are $\bar\partial$-exact forms cut off by a bump function. For $q = 1$ the difference of two solutions is a holomorphic form, and it is approximated by Laurent polynomials on $D$, which makes the corrected solutions converge. The $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma on a polydisc combines the Poincaré lemma with the Dolbeault-Grothendieck lemma for $\bar\partial$ and for $\partial$ to remove the unwanted type components of a primitive.

### Examples

**Example P6.1.39 (solving dbar on the plane).** Let $g(z) = \phi(|z|^2)$ with $\phi \in C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R})$, and put $H(s) = \int_0^s \phi(\sigma) \, d\sigma$. Since $H(s) = s h(s)$ with $h(s) = \int_0^1 \phi(s t) \, dt$ smooth, the function $u(z) = H(|z|^2)/z = \bar z \, h(|z|^2)$ is smooth on $\mathbb{C}$, and
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial \bar z} = \frac{1}{z} H'(z \bar z) \, z = \phi(|z|^2) = g.$$
Outside the support of $g$, $u = c/z$ with $c = \int_0^\infty \phi(\sigma) \, d\sigma = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{\mathbb{C}} g \, dx\, dy$, by polar coordinates. This is the solution of Theorem P6.1.8: the difference of the two solutions is an entire function, both tend to $0$ at infinity (the solution of Theorem P6.1.8 is bounded by $\frac{1}{\pi} \int |g(w)| \, |w - z|^{-1} \, ds\, dt$), and the difference vanishes by Liouville's theorem. So the form $\alpha = g \, d\bar z \in \mathcal{A}^{0,1}(\mathbb{C})$ is $\bar\partial u$, and by Example P6.1.17 it is not $\bar\partial$ of a compactly supported function when $c \neq 0$. Two polynomial cases: $d\bar z = \bar\partial \bar z$, and $\bar z \, dz \wedge d\bar z = \bar\partial(-\frac12 \bar z^2 \, dz)$, since $\bar\partial(\bar z^2 dz) = 2 \bar z \, d\bar z \wedge dz$.

**Example P6.1.40 (the annulus).** Let $A = \{ r < |z| < R \}$ with $0 \leq r < R \leq \infty$, for instance $\mathbb{C}^* = \{ 0 < |z| < \infty \}$. The $(1,0)$-form $dz/z$ is holomorphic, so $\bar\partial (dz/z) = 0$, and it is $d$-closed. It is not $d$-exact: $\int_{|z| = \rho} dz / z = 2 \pi i$ for $r < \rho < R$, while the integral of an exact form over a closed curve is $0$. So the Poincaré lemma fails on the annulus, which is not star-shaped. The form is $\partial$-exact, as the conjugate statement of Theorem P6.1.36 predicts: $\log |z|^2$ is smooth on $A$ and $\partial \log(z \bar z) = \bar z \, dz / (z \bar z) = dz / z$. Corollary P6.1.38 fails on products of annuli. On $(\mathbb{C}^*)^2$ the $(1,1)$-form $\alpha = \frac{dz_1}{z_1} \wedge \frac{d\bar z_2}{\bar z_2}$ is $d$-closed, since its coefficient $1/(z_1 \bar z_2)$ is holomorphic in $z_1$ and antiholomorphic in $z_2$, so $d$ of it only produces terms containing $dz_1$ or $d\bar z_2$ a second time. If $\alpha = \partial\bar\partial\gamma$, then $\alpha = d(\bar\partial \gamma)$ because $\bar\partial^2 = 0$, and Stokes' theorem on the torus $T = \{|z_1| = |z_2| = 1\}$ would give $\int_T \alpha = 0$. But by Fubini $\int_T \alpha = \int_{|z_1|=1} \frac{dz_1}{z_1} \int_{|z_2| = 1} \frac{d\bar z_2}{\bar z_2} = (2\pi i)(-2\pi i) = 4 \pi^2$. On products of annuli, $\bar\partial$-problems are solvable but the $d$-cohomology is not zero, and the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma needs both.

**Example P6.1.41 (the punctured plane C^2 \ {0}).** Theorem P6.1.36 fails on $X = \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, which is not a product. Let
$$\beta_0 = \frac{\bar z_1 \, d\bar z_2 - \bar z_2 \, d\bar z_1}{|z|^4} \in \mathcal{A}^{0,1}(X).$$
Then $\bar\partial \beta_0 = \bigl( \frac{\partial}{\partial \bar z_1} \frac{\bar z_1}{|z|^4} + \frac{\partial}{\partial \bar z_2} \frac{\bar z_2}{|z|^4} \bigr) d\bar z_1 \wedge d\bar z_2$, and $\frac{\partial}{\partial \bar z_j} (\bar z_j |z|^{-4}) = |z|^{-4} - 2 |z_j|^2 |z|^{-6}$, so the bracket is $2|z|^{-4} - 2 |z|^{-4} = 0$. Suppose $\beta_0 = \bar\partial f$ with $f \in C^\infty(X)$. Then $\beta_0 \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2 = \bar\partial f \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2 = d(f \, dz_1 \wedge dz_2)$, because $\partial f \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2$ is of type $(3,0)$ and vanishes. So the integral over the unit sphere $S^3$ of the closed $3$-form $\beta_0 \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2$ would be $0$. On $S^3$, $|z| = 1$, and by Stokes' theorem on the unit ball $B$,
$$\int_{S^3} (\bar z_1 \, d\bar z_2 - \bar z_2 \, d\bar z_1) \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2 = \int_B 2 \, d\bar z_1 \wedge d\bar z_2 \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2 = 8 \operatorname{vol}(B) = 4 \pi^2,$$
using $d\bar z_1 \wedge d\bar z_2 \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2 = -(d\bar z_1 \wedge dz_1) \wedge (d\bar z_2 \wedge dz_2) = -(2i)^2 dx_1 \wedge dy_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge dy_2$ and $\operatorname{vol}(B) = \pi^2/2$. This contradiction shows that $\beta_0$ is $\bar\partial$-closed and not $\bar\partial$-exact on $X$, whereas on $\mathbb{C}^2$ every $\bar\partial$-closed $(0,1)$-form is exact. Example P6.2.28 computes the whole space $H^{0,1}(X)$, which is infinite dimensional.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P6.1.33.* Part 1: for a function, $df = \partial f + \bar\partial f$ is the formula of Definition P6.1.2, and $d(dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J) = 0$, so $d\alpha = \sum d\alpha_{IJ} \wedge dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J = \partial \alpha + \bar\partial\alpha$. Part 2: for $\alpha$ of type $(p,q)$, $0 = d^2 \alpha = \partial^2 \alpha + (\partial \bar\partial + \bar\partial \partial)\alpha + \bar\partial^2 \alpha$, and the three terms have the different types $(p+2, q)$, $(p+1,q+1)$ and $(p, q+2)$, so each vanishes. Part 3: for $\alpha$ of type $(p,q)$ and degree $k$ and $\beta$ of type $(p', q')$, $\bar\partial(\alpha \wedge \beta)$ is the component of type $(p + p', q + q' + 1)$ of $d(\alpha \wedge \beta) = d\alpha \wedge \beta + (-1)^k \alpha \wedge d\beta$, which is $\bar\partial \alpha \wedge \beta + (-1)^k \alpha \wedge \bar\partial \beta$; the general case follows by additivity. Part 4: for $F = (F_1, \dots, F_n)$ holomorphic, $F^* dz_j = dF_j = \partial F_j$ has type $(1,0)$ and $F^* d\bar z_j = d \bar F_j = \overline{\partial F_j}$ has type $(0,1)$. Since $F^*$ is multiplicative, it preserves types. From $F^* d = d F^*$ and part 1, $F^* \partial \alpha + F^* \bar\partial \alpha = \partial F^* \alpha + \bar\partial F^* \alpha$, and comparing types gives $F^* \partial = \partial F^*$ and $F^* \bar\partial = \bar\partial F^*$. Part 5 follows from $\overline{\partial f / \partial z_j} = \partial \bar f / \partial \bar z_j$ and $\overline{dz_j} = d\bar z_j$. Part 6: for $\alpha = \sum_I \alpha_I dz_I$, $\bar\partial \alpha = \sum_{I, j} \frac{\partial \alpha_I}{\partial \bar z_j} d\bar z_j \wedge dz_I$, and the forms $d\bar z_j \wedge dz_I$ are linearly independent, so $\bar\partial \alpha = 0$ if and only if every $\alpha_I$ satisfies $\partial \alpha_I / \partial \bar z_j = 0$, that is, is holomorphic by Corollary P6.1.5.

*Proof of Theorem P6.1.34.* The set $W = \{ (t, x) \in \mathbb{R} \times U : tx \in U \}$ is open and contains $[0,1] \times U$ since $U$ is star-shaped, and $h(t,x) = tx$ is a smooth map $W \to U$. Every form $\gamma$ on $W$ is uniquely $\gamma = dt \wedge \gamma_1 + \gamma_2$, where $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are sums of terms $c(t,x) \, dx_I$. Define the $(k-1)$-form $K \gamma$ on $U$, for $\gamma$ of degree $k$, by integrating the coefficients of $\gamma_1$ over $t \in [0,1]$; write $d_x$ for the exterior derivative in $x$ alone and $\partial_t$ for differentiation of coefficients. Then $d\gamma = -dt \wedge d_x \gamma_1 + dt \wedge \partial_t \gamma_2 + d_x \gamma_2$, and differentiation under the integral gives
$$K d\gamma = -d K\gamma + \gamma_2|_{t=1} - \gamma_2|_{t=0} = -dK\gamma + i_1^* \gamma - i_0^* \gamma,$$
where $i_t(x) = (t, x)$. Apply this to $\gamma = h^* \alpha$, so that $d\gamma = h^* d\alpha = 0$. Here $h \circ i_1 = \mathrm{id}$ and $h \circ i_0$ is the constant map $0$, which pulls back forms of degree $k \geq 1$ to $0$. So $\alpha = d (K h^* \alpha)$. A closed $0$-form has vanishing gradient on the connected set $U$, so it is constant.

*Proof of Proposition P6.1.35.* In one variable, for $f$ holomorphic on an annulus $\{ r < |z| < R \}$ and $r < \rho < |z| < \sigma < R$, Cauchy's theorem gives $f(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{|\zeta| = \sigma} \frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta - z} d\zeta - \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{|\zeta| = \rho} \frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta - z} d\zeta$, and for a disc only the first term occurs. Iterating in each variable as in the proof of Theorem P6.1.3 writes $f(z)$, for $z$ in a product $Q = \prod_j Q_j$ of closed annuli $\rho'_j \leq |z_j| \leq \sigma'_j$ (closed discs $|z_j| \leq \sigma'_j$ for the disc factors) with $\rho_j < \rho'_j < \sigma'_j < \sigma_j$, as a sum of at most $2^n$ integrals over tori of $\pm f(\zeta) \prod_j (\zeta_j - z_j)^{-1}$. On $|\zeta_j| = \sigma_j$ expand $(\zeta_j - z_j)^{-1} = \sum_{k \geq 0} z_j^k \zeta_j^{-k-1}$, and on $|\zeta_j| = \rho_j$ expand $-(\zeta_j - z_j)^{-1} = \sum_{k \geq 0} \zeta_j^k z_j^{-k-1}$. Both converge geometrically, uniformly for $z \in Q$ and $\zeta$ on the circle, with ratios at most $\sigma'_j / \sigma_j$ and $\rho_j / \rho'_j$. Integrating termwise and collecting terms gives an absolutely and uniformly convergent expansion $f = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}^n} c_\alpha z^\alpha$ on $Q$, in which negative powers of $z_j$ occur only for annulus factors. Uniqueness: integrating a uniformly convergent expansion against $\zeta^{-\beta - 1}$ over a torus $\prod_j \{|\zeta_j| = t_j\}$ in $D$ gives $(2\pi i)^n c_\beta$, so the coefficients do not depend on $Q$, and the expansions on all such $Q$ agree. Every compact subset of $D$ lies in such a $Q$. The partial sums are Laurent polynomials in which $z_j$ appears with a negative exponent only when $D_j$ is an annulus, where $z_j \neq 0$, so they are holomorphic on $D$.

*Proof of Theorem P6.1.36.* Step 1 (local solution). Let $D' = \prod_j D'_j$ be a product of discs and annuli whose closure $\bar D' = \prod_j \bar D'_j$ is a compact subset of $D$. Say that a form *involves only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_k$* if its coefficients $\alpha_{IJ}$ vanish unless $J \subseteq \{1, \dots, k\}$. The claim $C(k)$ is: for every product $G = \prod_j G_j$ of open sets $G_j \subseteq D_j$ with $\bar D'_j \subseteq G_j$, and every $\bar\partial$-closed $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(G)$ involving only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_k$, there are open sets $V_j$ with $\bar D'_j \subseteq V_j \subseteq G_j$ and $\beta \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(V)$, $V = \prod_j V_j$, with $\bar\partial \beta = \alpha$ on $V$. If $k < q$ then $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 0$ works. Let $k \geq q \geq 1$ and assume $C(k-1)$. Write uniquely $\alpha = d\bar z_k \wedge \gamma + \mu$, where $\gamma \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(G)$ and $\mu \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q}(G)$ involve only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_{k-1}$. In $\bar\partial \alpha = \sum_j d\bar z_j \wedge \partial \alpha / \partial \bar z_j$, the terms with $j > k$ are the only ones that involve $d\bar z_j$, and for different $j > k$ they involve different basis forms. Since $\bar\partial \alpha = 0$, each $d\bar z_j \wedge (d\bar z_k \wedge \partial \gamma / \partial \bar z_j + \partial \mu / \partial \bar z_j)$ vanishes, and as the bracket involves only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_k$, the bracket vanishes; its two summands involve disjoint sets of basis forms (with and without $d\bar z_k$), so $\partial \gamma / \partial \bar z_j = 0$ and $\partial \mu / \partial \bar z_j = 0$ for $j > k$. Thus the coefficients of $\gamma$ are holomorphic in $z_{k+1}, \dots, z_n$.

Choose an open set $V_k$ with $\bar D'_k \subseteq V_k$ and $\bar V_k$ a compact subset of $G_k$, and $\chi \in C^\infty_c(G_k)$ with $\chi = 1$ on $V_k$. For each coefficient $\gamma_{IJ}$ of $\gamma$ put
$$\eta_{IJ}(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{\mathbb{C}} \frac{\chi(w) \gamma_{IJ}(z_1, \dots, z_{k-1}, w, z_{k+1}, \dots, z_n)}{w - z_k} \, dw \wedge d\bar w,$$
the integrand being extended by $0$ for $w \notin \operatorname{supp} \chi$. By part 2 of Theorem P6.1.8, with the variables $z_j$, $j \neq k$, as parameters in $\prod_{j \neq k} G_j$, $\eta_{IJ}$ is smooth on $G$, $\partial \eta_{IJ} / \partial \bar z_k = \chi \gamma_{IJ}$, and for $j > k$, $\partial \eta_{IJ} / \partial \bar z_j$ is the integral of $\chi \, \partial \gamma_{IJ} / \partial \bar z_j = 0$. So $\eta = \sum \eta_{IJ} \, dz_I \wedge d\bar z_J \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(G)$ involves only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_{k-1}$ and
$$\bar\partial \eta = d\bar z_k \wedge \chi \gamma + \sum_{j < k} d\bar z_j \wedge \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial \bar z_j}.$$
On $G' = G_1 \times \dots \times V_k \times \dots \times G_n$, where $\chi = 1$, the form $\alpha - \bar\partial \eta = \mu - \sum_{j<k} d\bar z_j \wedge \partial \eta / \partial \bar z_j$ is $\bar\partial$-closed and involves only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_{k-1}$. By $C(k-1)$ applied on $G'$ there are $V \subseteq G'$ as required and $\beta'$ on $V$ with $\bar\partial \beta' = \alpha - \bar\partial \eta$, and $\beta = \beta' + \eta$ proves $C(k)$. Every form involves only $d\bar z_1, \dots, d\bar z_n$, so $C(n)$ with $G = D$ gives a solution on a neighbourhood of $\bar D'$.

Step 2 (exhaustion). Choose products $D^{(m)} = \prod_j D_j^{(m)}$, $m \geq 1$, where $D_j^{(m)}$ is a disc $\{|z| < R_j^{(m)}\}$ if $D_j$ is a disc and an annulus $\{ r_j^{(m)} < |z| < R_j^{(m)} \}$ if $D_j$ is an annulus, with $r_j < r_j^{(m+1)} < r_j^{(m)}$, $R_j^{(m)} < R_j^{(m+1)} < R_j$, $r_j^{(m)} \to r_j$ and $R_j^{(m)} \to R_j$. Then $\bar D^{(m)}$ is a compact subset of $D^{(m+1)}$ and $D = \bigcup_m D^{(m)}$. By Step 1 there are $\beta_m \in \mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(W_m)$, $W_m$ an open neighbourhood of $\bar D^{(m)}$, with $\bar\partial \beta_m = \alpha$. Construct inductively $\beta'_m$ on open neighbourhoods $W'_m$ of $\bar D^{(m)}$ with $\bar\partial \beta'_m = \alpha$, starting with $\beta'_1 = \beta_1$. Given $\beta'_m$, the form $\delta_m = \beta_{m+1} - \beta'_m$ is defined on a neighbourhood of $\bar D^{(m)}$ and $\bar\partial \delta_m = 0$.

If $q \geq 2$, Step 1 applied to $\delta_m$ on the product $D^{(m)}$, with $D'$ replaced by $D^{(m-1)}$ (for $m \geq 2$), gives $\varepsilon_m$ on a neighbourhood $N$ of $\bar D^{(m-1)}$ with $\bar\partial \varepsilon_m = \delta_m$. Take $\chi_m \in C^\infty_c(N)$ equal to $1$ near $\bar D^{(m-1)}$ and put $\beta'_{m+1} = \beta_{m+1} - \bar\partial(\chi_m \varepsilon_m)$ on $W'_{m+1} = W_{m+1}$. Then $\bar\partial \beta'_{m+1} = \alpha$, and on $D^{(m-1)}$, $\beta'_{m+1} = \beta_{m+1} - \delta_m = \beta'_m$. So on each $D^{(m)}$ the sequence $(\beta'_l)_l$ is constant for $l \geq m + 1$, and its limit $\beta$ is a smooth form on $D$ with $\bar\partial \beta = \alpha$.

If $q = 1$, $\delta_m$ is a $(p,0)$-form with holomorphic coefficients on $D^{(m)}$ by Proposition P6.1.33. By Proposition P6.1.35 applied on $D^{(m)}$, whose factors have the same types as those of $D$, there is a $(p,0)$-form $L_m$ whose coefficients are Laurent polynomials holomorphic on $D$, with all coefficients of $\delta_m - L_m$ bounded by $2^{-m}$ on $\bar D^{(m-1)}$. Put $\beta'_{m+1} = \beta_{m+1} - L_m$ on $W_{m+1}$. Then $\bar\partial \beta'_{m+1} = \alpha$, and $\beta'_{m+1} - \beta'_m = \delta_m - L_m$ is a holomorphic $(p,0)$-form on $D^{(m)}$ with coefficients bounded by $2^{-m}$ on $D^{(m-1)}$. Fix $m_0$. For $l > m_0$ the forms $\beta'_{l+1} - \beta'_l$ are holomorphic on $D^{(m_0)}$ with coefficients bounded by $2^{-l}$, so $\beta'_l = \beta'_{m_0 + 1} + \sum_{m_0 < k < l} (\beta'_{k+1} - \beta'_k)$ converges uniformly on $D^{(m_0)}$ to $\beta'_{m_0+1}$ plus a holomorphic $(p,0)$-form, by Proposition P6.1.7. The limit $\beta$ is therefore smooth on each $D^{(m_0)}$, hence on $D$, and $\bar\partial \beta = \bar\partial \beta'_{m_0+1} = \alpha$ on $D^{(m_0)}$.

The conjugate statement: if $\partial \alpha = 0$ with $\alpha$ of type $(p,q)$, $p \geq 1$, then $\bar\alpha$ has type $(q,p)$ and $\bar\partial \bar\alpha = \overline{\partial \alpha} = 0$ by Proposition P6.1.33, so $\bar\alpha = \bar\partial \gamma'$ and $\alpha = \partial \bar{\gamma'}$ with $\bar{\gamma'} \in \mathcal{A}^{p-1,q}(D)$.

*Proof of Corollary P6.1.37.* The sequence is a complex because $\bar\partial^2 = 0$, and exactness of a sequence of sheaves can be checked on stalks (P2 Ch. 5). The map $\Omega^p_U \to \mathcal{A}^{p,0}_U$ is injective, and exactness at $\mathcal{A}^{p,0}_U$ is part 6 of Proposition P6.1.33. Let $q \geq 1$ and let $\alpha$ be a germ at $a$ of a $\bar\partial$-closed $(p,q)$-form. It is represented on a polydisc $\Delta(a, r)$, a product of discs, and by Theorem P6.1.36 (after translation, which preserves types by part 4 of Proposition P6.1.33) it is $\bar\partial \beta$ on $\Delta(a,r)$. On a complex manifold the sheaves $\mathcal{A}^{p,q}$, $\Omega^p$ and the operator $\bar\partial$ are defined chart by chart, and part 4 of Proposition P6.1.33 shows that holomorphic changes of charts respect them, so exactness at each point follows from the case of an open set of $\mathbb{C}^n$.

*Proof of Corollary P6.1.38.* Let $k = p + q$. The polydisc $\Delta$ is convex, so by Theorem P6.1.34, $\alpha = d\beta$ with $\beta \in \mathcal{A}^{k-1}(\Delta)$; write $\beta = \sum_{a + b = k-1} \beta^{a,b}$. The type $(a', b')$ component of $d\beta$ is $\partial \beta^{a'-1, b'} + \bar\partial \beta^{a', b'-1}$, and it is $0$ unless $(a', b') = (p,q)$. Let $b$ be the largest index with $\beta^{k-1-b, b} \neq 0$ and suppose $b > q$. The component of $d\beta$ of type $(k - 1 - b, b + 1)$ is $\bar\partial \beta^{k-1-b, b}$, since $\beta^{k-2-b, b+1} = 0$, and $(k-1-b, b+1) \neq (p,q)$, so $\bar\partial \beta^{k-1-b,b} = 0$ with $b \geq 1$. By Theorem P6.1.36, $\beta^{k-1-b, b} = \bar\partial \eta$ with $\eta$ of type $(k-1-b, b-1)$. Replacing $\beta$ by $\beta - d\eta = \beta - \bar\partial \eta - \partial \eta$ does not change $d\beta$, removes the component of type $(k-1-b, b)$ and changes only the component of type $(k - b, b-1)$. Repeating, $\beta^{a,b} = 0$ for $b > q$. In the same way, with the conjugate statement of Theorem P6.1.36, $\beta^{a,b} = 0$ for $a > p$; this does not reintroduce components with $b > q$. Then $\beta = \beta^{p-1, q} + \beta^{p, q-1}$, and the type components of $d\beta = \alpha$ give $\bar\partial \beta^{p-1,q} = 0$ (type $(p-1, q+1)$), $\partial \beta^{p, q-1} = 0$ (type $(p+1, q-1)$) and $\alpha = \partial \beta^{p-1,q} + \bar\partial \beta^{p,q-1}$. By Theorem P6.1.36 and its conjugate, since $q \geq 1$ and $p \geq 1$, $\beta^{p-1,q} = \bar\partial \mu$ and $\beta^{p,q-1} = \partial \nu$ with $\mu, \nu \in \mathcal{A}^{p-1,q-1}(\Delta)$. So $\alpha = \partial\bar\partial \mu + \bar\partial\partial \nu = \partial\bar\partial(\mu - \nu)$. If $\alpha$ is a real $(1,1)$-form, write $\alpha = \partial\bar\partial \gamma = i \partial\bar\partial(-i\gamma)$ with $\gamma$ a function, so $\alpha = i\partial\bar\partial \psi$ with $\psi = -i\gamma$. By part 5 of Proposition P6.1.33, $\overline{i \partial \bar\partial \psi} = -i \bar\partial \partial \bar\psi = i \partial\bar\partial \bar\psi$, so $\alpha = \bar\alpha = i\partial\bar\partial \bar\psi$, and $\alpha = i \partial\bar\partial \varphi$ with the real function $\varphi = \operatorname{Re} \psi$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $\partial$, $\bar\partial$ and $\partial\bar\partial$ of $|z|^2$ and of $\log(1 + |z|^2)$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$, and show that $i \partial\bar\partial \log(1 + |z|^2) = i \frac{\sum_j dz_j \wedge d\bar z_j}{1 + |z|^2} - i \frac{(\sum_j \bar z_j dz_j) \wedge (\sum_k z_k d\bar z_k)}{(1+|z|^2)^2}$.
2. (A) Show that on $\mathbb{C}$, $i \partial\bar\partial \varphi = \frac12 \Delta \varphi \, dx \wedge dy$ for a real function $\varphi$, where $\Delta$ is the Laplacian. Find $\varphi$ with $i\partial\bar\partial\varphi = dx \wedge dy$, and explain why Corollary P6.1.38 on a disc reduces to solving Poisson's equation.
3. (B) Find all smooth functions $u$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$ with $\bar\partial u = \bar z_2 \, d\bar z_1 + \bar z_1 \, d\bar z_2$, after checking that the right side is $\bar\partial$-closed. Show that the solution of Theorem P6.1.36 for $q = 1$ is unique up to a holomorphic $p$-form.
4. (B) Let $\alpha$ be a $d$-closed $(0,1)$-form on a polydisc $\Delta$. Show that $\alpha = \bar\partial \bar h$ for a holomorphic function $h$ on $\Delta$. Show by Example P6.1.40 that this fails on $\mathbb{C}^*$.
5. (B) Show that a $\partial\bar\partial$-closed function on a polydisc, that is, a real function $\varphi$ with $\partial\bar\partial \varphi = 0$, is the real part of a holomorphic function, and that $\log |z|^2$ on $\mathbb{C}^*$ is $\partial\bar\partial$-closed but not the real part of a holomorphic function.
6. (C) (Bochner-Martinelli.) With $\beta_0$ as in Example P6.1.41 and $f \in \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^2)$, show that $f \beta_0 \wedge dz_1 \wedge dz_2$ is closed on $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ and that its integral over every sphere $\{|z| = \varepsilon\}$ equals $4\pi^2 f(0)$. Deduce that $f\beta_0$ is not $\bar\partial$-exact on $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ when $f(0) \neq 0$.

### Lean

Mathlib has smooth manifolds, differential forms on normed spaces with the exterior derivative, and holomorphic functions of one variable; it does not have forms of type $(p,q)$, the operator $\bar\partial$ or Dolbeault cohomology. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Dolbeault proved in 1953 (Dol53) that the cohomology of the complex $(\mathcal{A}^{p,\bullet}(X), \bar\partial)$ computes $H^q(X, \Omega^p_X)$, the theorem of Chapter 2; the local exactness on which it rests was proved by Dolbeault and independently by Grothendieck, whence the name. The proof by induction on the number of $d\bar z_k$ and the Cauchy integral is the one of Griffiths and Harris, Section 0.2 (GH78), Huybrechts, Proposition 1.3.7 and Section 1.3 (Huy05), and Hörmander, Theorem 2.3.3 (Hor90). Huybrechts and Griffiths-Harris state it for polydiscs; the extension to products of annuli, which is needed for the Čech computation on the projective line in Example P6.2.27, uses Laurent polynomials in place of polynomials. The Poincaré lemma is in Bott and Tu, Section I.4 (BT82). The local $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma gives local potentials for Kähler forms (Proposition P6.4.2), and its global form on compact Kähler manifolds is part of Chapter 4. Example P6.1.41 is the first instance of the failure of the lemma on non-Stein open sets; the form $\beta_0$ is the Bochner-Martinelli kernel.
