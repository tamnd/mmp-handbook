## Plurisubharmonic functions, currents and singular metrics

### Statement

Throughout the section $\Omega$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$, $X$ is a complex manifold of dimension $n$, and $d\lambda$ is Lebesgue measure. For $a \in \mathbb{C}^n$ and $r > 0$, $B(a, r)$ is the open ball and $S(a, r)$ the sphere, and $\rho$ is a fixed smooth function on $\mathbb{C}^n$ with $\rho \geq 0$, $\rho(z)$ depending only on $|z|$, support in $B(0, 1)$ and $\int \rho \, d\lambda = 1$; put $\rho_\varepsilon(z) = \varepsilon^{-2n} \rho(z / \varepsilon)$. On $\mathbb{C}$ a function $u \colon U \to [-\infty, \infty)$ is *subharmonic* if it is upper semicontinuous, not identically $-\infty$ on any component of $U$, and satisfies the sub-mean value inequality $u(a) \leq \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} u(a + r e^{i\theta}) \, d\theta$ whenever the closed disc $\overline{D}(a, r)$ lies in $U$.

**Definition P6.8.1 (plurisubharmonic functions).** A function $\varphi \colon \Omega \to [-\infty, \infty)$ is *plurisubharmonic*, or *psh*, if it is upper semicontinuous, not identically $-\infty$ on any connected component of $\Omega$, and for all $a, b \in \mathbb{C}^n$ the function $\zeta \mapsto \varphi(a + \zeta b)$ satisfies the sub-mean value inequality on every closed disc of $\{\zeta : a + \zeta b \in \Omega\}$. A function on a complex manifold is psh if it is psh in every chart; by part 6 of Proposition P6.8.2 this does not depend on the chart. A function is *pluriharmonic* if it is locally the real part of a holomorphic function.

**Proposition P6.8.2 (first properties of psh functions).** Let $\varphi$ be psh on $\Omega$.

1. For $\overline{B}(a, r) \subseteq \Omega$, $\varphi(a)$ is at most the mean of $\varphi$ over $S(a, r)$ and at most the mean of $\varphi$ over $B(a, r)$. In particular $\varphi$ is locally integrable, and $\sup_{\overline{B}(a, r)} \varphi = \max_{S(a, r)} \varphi$.
2. A function of class $C^2$ is psh if and only if its Levi form $\sum_{j,k} \frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial z_j \partial \bar z_k} \xi_j \bar\xi_k$ is $\geq 0$ for every $\xi \in \mathbb{C}^n$ at every point.
3. On $\Omega_\varepsilon = \{ z : B(z, \varepsilon) \subseteq \Omega \}$ the convolution $\varphi_\varepsilon = \varphi * \rho_\varepsilon$ is smooth and psh, $\varphi_\varepsilon \geq \varphi$, and $\varphi_\varepsilon$ decreases to $\varphi$ pointwise as $\varepsilon$ decreases to $0$.
4. For every $\xi \in \mathbb{C}^n$ the distribution $\sum_{j,k} \frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial z_j \partial \bar z_k} \xi_j \bar\xi_k$ is a positive measure. Conversely, a locally integrable function whose Levi form is positive in this sense is equal almost everywhere to a unique psh function.
5. Sums and maxima of finitely many psh functions, positive multiples of psh functions and limits of decreasing sequences of psh functions, when not identically $-\infty$, are psh. If $f, f_1, \dots, f_r$ are holomorphic and not all identically zero, then $\log|f|$ and $\log \sum_j |f_j|^2$ are psh, and $\log|f|$ is pluriharmonic where $f \neq 0$.
6. If $F \colon \Omega' \to \Omega$ is holomorphic and $\varphi \circ F$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any component of $\Omega'$, then $\varphi \circ F$ is psh.

A *current of degree $k$* on $X$ is a continuous linear form on the space of smooth compactly supported forms of degree $2n - k$; a form $\alpha$ with locally integrable coefficients defines the current $\beta \mapsto \int \alpha \wedge \beta$, and $d$, $\partial$, $\bar\partial$ act on currents by duality with the signs that make them extend the operators on forms.

**Definition P6.8.3 (currents of bidegree $(1,1)$).** A *current of bidegree $(1,1)$* on $X$ is a current of degree $2$ that is written in each chart as $T = i \sum_{j,k} T_{jk} \, dz_j \wedge d\bar z_k$ with distributions $T_{jk}$. It is *real* if $\overline{T_{jk}} = T_{kj}$, *positive* if it is real and $\sum_{j,k} T_{jk} \xi_j \bar\xi_k$ is a positive measure for every $\xi \in \mathbb{C}^n$ in every chart, and *closed* if $dT = 0$. For $T$ positive and $S$ positive, $T \geq S$ means that $T - S$ is positive; for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\omega$, $T \geq \omega$ is meant in the same sense. If $Z \subseteq X$ is an irreducible hypersurface, the *current of integration* $[Z]$ is $\beta \mapsto \int_{Z_{\mathrm{reg}}} \beta$ on test forms of degree $2n - 2$, and for a divisor $D = \sum_j m_j Z_j$ with real coefficients, $[D] = \sum_j m_j [Z_j]$.

The integral defining $[Z]$ converges and $[Z]$ is a closed positive current: this is Lelong's theorem (Lel57a; Dem97a, Chapter III). By part 4 of Proposition P6.8.2, $i \partial\bar\partial \varphi$ is a closed positive current for every psh $\varphi$, and a positive current has measures as coefficients, since $T_{jk}$ is a combination of the measures $\sum T_{lm} \xi_l \bar\xi_m$ for $\xi = e_j + \epsilon e_k$, $\epsilon \in \{1, -1, i, -i\}$, by polarization.

**Theorem P6.8.4 (Poincaré-Lelong formula).** Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on $X$, not identically zero on any component, with divisor $\operatorname{div}(f) = \sum_j m_j Z_j$. Then
$$\frac{i}{\pi} \partial\bar\partial \log|f| = [\operatorname{div}(f)].$$

**Definition P6.8.5 (singular Hermitian metrics).** Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle on $X$. A *singular Hermitian metric* $h$ on $L$ assigns to each local holomorphic frame $e$ of $L$ over an open set $U$ a function $\varphi_U \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ with values in $[-\infty, \infty]$, its *weight*, so that $|e|_h^2 = e^{-2\varphi_U}$, compatibly with changes of frame: if $e_U = g \, e_V$ on $U \cap V$ with $g$ holomorphic and nowhere zero, then $\varphi_U = \varphi_V - \log|g|$ almost everywhere. The *curvature current* of $h$ is the current of bidegree $(1,1)$ equal to
$$i \Theta_h = 2 i \partial\bar\partial \varphi_U \quad \text{on } U,$$
and $c_1(L, h) = \frac{1}{2\pi} i\Theta_h = \frac{i}{\pi} \partial\bar\partial \varphi_U$. The metric is *smooth* if the weights are smooth, and then $i\Theta_h$ is the curvature form of the Chern connection of Chapter 3, since $\Theta_h = -\partial\bar\partial \log |e|_h^2 = 2 \partial\bar\partial \varphi_U$. If $i\Theta_h \geq 0$, the weights are almost everywhere equal to psh functions by part 4 of Proposition P6.8.2, and the weights are then taken psh.

**Proposition P6.8.6 (curvature currents and the first Chern class).** Let $h$ be a singular Hermitian metric on $L$.

1. The curvature current $i\Theta_h$ is well defined, real and closed.
2. If $h_0$ is a smooth Hermitian metric on $L$, then $h = h_0 e^{-2\psi}$ for a function $\psi \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)$, the weights satisfy $\varphi_U = \varphi_{0,U} + \psi$, and $i\Theta_h = i\Theta_{h_0} + 2 i \partial\bar\partial \psi$. In particular $c_1(L, h)$ and the smooth form $c_1(L, h_0)$ differ by $d$ of a current, and both represent the real first Chern class of Chapter 5 in the de Rham cohomology of currents.
3. If $X$ is compact, $h$ is smooth and $i\Theta_h \geq 0$, then $\deg L|_C = \int_{\tilde C} \nu^* c_1(L, h) \geq 0$ for every compact irreducible curve $C \subseteq X$ with normalization $\nu \colon \tilde C \to C$.

**Definition P6.8.7 (pseudo-effective line bundles).** A holomorphic line bundle $L$ on a compact complex manifold is *pseudo-effective* if it carries a singular Hermitian metric $h$ with $i\Theta_h \geq 0$.

**Proposition P6.8.8 (metrics from sections).** Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle on $X$.

1. Let $s_1, \dots, s_r \in H^0(X, L)$, not all zero on any component. On a local frame $e$ write $s_j = g_j e$ and put $\varphi = \frac12 \log \sum_j |g_j|^2$. These weights define a singular metric $h$ on $L$ with $|s_j|_h \leq 1$, psh weights and $i\Theta_h \geq 0$. It is smooth outside the common zero set $\operatorname{Bs}$ of the $s_j$, and there $i\Theta_h = \Phi^* \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, where $\Phi \colon X \setminus \operatorname{Bs} \to \mathbb{P}^{r-1}$ is the map $x \mapsto [s_1(x) : \dots : s_r(x)]$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}} = i \partial\bar\partial \log \sum_j |w_j|^2$ on $\mathbb{P}^{r-1}$.
2. For a single section $s \neq 0$ the metric $|e|_h^2 = |g|^{-2}$ has weight $\log|g|$ and curvature current $i\Theta_h = 2\pi [\operatorname{div}(s)]$.
3. Let $D = \sum_j c_j D_j$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with local equations $g_j$ of $D_j$, and put $\varphi_D = \sum_j c_j \log|g_j|$. If $m \geq 1$ and $mD$ is integral, then $m \varphi_D$ is the weight of the metric of part 2 on $\mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ for its canonical section, and $2 i \partial\bar\partial \varphi_D = 2\pi [D]$.

### Proof in words

A psh function is a function that is subharmonic on complex lines. The sub-mean value inequality on circles in all complex lines through a point averages to the inequality on spheres and balls, and this gives local integrability: on the set where a psh function is integrable, the ball averages control the function, and a connectedness argument spreads integrability to the whole domain. Convolution with a radial kernel averages sphere means, and the sphere means of a psh function increase with the radius, so the regularizations decrease to the function. Positivity of the Levi form is clear for smooth functions and passes to the limit.

The Poincaré-Lelong formula is the several variable form of $\Delta \log|z| = 2\pi \delta_0$ on $\mathbb{C}$. Away from the zeros of $f$, $\log|f|$ is pluriharmonic and both sides vanish. Near a smooth point of the reduced zero set, $f = u z_1^m$ in suitable coordinates, and the formula reduces to the one variable computation in the variable $z_1$. What is left is the singular locus of the zero set, which has codimension $2$; a positive current of bidimension $(n-1, n-1)$ puts no mass there, and the formula holds everywhere.

A singular metric is a metric whose weights are allowed to be infinite. Changes of frame change the weight by a pluriharmonic function, so the curvature current is global. The metric of a linear system is the pullback of the Fubini-Study metric where the system has no base points, and the metric of a section is the pullback of the trivial metric by division by the section, with curvature concentrated on its divisor.

### Examples

**Example P6.8.9 (the function $\log|z|$).** On $\mathbb{C}$, write $z = x + iy$ and $\Delta = \partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2 = 4 \partial_z \partial_{\bar z}$. The classical identity $\Delta \log|z| = 2\pi\delta_0$ gives $i\partial\bar\partial \log|z| = \frac{i}{4} \Delta \log|z| \, dz \wedge d\bar z = \frac{\pi}{2} \delta_0 \, i \, dz \wedge d\bar z = \pi \delta_0 \, dx \wedge dy$, since $i \, dz \wedge d\bar z = 2 \, dx \wedge dy$. So $\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\bar\partial \log|z| = [0]$, which is Theorem P6.8.4 for $f = z$. On $\mathbb{C}^n$ with $n \geq 2$ the function $\varphi = \log|z|$ is psh by part 5 of Proposition P6.8.2, smooth on $\mathbb{C}^n \setminus \{0\}$, and there
$$2 i \partial\bar\partial \log|z| = i \partial\bar\partial \log|z|^2 = i \frac{|z|^2 \sum_j dz_j \wedge d\bar z_j - \sum_{j,k} \bar z_j z_k \, dz_j \wedge d\bar z_k}{|z|^4}.$$
Its Levi form at $z$ is $(|z|^2 |\xi|^2 - |\langle \xi, z \rangle|^2)/|z|^4$, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality $\geq 0$, and zero exactly for $\xi$ proportional to $z$. It is the pullback of the Fubini-Study form by $\mathbb{C}^n \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. The function $e^{-2c\varphi} = |z|^{-2c}$ is integrable near $0$ if and only if $c < n$, by polar coordinates: $\int_{B(0,1)} |z|^{-2c} d\lambda = \sigma_{2n-1} \int_0^1 t^{2n - 1 - 2c} dt$ with $\sigma_{2n-1}$ the volume of the unit sphere.

**Example P6.8.10 (metrics on $\mathcal{O}(1)$).** On $\mathbb{P}^n$ with homogeneous coordinates $z_0, \dots, z_n$, the sections $z_0, \dots, z_n$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ have no common zero, and part 1 of Proposition P6.8.8 gives the Fubini-Study metric, with $i\Theta_h = \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, which is positive by Proposition P6.5.20 (the Fubini-Study metric). The single section $z_0$ gives a singular metric with weight $\log|z_0 / e|$ and curvature current $2\pi[H_0]$, $H_0 = \{z_0 = 0\}$. On $\mathbb{P}^2$, the sections $z_0, z_1$ have the base point $p = [0 : 0 : 1]$; in the chart $z_2 = 1$ with frame $z_2$ the weight is $\frac12 \log(|x|^2 + |y|^2) = \log|(x, y)|$, which is the function of Example P6.8.9, and off $p$ the curvature is the pullback of the Fubini-Study form of $\mathbb{P}^1$ by the projection from $p$. In all three cases $\int_{\ell} c_1(\mathcal{O}(1), h) = 1$ on a line $\ell$ not through the singular locus: for the Fubini-Study metric this is the computation of Chapter 5, and for $z_0$ it is $\int_\ell [H_0] = \#(\ell \cap H_0) = 1$.

**Example P6.8.11 (a pseudo-effective bundle with no semipositive smooth metric).** Let $\pi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up of a point, with exceptional curve $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$; the normal bundle of $E$ is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)$, the tautological bundle on the space of lines through the point, so $\mathcal{O}_X(E)|_E \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)$ has degree $E^2 = -1$. The line bundle $\mathcal{O}_X(E)$ has the section $s_E$ with divisor $E$, and part 2 of Proposition P6.8.8 gives a singular metric with $i\Theta_h = 2\pi[E] \geq 0$. So $\mathcal{O}_X(E)$ is pseudo-effective. It has no smooth metric $h'$ with $i\Theta_{h'} \geq 0$: by part 3 of Proposition P6.8.6 such a metric would give $\deg \mathcal{O}_X(E)|_E \geq 0$, while $\deg \mathcal{O}_X(E)|_E = E^2 = -1$. The singular metric fails the test on $E$ because $E$ is its polar set.

### Proof

*Proposition P6.8.2.* For part 1, let $\overline{B}(a, r) \subseteq \Omega$. The sphere $S(a, r)$ is the union of the circles $\{a + r e^{i\theta} b\}$ with $b$ in the unit sphere, and the normalized surface measure of $S(a, r)$ is the average over $b$, for the unitarily invariant probability measure on the space of complex lines, of the normalized arc length on these circles. Integrating the sub-mean value inequality on each circle against this measure gives $\varphi(a) \leq \operatorname{avg}_{S(a, r)} \varphi$, where the average exists in $[-\infty, \infty)$ because $\varphi$ is bounded above on compact sets, being upper semicontinuous. Integrating over $r$ against $r^{2n-1} dr$ gives the inequality for the ball. If $\varphi(z) > \max_{S(a, r)} \varphi$ for some $z \in B(a, r)$, the function $\varphi$ on the closed ball attains a maximum $M$ at an interior point $z_0$, by upper semicontinuity, and the ball inequality at $z_0$ forces $\varphi = M$ almost everywhere near $z_0$; the set where $\varphi = M$ is then open by the same argument applied to its points and closed in the open ball by upper semicontinuity, so $\varphi = M$ on $B(a, r)$ and, by the sphere inequality and upper semicontinuity, somewhere on $S(a, r)$, a contradiction. For local integrability, let $U$ be the set of points of $\Omega$ near which $\varphi$ is integrable; it is open. Let $a$ be in the closure of $U$ in a component $\Omega_0$, and choose $r$ with $\overline{B}(a, 2r) \subseteq \Omega$ and $b \in U$ with $|a - b| < r$ and $\varphi(b) > -\infty$, possible because $\varphi > -\infty$ almost everywhere near points of $U$. On $B(b, r)$, $\varphi \leq M$ for a constant $M$ and $\int_{B(b, r)} \varphi \geq \operatorname{vol}(B(b, r)) \varphi(b) > -\infty$, so $\varphi$ is integrable on $B(b, r) \ni a$. So $U \cap \Omega_0$ is closed in $\Omega_0$, and it is nonempty since the same argument applies at a point where $\varphi > -\infty$. So $U \supseteq \Omega_0$.

For part 2, if $\varphi$ is $C^2$, its restriction $u(\zeta) = \varphi(a + \zeta b)$ has $\partial_\zeta \partial_{\bar\zeta} u(0) = \sum_{j,k} \frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial z_j \partial \bar z_k}(a) b_j \bar b_k$, and a $C^2$ function of one variable is subharmonic if and only if its Laplacian is $\geq 0$, by the classical one variable theory: if $\Delta u \geq 0$, the mean $m(r)$ of $u$ over circles satisfies $(r m'(r))' = \frac{r}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \Delta u \, d\theta \geq 0$, so $m$ increases and $u(0) = m(0) \leq m(r)$; if $\Delta u(0) < 0$, then $m(r) < u(0)$ for small $r$ by the same identity.

For part 3, $\varphi_\varepsilon$ is smooth as the convolution of a locally integrable function with a smooth kernel. Since $\varphi_\varepsilon(z + \zeta b) = \int \varphi(z - w + \zeta b) \rho_\varepsilon(w) \, d\lambda(w)$ and $\varphi$ is bounded above on compact sets, Fubini's theorem transfers the sub-mean value inequality on circles to $\varphi_\varepsilon$, which is psh. Let $m(a, r)$ be the mean of $\varphi$ over $S(a, r)$. Since $\rho$ is radial, $\varphi_\varepsilon(a) = \int_0^1 m(a, \varepsilon t) \, c(t) \, dt$ with $c(t) = \sigma_{2n-1} t^{2n-1} \rho(t) \geq 0$ of integral $1$, writing $\rho(t)$ for the value on $|z| = t$. It remains to show that $m(a, r)$ increases in $r$. For a smooth psh function $\psi$, the Green formula gives $\frac{d}{dr} m_\psi(a, r) = \frac{1}{\sigma_{2n-1} r^{2n-1}} \int_{B(a, r)} \Delta \psi \, d\lambda \geq 0$, since $\Delta \psi = 4 \sum_j \partial_j \bar\partial_j \psi \geq 0$ by part 2. Apply this to $\psi = \varphi_\delta$: then $m_{\varphi_\delta}(a, r)$ increases in $r$. By part 1, $\varphi_\delta \geq \varphi$, and by upper semicontinuity $\limsup_{\delta \to 0} \varphi_\delta(z) \leq \limsup_{\delta \to 0} \sup_{B(z, \delta)} \varphi \leq \varphi(z)$, so $\varphi_\delta \to \varphi$ pointwise; the $\varphi_\delta$ are bounded above on compact sets uniformly in $\delta \leq 1$, so the reverse Fatou lemma and $\varphi_\delta \geq \varphi$ give $m_{\varphi_\delta}(a, r) \to m(a, r)$. So $m(a, r)$ increases in $r$, $\varphi_\varepsilon$ decreases as $\varepsilon$ decreases, and $\varphi_\varepsilon \geq \varphi$ with $\varphi_\varepsilon \to \varphi$ was just shown.

For part 4, let $P = \sum_{j,k} \xi_j \bar\xi_k \frac{\partial^2}{\partial z_j \partial \bar z_k}$ and let $\chi \geq 0$ be a test function. Then $\int \varphi_\varepsilon P\chi = \int (P\varphi_\varepsilon) \chi \geq 0$ by parts 2 and 3, and $\varphi \leq \varphi_\varepsilon \leq \varphi_{\varepsilon_0}$ for $\varepsilon \leq \varepsilon_0$, so dominated convergence gives $\int \varphi P \chi \geq 0$. A positive distribution is a positive measure. The converse statement is Dem97a, Chapter I, and is used only to choose psh representatives of weights: if $\varphi \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ has positive Levi form, then the $\varphi_\varepsilon$ are smooth psh, the identity $\varphi_\varepsilon * \rho_\delta = \varphi_\delta * \rho_\varepsilon$ and part 3 applied to the smooth psh functions $\varphi_\varepsilon$ show that $\varphi_\varepsilon$ decreases as $\varepsilon \to 0$, and its limit is psh by part 5 and equal to $\varphi$ almost everywhere, since $\varphi_\varepsilon \to \varphi$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$; uniqueness holds because a psh function is determined by its ball means, by part 3.

For part 5, sums, positive multiples and maxima preserve upper semicontinuity and the sub-mean value inequality. A decreasing limit of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous, and the inequality passes to the limit by monotone convergence. For holomorphic $f \not\equiv 0$, $\log|f| = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \frac12 \log(|f|^2 + \varepsilon)$, a decreasing limit, and $\frac12 \log(|f|^2 + \varepsilon)$ is smooth with Levi form $\frac{\varepsilon |\partial_\xi f|^2}{2 (|f|^2 + \varepsilon)^2} \geq 0$, where $\partial_\xi f = \sum_j \xi_j \partial_j f$. Where $f \neq 0$, locally $f = e^g$ and $\log|f| = \operatorname{Re} g$ is pluriharmonic. In the same way $\log(\sum_j |f_j|^2 + \varepsilon)$ is smooth with Levi form $\frac{(\sum_j |f_j|^2 + \varepsilon) \sum_j |\partial_\xi f_j|^2 - |\sum_j \bar f_j \partial_\xi f_j|^2}{(\sum_j |f_j|^2 + \varepsilon)^2} \geq 0$ by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and it decreases to $\log \sum_j |f_j|^2$.

For part 6, if $\varphi$ is smooth, the Levi form of $\varphi \circ F$ at $z$ in the direction $\xi$ is the Levi form of $\varphi$ at $F(z)$ in the direction $dF_z(\xi)$, because $F$ is holomorphic, so $\varphi \circ F$ is psh by part 2. In general $\varphi_\varepsilon \circ F$ is psh and decreases to $\varphi \circ F$, which is psh by part 5.

*Theorem P6.8.4.* The function $\log|f|$ is psh by part 5 of Proposition P6.8.2, so $T = \frac{i}{\pi}\partial\bar\partial \log|f|$ is a closed positive current, with measure coefficients. The statement is local. On $X \setminus f^{-1}(0)$, $\log|f|$ is pluriharmonic and $T = 0 = [\operatorname{div}(f)]$. Let $S$ be the union of the singular locus of the reduced hypersurface $f^{-1}(0)$, which contains the pairwise intersections of the $Z_j$; it is an analytic subset of codimension at least $2$. Near a point $p \in f^{-1}(0) \setminus S$, $p$ lies on exactly one $Z_j$, which is smooth at $p$, and there are coordinates with $Z_j = \{z_1 = 0\}$ near $p$. Since $z_1$ generates the ideal of $Z_j$ near $p$ and $f$ vanishes to order $m_j$ along $Z_j$, $f = u z_1^{m_j}$ with $u$ holomorphic and nowhere zero near $p$. Then $\log|f| = m_j \log|z_1| + \log|u|$ with $\log|u|$ pluriharmonic, and $\log|z_1|$ depends only on $z_1$, so by Fubini's theorem and Example P6.8.9, for a test form $\beta$ of degree $2n - 2$ supported near $p$, $\int T \wedge \beta = m_j \int_{\{z_1 = 0\}} \beta = m_j \int [Z_j] \wedge \beta$. So $T - [\operatorname{div}(f)]$ is a current of order $0$ supported on $S$. Both $T$ and $[\operatorname{div}(f)]$ are positive currents of bidimension $(n - 1, n - 1)$, and the trace measure of such a current puts no mass on an analytic set of dimension at most $n - 2$, since it is bounded on a ball of radius $r$ by a constant times $r^{2n-2}$, by Lelong's monotonicity formula, while such a set is covered by $O(r^{-(2n-4)})$ balls of radius $r$ (Dem97a, Chapter III; GH78, Chapter 3, Section 2). So $T - [\operatorname{div}(f)] = 0$.

*Proposition P6.8.6.* For part 1, if $e_U = g e_V$, then $\varphi_U = \varphi_V - \log|g|$ and $\partial\bar\partial \log|g| = 0$ by part 5 of Proposition P6.8.2, so $2 i\partial\bar\partial \varphi_U = 2 i \partial\bar\partial \varphi_V$ on $U \cap V$. It is real because $\varphi_U$ is real, and closed because $d(\partial\bar\partial \varphi) = 0$ on currents. For part 2, $\psi = \varphi_U - \varphi_{0,U}$ does not depend on $U$, because both weights change by the same $-\log|g|$, so $\psi$ is a global locally integrable function with $h = h_0 e^{-2\psi}$, and $i\Theta_h - i\Theta_{h_0} = 2 i \partial\bar\partial \psi = d(i \bar\partial \psi)$ is exact in the complex of currents, which computes the de Rham cohomology by de Rham's theorem (Dem97a, Chapter IV). The smooth form $\frac{1}{2\pi} i\Theta_{h_0}$ represents the real first Chern class by Chapter 5. For part 3, the pullback $\nu^* h$ is a smooth metric on $\nu^* L$ with curvature $\nu^* i\Theta_h$, and $\nu^* i\Theta_h \geq 0$ because the Levi form of $\varphi_U \circ \nu$ in a direction $\xi$ is the Levi form of $\varphi_U$ in the direction $d\nu(\xi)$, as in part 6 of Proposition P6.8.2. The degree of $L|_C$ is the degree of $\nu^* L$, which is $\int_{\tilde C} \nu^* c_1(L, h) \geq 0$ by Chapter 5.

*Proposition P6.8.8.* For part 1, if $e = g e'$ for another frame $e'$, then $s_j = g_j e = g_j g e'$ and $\frac12 \log \sum_j |g g_j|^2 = \varphi + \log|g|$; this is the change of weight required by Definition P6.8.5 for $|e'|^2 = |g|^2 |e|^2$. The weight is psh by part 5 of Proposition P6.8.2 and locally integrable by part 1. Also $|s_j|_h^2 = |g_j|^2 e^{-2\varphi} = |g_j|^2 / \sum_k |g_k|^2 \leq 1$. Off $\operatorname{Bs}$ the weight is smooth and $2 i\partial\bar\partial \varphi = i \partial\bar\partial \log \sum_j |g_j|^2 = \Phi^* \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, because $\Phi$ is given in homogeneous coordinates by $(g_1, \dots, g_r)$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}} = i\partial\bar\partial \log \sum_j |w_j|^2$ for any local holomorphic lift $w$. Part 2 is part 1 with $r = 1$ and Theorem P6.8.4: $2 i\partial\bar\partial \log|g| = 2\pi \cdot \frac{i}{\pi} \partial\bar\partial \log|g| = 2\pi [\operatorname{div}(g)]$. For part 3, the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ is $\prod_j g_j^{m c_j}$ in the frame dual to the local equation, so part 2 gives the weight $\sum_j m c_j \log|g_j| = m \varphi_D$ and curvature $2\pi [mD]$; dividing by $m$ gives $2 i\partial\bar\partial \varphi_D = 2\pi [D]$.

### Exercises

1. (A) On $\mathbb{C}^2$ compute $\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\bar\partial \log|z_1 z_2^2 (z_1 - z_2)|$ with Theorem P6.8.4, and evaluate it on the test form $\chi(z) \, \frac{i}{2} dz_2 \wedge d\bar z_2$ for a radial cut-off $\chi$ with $\chi(0) = 1$.
2. (A) Show that $\varphi(z_1, z_2) = \log(|z_1|^2 + |z_2|^4)$ is psh on $\mathbb{C}^2$ and compute its Levi form on $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$; find the directions in which it degenerates at the point $(0, 1)$.
3. (B) Let $X$ be a compact connected complex manifold. Show, with part 1 of Proposition P6.8.2, that every psh function on $X$ is constant.
4. (B) On $\mathbb{P}^1$ equip $\mathcal{O}(2)$ with the metric of part 1 of Proposition P6.8.8 for the sections $z_0^2, z_1^2$, and with the metric of the single section $z_0 z_1$. Compute both curvature currents in the chart $z_0 = 1$ and check that both have total mass $\int c_1 = 2$.
5. (B) In Example P6.8.11, let $C \subseteq X$ be the strict transform of a line through the blown up point. Compute $\int_C c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(E), h)$ for the singular metric of the example, by restricting the weight to $C$ and applying Theorem P6.8.4 on $C$, and compare with $E \cdot C = 1$.
6. (C) Let $\varphi$ be psh on a neighbourhood of $\overline{B}(0, R) \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ and $M(r) = \max_{S(0, r)} \varphi$. Show that $M$ is a convex increasing function of $\log r$ on $(0, R]$: first for $n = 1$ by comparing $\varphi$ on an annulus with the harmonic function $a + b \log|\zeta|$ equal to $M$ on the two boundary circles, then in general by taking the supremum over complex lines through $0$.

### Lean

Mathlib has no theory of currents on complex manifolds and no singular Hermitian metrics, so nothing in this section is formalized there. The project library has no statement about plurisubharmonic functions or curvature currents, and the handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Plurisubharmonic functions were introduced independently by Lelong and by Oka in 1942 in the study of pseudoconvexity, and Lelong defined the currents of integration on analytic sets and proved that they are closed and positive (Lel57a). The Poincaré-Lelong formula is the starting point of the theory of positive currents in Demailly's book, Chapter III (Dem97a), and appears in Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 3, Section 2 (GH78). The literature state of every result of the section is theorem. Singular Hermitian metrics with positive curvature current were made a tool of algebraic geometry by Demailly, who showed that on a projective manifold a line bundle is pseudo-effective in the sense of Definition P6.8.7 if and only if its first Chern class lies in the closure of the cone spanned by classes of effective divisors (Dem92a; Dem12a, Section 6); compare the pseudo-effective cone of P7, Chapter 5. Example P6.8.11 is the simplest case of the divisorial Zariski decomposition: the curvature current $2\pi[E]$ is the only closed positive current in the class of $E$, up to the factor, so $E$ is a fixed part of every positive current in the class.
