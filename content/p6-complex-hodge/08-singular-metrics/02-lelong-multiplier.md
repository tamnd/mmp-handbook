## Lelong numbers and analytic multiplier ideals

### Statement

Throughout the section $X$ is a complex manifold of dimension $n$. A function $\varphi$ on $X$ is *quasi-psh* if it is locally the sum of a psh function and a smooth function; by part 2 of Proposition P6.8.6, the weights of a singular metric $h = h_0 e^{-2\psi}$ with $\psi$ quasi-psh are quasi-psh, and the weights of a metric with $i\Theta_h \geq 0$ are psh. For $x \in X$, $\mathfrak{m}_x$ is the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$, and for a germ $g$, $\operatorname{ord}_x g$ is its order of vanishing at $x$. In local coordinates centred at $x$ and for $\varphi$ psh near $x$, put $M_x(r) = \sup_{B(x, r)} \varphi$.

**Definition P6.8.12 (Lelong numbers).** Let $\varphi$ be quasi-psh near $x$. The *Lelong number* of $\varphi$ at $x$ is
$$\nu(\varphi, x) = \liminf_{z \to x} \frac{\varphi(z)}{\log|z - x|},$$
computed in local coordinates centred at $x$. For a singular metric $h$ with quasi-psh weights, $\nu(h, x) = \nu(\varphi_U, x)$ for a weight on a neighbourhood of $x$.

**Proposition P6.8.13 (properties of Lelong numbers).** Let $\varphi$ be psh on a neighbourhood of $\overline{B}(x, R)$ in $\mathbb{C}^n$.

1. The function $t \mapsto M_x(e^t)$ is convex and increasing on $(-\infty, \log R)$, the limit $\lim_{r \to 0} M_x(r) / \log r$ exists, is finite and $\geq 0$, and equals $\nu(\varphi, x)$. For $0 < r_0 < R$ there is a constant $C$ with $\varphi(z) \leq \nu(\varphi, x) \log|z - x| + C$ for $|z - x| < r_0$.
2. The Lelong number does not depend on the coordinates, it does not change when a function that is bounded near $x$ is added to $\varphi$, and it is defined for quasi-psh functions and for singular metrics with quasi-psh weights.
3. If $f$ is holomorphic and not identically zero near $x$, then $\nu(\log|f|, x) = \operatorname{ord}_x f$. If $D = \sum_j c_j D_j$ is an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with local equations $g_j$ and weight $\varphi_D = \sum_j c_j \log|g_j|$ as in Proposition P6.8.8, then $\nu(\varphi_D, x) = \operatorname{mult}_x D = \sum_j c_j \operatorname{ord}_x g_j$. If $g_1, \dots, g_r$ generate an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ near $x$ and $c > 0$, then $\nu(\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |g_i|^2, x) = c \operatorname{ord}_x \mathfrak{a}$, where $\operatorname{ord}_x \mathfrak{a} = \min_i \operatorname{ord}_x g_i$.
4. The function $y \mapsto \nu(\varphi, y)$ is upper semicontinuous on $B(x, R)$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ with $\nu(\varphi, y) < \nu(\varphi, x) + \varepsilon$ for $|y - x| < \delta$.

**Theorem P6.8.14 (Siu).** Let $T$ be a closed positive current of bidegree $(1,1)$ on $X$, locally $T = i\partial\bar\partial\varphi$ with $\varphi$ psh, and $c > 0$. Then $E_c = \{ x \in X : \nu(\varphi, x) \geq c \}$ is a closed analytic subset of $X$.

**Definition P6.8.15 (analytic multiplier ideals).** Let $\varphi$ be quasi-psh on $X$. The *multiplier ideal sheaf* $\mathcal{J}(\varphi) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ is the sheaf of germs $f \in \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ such that $|f|^2 e^{-2\varphi}$ is integrable on some neighbourhood of $x$ with respect to $d\lambda$ in local coordinates. For a singular metric $h$ on a line bundle with quasi-psh weights, $\mathcal{J}(h) = \mathcal{J}(\varphi_U)$ on $U$. For an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ the *analytic multiplier ideal* of $D$ is $\mathcal{J}(\varphi_D)$, and for a nonzero ideal sheaf $\mathfrak{a}$ locally generated by $g_1, \dots, g_r$ and $c > 0$ it is $\mathcal{J}(\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |g_i|^2)$.

These definitions do not depend on choices. A change of coordinates multiplies $d\lambda$ by a smooth positive function. A change of frame changes the weight by $\log|g|$ with $g$ a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function, which is bounded near each point. Two systems of generators $g_i$ and $g'_j$ of $\mathfrak{a}$ near $x$ satisfy $g'_j = \sum_i a_{ji} g_i$ and $g_i = \sum_j b_{ij} g'_j$ with holomorphic $a_{ji}$, $b_{ij}$, so $\sum_j |g'_j|^2$ and $\sum_i |g_i|^2$ are bounded by constant multiples of each other near $x$, and the two weights differ by a bounded function. Adding a bounded function to $\varphi$ does not change $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)$, so $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)$ depends only on the psh part of a quasi-psh function up to a smooth function.

**Lemma P6.8.16 (integrability of monomials and radial weights).** Let $w = (w_1, \dots, w_n)$ be coordinates on a neighbourhood of $0$ in $\mathbb{C}^n$.

1. Let $h$ be holomorphic and not identically zero near $0$, let $a_i$ be the largest integer with $w_i^{a_i} \mid h$, and let $b_1, \dots, b_n \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $|h|^2 \prod_i |w_i|^{2 b_i}$ is integrable on some neighbourhood of $0$ if and only if $a_i + b_i > -1$ for every $i$.
2. Let $g$ be holomorphic near $0$, $g \neq 0$, and $c \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $|g|^2 |w|^{-2c}$ is integrable on some neighbourhood of $0$ if and only if $\operatorname{ord}_0 g > c - n$.

**Theorem P6.8.17 (Nadel's coherence theorem).** For every quasi-psh function $\varphi$ on $X$, the multiplier ideal sheaf $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)$ is a coherent ideal sheaf. More precisely, if $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ is a ball on which $\varphi$ is psh, then $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)|_\Omega$ is generated as an $\mathcal{O}_\Omega$-module by the holomorphic functions $f$ on $\Omega$ with $\int_\Omega |f|^2 e^{-2\varphi} d\lambda < \infty$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P6.8.18 (Skoda's estimates).** Let $\varphi$ be psh near $x \in X$.

1. If $\nu(\varphi, x) < 1$, then $e^{-2\varphi}$ is integrable near $x$, so $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)_x = \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$.
2. If $\nu(\varphi, x) \geq n + s$ for an integer $s \geq 0$, then $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)_x \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x^{s+1}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P6.8.19 (analytic and algebraic multiplier ideals).** Let $X$ be a smooth quasi-projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$, $D$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $X$, $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ a nonzero ideal sheaf and $c > 0$ a rational number. Let $\mathcal{J}(X, D)$ and $\mathcal{J}(X, \mathfrak{a}^c)$ be the algebraic multiplier ideals of Definition P7.10.2. Then, on the complex manifold $X^{\mathrm{an}}$,
$$\mathcal{J}(\varphi_D) = \mathcal{J}(X, D)^{\mathrm{an}}, \qquad \mathcal{J}\Big(\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |g_i|^2\Big) = \mathcal{J}(X, \mathfrak{a}^c)^{\mathrm{an}},$$
where the $g_i$ are local generators of $\mathfrak{a}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.8.20 (thresholds and Lelong numbers).** Let $X$ be a smooth quasi-projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$, $D$ a nonzero effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $x \in \operatorname{Supp} D$. Then the log canonical threshold of Definition P7.10.5 satisfies
$$\frac{1}{\operatorname{mult}_x D} \leq \operatorname{lct}_x(D) \leq \frac{n}{\operatorname{mult}_x D}.$$

### Proof in words

The Lelong number is the slope at $-\infty$ of the convex function $t \mapsto \sup_{|z - x| < e^t} \varphi$. Convexity is Hadamard's three circles theorem on each complex line through $x$, and it gives everything else: the slope at $-\infty$ is a limit, it bounds $\varphi$ from above by $\nu \log|z - x|$ plus a constant, and slopes at nearby points are controlled by values at two fixed radii. For $\log|f|$ the slope is the order of vanishing, read off on a line on which the leading form of $f$ does not vanish.

The multiplier ideal measures how far $e^{-2\varphi}$ is from being integrable. Two computations do all the work. In monomial coordinates integrability is a product of one variable conditions, $|w|^{2b}$ being integrable near $0$ in $\mathbb{C}$ if and only if $b > -1$. For radial weights integrability is a condition on the order of vanishing, because a holomorphic function with order $m$ at the origin has sphere means of size $t^{2m}$. The radial computation gives the half of Skoda's estimate that bounds the multiplier ideal from above, since a large Lelong number makes $e^{-2\varphi}$ larger than a negative power of $|z - x|$.

Coherence comes from Hörmander's estimate. The global $L^2$ holomorphic functions on a ball generate a coherent sheaf by the strong Noetherian property, and to show that they generate every germ of $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)$ one cuts off a local section and corrects it by a solution of a $\bar\partial$ equation with the extra weight $|z - x|^{-2(n+k)}$. The correction then vanishes to order $k + 1$ at $x$, and Krull's intersection theorem removes the error.

For divisors and ideals the analytic and algebraic ideals agree because both are computed on a log resolution. The change of variables formula moves the integral to the resolution, where the Jacobian contributes the relative canonical divisor and the weight becomes monomial. The monomial computation turns integrability into the condition $\operatorname{div}(f \circ \mu) + K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor \geq 0$, which defines the algebraic multiplier ideal.

### Examples

**Example P6.8.21 (snc divisors).** On $\mathbb{C}^n$ let $D = \sum_i a_i \{z_i = 0\}$ with $a_i \in \mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$, so $\varphi_D = \sum_i a_i \log|z_i|$ and $e^{-2\varphi_D} = \prod_i |z_i|^{-2a_i}$. By part 1 of Lemma P6.8.16 with $b_i = -a_i$, a germ $f$ lies in $\mathcal{J}(\varphi_D)_0$ if and only if $z_i^{a'_i} \mid f$ with $a'_i - a_i > -1$, that is $a'_i \geq \lfloor a_i \rfloor$, for every $i$. So $\mathcal{J}(\varphi_D) = (\prod_i z_i^{\lfloor a_i \rfloor})$, which is the algebraic computation of Example P7.10.6. The Lelong number at $0$ is $\sum_i a_i$, by part 3 of Proposition P6.8.13, while the ideal at $0$ is trivial exactly when every $a_i < 1$. For $D = \frac{9}{10}(\{z_1 = 0\} + \{z_2 = 0\})$ in $\mathbb{C}^2$ the Lelong number at $0$ is $\frac{9}{5} \geq 1$ and the ideal is trivial, so the hypothesis $\nu < 1$ of part 1 of Proposition P6.8.18 is sufficient and not necessary.

**Example P6.8.22 (the cusp).** Let $f = y^2 - x^3$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$, $D = \{f = 0\}$ and $\varphi = c \log|f|$ with $0 < c < 1$. The Lelong number at $0$ is $2c$, since $\operatorname{ord}_0 f = 2$. Let $U = \{ |x| < 1, |y| < 1 \}$ and $\sigma_\lambda(x, y) = (\lambda^2 x, \lambda^3 y)$ for $0 < \lambda \leq 1$. Then $f \circ \sigma_\lambda = \lambda^6 f$, and the real Jacobian of $\sigma_\lambda$ is $|\lambda^2|^2 |\lambda^3|^2 = \lambda^{10}$. The set $A = U \setminus \sigma_{1/2}(\overline{U})$ has closure disjoint from $0$, and $U \setminus \{0\}$ is the disjoint union of the sets $\sigma_{2^{-k}}(A)$ for $k \geq 0$ up to a null set. On $\overline{A}$ the curve $D$ is smooth and $f$ is part of a coordinate system near each of its points, so $\int_A |f|^{-2c} < \infty$ because $c < 1$. By the change of variables formula,
$$\int_{\sigma_\lambda(A)} |g|^2 |f|^{-2c} d\lambda = \lambda^{10} \int_A |g \circ \sigma_\lambda|^2 \lambda^{-12c} |f|^{-2c} d\lambda.$$
For $g = 1$ the sum over $\lambda = 2^{-k}$ is $\sum_k 2^{-k(10 - 12c)} \int_A |f|^{-2c}$, finite if and only if $c < \frac56$. For $g = x$ and $g = y$ the factors are $\lambda^{14 - 12c}$ and $\lambda^{16 - 12c}$, and the sums converge for all $c < 1$. So $\mathcal{J}(c \log|f|)_0 = \mathcal{O}$ for $c < \frac56$ and $\mathcal{J}(c \log|f|)_0 = \mathfrak{m}_0$ for $\frac56 \leq c < 1$, which is Example P7.10.7 computed without a resolution. Corollary P6.8.20 gives $\frac12 \leq \operatorname{lct}_0(D) \leq 1$, and the value $\frac56$ lies strictly between.

**Example P6.8.23 (powers of the maximal ideal).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}_0 = (z_1, \dots, z_n)$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$ and $c > 0$. The weight is $\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |z_i|^2 = c \log|z|$ and $e^{-2\varphi} = |z|^{-2c}$. By part 2 of Lemma P6.8.16, $f \in \mathcal{J}(\varphi)_0$ if and only if $\operatorname{ord}_0 f > c - n$, that is $\operatorname{ord}_0 f \geq \lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1$. So $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}$, with the convention $\mathfrak{m}^k = \mathcal{O}$ for $k \leq 0$, as in Example P7.10.8. The Lelong number is $c$. For $c = n + s$ the ideal is exactly $\mathfrak{m}^{s+1}$, so part 2 of Proposition P6.8.18 is sharp, and for $c < 1$ the ideal is trivial, as part 1 predicts.

### Proof

*Lemma P6.8.16.* For part 1, write $h = \prod_i w_i^{a_i} h'$ with $h'$ holomorphic and divisible by no $w_i$. The integrand is $|h'|^2 \prod_i |w_i|^{2(a_i + b_i)}$. If $a_i + b_i > -1$ for all $i$, then since $|h'|$ is bounded on a polydisc $\Delta^n$ and $\int_{|w_i| < \rho} |w_i|^{2e} d\lambda = 2\pi \int_0^\rho t^{2e + 1} dt < \infty$ for $e > -1$, Fubini's theorem gives integrability on $\Delta^n$. Conversely suppose $a_1 + b_1 \leq -1$. Since $w_1 \nmid h'$, the function $h'(0, w_2, \dots, w_n)$ is not identically zero, so in every neighbourhood of $0$ there is a point $q = (0, q_2, \dots, q_n)$ with $h'(q) \neq 0$ and $q_i \neq 0$ for $i \geq 2$. Near $q$ the integrand is bounded below by a positive constant times $|w_1|^{2(a_1 + b_1)}$, which is not integrable near $q$ because $\int_{|w_1| < \rho} |w_1|^{2e} = \infty$ for $e \leq -1$.

For part 2, let $m = \operatorname{ord}_0 g$ and write $g = \sum_{k \geq m} g_k$ with $g_k$ homogeneous of degree $k$ and $g_m \neq 0$, the series converging uniformly on a ball $B(0, \rho)$. In polar coordinates $w = t \theta$ with $\theta$ in the unit sphere $S$ and $\sigma$ its surface measure,
$$\int_{B(0, \rho)} |g|^2 |w|^{-2c} d\lambda = \int_0^\rho t^{2n - 1 - 2c} \int_S |g(t\theta)|^2 d\sigma(\theta) \, dt.$$
The rotations $\theta \mapsto e^{i\alpha} \theta$ preserve $\sigma$ and multiply $g_k$ by $e^{ik\alpha}$, so the $g_k$ are orthogonal in $L^2(S)$, and $\int_S |g(t\theta)|^2 d\sigma = \sum_{k \geq m} t^{2k} \int_S |g_k|^2 d\sigma$. This is at least $t^{2m} \int_S |g_m|^2 d\sigma$ with $\int_S |g_m|^2 d\sigma > 0$, and at most $C t^{2m}$ because $|g(w)| \leq C' |w|^m$ on $B(0, \rho)$. So the integral is finite if and only if $\int_0^\rho t^{2n - 1 - 2c + 2m} dt < \infty$, that is $2n - 1 - 2c + 2m > -1$, that is $m > c - n$.

*Proposition P6.8.13.* For part 1, work in coordinates with $x = 0$. For $b$ in the unit sphere, $u_b(\zeta) = \varphi(\zeta b)$ is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on $|\zeta| < R$, and $m_b(r) = \max_{|\zeta| = r} u_b$ is increasing in $r$ by the maximum principle (part 1 of Proposition P6.8.2 for $n = 1$). It is a convex function of $\log r$: for $0 < r_1 < r_2 < R$ let $\ell(\zeta) = \alpha + \beta \log|\zeta|$ be the harmonic function equal to $m_b(r_1)$ on $|\zeta| = r_1$ and $m_b(r_2)$ on $|\zeta| = r_2$. Then $u_b - \ell$ is subharmonic on the annulus and $\leq 0$ on its boundary, so $\leq 0$ inside by the maximum principle, and $m_b(r) \leq \alpha + \beta \log r$ for $r_1 \leq r \leq r_2$, which is convexity. By part 1 of Proposition P6.8.2, $M_0(r) = \max_{|z| = r} \varphi = \sup_b m_b(r)$, a supremum of convex increasing functions of $\log r$ which is finite, hence convex and increasing. A convex increasing function $\mu(t)$ on $(-\infty, \log R)$ has slopes $s(t, t_0) = (\mu(t_0) - \mu(t)) / (t_0 - t) \geq 0$ that increase in $t$ for fixed $t_0$, so $\nu = \lim_{t \to -\infty} s(t, t_0)$ exists in $[0, \infty)$, and $\mu(t) / t \to \nu$ as $t \to -\infty$ because $\mu(t) = \mu(t_0) - s(t, t_0)(t_0 - t)$. Also $s(t, t_0) \geq \nu$, that is $\mu(t) \leq \mu(t_0) + \nu (t - t_0)$. With $t = \log|z|$ and $t_0 = \log r_0$ this gives $\varphi(z) \leq M_0(|z|) \leq \nu \log|z| + C$ with $C = M_0(r_0) - \nu \log r_0$. It remains to identify $\nu$ with the Lelong number. For $|z| < 1$, $\log|z| < 0$ and $\varphi(z) \leq M_0(|z|)$ give $\varphi(z) / \log|z| \geq M_0(|z|) / \log|z|$, so $\nu(\varphi, 0) \geq \nu$. Conversely, $\varphi$ attains $M_0(r)$ at some $z_r$ with $|z_r| = r$, by upper semicontinuity on the sphere, and $\varphi(z_r) / \log r = M_0(r) / \log r \to \nu$, so $\nu(\varphi, 0) \leq \nu$.

For part 2, if $F$ is a biholomorphism near $x$ with $F(x) = x'$, then $C^{-1} |z - x| \leq |F(z) - x'| \leq C |z - x|$ near $x$, so $\log|F(z) - x'| / \log|z - x| \to 1$ as $z \to x$ and the two liminf's agree. If $|\psi| \leq C$ near $x$, then $\psi(z) / \log|z - x| \to 0$, so $\nu(\varphi + \psi, x) = \nu(\varphi, x)$. A quasi-psh function is psh plus a smooth function, which is bounded near $x$; two psh decompositions differ by a function that is bounded near $x$; and two weights of a metric differ by $\log|g|$ with $g$ a unit. So the definition for quasi-psh functions and metrics is consistent.

For part 3, take $x = 0$ and $m = \operatorname{ord}_0 f$, and write $f = f_m + (\text{higher terms})$ with $f_m \neq 0$ homogeneous. Then $|f(z)| \leq C|z|^m$ near $0$, so $\log|f| \leq m \log|z| + \log C$ and $\nu(\log|f|, 0) \geq m$. Choose $b$ with $|b| = 1$ and $f_m(b) \neq 0$. Then $f(\zeta b) = \zeta^m (f_m(b) + O(\zeta))$, so $\log|f(\zeta b)| / \log|\zeta| \to m$ as $\zeta \to 0$ and $\nu(\log|f|, 0) \leq m$. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor choose $k \geq 1$ with $k c_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ for all $j$. Then $k \varphi_D = \log|\prod_j g_j^{k c_j}|$, so $\nu(\varphi_D, 0) = \frac{1}{k} \operatorname{ord}_0 \prod_j g_j^{k c_j} = \sum_j c_j \operatorname{ord}_0 g_j$, which is $\operatorname{mult}_0 D$. For an ideal, let $m = \min_i \operatorname{ord}_0 g_i$, attained at $i_0$. On one side $\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |g_i|^2 \leq c \log (\sqrt{r} \max_i |g_i|) \leq c m \log|z| + C$ near $0$, so the Lelong number is at least $cm$. On the other side $\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |g_i|^2 \geq c \log|g_{i_0}|$, and dividing by $\log|z| < 0$ reverses the inequality, so the Lelong number is at most $\nu(c \log|g_{i_0}|, 0) = cm$.

For part 4, let $0 < r_0 < R/3$ and write $M_y$ for the function $M$ centred at $y$. For $0 < r < r_0$ and $|y - x| \leq r/4$, the inclusions $B(x, r/4) \subseteq B(y, r/2)$ and $B(y, r_0) \subseteq B(x, 2r_0)$ give $M_y(r/2) \geq M_x(r/4)$ and $M_y(r_0) \leq M_x(2r_0)$. By part 1 applied at $y$, the slope at $-\infty$ is at most the slope between $\log(r/2)$ and $\log r_0$, so
$$\nu(\varphi, y) \leq \frac{M_y(r_0) - M_y(r/2)}{\log r_0 - \log(r/2)} \leq \frac{M_x(2r_0) - M_x(r/4)}{\log(2 r_0 / r)}.$$
As $r \to 0$, $M_x(r/4) / \log r \to \nu(\varphi, x)$ by part 1 and $\log(2r_0 / r) / (-\log r) \to 1$, so the right side tends to $\nu(\varphi, x)$. Given $\varepsilon$, choose $r$ with the right side less than $\nu(\varphi, x) + \varepsilon$ and $\delta = r/4$.

*Theorem P6.8.14.* This is Siu's theorem (Siu74a). A proof by Kiselman's method and a proof based on Hörmander's estimate are in Demailly's book, Chapter III, and in Demailly's lectures, Section 2 (Dem97a; Dem12a). In the case of the weight of a divisor or of an ideal, where it is used below, it follows from part 3 of Proposition P6.8.13: the set $\{ \operatorname{mult}_x D \geq c \}$ is closed and analytic because the condition $\operatorname{ord}_x g \geq k$ is the vanishing of the partial derivatives of $g$ of order less than $k$.

*Theorem P6.8.17.* Coherence is local and the multiplier ideal does not change when a smooth function is subtracted from $\varphi$, so it suffices to treat $\varphi$ psh on a ball $\Omega$. Let $\mathcal{H}$ be the space of holomorphic functions $f$ on $\Omega$ with $\int_\Omega |f|^2 e^{-2\varphi} < \infty$ and let $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_\Omega$ be the ideal sheaf generated by $\mathcal{H}$. Choose a countable subset $\{ f_1, f_2, \dots \}$ of $\mathcal{H}$ whose span is dense in $\mathcal{H}$, possible because $\mathcal{H}$ is a closed subspace of a separable Hilbert space of functions. The ideal sheaves $\mathcal{E}_k$ generated by $f_1, \dots, f_k$ are coherent and increase with $k$, so by the strong Noetherian property of coherent sheaves (GR84a) the sequence is stationary on every compact subset of $\Omega$, and the union $\mathcal{E}'$ is coherent. A function $f \in \mathcal{H}$ is the limit in $\mathcal{H}$ of combinations of the $f_k$, and the $L^2$ limit of holomorphic functions converges locally uniformly by the mean value property, because $e^{-2\varphi}$ is bounded below on compact sets. On a compact set on which $\mathcal{E}' = \mathcal{E}_k$, the closedness of submodules of $\mathcal{O}^p$ under locally uniform convergence (GR84a) gives $f \in \mathcal{E}_k$. So $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{E}'$ is coherent. Clearly $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\varphi)$.

For the converse fix $x \in \Omega$ and $f \in \mathcal{J}(\varphi)_x$, represented on a ball $V \ni x$ with $\overline{V} \subseteq \Omega$ and $\int_V |f|^2 e^{-2\varphi} < \infty$. Let $k \geq 0$ and choose a smooth function $\theta$ with support in $V$ and $\theta = 1$ on a ball $V'$ around $x$. The function $\psi = 2\varphi + 2(n + k) \log|z - x|$ is psh on $\Omega$. The $(0,1)$-form $g = f \bar\partial\theta$ is $\bar\partial$-closed and vanishes on $V'$, so $|z - x|$ is bounded below on its support and $\int_\Omega |g|^2 e^{-\psi} < \infty$. By Hörmander's theorem, Theorem P6.8.24 of Section 8.3, which does not depend on this section, there is $u$ with $\bar\partial u = g$ and $\int_\Omega |u|^2 e^{-\psi} (1 + |z|^2)^{-2} < \infty$. Then $F = \theta f - u$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. Since $\Omega$ is bounded, $|z - x|^{-2(n+k)}$ and $(1 + |z|^2)^{-2}$ are bounded below on $\Omega$, so $\int_\Omega |u|^2 e^{-2\varphi} < \infty$, and with $\int_V |f|^2 e^{-2\varphi} < \infty$ this gives $F \in \mathcal{H}$. On $V'$, $u = f - F$ is holomorphic, and $e^{-2\varphi}$ is bounded below by a positive constant because $\varphi$ is bounded above, so $\int_{V'} |u|^2 |z - x|^{-2(n+k)} < \infty$. By part 2 of Lemma P6.8.16, $\operatorname{ord}_x u > k$. So $f_x = F_x + u_x \in \mathcal{E}_x + \mathfrak{m}_x^{k+1}$ for every $k$. The $\mathcal{O}_{\Omega,x}$-module $N = \mathcal{O}_{\Omega,x} / \mathcal{E}_x$ is finitely generated over a Noetherian local ring, so $\bigcap_k \mathfrak{m}_x^{k+1} N = 0$ by Krull's intersection theorem, and the image of $f_x$ in $N$ is zero. So $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)_x = \mathcal{E}_x$.

*Proposition P6.8.18.* Part 1 is Skoda's lemma (Sko72a; Dem12a, Section 5). In one variable it follows from the Riesz decomposition: near $x$, $\varphi(z) = \int_{B} \log|z - w| \, d\mu(w) + v(z)$ with $\mu = \frac{1}{2\pi} \Delta\varphi$ restricted to a small disc $B$ and $v$ harmonic, and $\mu(\{x\}) = \nu(\varphi, x)$, so $\mu(B) = \kappa < 1$ for $B$ small. Jensen's inequality for the probability measure $\mu / \kappa$ gives $e^{-2\varphi} \leq C \int_B |z - w|^{-2\kappa} \, d\mu(w) / \kappa$, and Fubini's theorem shows that the right side is integrable near $x$, since $\int_B |z - w|^{-2\kappa} d\lambda(z)$ is bounded in $w$. The general case reduces to this one by restricting to complex lines through points near $x$ and an estimate of Lelong numbers along these lines, or by the Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem (Dem12a, Section 5).

For part 2, part 1 of Proposition P6.8.13 gives $\varphi(z) \leq \nu(\varphi, x) \log|z - x| + C$ for $|z - x| < r_0 \leq 1$. Since $\log|z - x| < 0$ and $\nu(\varphi, x) \geq n + s$, this gives $\varphi(z) \leq (n + s) \log|z - x| + C$, so $e^{-2\varphi} \geq e^{-2C} |z - x|^{-2(n+s)}$. If $f \in \mathcal{J}(\varphi)_x$, then $|f|^2 |z - x|^{-2(n+s)}$ is integrable near $x$, and part 2 of Lemma P6.8.16 with $c = n + s$ gives $\operatorname{ord}_x f > s$, that is $f \in \mathfrak{m}_x^{s+1}$.

*Theorem P6.8.19.* By Definition P7.10.1 and Hironaka's theorem, Theorem 4.2.10, there is a log resolution $\mu \colon X' \to X$ of $D$: a projective birational morphism from a smooth variety such that the exceptional locus is a divisor and $\mu^* D + \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ has simple normal crossing support. Definition P7.10.2 gives $\mathcal{J}(X, D) = \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(P)$ with $P = K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor$, where $K_{X'/X}$ is the effective divisor of the Jacobian determinant of $\mu$, supported on the exceptional locus.

First the right side is described analytically. The morphism $\mu$ is an isomorphism over the complement of a closed subset $Z \subseteq X$ of codimension at least $2$, since $X$ is normal. A section of $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(P)$ over an open $V \subseteq X$ is a rational function $h$ on $\mu^{-1}(V)$ with $\operatorname{div}(h) + P \geq 0$. The non-exceptional components of $P$ have coefficients $\leq 0$, so $h$ is regular off the exceptional locus, and $h = f \circ \mu$ for a regular function $f$ on $V \setminus Z$, which extends to $V$ because $X$ is normal. So $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(P) = \{ f \in \mathcal{O}_X : \operatorname{div}(f \circ \mu) + P \geq 0 \}$. The same argument in the analytic category, with the Riemann extension theorem across $Z$ in place of normality, gives $\mu^{\mathrm{an}}_* \mathcal{O}_{X'^{\mathrm{an}}}(P) = \{ f \in \mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}} : \operatorname{div}(f \circ \mu) + P \geq 0 \}$. The morphism $\mu$ is proper, so the comparison theorem for proper morphisms (SGA1, Exposé XII, Théorème 4.2), the relative form of the GAGA theorems of Chapter 7, gives $(\mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(P))^{\mathrm{an}} = \mu^{\mathrm{an}}_* (\mathcal{O}_{X'}(P)^{\mathrm{an}})$.

Now the integral. Let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighbourhood $V$ of $x \in X$. The map $\mu$ is biholomorphic outside the exceptional locus, an analytic subset of measure zero, so the change of variables formula gives
$$\int_V |f|^2 e^{-2\varphi_D} d\lambda = \int_{\mu^{-1}(V)} |f \circ \mu|^2 e^{-2\varphi_D \circ \mu} |J_\mu|^2 d\lambda',$$
where in local coordinates on $X'$ the holomorphic Jacobian determinant $J_\mu$ has divisor $K_{X'/X}$ and $|J_\mu|^2$ is the real Jacobian. Near a point $p \in X'$ choose coordinates $w$ in which the components of $\mu^* D + \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ through $p$ are coordinate hyperplanes, and write $\mu^* D = \sum_i e_i \{w_i = 0\}$ and $K_{X'/X} = \sum_i k_i \{w_i = 0\}$ near $p$. Then $\varphi_D \circ \mu = \sum_i e_i \log|w_i| + (\text{bounded})$ and $|J_\mu|^2 = |\text{unit}|^2 \prod_i |w_i|^{2k_i}$, so the integrand is comparable near $p$ to $|f \circ \mu|^2 \prod_i |w_i|^{2(k_i - e_i)}$. By part 1 of Lemma P6.8.16 it is integrable near $p$ if and only if $\operatorname{ord}_{w_i}(f \circ \mu) + k_i - e_i > -1$ for every $i$. For the integer $N_i = \operatorname{ord}_{w_i}(f \circ \mu) + k_i$, the inequality $N_i > e_i - 1$ is equivalent to $N_i \geq \lfloor e_i \rfloor$. So the integrand is integrable near $p$ if and only if $\operatorname{div}(f \circ \mu) + P \geq 0$ near $p$, since components of $P$ that are not coordinate hyperplanes at $p$ do not pass through $p$.

If $f \in \mathcal{J}(\varphi_D)_x$, the integral over a small $V$ is finite, so the integrand on $X'$ is integrable near every $p \in \mu^{-1}(x)$ and $\operatorname{div}(f \circ \mu) + P \geq 0$ near $\mu^{-1}(x)$. Conversely, if this holds, the compact set $\mu^{-1}(x)$ is covered by finitely many open sets on which the integrand is integrable; their union $W$ contains $\mu^{-1}(V)$ for a small neighbourhood $V$ of $x$, because $\mu$ is proper and hence closed, and the integral over $V$ is finite. So $\mathcal{J}(\varphi_D)_x$ is the stalk at $x$ of $\mu^{\mathrm{an}}_* \mathcal{O}_{X'^{\mathrm{an}}}(P)$, which is $\mathcal{J}(X, D)^{\mathrm{an}}_x$ by the previous paragraph.

For the ideal, take a log resolution $\mu$ of $\mathfrak{a}$, so that $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$ with $F + \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ of simple normal crossing support, and $\mathcal{J}(X, \mathfrak{a}^c) = \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor cF \rfloor)$ by Definition P7.10.2. Near $p \in X'$ with $F = \sum_i f_i \{w_i = 0\}$, the functions $g_i \circ \mu$ generate the ideal of $F$, so $g_i \circ \mu = \prod_j w_j^{f_j} g'_i$ with $g'_1, \dots, g'_r$ generating the unit ideal near $p$. Then $\sum_i |g'_i|^2$ is bounded above and below by positive constants near $p$, and $\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |g_i \circ \mu|^2 = \sum_j c f_j \log|w_j| + (\text{bounded})$. The argument for divisors applies with $e_j = c f_j$.

*Corollary P6.8.20.* By Theorem P6.8.19, $\operatorname{lct}_x(D) = \sup\{ c > 0 : \mathcal{J}(c \varphi_D)_x = \mathcal{O}_{X,x} \}$, and by part 3 of Proposition P6.8.13, $\nu(c \varphi_D, x) = c \operatorname{mult}_x D$. If $c < 1 / \operatorname{mult}_x D$, the Lelong number is $< 1$ and the ideal is trivial at $x$ by part 1 of Proposition P6.8.18, which gives the lower bound. If $c \geq n / \operatorname{mult}_x D$, the Lelong number is $\geq n$ and $\mathcal{J}(c \varphi_D)_x \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x$ by part 2 with $s = 0$, which gives the upper bound.

### Notes and history

Lelong introduced the numbers $\nu(\varphi, x)$ for closed positive currents in 1957 (Lel57a) and proved that $\nu([Z], x)$ is the multiplicity of $Z$ at $x$. Siu proved the analyticity of the upper level sets in 1974 (Siu74a), a result conjectured by Lelong, and Skoda proved the integrability lemma in 1972 (Sko72a). Nadel introduced multiplier ideal sheaves in 1989 and 1990 (Nad90) in the study of Kähler-Einstein metrics on Fano manifolds, proving coherence and the vanishing theorem of Section 8.3. The comparison with the algebraic multiplier ideal is in Lazarsfeld, Section 9.3 (Laz04), and in Demailly's lectures (Dem12a). The literature state of every result of the section is theorem. Theorem P6.8.14 and part 1 of Proposition P6.8.18 are cited and not proved here in full; for divisors and ideals the lower bound of Corollary P6.8.20 also has an algebraic proof on a log resolution, and the later chapters of the handbook use only the algebraic form of P7, Chapter 10.
