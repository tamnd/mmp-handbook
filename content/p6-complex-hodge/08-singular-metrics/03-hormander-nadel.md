## Hörmander's estimates and Nadel vanishing

### Statement

Throughout the section $X$ is a complex manifold of dimension $n$ and $L$ a holomorphic line bundle on $X$. For a Hermitian metric $\omega$ on $X$ and a singular metric $h$ on $L$ with weights $\varphi$, the pointwise norm $|f|_{\omega, h}$ of an $L$-valued form $f$ is computed with $\omega$ and $h$, so that $|f|^2_{\omega, h} = |f_U|^2_\omega e^{-2\varphi_U}$ when $f = f_U \otimes e_U$, and $\|f\|^2 = \int_X |f|^2_{\omega, h} \, dV_\omega$ with $dV_\omega = \omega^n / n!$. Derivatives are taken in the sense of currents. An open set $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ is *pseudoconvex* if it has a continuous psh exhaustion function, and a complex manifold is *weakly pseudoconvex* if it has a smooth psh exhaustion function. Balls, polydiscs, $\mathbb{C}^n$ and closed complex submanifolds of $\mathbb{C}^N$ have the exhaustion $|z|^2$.

**Theorem P6.8.24 (Hörmander).** Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ be pseudoconvex and $\psi$ psh on $\Omega$. Let $g$ be a $(p, q)$-form on $\Omega$ with $q \geq 1$, locally square integrable coefficients, $\bar\partial g = 0$ and $\int_\Omega |g|^2 e^{-\psi} d\lambda < \infty$. Then there is a $(p, q - 1)$-form $u$ with locally square integrable coefficients, $\bar\partial u = g$ and
$$\int_\Omega |u|^2 e^{-\psi} (1 + |z|^2)^{-2} \, d\lambda \leq \int_\Omega |g|^2 e^{-\psi} \, d\lambda.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P6.8.25 ($L^2$ estimates for $(n, q)$-forms).** Let $(X, \omega)$ be a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifold and $h$ a singular metric on $L$ with quasi-psh weights and $i\Theta_h \geq \varepsilon \omega$ in the sense of currents, for a constant $\varepsilon > 0$. Let $q \geq 1$ and let $g$ be an $L$-valued $(n, q)$-form with $\bar\partial g = 0$ and $\|g\| < \infty$. Then there is an $L$-valued $(n, q - 1)$-form $f$ with $\bar\partial f = g$ and
$$\|f\|^2 \leq \frac{1}{q \varepsilon} \|g\|^2.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P6.8.26 (extension of $\bar\partial$ equations across a hypersurface).** Let $H \subseteq X$ be a closed analytic subset of codimension at least $1$, and let $f$ and $g$ be forms on $X$ with locally square integrable coefficients such that $\bar\partial f = g$ on $X \setminus H$. Then $\bar\partial f = g$ on $X$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P6.8.27 (Nadel vanishing, analytic form).** Let $X$ be a projective manifold with a Kähler form $\omega$, and let $h$ be a singular metric on $L$ with quasi-psh weights and $i\Theta_h \geq \varepsilon \omega$ for a constant $\varepsilon > 0$. Then
$$H^q(X, K_X \otimes L \otimes \mathcal{J}(h)) = 0 \quad \text{for } q \geq 1.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.8.28 (Nadel vanishing for divisors and ideals).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$ and $L$ a divisor on $X$.

1. If $D$ is an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $L - D$ is ample, then $H^q(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, D)) = 0$ for $q \geq 1$.
2. If $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ is a nonzero ideal sheaf, $c > 0$ is rational, $M$ is a divisor with $\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(M)$ globally generated and $L - cM$ is ample, then $H^q(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, \mathfrak{a}^c)) = 0$ for $q \geq 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Hörmander's estimate is the existence half of a Hilbert space duality. The Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano inequality of Chapter 6 says that, on $(n, q)$-forms, $\|\bar\partial v\|^2 + \|\bar\partial^* v\|^2$ is bounded below by the curvature term, which is at least $q \varepsilon \|v\|^2$ when $i\Theta_h \geq \varepsilon\omega$. For a $\bar\partial$-closed $g$, the map $\bar\partial^* v \mapsto \langle v, g \rangle$ is then a bounded linear functional, and a Riesz representative $f$ solves $\bar\partial f = g$ with the stated bound. Completeness of the metric makes the formal adjoint the Hilbert space adjoint, and singular weights are reached by smoothing the metric and passing to a weak limit.

Nadel vanishing follows from a fine resolution. The sheaves of $L$-valued $(n, q)$-forms that are locally square integrable with respect to $h$, together with their $\bar\partial$, are fine, and the kernel in degree $0$ is $K_X \otimes L \otimes \mathcal{J}(h)$, because for $(n, 0)$-forms the measure $|f|^2_\omega dV_\omega$ does not depend on $\omega$ and is $|F|^2 e^{-2\varphi} d\lambda$ up to a constant. Hörmander's theorem on small balls gives local exactness. The cohomology is then the cohomology of global $L^2$ forms, and a global $\bar\partial$-closed form is solved first on the complement $Y$ of a hyperplane section, which is affine and hence weakly pseudoconvex, with an $L^2$ bound, and the solution extends across the hyperplane section because $\bar\partial$ equations between $L^2$ forms extend across hypersurfaces.

The extension lemma is a cut-off argument: the cut-off functions $\chi_\varepsilon = \theta(|s| / \varepsilon)$ along a local equation $s$ have $\bar\partial$ bounded in $L^2$, because the coarea formula bounds the volume of the shell $\{ \varepsilon \leq |s| \leq 2\varepsilon \}$ weighted by $|\partial s|^2$ by a constant times $\varepsilon^2$. The algebraic Nadel vanishing is the analytic one for the metric with weight $\frac1m \varphi_A + \varphi_D$, where $A = m(L - D)$ is very ample, combined with the comparison of multiplier ideals and GAGA.

### Examples

**Example P6.8.29 (Kodaira vanishing).** If $h$ is a smooth metric on $L$ with $i\Theta_h$ positive in the sense of Definition P6.5.19, take $\omega = i\Theta_h$ and $\varepsilon = 1$. The weights are smooth, so locally bounded, and $\mathcal{J}(h) = \mathcal{O}_X$. Theorem P6.8.27 gives $H^q(X, K_X \otimes L) = 0$ for $q \geq 1$, which is Corollary P6.6.13 (Kodaira vanishing) on a projective manifold, the case $p = n$ of Theorem P6.6.12 (Kodaira-Akizuki-Nakano vanishing). On $\mathbb{P}^n$ with $L = \mathcal{O}(k)$, $k \geq 1$, and the Fubini-Study metric of Proposition P6.5.20, it gives $H^q(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(k - n - 1)) = 0$ for $q \geq 1$.

**Example P6.8.30 (the cuspidal cubic).** Let $C = \{ y^2 z = x^3 \} \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$, with its cusp at $p = [0 : 0 : 1]$, and $D = cC$ with $\frac56 \leq c < 1$. At $p$, in the chart $z = 1$, Example P6.8.22 gives $\mathcal{J}(X, D)_p = \mathfrak{m}_p$, and at the smooth points of $C$ the divisor $D$ is $c$ times a smooth curve with $c < 1$, so the ideal is trivial by Example P6.8.21. So $\mathcal{J}(X, D) = \mathfrak{m}_p$, the ideal sheaf of the reduced point $p$. With $L = dH$ for a line $H$, $K_{\mathbb{P}^2} = -3H$ and $L - D \equiv (d - 3c) H$, part 1 of Corollary P6.8.28 applies for $d \geq 3$ and gives $H^q(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathfrak{m}_p(d - 3)) = 0$ for $q \geq 1$. This can be checked directly. The sequence $0 \to \mathfrak{m}_p(k) \to \mathcal{O}(k) \to k(p) \to 0$ and $H^1(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(k)) = 0$ show that $H^1(\mathfrak{m}_p(k))$ is the cokernel of evaluation $H^0(\mathcal{O}(k)) \to k(p) = \mathbb{C}$, which is surjective for $k \geq 0$ and zero for $k < 0$. For $d = 2$, $H^1(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathfrak{m}_p(-1)) = \mathbb{C} \neq 0$: there the class $(2 - 3c) H$ is negative and the vanishing fails, so the positivity hypothesis on $L - D$ cannot be dropped.

**Example P6.8.31 (strict positivity is needed).** Let $X$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g \geq 1$, $L = \mathcal{O}_X$ with the trivial metric, so $i\Theta_h = 0 \geq 0$ and $\mathcal{J}(h) = \mathcal{O}_X$. Then $H^1(X, K_X \otimes L) = H^1(X, K_X)$ is dual to $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = \mathbb{C}$ by Serre duality of Chapter 4, so it is nonzero. Semipositive curvature is not enough in Theorem P6.8.27; the curvature must dominate a Kähler form. For $g = 0$ the same computation gives $H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, K) \neq 0$ as well, so the failure has nothing to do with the genus.

### Proof

*Theorem P6.8.24.* This is Hörmander's theorem (Hor90a, Theorem 4.4.2; Hor65a). Its proof is the case $X = \Omega$ of the argument sketched below for Theorem P6.8.25, applied on the sublevel sets of the exhaustion, for the trivial bundle with the weight $\psi + 2 \log(1 + |z|^2)$, whose Levi form dominates $2(1 + |z|^2)^{-2}$ times the Euclidean one; Hörmander first treats smooth $\psi$ and then decreasing limits of smooth psh functions.

*Theorem P6.8.25.* This is the theorem of Andreotti-Vesentini and Hörmander in the form of Demailly (AV65a; Hor65a; Dem12a, Section 5; Dem97a, Chapter VIII), and only a sketch is given. First let $h$ be smooth and $\omega$ complete. By Theorem P6.6.4 (Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity) and Corollary P6.6.6 (the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano inequality), for every smooth compactly supported $L$-valued $(n, q)$-form $v$,
$$\|\bar\partial v\|^2 + \|\bar\partial^* v\|^2 \geq \int_X \langle [i\Theta_h, \Lambda] v, v \rangle \, dV_\omega,$$
and by Proposition P6.6.5 (the curvature term for a line bundle), at a point where $\omega = i\sum_j dz_j \wedge d\bar z_j$ and $i\Theta_h = i \sum_j \lambda_j dz_j \wedge d\bar z_j$, the operator $[i\Theta_h, \Lambda]$ acts on $dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n \wedge d\bar z_J$ by multiplication with $\sum_{j \in J} \lambda_j \geq q \varepsilon$ for $|J| = q$. Since $\omega$ is complete, smooth compactly supported forms are dense in the domains of $\bar\partial$ and $\bar\partial^*$ for the graph norm (AV65a), and the inequality $\|\bar\partial v\|^2 + \|\bar\partial^* v\|^2 \geq q\varepsilon \|v\|^2$ holds for all $v$ in both domains. Given $g$ with $\bar\partial g = 0$, let $v$ be in the domain of $\bar\partial^*$ and write $v = v_1 + v_2$ with $v_1 \in \ker\bar\partial$ and $v_2 \perp \ker\bar\partial$. Then $v_2 \perp \operatorname{im}\bar\partial$, so $\bar\partial^* v_2 = 0$ and $\bar\partial^* v = \bar\partial^* v_1$, and
$$|\langle g, v \rangle|^2 = |\langle g, v_1 \rangle|^2 \leq \|g\|^2 \|v_1\|^2 \leq \frac{\|g\|^2}{q\varepsilon} \|\bar\partial^* v_1\|^2 = \frac{\|g\|^2}{q\varepsilon} \|\bar\partial^* v\|^2.$$
So $\bar\partial^* v \mapsto \langle v, g \rangle$ is a well defined linear functional of norm at most $\|g\| / \sqrt{q\varepsilon}$ on the image of $\bar\partial^*$, and the Riesz representation theorem gives $f$ with $\|f\|^2 \leq \|g\|^2 / (q\varepsilon)$ and $\langle f, \bar\partial^* v \rangle = \langle g, v \rangle$ for all $v$, that is $\bar\partial f = g$. On a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifold with exhaustion $\rho$, the sublevel sets $X_c = \{\rho < c\}$ carry complete Kähler metrics $\omega + \delta \, i\partial\bar\partial \chi(\rho)$ for a convex increasing $\chi$ with $\chi(t) \to \infty$ as $t \to c$, and the estimate for these metrics, letting $\delta \to 0$, holds with $\omega$, because for $(n, q)$-forms the quantity $|g|^2_\omega dV_\omega$ decreases when $\omega$ increases. Solutions on $X_c$ with uniform bounds have a weak limit as $c \to \infty$. For singular $h$, Demailly's regularization (Dem92a) gives, on each $X_c$, smooth metrics $h_k$ increasing to $h$ with $i\Theta_{h_k} \geq (\varepsilon - \delta_k) \omega$ and $\delta_k \to 0$; the solutions $f_k$ for $h_k$ are bounded in $L^2(h_j)$ for $k \geq j$, and a weak limit and monotone convergence give the solution for $h$.

*Lemma P6.8.26.* The statement is local and forms are treated coefficient by coefficient, so let $X$ be a neighbourhood of $0$ in $\mathbb{C}^n$ with $0 \in H$, and let $s$ be holomorphic near $0$, not identically zero, with $H \subseteq \{s = 0\}$ near $0$; such an $s$ exists because a nonzero element of the ideal of $H$ at $0$ exists. Let $m = \operatorname{ord}_0 s \geq 1$ and $s_m$ the homogeneous part of degree $m$. After a linear change of coordinates $s_m(e_j) \neq 0$ for $j = 1, \dots, n$, because $\{ s_m \neq 0 \}$ is dense and the set of bases with all vectors in it is open and nonempty.

*Step 1: fibres of $s$ have bounded volume.* Fix $j$. Since $s(\zeta e_j) = \zeta^m(s_m(e_j) + O(\zeta))$, there are $\delta > 0$ and $c > 0$ such that $\zeta \mapsto s(\zeta e_j)$ has exactly $m$ zeros in $|\zeta| < \delta$, counted with multiplicity, and $|s(\zeta e_j)| \geq c\delta^m$ for $|\zeta| = \delta$. By uniform continuity there are $\eta \in (0, \delta]$ and $\tau > 0$ such that $|s(z) - s(z_j e_j)| + |t| < c\delta^m$ whenever $|z_j| = \delta$, $|z_k| < \eta$ for $k \neq j$ and $|t| < \tau$. By Rouché's theorem, for such $z_k$ and $t$ the function $z_j \mapsto s(z) - t$ has exactly $m$ zeros in $|z_j| < \delta$. Choose $\eta$ and $\tau$ that work for every $j$ and let $P = \{ |z_k| < \eta \text{ for all } k \}$. For $|t| < \tau$ let $S_t$ be the set of points of $s^{-1}(t) \cap P$ where $\partial s \neq 0$, a complex hypersurface. With $\beta = \frac{i}{2}\sum_k dz_k \wedge d\bar z_k$, one has $\beta^{n-1} / (n-1)! = \sum_j \beta_{\hat j}$ with $\beta_{\hat j} = \prod_{k \neq j} \frac{i}{2} dz_k \wedge d\bar z_k$, and $\beta_{\hat j}|_{S_t}$ is the pullback of Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{C}^{n-1}$ by the projection $\pi_j$ that forgets $z_j$, a positive form on $S_t$ away from the critical points of $\pi_j|_{S_t}$. So by the area formula
$$\operatorname{vol}(S_t) = \sum_j \int_{S_t} \beta_{\hat j} = \sum_j \int_{\mathbb{C}^{n-1}} \#(\pi_j^{-1}(y) \cap S_t) \, d\lambda(y) \leq n m (\pi \eta^2)^{n-1},$$
because $\pi_j(S_t)$ lies in a polydisc of volume $(\pi\eta^2)^{n-1}$ and each fibre $\pi_j^{-1}(y) \cap S_t$ consists of zeros of $z_j \mapsto s(z) - t$ with $|z_j| < \eta \leq \delta$, of which there are at most $m$.

*Step 2: the cut-off functions.* For a $(1,0)$-form $\alpha = \sum_k \alpha_k dz_k$ put $|\alpha|^2 = \sum_k |\alpha_k|^2$. A direct computation in coordinates gives
$$\tfrac{i}{2} \partial s \wedge \overline{\partial s} \wedge \frac{\beta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!} = |\partial s|^2 \frac{\beta^n}{n!} = |\partial s|^2 \, d\lambda,$$
and the left side is $s^*(\frac{i}{2} dt \wedge d\bar t) \wedge \beta^{n-1}/(n-1)!$. On the open set where $\partial s \neq 0$, $s$ is a submersion, and the coarea formula for the submersion $s$ gives, for every Borel set $A \subseteq \mathbb{C}$,
$$\int_{P \cap s^{-1}(A)} |\partial s|^2 \, d\lambda = \int_A \operatorname{vol}(S_t) \, d\lambda(t) \leq n m (\pi\eta^2)^{n-1} \lambda(A)$$
for $A$ inside $\{|t| < \tau\}$; the critical set of $s$ contributes nothing, because the integrand vanishes there. Choose a smooth function $\theta$ on $\mathbb{R}$ with $0 \leq \theta \leq 1$, $\theta(r) = 0$ for $r \leq 1$ and $\theta(r) = 1$ for $r \geq 2$, and put $\chi_\varepsilon = \theta(|s| / \varepsilon)$ for $2\varepsilon < \tau$. Then $\chi_\varepsilon$ is smooth, vanishes near $\{s = 0\}$, tends to $1$ almost everywhere as $\varepsilon \to 0$, and $\bar\partial\chi_\varepsilon = \varepsilon^{-1} \theta'(|s|/\varepsilon) \bar\partial|s|$ is supported in $\Sigma_\varepsilon = P \cap \{ \varepsilon \leq |s| \leq 2\varepsilon \}$. Where $s \neq 0$, $\bar\partial|s| = \bar\partial (s\bar s)^{1/2} = \frac{s \, \overline{\partial s}}{2|s|}$, so $|\bar\partial |s|| = \frac12 |\partial s|$. With $A = \{\varepsilon \leq |t| \leq 2\varepsilon\}$, of measure $3\pi\varepsilon^2$,
$$\int_P |\bar\partial\chi_\varepsilon|^2 d\lambda \leq \frac{\sup|\theta'|^2}{4\varepsilon^2} \int_{\Sigma_\varepsilon} |\partial s|^2 d\lambda \leq \frac{3\pi}{4} \sup|\theta'|^2 \, n m (\pi\eta^2)^{n-1},$$
a bound independent of $\varepsilon$.

*Step 3: the extension.* Let $\gamma$ be a smooth compactly supported form in $P$ of complementary degree. The form $\chi_\varepsilon \gamma$ has compact support in $P \setminus H$, so by hypothesis $\int g \wedge \chi_\varepsilon \gamma = (-1)^{k+1} \int f \wedge \bar\partial(\chi_\varepsilon \gamma)$, where $k$ is the degree of $f$ and the sign is the one defining $\bar\partial$ on currents. Expand $\bar\partial(\chi_\varepsilon \gamma) = \bar\partial\chi_\varepsilon \wedge \gamma + \chi_\varepsilon \bar\partial\gamma$. As $\varepsilon \to 0$, dominated convergence gives $\int g \wedge \chi_\varepsilon \gamma \to \int g \wedge \gamma$ and $\int f \wedge \chi_\varepsilon \bar\partial\gamma \to \int f \wedge \bar\partial\gamma$, because $f$ and $g$ are locally integrable. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
$$\Big| \int f \wedge \bar\partial\chi_\varepsilon \wedge \gamma \Big| \leq C_\gamma \Big( \int_{\Sigma_\varepsilon \cap \operatorname{supp}\gamma} |f|^2 d\lambda \Big)^{1/2} \Big( \int_P |\bar\partial\chi_\varepsilon|^2 d\lambda \Big)^{1/2},$$
and the first factor tends to $0$ because $f$ is square integrable on $\operatorname{supp}\gamma$ and $\Sigma_\varepsilon$ decreases to a subset of $\{s = 0\}$, a set of measure zero, while the second is bounded by Step 2. So $\int g \wedge \gamma = (-1)^{k+1} \int f \wedge \bar\partial\gamma$, that is $\bar\partial f = g$ near $0$.

*Theorem P6.8.27.* For an open set $U \subseteq X$ let $\mathcal{L}^{q}(U)$ be the space of $L$-valued $(n, q)$-forms $f$ on $U$ with measurable coefficients such that $|f|^2_{\omega, h}$ and $|\bar\partial f|^2_{\omega, h}$ are integrable on every compact subset of $U$. This defines sheaves $\mathcal{L}^q$ with maps $\bar\partial \colon \mathcal{L}^q \to \mathcal{L}^{q+1}$. Since $\bar\partial(\chi f) = \bar\partial\chi \wedge f + \chi \bar\partial f$, they are modules over the sheaf of smooth functions, hence fine and acyclic.

The kernel of $\bar\partial \colon \mathcal{L}^0 \to \mathcal{L}^1$ is $K_X \otimes L \otimes \mathcal{J}(h)$. Locally $f = F \, dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n \otimes e$, and $\bar\partial f = 0$ in the sense of currents means that $F$ is a distribution with $\partial F / \partial \bar z_k = 0$ for all $k$, hence holomorphic, by ellipticity of $\bar\partial$ on functions (convolution with $\rho_\varepsilon$ gives holomorphic functions converging to $F$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, hence locally uniformly). For an $(n, 0)$-form, $|dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n|^2_\omega \, dV_\omega = 2^n d\lambda$ for every Hermitian metric $\omega$, as one checks at a point in coordinates in which $\omega$ is diagonal. So $|f|^2_{\omega, h} dV_\omega = 2^n |F|^2 e^{-2\varphi} d\lambda$, and $f$ is locally square integrable if and only if $F \in \mathcal{J}(\varphi)$ at every point.

Local exactness in degree $q \geq 1$. Let $g \in \mathcal{L}^q(U)$ with $\bar\partial g = 0$ and $x \in U$. Choose a ball $B$ around $x$ in coordinates, relatively compact in $U$, on which $L$ has a frame with weight $\varphi$. Then $2 i\partial\bar\partial\varphi = i\Theta_h \geq \varepsilon\omega > 0$, so $\varphi$, which is quasi-psh, has positive Levi form as a current, and by part 4 of Proposition P6.8.2 it is almost everywhere equal to a psh function, which may be used as the weight. On $B$, $\omega$ is comparable to the Euclidean metric, so the norms $\int_B |g|^2_{\omega, h} dV_\omega$ and $\int_B |G|^2 e^{-2\varphi} d\lambda$ are comparable, where $G$ is the form $g$ in the frame. Theorem P6.8.24 with $\psi = 2\varphi$ gives $u$ with $\bar\partial u = G$ and $\int_B |u|^2 e^{-2\varphi} (1 + |z|^2)^{-2} \leq \int_B |G|^2 e^{-2\varphi}$. Since $B$ is bounded, $(1 + |z|^2)^{-2}$ is bounded below on $B$, so $u \otimes e \in \mathcal{L}^{q-1}(B)$ and solves the equation on $B$.

So $0 \to K_X \otimes L \otimes \mathcal{J}(h) \to \mathcal{L}^0 \to \mathcal{L}^1 \to \dots$ is a fine resolution, and $H^q(X, K_X \otimes L \otimes \mathcal{J}(h))$ is the $q$-th cohomology of the complex $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^\bullet)$. Because $X$ is compact, $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^q)$ consists of forms $g$ with $\|g\| < \infty$ and $\|\bar\partial g\| < \infty$. Let $q \geq 1$ and $g \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^q)$ with $\bar\partial g = 0$. Embed $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$, let $H$ be the intersection of $X$ with a hyperplane not containing any component of $X$, and $Y = X \setminus H$. Then $Y$ is a closed submanifold of $\mathbb{A}^N = \mathbb{P}^N \setminus (\text{hyperplane})$, so $|z|^2$ is a smooth psh exhaustion of $Y$ and $(Y, \omega|_Y)$ is a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifold. The restriction of $h$ to $Y$ satisfies $i\Theta_h \geq \varepsilon\omega$. Theorem P6.8.25 gives an $L$-valued $(n, q-1)$-form $f$ on $Y$ with $\bar\partial f = g$ on $Y$ and $\int_Y |f|^2_{\omega, h} dV_\omega \leq (q\varepsilon)^{-1} \|g\|^2$. Extend $f$ by $0$ across $H$, a set of measure zero. Then $f$ and $g$ are square integrable with respect to $h$ and hence, because the weights are bounded above on compact sets, locally square integrable in the ordinary sense. By Lemma P6.8.26, $\bar\partial f = g$ on $X$, so $f \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{q-1})$ and the class of $g$ is zero.

*Corollary P6.8.28.* For part 1, choose $m \geq 1$ such that $mD$ is integral and $A = m(L - D)$ is a very ample divisor. Let $h_A$ be the metric on $\mathcal{O}_X(A)$ pulled back from the Fubini-Study metric by the embedding given by $|A|$. By Proposition P6.5.20, $\omega = i\Theta_{h_A}$ is the pullback of the Fubini-Study form by an embedding, hence a Kähler form. Let $h_{mD}$ be the singular metric of the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ from part 2 of Proposition P6.8.8. Since $mL = A + mD$, the metric $h_A \otimes h_{mD}$ on $\mathcal{O}_X(mL)$ has a unique $m$-th root $h$ on $\mathcal{O}_X(L)$, defined by $|e|_h = |e^{\otimes m}|^{1/m}_{h_A \otimes h_{mD}}$, and its weights are $\varphi = \frac1m \varphi_A + \varphi_D$ in compatible frames, by part 3 of Proposition P6.8.8. They are quasi-psh, and
$$i\Theta_h = \frac1m i\Theta_{h_A} + 2 i\partial\bar\partial\varphi_D = \frac1m \omega + 2\pi[D] \geq \frac1m \omega.$$
Because $\varphi_A$ is smooth, $\mathcal{J}(h) = \mathcal{J}(\varphi_D)$, which is $\mathcal{J}(X, D)^{\mathrm{an}}$ by Theorem P6.8.19. Theorem P6.8.27 with $\varepsilon = \frac1m$ gives $H^q(X^{\mathrm{an}}, (\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, D))^{\mathrm{an}}) = 0$ for $q \geq 1$. The sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(X, D)$ is coherent on the projective variety $X$, so by GAGA (Chapter 7) its algebraic cohomology is isomorphic to the analytic cohomology of its analytification, and it vanishes.

For part 2, let $s_1, \dots, s_r \in H^0(X, \mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(M))$ generate this sheaf. In a local frame $e_M$, $s_i = g'_i e_M$ with $g'_1, \dots, g'_r$ generating $\mathfrak{a}$. Part 1 of Proposition P6.8.8 gives a metric $h_M$ on $\mathcal{O}_X(M)$ with weight $\varphi_M = \frac12 \log \sum_i |g'_i|^2$ and $i\Theta_{h_M} \geq 0$. Choose $m$ with $mc \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $A = m(L - cM)$ very ample, and let $h$ be the $m$-th root of $h_A \otimes h_M^{\otimes mc}$ on $\mathcal{O}_X(mL)$, with weights $\frac1m \varphi_A + c\varphi_M$ and $i\Theta_h = \frac1m \omega + c \, i\Theta_{h_M} \geq \frac1m \omega$. Then $\mathcal{J}(h) = \mathcal{J}(\frac{c}{2} \log \sum_i |g'_i|^2) = \mathcal{J}(X, \mathfrak{a}^c)^{\mathrm{an}}$ by Theorem P6.8.19, and Theorem P6.8.27 and GAGA conclude as in part 1.

### Lean

Mathlib has Hilbert spaces, the Riesz representation theorem and Lebesgue integration on $\mathbb{C}^n$, but no $\bar\partial$ operator on forms, no $L^2$ cohomology and no multiplier ideals, so nothing in this section is formalized. The project library has no statement of Hörmander's estimate or Nadel vanishing, and the handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Hörmander proved the $L^2$ existence theorem for $\bar\partial$ on pseudoconvex domains in 1965 (Hor65a), and Andreotti and Vesentini proved the version on complete Kähler manifolds in the same year (AV65a). Demailly formulated the estimate for line bundles with singular metrics and showed how it yields vanishing theorems (Dem92a; Dem97a, Chapter VIII; Dem12a, Section 5). Nadel proved the vanishing theorem in 1990 (Nad90). Kawamata and Viehweg had proved the algebraic special case for snc $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors by covering tricks, and the algebraic form with multiplier ideals is in Lazarsfeld, Section 9.4 (Laz04). The literature state of every result of the section is theorem. Theorems P6.8.24 and P6.8.25 are cited with sketches. Corollary P6.8.28 is proved here when $L - D$ is ample; the case of P7, Chapter 10, where $L - D$ is only nef and big, needs more: by Kodaira's lemma $L - D \equiv A + E$ with $A$ an ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $E$ effective, and for $0 < \delta \ll 1$ the divisor $D' = D + \delta E$ has $L - D' = (1 - \delta)(L - D) + \delta A$ ample and $\mathcal{J}(X, D') = \mathcal{J}(X, D)$ (Laz04, Section 9.4).
