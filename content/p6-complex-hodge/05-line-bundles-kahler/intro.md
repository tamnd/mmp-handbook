# Line bundles on compact Kähler manifolds

A line bundle on a projective variety is the algebraic object behind divisors, linear systems and embeddings, and most of P3 and P7 is about line bundles. On a compact Kähler manifold the same objects can be studied with Hodge theory. Their isomorphism classes form the group $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^\times)$, and the exponential sequence relates this group to $H^1$ and $H^2$ of the structure sheaf and to integral cohomology. Two pieces come out: a continuous part, a complex torus built from $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$, and a discrete part, the Néron-Severi group of integral classes of type $(1,1)$. A metric on a line bundle gives a curvature form that represents the first Chern class in de Rham cohomology, and positivity of that form is the analytic counterpart of ampleness.

Section 5.1 sets up holomorphic line bundles and divisors on a complex manifold, defines the first Chern class through the exponential sequence and proves that the curvature form of any Hermitian metric represents it. It then proves that the Picard group of a compact Kähler manifold is an extension of a finitely generated group by a compact torus of dimension $h^{0,1}$. Section 5.2 proves the Lefschetz theorem on $(1,1)$-classes: an integral class is a Chern class exactly when it has type $(1,1)$. The worked examples are tori, where everything is explicit: the square of an elliptic curve has Picard number $3$ or $4$ according as the curve has complex multiplication, and an explicit two-dimensional torus has Picard number $0$. Section 5.3 defines positive line bundles, proves that the Fubini-Study metric makes $\mathcal{O}(1)$ positive, shows that positivity depends only on the Chern class and that very ample line bundles are positive, and characterizes Hodge manifolds as the compact complex manifolds with a positive line bundle. The converse, that a positive line bundle has a very ample power, is the Kodaira embedding theorem of Chapter 6.

Throughout the chapter $X$ is a connected complex manifold, compact and Kähler in most statements. The chapter uses Dolbeault cohomology and the Dolbeault isomorphism (Theorem P6.2.24), Hermitian metrics and Chern connections (Theorem P6.3.16, Corollary P6.3.17), and the Hodge decomposition (Theorem P6.4.16) and the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma (Corollary P6.4.18) from Chapters 2 to 4.

The chapter follows Huybrechts, Sections 2.3, 3.3, 4.4 and 5.3 (Huy05), Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 1, Sections 1 and 2 (GH78), and Voisin, Chapter 7 (Voi02), with Mumford's book (Mum70a) for the examples on tori.
