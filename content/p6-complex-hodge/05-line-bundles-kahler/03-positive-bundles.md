## Positive line bundles

### Statement

Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$. At a point $x$, a real $(1,1)$-form is $\alpha_x = i \sum_{j,k} a_{j\bar k} \, dz_j \wedge d\bar z_k$ with $(a_{j\bar k})$ a Hermitian matrix; reality of $\alpha$ is exactly the Hermitian symmetry $\overline{a_{j \bar k}} = a_{k \bar j}$. The Hermitian form $\xi \mapsto \sum a_{j\bar k} \xi_j \bar\xi_k$ on $T_x X$ does not depend on the coordinates.

**Definition P6.5.19 (positive forms and positive line bundles).** A real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha$ is *positive*, written $\alpha > 0$, if the Hermitian matrix $(a_{j\bar k}(x))$ is positive definite at every point $x$, and *semipositive*, written $\alpha \geq 0$, if it is positive semidefinite everywhere. A Kähler form is a closed positive real $(1,1)$-form. A holomorphic line bundle $L$ is *positive* if it has a Hermitian metric $h$ with $i \Theta_h > 0$, *semipositive* if it has one with $i\Theta_h \geq 0$, and *negative* if $L^{-1}$ is positive.

So $L$ is positive if and only if it has a metric for which $\frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_h$ is a Kähler form, and a manifold that carries a positive line bundle is Kähler.

**Proposition P6.5.20 (the Fubini-Study metric).** On $\mathbb{P}^n$ there is a Hermitian metric $h_{\mathrm{FS}}$ on $\mathcal{O}(1)$ with $|\ell|^2_{h_{\mathrm{FS}}}([z]) = |\ell(z)|^2 / |z|^2$ for every linear form $\ell$, viewed as a section of $\mathcal{O}(1)$. Its curvature satisfies
$$\frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_{h_{\mathrm{FS}}} = \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}, \qquad \omega_{\mathrm{FS}} = \frac{i}{2\pi} \partial \bar\partial \log |z|^2,$$
where $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is the Fubini-Study form, a Kähler form invariant under the unitary group with $\int_{\ell} \omega_{\mathrm{FS}} = 1$ on every line $\ell \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$. In particular $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is positive and $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))$ is the generator of $H^2(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathbb{Z})$ that is positive on lines.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P6.5.21 (positivity and Kähler classes).** Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold and $L$ a holomorphic line bundle.

1. For every closed real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha$ with $[\alpha] = c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$ there is a Hermitian metric $h$ on $L$ with $\frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_h = \alpha$.
2. $L$ is positive if and only if $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$ is the class of a Kähler form.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P6.5.22 (first properties).** Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold and $L, M$ holomorphic line bundles on $X$.

1. If $L$ and $M$ are positive, so is $L \otimes M$. If $L$ is positive, then $L^k \otimes M$ is positive for every $k \geq k_0(M)$.
2. If $f \colon Y \to X$ is a holomorphic immersion, for instance the inclusion of a complex submanifold, and $L$ is positive, then $f^* L$ is positive. If $f$ is any holomorphic map and $L$ is semipositive, $f^*L$ is semipositive.
3. If $L$ is generated by global sections and the holomorphic map $\phi_L \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^N$ defined by a basis of sections is an immersion, then $L \cong \phi_L^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ is positive. In particular a very ample line bundle, one for which $\phi_L$ is a closed embedding, is positive, and so is every line bundle some power of which is very ample.
4. If $L$ is positive and $\nu \colon C \to X$ is a nonconstant holomorphic map from a compact Riemann surface, then $\deg \nu^* L > 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P6.5.23 (Hodge manifolds).** A *Hodge manifold* is a compact Kähler manifold that has a Kähler form $\omega$ whose class lies in the image of $H^2(X, \mathbb{Q}) \to H^2(X, \mathbb{R})$.

**Corollary P6.5.24 (Hodge manifolds and positive line bundles).** A compact complex manifold is a Hodge manifold if and only if it carries a positive line bundle.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The Fubini-Study metric measures a linear form by its size relative to the length of the homogeneous coordinate vector, and its curvature is $\partial\bar\partial$ of $\log |z|^2$, which is a strictly plurisubharmonic function on each affine chart up to a pluriharmonic term. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows positivity, and a one-variable integral gives the value $1$ on a line.

Positivity of a line bundle depends only on its Chern class on a compact Kähler manifold: two closed real $(1,1)$-forms in the same class differ by $i\partial\bar\partial$ of a real function, by the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma (Corollary P6.4.18), and multiplying a metric by $e^{-2\pi u}$ adds exactly that term to its normalized curvature. So any Kähler form in $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$ is the curvature of a metric. Pullbacks preserve semipositivity, and positivity survives pullback by an immersion because a positive Hermitian form restricts to a positive form on a subspace. A very ample line bundle is the pullback of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ by an embedding, so it is positive; Chapter 6 proves the converse, which is the Kodaira embedding theorem. On a curve the degree is the integral of the curvature, which is positive. Finally a rational Kähler class becomes integral after scaling, the Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem realizes it as a Chern class, and the previous proposition makes the line bundle positive.

### Examples

**Example P6.5.25 (curves).** Let $C$ be a compact Riemann surface. A line bundle $L$ on $C$ is positive if and only if $\deg L > 0$. If $L$ is positive, $\deg L = \int_C \frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_h > 0$ by Proposition P6.5.6 and Example P6.5.10. Conversely, let $\deg L = d > 0$ and let $\omega$ be any Kähler form with $\int_C \omega = d$, for instance a multiple of the area form of a Riemannian metric. Since $H^2(C, \mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}$ by integration, $[\omega] = c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$, and Proposition P6.5.21 gives a metric with $\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_h = \omega$. On a curve every Kähler form has a class that is a real multiple of an integral class, so every compact Riemann surface is a Hodge manifold.

**Example P6.5.26 (the quadric surface).** On $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ every line bundle is $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ by Example P6.5.18. It is positive if and only if $a > 0$ and $b > 0$. If $a, b > 0$, the metric $h = \mathrm{pr}_1^* h_{\mathrm{FS}}^a \otimes \mathrm{pr}_2^* h_{\mathrm{FS}}^b$ has $\frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_h = a \omega_1 + b \omega_2$ with $\omega_k = \mathrm{pr}_k^* \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$. At a point, in the coordinates $(z, w)$ of the two factors, $\omega_1 = i \phi(z) dz \wedge d\bar z$ and $\omega_2 = i \psi(w) dw \wedge d\bar w$ with $\phi, \psi > 0$, so $a \omega_1 + b\omega_2$ has the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(a\phi, b\psi)$, which is positive definite. Conversely, the restriction of $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ to $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \{q\}$ is $\mathcal{O}(a)$ of degree $a$, and to $\{p\} \times \mathbb{P}^1$ it is $\mathcal{O}(b)$; by Proposition P6.5.22, part 4, positivity forces $a > 0$ and $b > 0$. The bundle $\mathcal{O}(1, 0)$ is semipositive and not positive: its form $\omega_1$ has the matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\phi, 0)$.

**Example P6.5.27 (complex tori).** Let $X = V / \Lambda$ be a complex torus and $L$ a line bundle with $c_1(L)$ the integral alternating form $E$ on $\Lambda$, which has type $(1,1)$ by Theorem P6.5.13. Then $L$ is positive if and only if $E(v, iv) > 0$ for every nonzero $v \in V$, the *Riemann condition*. Indeed, if $\omega$ is a Kähler form in $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$, its average $\bar\omega = \int_X t_y^* \omega \, dy$ over translations $t_y$, for the Haar probability measure $dy$, is a translation invariant positive $(1,1)$-form, closed, and in the same class because each $t_y$ is homotopic to the identity. An invariant form is harmonic for the flat metric, so $\bar\omega$ is the constant form $E$, and $E$ is positive. For the constant form $\frac{i}{2} dz \wedge d\bar z = dx \wedge dy$ in one variable, $(dx \wedge dy)(v, iv) = |v|^2$, and in general a constant real $(1,1)$-form $E$ is positive exactly when $E(v, iv) > 0$ for $v \neq 0$. Conversely, if $E(v, iv) > 0$, the constant form $E$ is a Kähler form in $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$, and Proposition P6.5.21 applies. For the torus of Example P6.5.17 every line bundle has $E = 0$, so no line bundle is positive and $X$ is not a Hodge manifold. For $E_\tau \times E_\tau$ the class $f_1 + f_2$ of Example P6.5.16 is positive.

### Proof

*Proposition P6.5.20.* Let $U_0 = \{z_0 \neq 0\}$ with affine coordinates $w_j = z_j / z_0$, and let $e_0$ be the section $z_0$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$, a frame on $U_0$. The formula defines a metric: if $\ell$ is a linear form and $\ell = g e_0$ on $U_0$, with $g = \ell(z)/z_0$, then $|\ell(z)|^2 / |z|^2 = |g|^2 \, |z_0|^2/|z|^2$, which is $|g|^2 |e_0|^2_h$ with $|e_0|^2_h = (1 + |w|^2)^{-1}$, smooth and positive, and the expression does not change when $z$ is scaled. By definition of the Chern curvature, on $U_0$
$$\Theta_{h_{\mathrm{FS}}} = -\partial \bar\partial \log (1 + |w|^2)^{-1} = \partial\bar\partial \log(1 + |w|^2),$$
and since $\log |z|^2 = \log |z_0|^2 + \log (1 + |w|^2)$ with $\log|z_0|^2$ pluriharmonic on $U_0$, this is $\partial\bar\partial \log |z|^2$ in the sense that the right side is a well-defined form on $\mathbb{P}^n$. So $\frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_{h_{\mathrm{FS}}} = \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$. In the coordinates $w$,
$$\partial_j \bar\partial_k \log(1 + |w|^2) = \frac{\delta_{jk}}{1 + |w|^2} - \frac{\bar w_j w_k}{(1 + |w|^2)^2},$$
so for $\xi \in \mathbb{C}^n$
$$\sum_{j,k} \partial_j \bar\partial_k \log(1 + |w|^2) \, \xi_j \bar\xi_k = \frac{(1 + |w|^2)|\xi|^2 - |\sum_j \bar w_j \xi_j|^2}{(1 + |w|^2)^2} \geq \frac{|\xi|^2}{(1 + |w|^2)^2}$$
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality $|\sum \bar w_j \xi_j|^2 \leq |w|^2 |\xi|^2$. So $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}} > 0$ on $U_0$, and by symmetry everywhere. It is closed, being a curvature form, and invariant under $U(n+1)$ because $|z|^2$ is. A line is a unitary translate of $\{z_2 = \cdots = z_n = 0\}$, on which $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}} = \frac{i}{2\pi} \frac{dw \wedge d\bar w}{(1 + |w|^2)^2}$ off one point. With $w = re^{i\theta}$, $i \, dw \wedge d\bar w = 2 r \, dr \wedge d\theta$, and
$$\int_\ell \omega_{\mathrm{FS}} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^\infty \frac{2 r \, dr}{(1 + r^2)^2} d\theta = \int_0^\infty \frac{2r\, dr}{(1+r^2)^2} = 1.$$
By Proposition P6.5.6, $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))$ pairs to $1$ with the class of a line, which generates $H_2(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathbb{Z})$, so it generates $H^2(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Z})$.

*Proposition P6.5.21.* Part 1: choose any metric $h_0$ on $L$. By Proposition P6.5.6, $\beta = \alpha - \frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_{h_0}$ is a $d$-exact real $(1,1)$-form. By the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma (Corollary P6.4.18) there is a smooth function $u$ with $\beta = i \partial \bar\partial u$; replacing $u$ by its real part does not change $i \partial\bar\partial u$, because $i\partial\bar\partial$ maps real functions to real forms and $\beta$ is real. Put $h = e^{-2\pi u} h_0$. Locally $\log |e|^2_h = \log |e|^2_{h_0} - 2\pi u$, so
$$\frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_h = \frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_{h_0} + \frac{i}{2\pi} \partial\bar\partial(2\pi u) = \frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_{h_0} + \beta = \alpha.$$
Part 2: if $L$ is positive with metric $h$, then $\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_h$ is a Kähler form in $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$ by Proposition P6.5.6. Conversely, apply part 1 to a Kähler form $\alpha$ in the class.

*Proposition P6.5.22.* Part 1: for metrics $h_L$, $h_M$, the product metric on $L \otimes M$ has $\Theta = \Theta_{h_L} + \Theta_{h_M}$, since $\log |e \otimes e'|^2 = \log |e|^2 + \log |e'|^2$, and a sum of positive forms is positive. If only $L$ is positive, compactness gives a constant $C$ with $i\Theta_{h_M} \geq -C \, i\Theta_{h_L}$, meaning that the difference is semipositive: the continuous function $x \mapsto \min_{|\xi|_{h_L} = 1} (i\Theta_{h_M})(\xi, \bar \xi)$, with $|\xi|_{h_L}$ measured by the Kähler metric $i\Theta_{h_L}$, is bounded below on the compact unit sphere bundle. Then $k \, i\Theta_{h_L} + i\Theta_{h_M} > 0$ for $k > C$.

Part 2: for a local frame $e$ of $L$, $f^* e$ is a frame of $f^*L$ and $|f^* e|^2_{f^* h} = |e|^2_h \circ f$, so $\Theta_{f^* h} = f^* \Theta_h$. At $y \in Y$, the Hermitian form of $f^*(i\Theta_h)$ on $T_y Y$ is the form of $i\Theta_h$ on $T_{f(y)} X$ composed with the complex linear map $df_y$. It is semidefinite if $i\Theta_h$ is, and definite if $i\Theta_h$ is definite and $df_y$ is injective.

Part 3: if $s_0, \dots, s_N$ is a basis of $H^0(X, L)$ without common zero, $\phi_L(x) = [s_0(x) : \cdots : s_N(x)]$ in any local frame, and $\phi_L^* z_j = s_j$ identifies $\phi_L^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ with $L$, because the frames $z_j$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ on $\{z_j \neq 0\}$ pull back to the frames $s_j$ of $L$ on $\{s_j \neq 0\}$ with the same transition functions $s_j / s_k$. So $L$ is positive by part 2 and Proposition P6.5.20. If $L^k$ is positive with a metric $h$, then $h^{1/k}$ is a metric on $L$ with $\Theta_{h^{1/k}} = \frac{1}{k}\Theta_h$, so $L$ is positive.

Part 4: by part 2, $\nu^*(i\Theta_h) \geq 0$ on $C$, and it is positive at every point where $d\nu \neq 0$. Since $\nu$ is nonconstant, $d\nu$ vanishes only at isolated points, so $\deg \nu^* L = \int_C \frac{i}{2\pi} \nu^* \Theta_h > 0$ by Example P6.5.10 and Proposition P6.5.6, part 3.

*Corollary P6.5.24.* If $L$ is positive with metric $h$, then $\omega = \frac{i}{2\pi} \Theta_h$ is a Kähler form whose class $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$ is integral, so $X$ is a Hodge manifold. Conversely, let $\omega$ be a Kähler form with $[\omega]$ the image of a rational class. For a suitable integer $m \geq 1$, $[m\omega]$ is the image of a class $c \in H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})$. The image of $c$ in $H^2(X, \mathbb{C})$ is represented by the $(1,1)$-form $m\omega$, so $c \in H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{Z})$, and by Theorem P6.5.13 there is a line bundle $L$ with $c_1(L) = c$. Then $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R} = [m\omega]$ is a Kähler class, and $L$ is positive by Proposition P6.5.21.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\mathcal{O}(d)$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is positive for $d > 0$, negative for $d < 0$ and neither for $d = 0$, using the metric $h_{\mathrm{FS}}^d$ and Proposition P6.5.22, part 4.
2. (A) Compute $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^n$ on $U_0 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ and show that $\int_{\mathbb{P}^n} \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^n = 1$. Hint: the determinant of $(1 + |w|^2) I - \bar w w^{T}$ is $1 + |w|^2$.
3. (B) Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ be a compact complex submanifold of dimension $n$. Show that $\int_X \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^n > 0$ and that it is an integer.
4. (B) Show that the tautological bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ with the metric induced by the standard Hermitian product of $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ is the dual of $(\mathcal{O}(1), h_{\mathrm{FS}})$, and compute its curvature directly from the definition.
5. (B) Let $X = E_\tau \times E_\tau$ with $\tau$ not quadratic. Using Example P6.5.16 and Example P6.5.27, determine the classes $x f_1 + y f_2 + z \delta$, with $\delta = dx_1 \wedge dy_2 - dy_1 \wedge dx_2$, that are Chern classes of positive line bundles.
6. (C) Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold with $h^{2,0}(X) = 0$. Show that rational classes are dense in $H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{R})$, that the Kähler forms have classes forming an open cone in $H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{R})$, and deduce that $X$ is a Hodge manifold.

### Notes and history

Positive line bundles in this sense were introduced by Kodaira (Kod54a), who called a compact Kähler manifold with a rational Kähler class a Hodge manifold after Hodge's use of such metrics on projective varieties; Chapter 6 proves Kodaira's theorem that Hodge manifolds are projective. The term ample is reserved in the handbook for the algebraic notion of Definition P3.6.22, and Chapter 6 shows that the two notions agree for line bundles on compact complex manifolds. The presentation follows Huybrechts, Sections 4.3 and 5.3 (Huy05), and Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 1, Section 2 (GH78); Voisin treats positivity through Kähler classes in Chapter 7 (Voi02). The Riemann condition of Example P6.5.27 is Mumford, Section 3 (Mum70a), and Exercise 6, with the embedding theorem of Chapter 6, is Kodaira's theorem that a compact Kähler manifold with $h^{2,0} = 0$ is projective (Kod54a).
