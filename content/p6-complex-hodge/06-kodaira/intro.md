# The Kodaira vanishing and embedding theorems

Chapter 5 showed that a very ample line bundle on a compact complex manifold carries a metric of positive curvature. This chapter proves the converse: a positive line bundle has a power whose sections embed the manifold in projective space. The route goes through a vanishing theorem. If $L$ is positive, the cohomology groups $H^q(X, \Omega^p_X \otimes L)$ vanish for $p + q > n$, and applied on blow-ups of $X$ at one or two points this vanishing produces enough sections of $L^k$ to separate points and tangent vectors.

Section 6.1 proves the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity. For a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle $E$ on a Kähler manifold, the two Laplacians $\Delta''$ and $\Delta'$ built from the $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ parts of the Chern connection differ by the zeroth order operator $[i\Theta(E), \Lambda]$. The proof twists the Kähler identities (Theorem P6.4.12) by $E$, using a holomorphic frame that is orthonormal to second order at a point, and then applies the graded Jacobi identity. For a line bundle the curvature term is diagonal in a suitable frame, with an explicit eigenvalue on each form $dz_J \wedge d\bar z_K$. Section 6.2 combines the identity with Hodge theory for bundle-valued forms and proves the Kodaira-Akizuki-Nakano vanishing theorem and its special case, the Kodaira vanishing theorem. Examples on projective space and on curves show that the bound $p + q > n$ cannot be improved and that semipositivity is not enough. Section 6.3 constructs the blow-up of a point, puts a positive metric on the line bundles $\pi^* L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-mE)$ for $k$ large uniformly in the point, and proves the Kodaira embedding theorem. Consequences are that Hodge manifolds are projective and that a compact Kähler manifold with $h^{2,0} = 0$ is projective, and examples are curves and complex tori.

Throughout the chapter $X$ is a compact connected complex manifold of dimension $n$. The chapter uses Dolbeault cohomology (Theorem P6.2.24), Chern connections (Theorem P6.3.16), the Kähler identities (Theorem P6.4.12), Lefschetz linear algebra (Theorem P6.4.9) and Hodge theory (Theorem P6.4.14), and positive line bundles from Chapter 5. The Hodge theorem for bundle-valued forms is cited, as Theorem P6.4.14 is. Chapter 7 turns the analytic statements into algebraic ones by GAGA, so that Theorem P7.8.2 of P7, which treats Kodaira vanishing as a black box, can cite Corollary P6.6.13 together with Corollary P6.7.17.

The chapter follows Huybrechts, Sections 4.1, 5.2 and 5.3 (Huy05), Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 1, Sections 2 and 4 (GH78), and Demailly, Chapter VII (Dem97a).
