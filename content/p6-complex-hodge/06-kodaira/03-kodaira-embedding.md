## The Kodaira embedding theorem

### Statement

Let $X$ be a compact connected complex manifold of dimension $n$ and $L$ a holomorphic line bundle on $X$. If $L$ is generated by its global sections, a basis $s_0, \dots, s_N$ of $H^0(X, L)$ defines the holomorphic map $\phi_L \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^N$, $x \mapsto [s_0(x) : \dots : s_N(x)]$, read in any local frame of $L$, as in Chapter 5. The line bundle $L$ is *very ample* if $L$ is generated by global sections and $\phi_L$ is a holomorphic embedding, that is, an injective immersion, and then $\phi_L^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong L$. The section proves the converse of part 3 of Proposition P6.5.22: a positive line bundle has a very ample power. The tool is the blow-up of a point, the complex analytic version of the blow-ups of P3, Chapter 11, and Example P6.2.21 treats the blow-up of the origin in $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$.

**Definition P6.6.18 (blow-up of a point).** Let $x \in X$ and let $z = (z_1, \dots, z_n)$ be holomorphic coordinates on a neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ with $z(x) = 0$ and $z(U) \supseteq B = \{|z| < 1\}$. Let
$$\tilde B = \{(z, \ell) \in B \times \mathbb{P}^{n-1} : z \in \ell\},$$
with projections $\pi \colon \tilde B \to B$ and $p \colon \tilde B \to \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. The *blow-up* of $X$ at $x$ is the space $\tilde X$ obtained by gluing $\tilde B$ to $X \setminus \{x\}$ along $\pi \colon \tilde B \setminus \pi^{-1}(0) \to B \setminus \{0\}$, with the map $\pi \colon \tilde X \to X$ that is the identity on $X \setminus \{x\}$. The *exceptional divisor* is $E = \pi^{-1}(x) = \{0\} \times \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. The blow-up at finitely many distinct points is defined by doing this at each point, with disjoint coordinate balls. For $n = 1$, $\tilde X = X$ and $E = \{x\}$.

**Proposition P6.6.19 (the blow-up of a point).** In the notation of Definition P6.6.18:

1. $\tilde X$ is a compact connected complex manifold, $\pi$ is holomorphic and proper, $\pi$ is biholomorphic from $\tilde X \setminus E$ onto $X \setminus \{x\}$, and $E \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is a smooth hypersurface, identified with the space $\mathbb{P}(T_x X)$ of lines in $T_x X$.
2. On $\tilde B$ there is an isomorphism $\mathcal{O}(E)|_{\tilde B} \cong p^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)$ under which the canonical section $s_E$ is the tautological section $(z, \ell) \mapsto z \in \ell$. In particular $\mathcal{O}(-E)|_E \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(1)$. If $f$ is a holomorphic function near $x$ with $f(x) = 0$, then $\pi^* f = s_E \cdot g$ for a section $g$ of $\mathcal{O}(-E)$ near $E$, and $g|_E \in H^0(E, \mathcal{O}(1)) = T_x^* X$ is the differential $df(x)$.
3. $K_{\tilde X} \cong \pi^* K_X \otimes \mathcal{O}((n-1)E)$.
4. For a holomorphic line bundle $F$ on $X$ and $m \geq 0$, pulling back gives an isomorphism from the space of sections of $F$ that vanish to order at least $m$ at $x$ onto $H^0(\tilde X, \pi^* F \otimes \mathcal{O}(-mE))$. The same holds for the blow-up at finitely many points, with $\mathcal{O}(-\sum_j m_j E_j)$.

**Lemma P6.6.20 (a positive metric on the blow-up).** Let $L$ be a positive line bundle and $F$ a holomorphic line bundle on $X$, and fix a Riemannian distance on $X$. For every $\rho > 0$ and integer $M \geq 1$ there is $k_0 = k_0(F, \rho, M)$ with the following property. Let $x_1, \dots, x_N$ be $N \leq 2$ distinct points at mutual distance at least $\rho$, let $\pi \colon \tilde X \to X$ be the blow-up at these points with exceptional divisors $E_1, \dots, E_N$, and let $1 \leq m_j \leq M$. Then for every $k \geq k_0$ the line bundle
$$\pi^*(L^k \otimes F) \otimes \mathcal{O}\Big(-\sum_j m_j E_j\Big)$$
on $\tilde X$ is positive.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P6.6.21 (Kodaira embedding).** Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold and $L$ a positive holomorphic line bundle on $X$. Then there is $k_0$ such that $L^k$ is very ample for all $k \geq k_0$. In particular $X$ is isomorphic to a closed complex submanifold of some $\mathbb{P}^N$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.6.22 (Hodge manifolds are projective).** A Hodge manifold carries a positive line bundle and is isomorphic to a closed complex submanifold of a projective space.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P6.6.23 (compact Kähler manifolds with $h^{2,0} = 0$).** Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold with $H^{2,0}(X) = 0$. Then $X$ carries a positive line bundle and is isomorphic to a closed complex submanifold of a projective space.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

By Chow's theorem in Chapter 7 the image of the embedding is a projective algebraic variety, so these results say that the manifolds in question are projective varieties.

### Proof in words

To embed $X$ by sections of $L^k$, the sections must generate $L^k$, they must have every covector at every point as a differential, and they must separate any two points. Each of these is the surjectivity of a restriction map from $H^0(X, L^k)$ to a finite-dimensional space: the fibre at a point, the cotangent space at a point, or the sum of the fibres at two points. On $X$ itself the kernels of these maps are sheaves of sections vanishing at points, which are not line bundles, so the vanishing theorem does not apply. On the blow-up at the points they become line bundles $\pi^* L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\sum m_j E_j)$, and the restriction maps become restrictions to the exceptional divisors. The obstruction is an $H^1$ on $\tilde X$, and Kodaira vanishing on $\tilde X$ kills it once $\pi^*L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\sum m_j E_j) \otimes K_{\tilde X}^{-1}$ is positive.

The negative sign in $\mathcal{O}(-E)$ is the good one. Its restriction to $E$ is $\mathcal{O}(1)$, positive along $E$, while the pull-back of a positive form from $X$ is positive away from $E$ and degenerate only along the tangent directions of $E$. A metric on $\mathcal{O}(-E)$ built from $\log|z|^2$ and a cutoff has curvature equal to the Fubini-Study form near $E$ and zero far from $E$, and it is negative only on an annulus. A large multiple of $\pi^*\omega$ absorbs that annulus. All constants depend only on the radius of the annulus, which leads to a $k_0$ that works uniformly for all points, and for all pairs of points at distance at least $\rho$. Pairs of nearby points are handled instead by the local injectivity of an immersion.

### Examples

**Example P6.6.24 (curves).** Let $X$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$ and $L$ a line bundle of degree $d$. Here the blow-up is the identity and $\mathcal{O}(-E) = \mathcal{O}(-x)$, so the proof of Theorem P6.6.21 reads as follows. If $d \geq 2g + 1$, then for all points $x, y$, not necessarily distinct, $\deg L(-x-y) \geq 2g - 1 > 2g - 2$, so $H^1(X, L(-x-y)) = 0$ by Exercise 2 of Section 6.2. The sequence $0 \to L(-x-y) \to L \to L|_{x+y} \to 0$ shows that $H^0(X, L)$ surjects onto the two-dimensional space $H^0(L|_{x+y})$. For $x \neq y$ this is $L_x \oplus L_y$, and for $x = y$ it gives sections with prescribed value and derivative at $x$. So $L$ is very ample. For $\mathbb{P}^1$ ($g = 0$), $\mathcal{O}(1)$ gives the identity $\mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ and $\mathcal{O}(2)$ the conic $[s : t] \mapsto [s^2 : st : t^2]$. For an elliptic curve ($g = 1$), a line bundle of degree $3$ embeds the curve as a plane cubic, since $h^0 = 3$ by Riemann-Roch. The bound cannot be lowered to $d = 2$ on an elliptic curve: then $h^0(L) = 2$ and $\phi_L$ is a map of degree $2$ onto $\mathbb{P}^1$, not injective. Positivity is needed: a line bundle of degree $0$ that is not torsion has no nonzero section of any power, by Example P6.5.10, so no power of it is very ample.

**Example P6.6.25 (complex tori).** Let $X = V/\Lambda$ be a complex torus. By Example P6.5.27 and Theorem P6.6.21, $X$ is isomorphic to a closed complex submanifold of projective space if and only if there is an integral alternating form $E$ on $\Lambda$ of type $(1,1)$ with $E(v, iv) > 0$ for every nonzero $v \in V$, the Riemann conditions. Indeed, such an $E$ is $c_1(L)$ for a positive $L$, and conversely an embedding $\iota$ gives the positive line bundle $\iota^*\mathcal{O}(1)$ by part 2 of Proposition P6.5.22. For an elliptic curve $X = \mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z}\tau)$ with $\operatorname{Im}\tau > 0$, the form $E(a + b\tau, c + d\tau) = ad - bc$ extends $\mathbb{R}$-linearly to $E(v, w) = \operatorname{Im}(\bar v w)/\operatorname{Im}\tau$. It has $E(1, \tau) = 1$ and $E(v, iv) = |v|^2/\operatorname{Im}\tau > 0$, so every elliptic curve is projective, in agreement with Example P6.6.24. These are the Riemann conditions of Mumford, Chapter 1, Section 3 (Mum70a).

**Example P6.6.26 (the hypothesis $h^{2,0} = 0$).** The torus $X = \mathbb{C}^2/\Lambda$ of Example P6.5.17 is a compact Kähler manifold with $\operatorname{NS}(X) = 0$. It carries no positive line bundle, since the first Chern class of a positive line bundle is nonzero, so by part 2 of Proposition P6.5.22 it admits no holomorphic embedding into a projective space. Here $h^{2,0}(X) = 1$, so Corollary P6.6.23 does not apply, and the example shows that its hypothesis cannot be dropped. On the other hand $E \times E$ for an elliptic curve $E$ also has $h^{2,0} = 1$, but it is projective, for instance by the Segre embedding of the product of two plane cubics, and its Picard number is at least $3$ by Example P6.5.16. So the condition $h^{2,0} = 0$ is sufficient, not necessary. What decides the question is whether the open Kähler cone in $H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{R})$ meets the image of $\operatorname{NS}(X)$.

### Proof

*Proposition P6.6.19.* (1) $\tilde B \subseteq B \times \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is covered by the charts $V_i = \{\ell_i \neq 0\}$, $i = 1, \dots, n$, where $\ell = [\ell_1 : \dots : \ell_n]$. On $V_i$ the coordinates $(t, w) = (t, (w_j)_{j \neq i})$ with $z_i = t$ and $z_j = t w_j$ for $j \neq i$ give a biholomorphism of $V_i$ with an open subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$, since $\ell = [w_1 : \dots : 1 : \dots : w_n]$ and $z$ is determined by $(t, w)$. So $\tilde B$ is a complex manifold, $\pi(t, w) = (tw_1, \dots, t, \dots, tw_n)$ is holomorphic, and $E \cap V_i = \{t = 0\}$ is a smooth hypersurface. Over $z \neq 0$ the line $\ell$ is determined by $z$, so $\pi$ is biholomorphic over $B \setminus \{0\}$, and the gluing is a complex manifold. It is Hausdorff because $\pi^{-1}$ of the closed ball of radius $\frac12$ is compact. Then $\pi$ is proper with fibres points or $E$, so $\tilde X$ is compact, and it is connected because $\tilde X \setminus E \cong X \setminus \{x\}$ is connected and dense. The tangent space $T_x X$ is $\mathbb{C}^n$ in the coordinates $z$, which identifies $E$ with $\mathbb{P}(T_x X)$. This identification does not depend on the coordinates, because a change of coordinates acts on lines through $0$ to first order by its differential at $x$, which is linear.

(2) The tautological bundle $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{n-1} \times \mathbb{C}^n$ has fibre $\ell$ over $\ell$, and $\sigma(z, \ell) = z$ is a holomorphic section of $p^*\mathcal{O}(-1)$ on $\tilde B$. In the chart $V_i$, $p^*\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has the frame $\epsilon_i(t, w) = (w_1, \dots, 1, \dots, w_n)$ and $\sigma = t \epsilon_i$, so $\sigma$ vanishes exactly on $E$, to order $1$. The line bundle $\mathcal{O}(E)$ of Chapter 5 has a canonical section $s_E$ with the same divisor. The ratio $\sigma / s_E$ is a nowhere vanishing section of $p^* \mathcal{O}(-1) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-E)$ on $\tilde B$, which gives the isomorphism $\mathcal{O}(E)|_{\tilde B} \cong p^*\mathcal{O}(-1)$ sending $s_E$ to $\sigma$. Since $p|_E \colon E \to \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is the identity, $\mathcal{O}(E)|_E \cong \mathcal{O}(-1)$ and $\mathcal{O}(-E)|_E \cong \mathcal{O}(1)$. Now let $f(x) = 0$ and write $f = f_1 + f_{\geq 2}$ with $f_1 = \sum_j \frac{\partial f}{\partial z_j}(0) z_j$, which is $df(x)$, and $f_{\geq 2}$ vanishing to order $2$. In $V_i$, $\pi^* f = t \big(f_1(w_1, \dots, 1, \dots, w_n) + t \, r(t, w)\big)$ with $r$ holomorphic. Since $s_E$ corresponds to $t \epsilon_i$, $g = \pi^* f / s_E$ is holomorphic, and on $E$ it is the section $\ell \mapsto f_1|_\ell$ of $\mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(-1)^*$, that is, the linear form $df(x)$ viewed as a section of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ on $\mathbb{P}(T_xX)$.

(3) On $V_i$, with $i = 1$ for definiteness, $\pi^*(dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n) = dt \wedge (t\,dw_2 + w_2\, dt) \wedge \dots \wedge (t\,dw_n + w_n\, dt) = t^{n-1} \, dt \wedge dw_2 \wedge \dots \wedge dw_n$. So the section $\pi^*(dz_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n)$ of $\pi^* K_X$, viewed as a section of $K_{\tilde X}$, vanishes to order exactly $n - 1$ along $E$ and nowhere else in $\tilde B$. Away from $E$, $\pi$ is biholomorphic, and $\pi^* \colon \pi^* K_X \to K_{\tilde X}$ is an isomorphism. Hence $\pi^*$ defines an injection $\pi^* K_X \to K_{\tilde X}$ whose image is $K_{\tilde X}(-(n-1)E)$, that is, $K_{\tilde X} \cong \pi^* K_X \otimes \mathcal{O}((n-1)E)$.

(4) Let $\tilde s \in H^0(\tilde X, \pi^* F)$. Its restriction to $\tilde X \setminus E \cong X \setminus \{x\}$ is a section of $F$ on $X \setminus \{x\}$. For $n \geq 2$ it extends to a section $s$ of $F$ on $X$ by the Hartogs extension theorem (Theorem P6.1.14), and $\pi^* s = \tilde s$ because they agree on the dense set $\tilde X \setminus E$. For $n = 1$ there is nothing to prove. So $\pi^* \colon H^0(X, F) \to H^0(\tilde X, \pi^* F)$ is bijective. In a frame of $F$ near $x$ write a section as a function $f = \sum_{d \geq d_0} f_d$ with $f_d$ homogeneous of degree $d$ and $f_{d_0} \neq 0$. In $V_i$, $\pi^* f = \sum_d t^d f_d(w_1, \dots, 1, \dots, w_n)$, and $f_{d_0}(w_1, \dots, 1, \dots, w_n) \not\equiv 0$ for a suitable $i$. So the order of vanishing of $\pi^* f$ along $E$ is the order of vanishing $d_0$ of $f$ at $x$. A section of $\pi^* F$ lies in the subsheaf $\pi^*F \otimes \mathcal{O}(-mE)$, the image of multiplication by $s_E^m$, exactly when it vanishes to order at least $m$ along $E$. This proves (4), and the version with several points is the same at each point.

*Lemma P6.6.20.* Fix a smooth metric $h_L$ on $L$ with $\omega := i\Theta(L) > 0$, a Kähler form, and a metric $h_F$ on $F$. By compactness there is $C_F$ with $i\Theta(F) \geq -C_F \omega$.

Uniform coordinates. Choose finitely many holomorphic charts $\varphi_\alpha \colon U_\alpha \to \mathbb{C}^n$ and compact sets $K_\alpha \subseteq U_\alpha$ covering $X$, and $r_0 \in (0, 1)$ such that for every $x \in K_\alpha$ the ball of radius $2 r_0$ around $\varphi_\alpha(x)$ lies in $\varphi_\alpha(U_\alpha)$. By compactness there is $c \geq 1$ with $c^{-1} \omega_0 \leq \omega \leq c\, \omega_0$ on $\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$ of these balls for all $\alpha$, where $\omega_0 = \frac{i}{2}\sum_j dz_j \wedge d\bar z_j$ is the Euclidean form, and the Riemannian distance is comparable to the Euclidean one there. For $x \in K_\alpha$ use the coordinates $z = \varphi_\alpha - \varphi_\alpha(x)$ centred at $x$, scaled by $1/(2r_0)$ so that they map onto a ball containing the unit ball, as in Definition P6.6.18. Choose $r = r(\rho) \in (0, \frac12)$ so small that the set $\{|z| < r\}$ around each point $x$ lies in the Riemannian ball of radius $\rho/2$ around $x$. Then for points at mutual distance at least $\rho$ these sets are disjoint, and the blow-up at the points can be done inside them.

A metric on $\mathcal{O}(E)$. Fix a point $x$ and its coordinate $z$ as above. Let $\chi \colon \mathbb{R} \to [0, 1]$ be smooth with $\chi = 1$ on $(-\infty, \frac14]$ and $\chi = 0$ on $[1, \infty)$. On $\tilde X \setminus E$, $s_E$ is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic section of $\mathcal{O}(E)$, so a metric on $\mathcal{O}(E)|_{\tilde X \setminus E}$ is given by prescribing $|s_E|^2$. Set
$$\log |s_E|^2_{h_E} = \chi\big(|z|^2 / r^2\big) \log |z|^2 \quad \text{on } \pi^{-1}(\{|z| < r\}) \setminus E, \qquad |s_E|_{h_E} = 1 \text{ elsewhere}.$$
Near $E$, where $\chi = 1$, this says $|s_E|^2_{h_E} = |z|^2$. Under the isomorphism of Proposition P6.6.19(2) this is the restriction to $\tilde B$ of the Euclidean metric of $\mathbb{C}^n$ on $p^*\mathcal{O}(-1)$, since $s_E$ corresponds to $\sigma(z, \ell) = z$. So $h_E$ extends to a smooth metric on $\mathcal{O}(E)$. By the formula $\Theta = -\partial\bar\partial \log |s|^2$ for a nowhere vanishing holomorphic section $s$ (Corollary P6.3.17), the dual metric on $\mathcal{O}(-E)$ has curvature
$$\theta_x := i\Theta(\mathcal{O}(-E)) = i \partial \bar\partial \big(\chi(|z|^2/r^2) \log |z|^2\big)$$
away from $E$, and this form extends smoothly across $E$. Its behaviour is as follows.

1. On $\pi^{-1}(\{|z| < r/2\})$, where $\chi = 1$, $\theta_x = i\partial\bar\partial \log |z|^2 = 2\pi\, p^* \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ by Proposition P6.5.20, applied on $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. This is semipositive, and its restriction to $E$ is $2\pi\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, which is positive on $TE$.
2. Outside $\pi^{-1}(\{|z| < r\})$, $\theta_x = 0$.
3. On the annulus $A = \{r/2 \leq |z| \leq r\}$, where $\pi$ is biholomorphic, $\theta_x$ is the push-forward of the smooth form $i\partial\bar\partial(\chi(|z|^2/r^2) \log |z|^2)$ on the annulus in $\mathbb{C}^n$. This form depends only on $r$ and $\chi$, not on $x$, so there is $C(r)$ with $\theta_x \geq -C(r)\omega_0 \geq -c\, C(r)\, \pi^*\omega$ on $A$.

Positivity. Let $x_1, \dots, x_N$ and $m_j$ be as in the statement, and give $G = \pi^*(L^k \otimes F) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\sum_j m_j E_j)$ the product metric. Its curvature is
$$i\Theta(G) = \pi^*\big(k\omega + i\Theta(F)\big) + \sum_j m_j \theta_{x_j} \geq (k - C_F)\, \pi^*\omega + \sum_j m_j \theta_{x_j}.$$
Let $k_0 = C_F + M c\, C(r) + 1$, which depends only on $F$, $\rho$ and $M$, and let $k \geq k_0$. Let $y \in \tilde X$ and $v \neq 0$ a tangent vector at $y$, and write $\beta(v) = \beta(v, Jv)$ for a real $(1,1)$-form $\beta$, with $J$ the complex structure, so that $\beta > 0$ means $\beta(v) > 0$ for all $v \neq 0$. The sets $\{|z| < r\}$ around the $x_j$ are disjoint, so at most one $\theta_{x_j}$ is nonzero at $y$. If $y$ is outside all of them, $i\Theta(G)(v) \geq (k - C_F)\, \omega(d\pi\, v) > 0$, since $\pi$ is biholomorphic there. If $y$ lies over an annulus $A$ around $x_j$, then $i\Theta(G)(v) \geq (k - C_F - m_j c\, C(r))\, \omega(d\pi\, v) > 0$. If $y$ lies over $\{|z| < r/2\}$ around $x_j$, both terms are semipositive. If $d\pi_y v \neq 0$, the first term is positive at $v$. If $d\pi_y v = 0$, then $y \in E_j$, because $\pi$ is biholomorphic off $E_j$. In the chart $V_i$ at a point $t = 0$, $d\pi(\partial_t) = \partial_{z_i} + \sum_{l \neq i} w_l \partial_{z_l} \neq 0$ and $d\pi(\partial_{w_l}) = t\, \partial_{z_l} = 0$, so the kernel of $d\pi_y$ is $T_y E_j$. Then $v \in T_y E_j$, and the second term is $2\pi m_j\, \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}(v) > 0$ by item 1. In all cases $i\Theta(G)(v) > 0$, so $G$ is positive.

*Theorem P6.6.21.* Write $\mathfrak{m}_x$ for sections vanishing at $x$. For a point $x$ and $m \geq 1$ let $\pi \colon \tilde X \to X$ be the blow-up at $x$. By Proposition P6.6.19(3),
$$\pi^* L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-mE) = K_{\tilde X} \otimes G_{k,m}, \qquad G_{k,m} = \pi^*(L^k \otimes K_X^{-1}) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-(m + n - 1)E).$$
Similarly, for two distinct points $x, y$ and the blow-up at both, $\pi^* L^k \otimes \mathcal{O}(-E_x - E_y) = K_{\tilde X} \otimes \pi^*(L^k \otimes K_X^{-1}) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-nE_x - nE_y)$. Lemma P6.6.20 applies with $F = K_X^{-1}$ and $M = n + 1$, and Corollary P6.6.13 on the compact complex manifold $\tilde X$ then gives the vanishing of $H^1$ of these line bundles.

Step 1 (generation and immersion). Apply Lemma P6.6.20 with $N = 1$ to get $k_1$ such that $G_{k,1}$ and $G_{k,2}$ are positive for every point $x$ and every $k \geq k_1$. For $N = 1$ the distance condition is empty, and only the coordinate scale $r(\rho)$ for some fixed $\rho$ enters. By Corollary P6.6.13, $H^1(\tilde X, \pi^*L^k(-E)) = 0$ and $H^1(\tilde X, \pi^*L^k(-2E)) = 0$. The exact sequence
$$0 \to \pi^* L^k(-E) \to \pi^* L^k \to \pi^*L^k|_E \to 0,$$
in which $\pi^*L^k|_E$ is the trivial bundle with fibre $L^k_x$, and Proposition P6.6.19(4) show that $H^0(X, L^k) \to L^k_x$ is surjective. Likewise the sequence
$$0 \to \pi^* L^k(-2E) \to \pi^* L^k(-E) \to \pi^*L^k(-E)|_E \to 0,$$
with $\pi^*L^k(-E)|_E \cong L_x^k \otimes \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(1)$, whose space of sections is $L^k_x \otimes T^*_x X$, shows that $H^0(X, L^k \otimes \mathfrak{m}_x) \to L^k_x \otimes T^*_x X$ is surjective. By Proposition P6.6.19(2) this map is $s \mapsto ds(x)$, computed in a local frame of $L^k$, which is well defined for $s(x) = 0$. So for $k \geq k_1$, $L^k$ is generated by global sections. Choose a section $s_0$ with $s_0(x) \neq 0$ and sections $s_1, \dots, s_n$ vanishing at $x$ whose differentials at $x$ form a basis of $L^k_x \otimes T^*_x X$. Then near $x$ the functions $s_1/s_0, \dots, s_n/s_0$ form a coordinate system, and they are affine coordinates of $\phi_{L^k}$ composed with a linear projection. So $\phi_{L^k}$ is an immersion at $x$, for every $x$.

Step 2 (nearby points). Fix $k = k_1$. The immersion $\phi = \phi_{L^{k_1}}$ is locally injective: by the inverse function theorem each $x$ has a neighbourhood $U_x$ on which $\phi$ is injective. Let $\delta > 0$ be a Lebesgue number of the cover $\{U_x\}$ for the Riemannian distance, so every two points at distance less than $\delta$ lie in a common $U_x$. Hence $\phi(x) \neq \phi(y)$ for $x \neq y$ at distance less than $\delta$. Since $L^{k_1}$ is generated by global sections, $\phi(x) \neq \phi(y)$ means that some hyperplane contains $\phi(x)$ but not $\phi(y)$, that is, some $s \in H^0(X, L^{k_1})$ has $s(x) = 0 \neq s(y)$.

Step 3 (distant points). Apply Lemma P6.6.20 with $N = 2$, $\rho = \delta$, $F = K_X^{-1}$ and $M = n$ to get $k_2$. For $k \geq k_2$ and points $x, y$ at distance at least $\delta$, Corollary P6.6.13 gives $H^1(\tilde X, \pi^*L^k(-E_x - E_y)) = 0$. The sequence $0 \to \pi^*L^k(-E_x - E_y) \to \pi^*L^k \to \pi^*L^k|_{E_x \sqcup E_y} \to 0$ and Proposition P6.6.19(4) show that $H^0(X, L^k) \to L^k_x \oplus L^k_y$ is surjective. In particular some section vanishes at $x$ but not at $y$.

Step 4 (conclusion). Let $k \geq k_0 := \max(2k_1, k_2)$. By Step 1, $L^k$ is generated by global sections and $\phi_{L^k}$ is an immersion. For points $x \neq y$ at distance at least $\delta$, Step 3 gives a section of $L^k$ vanishing at $x$ but not at $y$. For $x \neq y$ at distance less than $\delta$, take $s$ from Step 2 and, since $k - k_1 \geq k_1$, by Step 1 a section $t \in H^0(X, L^{k - k_1})$ with $t(y) \neq 0$. Then $st \in H^0(X, L^k)$ vanishes at $x$ but not at $y$. In both cases the hyperplane defined by this section contains $\phi_{L^k}(x)$ but not $\phi_{L^k}(y)$, so $\phi_{L^k}$ is injective. An injective immersion of a compact manifold is a homeomorphism onto its image, which is closed, so $\phi_{L^k}$ is a holomorphic embedding onto a closed complex submanifold of $\mathbb{P}^N$ (Exercise 4), and $\phi_{L^k}^*\mathcal{O}(1) \cong L^k$ because the sections $s_i$ are the pull-backs of the coordinate functions. So $L^k$ is very ample.

*Corollary P6.6.22.* By Corollary P6.5.24 a Hodge manifold carries a positive line bundle, and Theorem P6.6.21 applies.

*Corollary P6.6.23.* Since $H^{2,0}(X) = 0$, also $H^{0,2}(X) = \overline{H^{2,0}(X)} = 0$, and the Hodge decomposition (Theorem P6.4.16) gives $H^2(X, \mathbb{C}) = H^{1,1}(X)$, so $H^2(X, \mathbb{R}) = H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{R})$. The set of classes of Kähler forms is a nonempty open cone in $H^{1,1}(X, \mathbb{R})$: it contains the class of the given Kähler form $\omega$, and if $\alpha$ is a closed real $(1,1)$-form, then $\omega + \epsilon \alpha$ is Kähler for $|\epsilon|$ small by compactness. The image of $H^2(X, \mathbb{Q})$ is dense in $H^2(X, \mathbb{R})$, so it meets this cone in some class $[\omega']$. A positive integer multiple $d[\omega']$ lies in the image of $H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})$, and it has type $(1,1)$. By Theorem P6.5.13 it is $c_1(L)_\mathbb{R}$ for a line bundle $L$, and $L$ is positive by Proposition P6.5.21, since $d\omega'$ is a Kähler form. Theorem P6.6.21 applies.

### Notes and history

Kodaira proved the embedding theorem in 1954 (Kod54a), answering the question, raised by Hodge's work, of which compact Kähler manifolds are projective: exactly those with a Kähler form in a rational class. His proof used the blow-up of points to reduce the separation of points and tangent vectors to the vanishing theorem of Section 6.2. The proof given here follows Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 1, Section 4 (GH78), and Huybrechts, Section 5.3 (Huy05), with the uniform choice of $k$ arranged by fixing the radius of the cutoff. Voisin, Chapter 7 (Voi02), gives another account. Together with Chow's theorem in Chapter 7 the theorem says that a positive line bundle on a compact complex manifold comes from an ample line bundle on a projective variety, so that positive and ample are the same notion there. The Riemann conditions of Example P6.6.25 were known to Riemann for Jacobians and are treated in Mumford, Chapter 1 (Mum70a). Corollary P6.6.23 does not apply to K3 surfaces, which have $h^{2,0} = 1$, and indeed most K3 surfaces are not projective.
