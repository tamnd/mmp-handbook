# Complex geometry and Hodge theory

The minimal model program over the complex numbers rests on transcendental input. Kodaira vanishing and its descendants are proved with harmonic forms and curvature, the theorem of the base and the Lefschetz theorem on $(1,1)$ classes come from the Hodge decomposition of $H^2$, and the multiplier ideals of the later volumes are defined by integrability of $e^{-2\varphi}$ for a plurisubharmonic weight $\varphi$. This volume builds that analytic and Hodge-theoretic background on compact complex manifolds, and then passes back to algebraic geometry through Chow's theorem and GAGA. P7 Ch. 8 and Volume 5 cite Kodaira vanishing as a black box with a reference to this volume.

The volume has eight chapters in three groups.

- **Local and global foundations, Chapters 1 and 2.** Chapter 1 develops holomorphic functions of several variables: the Cauchy integral formula on polydiscs and power series, the Hartogs and Riemann extension theorems, the Weierstrass preparation and division theorems, the proof that the ring $\mathcal{O}_n$ of convergent power series is a Noetherian factorial regular local ring, and the Dolbeault-Grothendieck lemma, which solves $\bar\partial \beta = \alpha$ on products of discs and annuli. Chapter 2 defines complex manifolds and holomorphic vector bundles, with projective space, complex tori and Hopf surfaces as the running examples, identifies the Picard group with $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*)$, computes the canonical bundle of projective space and of its hypersurfaces, proves the theorems of Dolbeault and de Rham by fine resolutions, and computes the Dolbeault cohomology of the projective line, of $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ and of complex tori.
- **Metrics, curvature and Hodge theory, Chapters 3 and 4.** Chapter 3 treats Hermitian metrics, connections and curvature, constructs the Chern connection of a Hermitian holomorphic bundle, defines Chern classes by Chern-Weil theory, compares the first Chern class with the connecting map of the exponential sequence, and computes the Fubini-Study metric and $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))$. Chapter 4 introduces Kähler manifolds, proves the Kähler identities from the Lefschetz linear algebra, takes the Hodge theorem for elliptic operators as its one black box, and deduces the Hodge decomposition and Hodge symmetry, the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma, the hard Lefschetz theorem and the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations. The worked examples are the Hodge numbers of projective space, of curves, of tori and of surfaces such as products of curves and quartic surfaces.
- **Line bundles, vanishing and algebraization, Chapters 5 to 8.** Chapter 5 studies line bundles on compact Kähler manifolds through the exponential sequence: the Picard torus $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$, the Néron-Severi group and the Lefschetz theorem on $(1,1)$ classes. Chapter 6 proves the Kodaira and Akizuki-Nakano vanishing theorems by the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity and the Kodaira embedding theorem. Chapter 7 proves Chow's theorem and states and applies Serre's GAGA, with the Lefschetz principle that transfers results from $\mathbb{C}$ to an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. Chapter 8 treats plurisubharmonic functions, singular Hermitian metrics, Lelong numbers, analytic multiplier ideals and Nadel vanishing through Hörmander's $L^2$ estimates.

The spine is Huybrechts, *Complex geometry: an introduction* (Huy05), Voisin, *Hodge theory and complex algebraic geometry I* (Voi02), and Griffiths and Harris, *Principles of algebraic geometry*, Chapters 0 and 1 (GH78). Chapter 1 follows Huybrechts, Section 1.1, Voisin, Chapter 1, and Hörmander's *An introduction to complex analysis in several variables* (Hor90), Chapters II and VI. Chapters 2 and 3 follow Huybrechts, Chapters 2 and 4, and Wells, *Differential analysis on complex manifolds* (Wel80), Chapters II and III. Chapter 4 follows Huybrechts, Chapter 3, Voisin, Chapters 5 and 6, and Wells, Chapter V. Each section lists in `reads_with` the places of the spine texts that read in parallel.

The volume needs P1 for Noetherian and factorial rings, completion and regular local rings, which Chapter 1 applies to $\mathcal{O}_n$, and P2 for sheaves, sheaf cohomology, Čech cohomology, acyclic resolutions and spectral sequences, which Chapter 2 applies to the Dolbeault and de Rham complexes. P3 and P4 supply the algebraic side that Chapter 7 compares with the analytic side. The volume can be read in parallel with P5 or P7. Throughout, a complex manifold is Hausdorff and second countable, its dimension is its complex dimension, and smooth means $C^\infty$. Facts of differential topology that the volume does not prove, such as homotopy invariance of de Rham cohomology, the Künneth formula and the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, are cited from Bott and Tu (BT82) at each use.
