## Numerical classes over a base

### Statement

From now on $f \colon X \to Z$ is a projective morphism of quasi-projective varieties (Definition P3.4.29). A *curve* of $X$ is an integral closed subscheme of dimension 1 that is proper over $k$. A *curve over $Z$* is a curve $C \subset X$ such that $f(C)$ is a point. Each closed integral subscheme $C$ of dimension 1 with $f(C)$ a point is a curve over $Z$: the image $f(C)$ is closed because $f$ is proper (Corollary P3.4.30), so it is a closed point $z$, and $C$ is closed in the fibre $X_z$, which is projective over $k(z) = k$.

Let $C$ be a curve of $X$ and $\nu \colon \tilde C \to X$ the composite of the normalization $\tilde C \to C$ with the inclusion. Then $\tilde C$ is a smooth projective curve (Theorem P5.1.3).

**Definition 1.2.13 (the intersection number with a curve).** For a line bundle $L$ on $X$ and a curve $C$ of $X$, put $L \cdot C = \deg \nu^* L$. For $\lambda = \sum_i r_i L_i$ in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ put $\lambda \cdot C = \sum_i r_i (L_i \cdot C)$. For an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor $D$ on a normal $X$, put $D \cdot C = [D] \cdot C$. For a real 1-cycle $z = \sum_j a_j C_j$, a finite formal sum of curves with real coefficients, put $\lambda \cdot z = \sum_j a_j (\lambda \cdot C_j)$.

The degree is a homomorphism $\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde C) \to \mathbb{Z}$, so $L \mapsto L \cdot C$ is a homomorphism $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \mathbb{Z}$, and its extension to $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ is well defined. Two examples. On $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ a line $\ell$ has $\nu$ the inclusion and $\mathcal{O}(1)|_\ell = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)$, so $\mathcal{O}(1) \cdot \ell = 1$. On the nodal cubic $C = \{y^2 z = x^3 + x^2 z\} \subset \mathbb{P}^2$, the normalization is $\nu(s : t) = (s(t^2 - s^2) : t(t^2 - s^2) : s^3)$, and the pullback of the line $\{x = 0\}$ is the divisor of $s(t - s)(t + s)$, so $\mathcal{O}(1) \cdot C = 3$ (Exercise 2).

**Lemma 1.2.14 (properties of the intersection number).** Let $C$ be a curve of $X$ and $L$ a line bundle on $X$.

1. $L \cdot C$ depends only on $L|_C$.
2. If $D$ is an effective Cartier divisor on $X$ with $C \not\subset \operatorname{Supp} D$, then $\mathcal{O}_X(D) \cdot C = \deg \nu^* D \ge 0$, where $\nu^* D$ is the pullback of Definition P3.9.26.
3. (Projection formula.) Let $h \colon X' \to X$ be a morphism of varieties and $C'$ a curve of $X'$. If $h(C')$ is a point, then $h^* L \cdot C' = 0$. Otherwise $C = h(C')$ is a curve and $h^* L \cdot C' = [k(C') : k(C)] \, (L \cdot C)$.
4. If $L|_W$ is generated by global sections for an open set $W \supset C$, then $L \cdot C \ge 0$.
5. If $L|_W$ is ample for an open set $W \supset C$, then $L \cdot C > 0$.
6. If $X$ is a smooth projective surface and $C$ is a smooth curve, then $L \cdot C$ is the intersection number $(L \cdot \mathcal{O}_X(C))$ of Definition P5.5.4.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

*Proof.* Part 1 holds because $\nu$ factors through $C$. For part 2, $\nu$ maps the generic point of $\tilde C$ to the generic point of $C$, which is not in $\operatorname{Supp} D$. So $\nu^* D$ is defined, it is effective, and $\mathcal{O}_{\tilde C}(\nu^* D) \cong \nu^* \mathcal{O}_X(D)$ by Proposition P3.9.27. An effective divisor on $\tilde C$ has degree at least 0.

For part 3, if $h(C') = \{x\}$ then $h^*L|_{C'}$ is the pullback of the restriction of $L$ to the point $x$, so it is trivial and has degree 0. Otherwise $h(C')$ is closed, since $C'$ is proper, and irreducible of dimension 1, so it is a curve $C$, and $h$ gives a finite extension $k(C) \subset k(C')$ of degree $d = [k(C') : k(C)]$. The rational map $\tilde C' \dashrightarrow \tilde C$ that it defines extends to a morphism $g$ (Proposition P5.1.2), and $\nu \circ g = h \circ \nu'$ because both agree on a dense open set and $X$ is separated. The morphism $g$ is finite of degree $d$, and $\deg g^* M = d \deg M$ for every line bundle $M$ on $\tilde C$ by Proposition P5.1.4. With $M = \nu^* L$ this gives $\deg \nu'^* h^* L = d \, (L \cdot C)$.

For part 4, choose a closed point $x \in C$ and a section $s \in H^0(W, L)$ with $s(x) \ne 0$. Then $\nu^* s$ is a section of $\nu^* L$ that does not vanish at the points over $x$, so it is not zero, and $\nu^*L \cong \mathcal{O}_{\tilde C}(\operatorname{div} \nu^* s)$ with $\operatorname{div} \nu^* s$ effective. So $\deg \nu^* L \ge 0$.

For part 5, $W$ is of finite type over $k$, so by Theorem P3.6.25 some power $L^m|_W$ is very ample: there is an immersion $i \colon W \to \mathbb{P}^N$ with $i^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong L^m|_W$. Since $C$ is proper, $i(C)$ is a closed curve in $\mathbb{P}^N$. Choose distinct closed points $x, y \in C$ and a hyperplane $H$ that contains $i(x)$ and not $i(y)$. The linear form of $H$ gives a section $s$ of $L^m|_W$ with $s(x) = 0$ and $s(y) \ne 0$. As in part 4, $\nu^* s$ is a nonzero section of $\nu^* L^m$, and it vanishes at the points over $x$. So its divisor is effective and not zero, and $m (L \cdot C) = \deg \nu^* L^m > 0$.

For part 6, $\nu$ is the inclusion, since $C$ is smooth. Proposition P5.5.12 gives $(L \cdot \mathcal{O}_X(C)) = \chi(L|_C) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_C)$, and Proposition P4.3.18 gives $\chi(L|_C) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_C) = \deg L|_C$. $\square$

Let $Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R}$ be the real vector space with basis the curves over $Z$. The intersection number gives a bilinear pairing $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R} \times Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$.

**Definition 1.2.15 (the spaces $N^1(X/Z)$ and $N_1(X/Z)$ and the relative cone).** An element $\lambda \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ is *numerically trivial over $Z$*, written $\lambda \equiv_Z 0$, if $\lambda \cdot C = 0$ for every curve $C$ over $Z$. A 1-cycle $z \in Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R}$ is *numerically trivial* if $L \cdot z = 0$ for every line bundle $L$. Put
$$N^1(X/Z) = \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R} / \{\lambda \equiv_Z 0\}, \qquad N_1(X/Z) = Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R} / \{z \equiv 0\},$$
and let $\rho(X/Z) = \dim N^1(X/Z)$, the *relative Picard number*. Write $\lambda \equiv_Z \lambda'$ if $\lambda - \lambda' \equiv_Z 0$, and in the same way for $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisors on a normal $X$, through their classes. The *relative cone of curves* $NE(X/Z) \subset N_1(X/Z)$ is the convex cone of the classes $\sum a_j [C_j]$ with $a_j \ge 0$ and $C_j$ curves over $Z$, and $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$ is its closure.

By construction the pairing $N^1(X/Z) \times N_1(X/Z) \to \mathbb{R}$ is well defined and nondegenerate on both sides, and $N_1(X/Z)$ is spanned by the classes of curves over $Z$. Theorem 1.2.20 below shows that both spaces have finite dimension, so that the closure is taken in a finite dimensional real vector space with its Euclidean topology. When $Z$ is a point and $X$ is projective and normal, every $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier class comes from $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ by Proposition 1.2.5, and these are the spaces $N^1(X)$, $N_1(X)$ and the cone $\overline{NE}(X)$ of Definition 1.1.8.

**Example 1.2.16 (projective space over a point).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $Z$ a point. By Corollary P3.9.11, $\operatorname{Pic}(X) = \mathbb{Z} \, \mathcal{O}(1)$. For a curve $C$ put $d_C = \mathcal{O}(1) \cdot C$, a positive integer by part 5 of Lemma 1.2.14. Every line bundle $\mathcal{O}(a)$ has $\mathcal{O}(a) \cdot C = a d_C = d_C \, (\mathcal{O}(a) \cdot \ell)$ for a line $\ell$. So $[C] = d_C [\ell]$ in $N_1(X)$, and $[\ell] \ne 0$ because $\mathcal{O}(1) \cdot \ell = 1$. Hence $N^1(X) = N_1(X) = \mathbb{R}$, $\rho(X) = 1$, and $NE(X) = \overline{NE}(X) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [\ell]$.

**Example 1.2.17 (a finite morphism).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be finite, for example the normalization of the nodal cubic, or a closed immersion. Every fibre is finite, so no curve maps to a point, and $Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R} = 0$. Then every $\lambda$ is numerically trivial over $Z$, so $N^1(X/Z) = N_1(X/Z) = 0$ and $\rho(X/Z) = 0$, even when $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ is large.

**Lemma 1.2.18 (the perfect pairing).** If $N^1(X/Z)$ or $N_1(X/Z)$ has finite dimension, then both have the same finite dimension, and the pairing identifies each with the dual of the other.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

*Proof.* Nondegeneracy gives injective linear maps $N_1(X/Z) \to N^1(X/Z)^*$ and $N^1(X/Z) \to N_1(X/Z)^*$. If $\dim N^1(X/Z) = r$ is finite, the first map gives $\dim N_1(X/Z) \le r$, so $N_1(X/Z)$ is finite dimensional, and the second gives $r \le \dim N_1(X/Z)$. So the dimensions are equal, and both injective maps between spaces of the same finite dimension are isomorphisms. The case where $N_1(X/Z)$ is finite dimensional is the same with the roles exchanged. $\square$

**Lemma 1.2.19 (lifting curves).** Let $\mu \colon Y \to X$ be a proper surjective morphism from a quasi-projective variety $Y$, and let $C$ be a curve of $X$. Then there is a curve $C' \subset Y$ with $\mu(C') = C$. If $C$ is a curve over $Z$, then $C'$ is a curve over $Z$ for $f \circ \mu$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

*Proof.* The scheme $\mu^{-1}(C)$ is proper over $C$, so proper over $k$, and it is closed in the quasi-projective $Y$, so it is projective. Its finitely many irreducible components have closed images whose union is $C$, so one of them, $W$ with its reduced structure, maps onto $C$. Let $d = \dim W$. If $d = 1$, put $C' = W$. If $d \ge 2$, embed $W$ in some $\mathbb{P}^N$. By Theorem P3.7.6, applied to the dominant morphism $W \to C$, every irreducible component of every closed fibre $W_c$ has dimension at least $d - 1 \ge 1$. Choose a hyperplane $H$ that does not contain $W$. A hyperplane meets every closed subvariety of $\mathbb{P}^N$ of positive dimension (Har77, Theorem I.7.2), so $W \cap H$ meets every closed fibre $W_c$. The image of $W \cap H$ in $C$ is closed and contains every closed point of $C$, so it is $C$, and one irreducible component $W_1$ of $W \cap H$ maps onto $C$. By Proposition P3.7.5, applied on the affine charts of $\mathbb{P}^N$, every component of $W \cap H$ has dimension $d - 1$. Replace $W$ by $W_1$ with its reduced structure and repeat. After $d - 1$ steps $W$ is a curve $C'$ with $\mu(C') = C$. If $f(C)$ is a point, so is $f(\mu(C'))$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.2.20 (finite dimension).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective morphism of quasi-projective varieties. Then $N^1(X/Z)$ and $N_1(X/Z)$ are finite dimensional of the same dimension $\rho(X/Z)$, and the intersection pairing makes them dual to each other. If $\bar X$ is a projective closure of $X$ and $\mu \colon X' \to \bar X$ is a resolution of singularities by a smooth projective variety, then $\rho(X/Z) \le \rho(X')$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Lemma 1.2.21 (pullbacks and numerically trivial bundles).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be as in the theorem.

1. For every $\beta \in \operatorname{Pic}(Z)_\mathbb{R}$, $f^* \beta \equiv_Z 0$. So $\lambda \sim_{\mathbb{R}, Z} \lambda'$ implies $\lambda \equiv_Z \lambda'$.
2. Let $K \subset \operatorname{Pic}(X)$ be the subgroup of the line bundles $L$ with $L \equiv_Z 0$. Then the kernel of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R} \to N^1(X/Z)$ is $K_\mathbb{R}$, embedded in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ by flatness. So every $\lambda \equiv_Z 0$ is a real combination of line bundles $L \equiv_Z 0$.
3. If $X$ is projective over $k$, the inclusion $Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R} \subset Z_1(X)_\mathbb{R}$ induces an injective linear map $N_1(X/Z) \to N_1(X)$ that maps $NE(X/Z)$ into $NE(X)$. Its image is the span of the classes of curves over $Z$, and $\rho(X/Z) \le \rho(X)$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

*Proof.* Part 1 is part 3 of Lemma 1.2.14 with $h = f$: a curve $C$ over $Z$ has $f(C)$ a point, so $f^*M \cdot C = 0$ for each line bundle $M$ on $Z$, and so for each $\beta$.

For part 2, let $\rho = \rho(X/Z)$ and choose curves $C_1, \dots, C_\rho$ over $Z$ whose classes form a basis of $N_1(X/Z)$, which is possible because the classes of curves span it. A class $\lambda$ is $\equiv_Z 0$ if and only if $\lambda \cdot C_i = 0$ for all $i$. Let $e \colon \operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \mathbb{Z}^\rho$ be $L \mapsto (L \cdot C_i)_i$. Then $\ker e = K$, and the image $A = e(\operatorname{Pic}(X))$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}^\rho$, so it is free of finite rank, and a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $A$ is linearly independent over $\mathbb{R}$ in $\mathbb{R}^\rho$. Tensor the exact sequence $0 \to K \to \operatorname{Pic}(X) \to A \to 0$ with $\mathbb{R}$. It stays exact, and $A_\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^\rho$ is injective. So the kernel of $e_\mathbb{R} \colon \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^\rho$ is $K_\mathbb{R}$, and this kernel is the set of $\lambda \equiv_Z 0$.

For part 3, a cycle $z \in Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R}$ is numerically trivial in both spaces under the same condition, $L \cdot z = 0$ for every line bundle $L$ on $X$. So the induced map is well defined and injective, and the rest is clear. $\square$

### Proof in words

The space $N_1(X/Z)$ is spanned by curves in the fibres of $f$, and the fibres may be singular and reducible, so the theorem of the base does not apply to them directly. The proof moves to one smooth projective variety that sees all fibres at once: a resolution $X'$ of a projective closure of $X$. Every curve over $Z$ lifts to a curve of $X'$ (Lemma 1.2.19), and the projection formula turns the intersection numbers on $X$ into intersection numbers on $X'$. Every line bundle of the open part of $X'$ over $X$ extends to $X'$, because $X'$ is smooth. So a line bundle on $X$ is numerically trivial over $Z$ as soon as its pullback is zero on a subspace of $N_1(X')$, and $N^1(X/Z)$ is a quotient of a subspace of the dual of $N_1(X')$. The theorem of the base gives $\dim N_1(X') < \infty$.

### Examples

**Example 1.2.22 (a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over a curve).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve, $\mathcal{E}$ a locally free sheaf of rank 2 on $C$, and $\pi \colon X = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}) \to C$ the projective bundle with its line bundle $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ (Definition P3.6.33). The morphism $\pi$ is projective (Har77, Section II.7). Over an open set $U$ where $\mathcal{E}$ is trivial, $\pi^{-1}(U) \cong \mathbb{P}^1_U$ (part 3 of Proposition P3.6.35), so $X$ is a smooth projective surface, $\pi$ is flat, and every fibre $F_c = \pi^{-1}(c)$ is $\mathbb{P}^1$. A curve over $C$ lies in a fibre, so it is a fibre. For a line bundle $L$, $L \cdot F_c = \deg L|_{F_c}$, and $\chi(F_c, L|_{F_c}) = L \cdot F_c + 1$ by Proposition P4.3.18. By Theorem P4.5.13, applied to $L$, which is flat over $C$, this Euler characteristic is locally constant on the connected curve $C$, so it is constant. So all fibres have the same class $[F]$ in $N_1(X/C)$, and $\mathcal{O}_X(1) \cdot F = 1$. Hence
$$\rho(X/C) = 1, \qquad N_1(X/C) = \mathbb{R} [F], \qquad NE(X/C) = \overline{NE}(X/C) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [F].$$
Here numerical and linear equivalence over $C$ agree. Let $L \equiv_C 0$. Then $L|_{F_c} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}$ for every $c$, so $h^0(F_c, L|_{F_c}) = 1$ is constant. By Theorem P4.5.16, $M = \pi_* L$ is a line bundle and $M \otimes k(c) \to H^0(F_c, L|_{F_c})$ is an isomorphism for every $c$. A nonzero section of the trivial bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ vanishes nowhere, so the evaluation map $\pi^* M \to L$ is surjective at every point. A surjective map of line bundles is an isomorphism, so $L \cong \pi^* M$ and $L \sim_C 0$. So $K = \pi^* \operatorname{Pic}(C)$ in Lemma 1.2.21, and by part 2 of that lemma, $\lambda \equiv_C 0$ implies $\lambda \sim_{\mathbb{R}, C} 0$ for every $\lambda \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$. For every $L$, the bundle $L \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(-a)$ with $a = L \cdot F$ is $\equiv_C 0$, so $\operatorname{Pic}(X) = \pi^* \operatorname{Pic}(C) \oplus \mathbb{Z} \, \mathcal{O}_X(1)$, where $\pi^*$ is injective because $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_C$ (part 3 of Proposition P3.6.35) and the projection formula $\pi_* \pi^* M \cong M$.

**Example 1.2.23 ($E \times E$ over $E$: numerically trivial, not linearly trivial).** Let $E$ be an elliptic curve with origin $O$, $X = E \times E$, and $f = p_2 \colon X \to E$. Write $F_z = E \times \{z\}$ for the fibre over $z$, $f_1 = \{O\} \times E$, and $\Delta$ for the diagonal. All of these are smooth curves isomorphic to $E$, and by part 6 of Lemma 1.2.14 the intersection numbers of Example P5.12.9 apply: $\Delta^2 = 0$, $f_1^2 = 0$, $\Delta \cdot f_1 = 1$. The curves over $E$ are the fibres $F_z$. As in Example 1.2.22, $p_2$ is flat and $\chi(F_z, L|_{F_z}) = \deg L|_{F_z}$ is constant in $z$, so all fibres have the same class $[F]$ in $N_1(X/E)$. The scheme $\Delta \times_X F_z$ is the reduced point $(z, z)$, because $p_2|_\Delta \colon \Delta \to E$ is an isomorphism, so by part 2 of Proposition P3.9.27 the pullback of $\Delta$ to $F_z \cong E$ is the point $z$, and $\Delta \cdot F = 1$. So $\rho(X/E) = 1$ and $NE(X/E) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [F]$, while $\rho(X) \ge 3$ by Example P5.12.9.

Let $L = \mathcal{O}_X(\Delta - f_1)$. In the same way the pullback of $f_1$ to $F_z$ is the point $O$, so $L|_{F_z} \cong \mathcal{O}_E(z - O)$. It has degree 0, so $L \equiv_E 0$. For $z \ne O$ the bundle $\mathcal{O}_E(z - O)$ is not trivial, by Proposition P5.1.13, while every $f^* M$ is trivial on every fibre. So $L \not\sim_E 0$. Even $L \not\sim_{\mathbb{R}, E} 0$. The curves $\Delta$ and $f_1$ both map isomorphically onto $E$ by $p_2$, so by part 3 of Lemma 1.2.14, $f^* \beta \cdot \Delta = f^* \beta \cdot f_1$ for every $\beta \in \operatorname{Pic}(E)_\mathbb{R}$. But
$$L \cdot \Delta = \Delta^2 - f_1 \cdot \Delta = -1, \qquad L \cdot f_1 = \Delta \cdot f_1 - f_1^2 = 1.$$
So in this family the kernel $K_\mathbb{R}$ of Lemma 1.2.21 is larger than the image of $f^* \operatorname{Pic}(E)_\mathbb{R}$, and numerical equivalence over $E$ is weaker than $\mathbb{R}$-linear equivalence over $E$.

**Example 1.2.24 (the pullback of a bundle of degree 0).** In Example 1.2.23 let $M$ be a line bundle of degree 0 on $E$ that is not trivial, and $L = p_1^* M$. Since $p_1|_{F_z} \colon F_z \to E$ is an isomorphism, $L|_{F_z} \cong M$ for every $z$. So $L \equiv_E 0$, and $L \not\sim_E 0$ because $M$ is not trivial. If $M = \mathcal{O}_E(P - O)$ with $2P = O$ as in Example 1.2.8, then $L^2 \cong \mathcal{O}_X$, so $L \sim_{\mathbb{Q}, E} 0$. Here the failure is only a failure of integral equivalence, while in Example 1.2.23 the class $L \cdot \Delta \ne L \cdot f_1$ shows a failure in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$.

### Proof

Let $\bar X$ be the closure of $X$ in a projective space that contains $X$ as a locally closed subvariety. Then $X$ is open and dense in the projective variety $\bar X$. By Hironaka's theorem (Hir64), since $k$ has characteristic 0, there is a projective birational morphism $\mu \colon X' \to \bar X$ from a smooth projective variety $X'$. It is surjective, since its image is closed and dense. Put $X'_0 = \mu^{-1}(X)$, an open subset of $X'$, and $\mu_0 = \mu|_{X'_0} \colon X'_0 \to X$, which is proper and surjective.

Step 1: every line bundle on $X'_0$ extends to $X'$. Both $X'$ and $X'_0$ are smooth, so $\operatorname{Pic} = \operatorname{Cl}$ for both (Corollary P3.9.11), and $\operatorname{Div}(X') \to \operatorname{Div}(X'_0)$ is surjective by Theorem P3.8.13. So $\operatorname{Pic}(X') \to \operatorname{Pic}(X'_0)$ is surjective.

Step 2: a map to a finite dimensional space. The theorem of the base, as used in Definition 1.1.8 (Kle66; SGA6, Exposé XIII; Laz04, Section 1.1.C), says that $N^1(X')$ is finite dimensional. So $N_1(X')$ has dimension $\rho(X')$ by Lemma 1.2.18 with $Z$ a point. Let $V \subset N_1(X')$ be the span of the classes of the curves $C' \subset X'_0$ such that $f(\mu(C'))$ is a point. Define $\Phi \colon \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R} \to V^*$ by
$$\Phi(\lambda)\Big(\sum_i a_i [C'_i]\Big) = \sum_i a_i \, (\mu_0^* \lambda \cdot C'_i).$$
This is well defined. Let $\lambda = \sum_j r_j L_j$ and suppose $\sum_i a_i [C'_i] = 0$ in $N_1(X')$. By Step 1 choose line bundles $M_j$ on $X'$ with $M_j|_{X'_0} \cong \mu_0^* L_j$. Each $C'_i$ lies in $X'_0$, so $\mu_0^* L_j \cdot C'_i = M_j \cdot C'_i$ by part 1 of Lemma 1.2.14, and $\sum_i a_i (M_j \cdot C'_i) = 0$. So the value does not depend on the expression of the class.

Step 3: the kernel of $\Phi$ is numerically trivial over $Z$. Let $\Phi(\lambda) = 0$ and let $C$ be a curve over $Z$. By Lemma 1.2.19 there is a curve $C' \subset X'_0$ with $\mu_0(C') = C$, and $f(\mu(C')) = f(C)$ is a point, so $[C'] \in V$. By part 3 of Lemma 1.2.14,
$$0 = \mu_0^* \lambda \cdot C' = [k(C') : k(C)] \, (\lambda \cdot C),$$
so $\lambda \cdot C = 0$. Hence the kernel of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R} \to N^1(X/Z)$ contains $\ker \Phi$, and $N^1(X/Z)$ is a quotient of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}/\ker \Phi$, which embeds in $V^*$. So
$$\rho(X/Z) \le \dim V \le \dim N_1(X') = \rho(X') < \infty.$$
By Lemma 1.2.18, $N_1(X/Z)$ has the same dimension and the pairing is perfect. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with the fibres $F_1$ and $F_2$ of Example 1.2.9. Show that $N_1(X) = \mathbb{R} [F_1] \oplus \mathbb{R} [F_2]$, and that for $f = p_2$ the subspace $N_1(X/\mathbb{P}^1)$ of Lemma 1.2.21 is the line $\mathbb{R}[F_2]$.
2. (A) Check the formula for the normalization $\nu$ of the nodal cubic in the text, and show that $\nu^*$ of each of the lines $\{x = 0\}$, $\{y = 0\}$ and $\{z = 0\}$ has degree 3. Which of these lines pass through the node, and how is this visible in the pullback?
3. (A) Let $\pi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up of a point $p$, and $C' \subset X$ a curve that is not the exceptional curve. Show that $\pi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cdot C' = d_C$, where $C = \pi(C')$ and $d_C$ is as in Example 1.2.16, and that $\pi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cdot E = 0$ for the exceptional curve $E$.
4. (B) Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be flat and projective with $Z$ a connected smooth curve and every closed fibre a smooth curve. Show that all closed fibres have the same class in $N_1(X/Z)$. If the fibres are irreducible and $X \to Z$ has no other curves over $Z$, deduce $\rho(X/Z) = 1$.
5. (B) Let $U \subset Z$ be open and $X_U = f^{-1}(U)$. Show that the inclusion of curves gives a well defined linear map $N_1(X_U/U) \to N_1(X/Z)$ that maps $NE(X_U/U)$ into $NE(X/Z)$, and that it is surjective when every curve over $Z$ maps into $U$.
6. (A) Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve over a point. Show that $\rho(C) = 1$ and $NE(C) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}[C]$. For a nonconstant morphism $g \colon C \to \mathbb{P}^1$, show that $\rho(C/\mathbb{P}^1) = 0$, and compare with Example 1.2.17.

### Lean

The Lean library has the intersection data `MinimalModel.IntersectionData` of a normal projective variety: its field `dot` is the intersection number of an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor with a curve, the field `dot_cartier` fixes it on Cartier divisors as the degree on the normalization, and the field `finiteDim` is the theorem of the base. The relative notions use `MinimalModel.IsContracted` for the curves over the base, `MinimalModel.IsRIntersectionNumber` for real intersection numbers, and `MinimalModel.IsNumTrivialRel` for $\equiv_Z 0$. The lemmas and Theorem 1.2.20 are not yet in Lean.

### Notes and history

The finite dimension of $N^1(X)$ for a projective $X$ goes back to the theorem of the base of Néron and Severi, and Kleiman proved the numerical form used here (Kle66; SGA6, Exposé XIII). The relative version is in KM98, Section 1.4 and before Theorem 1.44, and in Deb01, Chapter 1. Laz04, Section 1.4, treats numerical equivalence over a point, and Laz04, Section 1.7, ampleness in families. The proof by a resolution of a projective closure follows KM98. Example 1.2.23 is a standard warning. The contraction theorem, Theorem 1.1.12, proves that a line bundle that is numerically trivial on the contracted curves is a pullback from the base, and this is a real theorem: for $E \times E \to E$ the same statement is false.
