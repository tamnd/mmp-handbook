## Relative ampleness and the relative Kleiman criterion

### Statement

As in Section 2.2, $f \colon X \to Z$ is a projective morphism of quasi-projective varieties. If $X$ is projective over $k$, then every morphism $X \to Z$ to a quasi-projective variety is projective: its graph is a closed immersion $X \to X \times Z$, and $X \times Z$ is closed in $\mathbb{P}^N \times Z = \mathbb{P}^N_Z$.

**Definition 1.2.25 (relative ampleness and relative nefness).** A line bundle $L$ on $X$ is *$f$-ample*, or *ample over $Z$*, if $L|_{f^{-1}(U)}$ is ample for every affine open set $U \subset Z$. An element $\lambda \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ is *$f$-ample* if $\lambda = \sum_i a_i L_i$ with real $a_i > 0$ and $f$-ample line bundles $L_i$. An $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor on a normal $X$ is $f$-ample if its class is. An element $\lambda$, or an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor, is *$f$-nef* if $\lambda \cdot C \ge 0$ for every curve $C$ over $Z$. The *relative ample cone* $\operatorname{Amp}(X/Z) \subset N^1(X/Z)$ is the set of classes of $f$-ample elements, and the *relative nef cone* $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$ is the set of classes of $f$-nef elements.

Relative ampleness is local on $Z$: if $Z$ is covered by open sets $U_i$ such that $L|_{f^{-1}(U_i)}$ is ample over $U_i$, then $L$ is $f$-ample (EGA II, Proposition 4.6.4). When $Z$ is a point, $f$-ample means ample. An $f$-ample line bundle is $f$-ample as an element of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$, and Corollary 1.2.31 shows the converse.

**Example 1.2.26 (the hyperplane bundle of an embedding).** By Definition P3.4.29, $f$ factors as a closed immersion $X \subset \mathbb{P}^N_Z$ and the projection. Let $H = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N_Z}(1)|_X$. For an affine open $U = \operatorname{Spec} A$ of $Z$, $f^{-1}(U)$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^N_A$, proper over $A$, and $H|_{f^{-1}(U)}$ is very ample over $A$, so it is ample by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.24. So $H$ is $f$-ample, and every projective morphism has an $f$-ample line bundle. If $M$ is a line bundle on $Z$, then $H \otimes f^*M$ is also $f$-ample, because $M|_U$ is trivial on a cover of $Z$ by affine open sets $U$, and $f$-ampleness is local on $Z$.

**Example 1.2.27 (a relatively ample bundle that is not ample).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $f = p_2$, and let $\mathcal{O}(a, b) = p_1^* \mathcal{O}(a) \otimes p_2^* \mathcal{O}(b)$. Then $\mathcal{O}(1, 0)$ is the hyperplane bundle of $X = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{P}^1}$, so by Example 1.2.26 every $\mathcal{O}(1, b)$ is $f$-ample. For $b \le 0$ it is not ample: the fibre $C = \{pt\} \times \mathbb{P}^1$ of $p_1$ has $\mathcal{O}(1, b) \cdot C = b \le 0$, against part 5 of Lemma 1.2.14.

**Lemma 1.2.28 (bounds from a relatively ample bundle).** Let $L$ be an $f$-ample line bundle and $M$ a line bundle on $X$. Then there is an integer $n$ such that $|M \cdot z| \le n \, (L \cdot z)$ for every $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$. In particular $L \cdot z \ge 0$ on $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$, and $L \cdot z = 0$ for $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$ only if $z = 0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

*Proof.* Cover $Z$ by finitely many affine open sets $U_1, \dots, U_s$, and put $X_i = f^{-1}(U_i)$, a Noetherian scheme on which $L$ is ample. By Definition P3.6.22 there is $n$ such that $(M \otimes L^n)|_{X_i}$ and $(M^{-1} \otimes L^n)|_{X_i}$ are generated by global sections for every $i$. A curve $C$ over $Z$ maps to a point of some $U_i$, so $C \subset X_i$, and part 4 of Lemma 1.2.14 gives $(\pm M + nL) \cdot C \ge 0$. So the linear forms $\pm M + nL$ are nonnegative on $NE(X/Z)$, and so on its closure. This is the inequality. Adding the two forms gives $2n (L \cdot z) \ge 0$. Now choose line bundles $M_1, \dots, M_\rho$ whose classes span $N^1(X/Z)$. If $L \cdot z = 0$, the inequality gives $M_j \cdot z = 0$ for all $j$, so $z$ pairs to zero with all of $N^1(X/Z)$, and $z = 0$ by Theorem 1.2.20. $\square$

**Lemma 1.2.29 (ampleness on the fibres).** Let $L$ be a line bundle on $X$. The following are equivalent.

1. $L$ is $f$-ample.
2. $L|_{X_z}$ is ample for every closed point $z \in Z$.
3. For every closed point $z \in Z$, $L|_V$ is ample for every irreducible component $V$ of the reduced fibre $(X_z)_{\mathrm{red}}$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

*Proof.* If $Y \subset W$ is a closed subscheme of a Noetherian scheme and $L$ is ample on $W$, then $L|_Y$ is ample: for a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $Y$, $i_* \mathcal{F} \otimes L^n$ is generated by global sections for large $n$, and so is its restriction $\mathcal{F} \otimes L|_Y^n$. Since $X_z$ is closed in $f^{-1}(U)$ for an affine $U \ni z$, and $V$ is closed in $X_z$, this gives 1 implies 2 and 2 implies 3. Statement 3 implies 2 by Har77, Exercise III.5.7: a line bundle on a Noetherian scheme is ample if it is ample on the reduced scheme, and on the reduced scheme if it is ample on each irreducible component. For 2 implies 1, let $z$ be a closed point with $L|_{X_z}$ ample. By EGA III, Théorème 4.7.1 (see also Laz04, Section 1.7), there is an open $U_z \ni z$ such that $L$ is ample over $U_z$. Let $U$ be the union of all open sets of $Z$ over which $L$ is ample. By locality, $L$ is ample over $U$, and $U$ contains every closed point. If $Z \setminus U$ were not empty, it would contain a closed point of $Z$, since $Z$ is of finite type over $k$. So $U = Z$. $\square$

**Theorem 1.2.30 (Kleiman's criterion over a base).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a projective morphism of quasi-projective varieties and $\lambda \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$. Then $\lambda$ is $f$-ample if and only if $\lambda \cdot z > 0$ for every nonzero $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$. The same holds for an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor on a normal $X$, through its class.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 1.2.31 (the relative cones).** In the situation of the theorem:

1. Whether $\lambda$ is $f$-ample depends only on its class in $N^1(X/Z)$. A line bundle that is $f$-ample as an element of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ is $f$-ample as a line bundle. The sum of an $f$-ample and an $f$-nef element is $f$-ample, and $\lambda + f^* \beta$ is $f$-ample for $f$-ample $\lambda$ and every $\beta \in \operatorname{Pic}(Z)_\mathbb{R}$.
2. $\operatorname{Amp}(X/Z)$ is an open convex cone, it is the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$, and $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$ is its closure.
3. $\overline{NE}(X/Z) = \{ z \in N_1(X/Z) : x \cdot z \ge 0 \text{ for all } x \in \operatorname{Nef}(X/Z) \}$, so the two closed cones are dual.
4. When $Z$ is a point and $X$ is projective and normal, the theorem is Theorem 1.1.9.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

### Proof in words

One direction is a bound: an $f$-ample $L$ dominates every line bundle on the cone, after a multiple, by global generation on an affine cover (Lemma 1.2.28). So $L$ cannot vanish on a nonzero class of $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$. For the converse, ampleness over $Z$ is ampleness on the fibres, and ampleness on a fibre is ampleness on each component of the reduced fibre, which is a projective variety. On such a component $V$ the absolute criterion, Theorem 1.1.9, applies. The cone $\overline{NE}(V)$ maps into $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$, and a nonzero class of $\overline{NE}(V)$ stays nonzero there, because the $f$-ample hyperplane bundle is positive on it. For real coefficients the positive classes form an open cone, so a positive real class is a convex combination of positive rational classes, which are line bundles up to a multiple. The numerically trivial part that remains is absorbed into one of the line bundles.

### Examples

**Example 1.2.32 (the blow-up of a point of $\mathbb{P}^n$).** Let $n \ge 2$, $p \in \mathbb{P}^n$, and $\pi \colon X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^n \to \mathbb{P}^n$ the blow-up, with exceptional divisor $E$, an effective Cartier divisor (Theorem P3.11.3). The charts of Example 4.1.6 show that $X$ is smooth and $E \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. The morphism $\pi$ is projective, since $X$ is the Proj of the Rees algebra of the ideal of $p$, and so $X$ is a smooth projective variety.

The curves over $\mathbb{P}^n$ are the curves in $E$. Indeed $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $\mathbb{P}^n \setminus \{p\}$, so a curve $C$ with $\pi(C) = q \ne p$ would lie in the single point $\pi^{-1}(q)$. For a line bundle $L$ on $X$, $L|_E \cong \mathcal{O}_E(a)$ for some $a$ (Corollary P3.9.11). For a curve $C \subset E$ let $d_C = \mathcal{O}_E(1) \cdot C > 0$, as in Example 1.2.16 inside $E$. Then $L \cdot C = a \, d_C$ by part 1 of Lemma 1.2.14. For a line $\ell \subset E$, $d_\ell = 1$, so $[C] = d_C [\ell]$ in $N_1(X/\mathbb{P}^n)$.

Now compute $E \cdot \ell$. Take affine coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$ with $p = 0$, and the chart $x_1 = y_1$, $x_i = y_1 y_i$ for $i \ge 2$ of Example 4.1.6, where $E = \{y_1 = 0\}$ and $y_2, \dots, y_n$ are affine coordinates on $E$. In the homogeneous coordinates $(v_1 : \dots : v_n)$ of $E$ these are $y_i = v_i/v_1$. Let $H = \{x_2 = 0\}$, a hyperplane through $p$, and $\tilde H$ its strict transform. In the chart, $\pi^* x_2 = y_1 y_2$, so the Weil divisor of $\pi^* H$ is $\tilde H + E$, with $\tilde H = \{y_2 = 0\}$ in the chart. In the chart $x_2 = y'_2$, $x_i = y'_2 y'_i$ for $i \ne 2$, the function $\pi^* x_2 = y'_2$ is an equation of $E$, so $\tilde H$ does not meet this chart. On the smooth $X$ Cartier divisors are determined by their Weil divisors (Proposition P3.9.9), so $\pi^* H = \tilde H + E$ as Cartier divisors. Let $\ell = \{v_3 = \dots = v_n = 0\} \subset E$. In the first chart it is the line $y_1 = y_3 = \dots = y_n = 0$ with coordinate $y_2$, and its last point $(0 : 1 : 0 : \dots : 0)$ lies in the second chart. So $\ell \not\subset \tilde H$, and the pullback of $\tilde H$ to $\ell$ is the divisor of $y_2$ on the line, one reduced point. By part 2 of Lemma 1.2.14, $\tilde H \cdot \ell = 1$. By part 3, $\pi^* H \cdot \ell = 0$, since $\ell$ is contracted. So
$$E \cdot \ell = \pi^* H \cdot \ell - \tilde H \cdot \ell = -1.$$
Hence $[\ell] \ne 0$, and
$$\rho(X/\mathbb{P}^n) = 1, \qquad NE(X/\mathbb{P}^n) = \overline{NE}(X/\mathbb{P}^n) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [\ell].$$
By Theorem 1.2.30 an element $\lambda$ is $\pi$-ample if and only if $\lambda \cdot \ell > 0$. So $-E$ and $\tilde H = \pi^*H - E$ are $\pi$-ample, and $E$ and $\pi^* H$ are not. By Example 4.1.6, computed in the affine chart around $p$ and extended by the isomorphism away from $E$, $K_X = \pi^* K_{\mathbb{P}^n} + (n - 1) E$. So $K_X \cdot \ell = -(n - 1) < 0$ and $-K_X$ is $\pi$-ample: $\pi$ is a $K_X$-negative contraction with relative Picard number 1.

**Example 1.2.33 (Hirzebruch surfaces over $\mathbb{P}^1$).** Let $n \ge 1$ and $\pi \colon F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1$ the Hirzebruch surface, a smooth projective surface with the curves $C_-$ and $F$ of Example P5.12.8. By Theorem P3.11.25 every fibre $F_c$ over a closed point is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, so the curves over $\mathbb{P}^1$ are the fibres, and $C_-$ maps isomorphically onto $\mathbb{P}^1$. The divisor $F_c = \pi^*[c]$ has $\mathcal{O}(F_c) \cong \pi^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ for all $c$, so by part 6 of Lemma 1.2.14 the number $L \cdot F_c = (L \cdot \pi^* \mathcal{O}(1))$ does not depend on $c$. So all fibres have one class $[F]$, and $C_- \cdot F = 1$ by Example P5.12.8. Hence $\rho(F_n/\mathbb{P}^1) = 1$, $\overline{NE}(F_n/\mathbb{P}^1) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}[F]$, and $aC_- + bF$ is $\pi$-ample if and only if $a > 0$. In particular $C_-$ is $\pi$-ample. It is not ample, because $C_- \cdot C_- = -n < 0$, against part 5 of Lemma 1.2.14.

**Example 1.2.34 (a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over a curve).** In Example 1.2.22, $\overline{NE}(X/C) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}[F]$, so $\lambda \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ is $\pi$-ample if and only if $\lambda \cdot F > 0$. For a line bundle $L = \pi^* M \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(a)$, this means $a > 0$, for every $M$. This agrees with Example 1.2.26 for $a = 1$.

**Example 1.2.35 ($E \times E$ over $E$).** In Example 1.2.23, $\lambda$ is $p_2$-ample if and only if $\lambda \cdot F > 0$. So $\Delta$ and $f_1$ are $p_2$-ample, and the fibre class $F$ and $\Delta - f_1$ are not. For real $c > 0$ let $D_c = (1 + c) \Delta - c f_1$. Then $D_c \cdot F = 1$, so $D_c$ is $p_2$-ample, while $D_c \cdot \Delta = -c < 0$, so $D_c$ is not ample on $X$, by the theorem over a point. The classes $D_c$ differ from $\Delta$ by $c(\Delta - f_1)$, which is numerically trivial over $E$, as part 1 of the corollary requires.

### Proof

Step 1: $f$-ample implies positive. Let $\lambda = \sum_i a_i L_i$ with $a_i > 0$ and $L_i$ $f$-ample, and let $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$ be nonzero. By Lemma 1.2.28, $L_i \cdot z > 0$ for each $i$, so $\lambda \cdot z > 0$.

Step 2: a positive line bundle is $f$-ample. Let $L$ be a line bundle with $L \cdot z > 0$ for every nonzero $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$. By Lemma 1.2.29 it suffices to show that $L|_V$ is ample for every irreducible component $V$ of $(X_z)_{\mathrm{red}}$ and every closed $z \in Z$. Such a $V$ is a projective variety, and $L|_V \cong \mathcal{O}_V(D)$ for a Cartier divisor $D$ by Theorem P3.9.4. Every curve of $V$ is a curve of $X$ over $Z$, which gives a linear map $\iota \colon Z_1(V)_\mathbb{R} \to Z_1(X/Z)_\mathbb{R}$. By part 1 of Lemma 1.2.14, $M \cdot \iota(w) = M|_V \cdot w$ for every line bundle $M$ on $X$. So a numerically trivial cycle on $V$ goes to a numerically trivial cycle, and $\iota$ induces a linear map $N_1(V) \to N_1(X/Z)$ that maps $NE(V)$ into $NE(X/Z)$, and by continuity $\overline{NE}(V)$ into $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$. Let $H$ be the $f$-ample bundle of Example 1.2.26. Then $H|_V$ is ample by Lemma 1.2.29. Let $w \in \overline{NE}(V)$ be nonzero. By Theorem 1.1.9 for $H|_V$, $H \cdot \iota(w) = H|_V \cdot w > 0$, so $\iota(w) \ne 0$, and $\iota(w) \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$. By the hypothesis, $L|_V \cdot w = L \cdot \iota(w) > 0$. So $L|_V$ is ample by Theorem 1.1.9. When $V$ is a point, $L|_V$ is ample since $V$ is affine.

Step 3: the positive classes form an open cone. Let $\mathcal{P} \subset N^1(X/Z)$ be the set of classes $x$ with $x \cdot z > 0$ for every nonzero $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$. Fix norms on both spaces, and let $S$ be the set of $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$ of norm 1, a compact set. Since $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$ is a cone, $x \in \mathcal{P}$ if and only if $x > 0$ on $S$. If $S$ is empty, $\mathcal{P} = N^1(X/Z)$. Otherwise, for $x \in \mathcal{P}$ the continuous function $s \mapsto x \cdot s$ has a minimum $\varepsilon > 0$ on $S$, and $|y \cdot s - x \cdot s| \le c \, \|y - x\|$ on $S$ for a constant $c$. So the ball of radius $\varepsilon / c$ around $x$ lies in $\mathcal{P}$, and $\mathcal{P}$ is open. It is a convex cone.

Step 4: a positive real class is $f$-ample. Let $\lambda \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ have class in $\mathcal{P}$. Let $\rho = \rho(X/Z)$ and choose line bundles $M_1, \dots, M_\rho$ whose classes form a basis of $N^1(X/Z)$, and write the class of $\lambda$ as $\sum_j d_j [M_j]$. Since $\mathcal{P}$ is open, there are rational numbers $a_j < d_j < b_j$ such that the box $B = \prod_j [a_j, b_j]$, in these coordinates, lies in $\mathcal{P}$. Write $d_j = t_j a_j + (1 - t_j) b_j$ with $0 < t_j < 1$. Then the point $d$ is the convex combination $\sum_v \mu_v v$ of the $2^\rho$ vertices $v$ of $B$, where $\mu_v$ is the product over $j$ of $t_j$ or $1 - t_j$, according as $v_j = a_j$ or $v_j = b_j$. So every $\mu_v > 0$ and $\sum_v \mu_v = 1$. Each vertex $v$ is rational. Let $N_v \ge 1$ be a common denominator of its coordinates, and $L_v = \bigotimes_j M_j^{N_v v_j}$, a line bundle with class $N_v v \in \mathcal{P}$. By Step 2, $L_v$ is $f$-ample. Put
$$\lambda' = \sum_v \frac{\mu_v}{N_v} L_v \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}.$$
Then $\lambda - \lambda' \equiv_Z 0$. When $\rho = 0$, put instead $\lambda' = 1 \cdot \mathcal{O}_X$, where $\mathcal{O}_X$ is $f$-ample by Step 2, since $\overline{NE}(X/Z) = 0$. By part 2 of Lemma 1.2.21, $\lambda - \lambda' = \sum_{i=1}^s r_i N_i$ with $r_i \in \mathbb{R}$ and line bundles $N_i \equiv_Z 0$.

It remains to absorb this remainder. Choose one term $c L_0$ of $\lambda'$, with $c > 0$ and $L_0$ $f$-ample. Then
$$c L_0 + \sum_{i=1}^s r_i N_i = \sum_{i=1}^s \frac{c}{s} \big( L_0 + u_i N_i \big), \qquad u_i = \frac{s r_i}{c}.$$
For a real $u$ write $u = m + \theta$ with $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $0 \le \theta < 1$. Then $L_0 + u N_i = (1 - \theta)(L_0 \otimes N_i^m) + \theta (L_0 \otimes N_i^{m+1})$ in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$. The line bundles $L_0 \otimes N_i^m$ and $L_0 \otimes N_i^{m+1}$ have the same class as $L_0$ in $N^1(X/Z)$, which lies in $\mathcal{P}$ by Step 1, so they are $f$-ample by Step 2. So $\lambda$ is a combination of $f$-ample line bundles with nonnegative coefficients, not all zero, and dropping the zero terms shows that $\lambda$ is $f$-ample. With Step 1 this proves the theorem, and the case of $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisors is the same statement for their classes. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 1.2.31.* Part 1: the condition of the theorem depends only on the class. A line bundle $L$ that is $f$-ample in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ is positive on $\overline{NE}(X/Z) \setminus \{0\}$ by the theorem, so it is $f$-ample by Step 2. If $\lambda$ is $f$-ample and $\nu$ is $f$-nef, then $\nu \ge 0$ on $NE(X/Z)$, so on its closure, and $\lambda + \nu > 0$ on the nonzero classes. By part 1 of Lemma 1.2.21, $f^*\beta \equiv_Z 0$, so it is $f$-nef.

Part 2: by the theorem $\operatorname{Amp}(X/Z) = \mathcal{P}$, which is an open convex cone by Step 3. A class is $f$-nef if and only if it is nonnegative on $NE(X/Z)$, so on $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$, and $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$ is a closed convex cone that contains $\mathcal{P}$. So $\mathcal{P}$ lies in the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$. Conversely let $x$ be an interior point, and $h$ the class of the bundle $H$ of Example 1.2.26. For small $\varepsilon > 0$, $x - \varepsilon h \in \operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$, so $x \cdot z \ge \varepsilon \, h \cdot z > 0$ for nonzero $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$, by Step 1. So $x \in \mathcal{P}$. For $x \in \operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$, the classes $x + \varepsilon h$ lie in $\mathcal{P}$ and tend to $x$, so $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$ is the closure of $\mathcal{P}$.

Part 3: every $z \in \overline{NE}(X/Z)$ is nonnegative on $\operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$ by definition. If $z \notin \overline{NE}(X/Z)$, the separation theorem for the closed convex cone $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$ in a finite dimensional space gives a linear form, which by Theorem 1.2.20 is a class $x \in N^1(X/Z)$, with $x \ge 0$ on $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$ and $x \cdot z < 0$. Then $x \in \operatorname{Nef}(X/Z)$. Part 4: over a point $f$-ample means ample, and $N^1(X/Z)$, $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$ are the spaces of Definition 1.1.8, so the theorem becomes Theorem 1.1.9, which its proof used for the components of the fibres. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) On $F_n$ with $n \ge 1$, show that $aC_- + bF$ is $\pi$-ample if and only if $a > 0$, and, using the nef cone of Example P5.12.8 and part 2 of Corollary 1.2.31 over a point, that it is ample if and only if $a > 0$ and $b > na$.
2. (A) In Example 1.2.32, compute $(\pi^* H - tE) \cdot \ell$ and find all real $t$ for which $\pi^*H - tE$ is $\pi$-ample.
3. (A) On $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with $f = p_2$, show that $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ is $f$-ample if and only if $a > 0$, and ample if and only if $a > 0$ and $b > 0$.
4. (B) Let $L$ be $f$-ample and $M$ any line bundle on $X$. Show from Step 3 of the proof, and again from Lemma 1.2.28, that $M \otimes L^m$ is $f$-ample for all large $m$.
5. (B) Let $f$ be finite. Show that every line bundle is $f$-ample, first from Theorem 1.2.30 and Example 1.2.17, and then directly from part 2 of Proposition P3.6.24, using that $f^{-1}(U)$ is affine for $U$ affine.
6. (B) Let $U \subset Z$ be open and $f_U \colon X_U = f^{-1}(U) \to U$. Show that the restriction of an $f$-ample line bundle is $f_U$-ample. For the blow-up $\pi$ of Example 1.2.32 and $U = \mathbb{P}^n \setminus \{p\}$, show that $E|_{X_U}$ is $\pi_U$-ample while $E$ is not $\pi$-ample.

### Lean

The Lean library defines `MinimalModel.IsRelAmple`, a Cartier divisor that is ample on the inverse image of each affine open set of the base, and `MinimalModel.IsRAmpleOver`, an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor that is $\mathbb{R}$-linearly equivalent to a positive combination of relatively ample Cartier divisors, which is Definition 1.2.25. It has `MinimalModel.IsNefRel` and `MinimalModel.IsNefOver` for relative nefness. The absolute criterion is the statement `MinimalModel.KleimanCriterion`, the node of Theorem 1.1.9. The relative criterion, Theorem 1.2.30, is not yet a statement of the library.

### Notes and history

Kleiman proved the criterion over a point in 1966 (Kle66), together with the finite dimension of $N^1$, and the relative form follows from it by the reduction to the fibres given here. KM98, Theorem 1.18, states the absolute criterion, and KM98, Theorem 1.44, the relative one, for a projective morphism. Laz04, Theorem 1.4.23, gives the absolute criterion for $\mathbb{R}$-divisors, and Laz04, Section 1.7, the theorem that ampleness on a fibre spreads to a neighbourhood (EGA III, Théorème 4.7.1). Deb01, Chapter 1, has the same material with many examples. Relative ampleness and the relative cone are the language of the program over a base: a step $X \to Z$ of the program is a contraction of an extremal ray of $\overline{NE}(X/Z)$, and the theorem says which divisors are ample over $Z$ after the step.
