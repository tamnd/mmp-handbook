# Exceptional loci and the negativity lemma

Every step of the minimal model program is a projective morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ with $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y$, a contraction. When $f$ is birational, it is an isomorphism outside a closed set, the exceptional locus, and the program needs to know two things about that set: its dimension, which decides whether $f$ is divisorial or small, and the signs of the divisors supported on it, which decide how discrepancies change. This chapter proves the facts that the rest of the handbook uses on both questions.

Throughout, $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, a variety is an integral separated scheme of finite type over $k$, and a curve on a variety is an integral closed subscheme of dimension 1. Section 1 collects the consequences of Zariski's main theorem and of the Stein factorization of P4 Chapter 6 in the form in which the program uses them: the first properties of contractions, the rigidity lemma, which says when a morphism factors through a contraction, and the exceptional locus of a projective birational morphism onto a normal variety, with its characterization by isolated points of fibres. Section 2 proves van der Waerden's purity theorem: if the target is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, the exceptional locus is a divisor. For a smooth target it also proves that the relative canonical divisor $K_{X/Y}$ is effective with support the exceptional locus, and it computes $K$ of the blow-up of a smooth centre of codimension $c$: $K_{\tilde X} = \pi^* K_X + (c - 1)E$. Section 3 proves the negativity lemma in all dimensions (Kollár-Mori, Lemma 3.39): if $-B$ is nef over $Y$, then $B$ is effective as soon as its pushforward is. The proof reduces to the surface case, the argument of Corollary P5.8.4, by cutting with general hyperplanes, first from $Y$ and then from $X$. As a corollary, a projective birational morphism from a normal variety onto a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial one has an effective exceptional divisor $F$ with $-F$ positive on every contracted curve and $\operatorname{Supp} F = \operatorname{Exc}(f)$.

The worked example of the chapter is the blow-up $\pi \colon Y \to X = \mathbb{P}^n$ of a point. Example 1.5.15 computes in full that $K_Y = \pi^* K_X + (n - 1)E$, that $\mathcal{O}_E(E) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)$, that $E^n = (-1)^{n-1}$, and that $(-K_Y)^n = (n+1)^n - (n-1)^n$. The counterexample is the small contraction of the cone over $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, the Atiyah contraction of Example P4.6.13: its exceptional locus is a curve in a threefold, so purity shows again that the cone is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial. The chapter needs P3 Chapters 7, 9, 11 and 12, P4 Chapter 6 and P5 Chapters 5 and 8. It ends with problems that compute exceptional loci, relative canonical divisors and intersection numbers on blow-ups by hand.
