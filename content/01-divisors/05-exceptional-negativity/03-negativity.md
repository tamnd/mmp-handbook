## The negativity lemma

### Statement

The notation of Sections 1 and 2 is in force. An *$\mathbb{R}$-divisor* on a normal variety $X$ is a finite formal sum $B = \sum_j b_j B_j$ of distinct prime divisors with real coefficients. It is *effective*, $B \geq 0$, if every $b_j \geq 0$, and it is *$\mathbb{R}$-Cartier* if it is a real linear combination of Cartier divisors, viewed as Weil divisors. The negativity lemma is the step of the minimal model program that compares divisors on the two sides of a birational morphism. It needs intersection numbers with curves, and only the following ones.

**Definition 1.5.19 (degrees on curves, relative nefness).** Let $X$ be a normal variety.

1. Let $C \subseteq X$ be a curve that is proper over $k$, and $L$ an invertible sheaf on $X$. The *degree* of $L$ on $C$ is $\deg_C L = \chi(C, L|_C) - \chi(C, \mathcal{O}_C)$. For a Cartier divisor $D$, $D \cdot C = \deg_C \mathcal{O}_X(D)$.
2. For an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor $B = \sum_i r_i D_i$ with $D_i$ Cartier and $r_i \in \mathbb{R}$, $B \cdot C = \sum_i r_i (D_i \cdot C)$.
3. Let $h \colon X \to Y$ be a proper morphism. A curve $C \subseteq X$ is *$h$-contracted* if $h(C)$ is a point. An $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor $B$ is *$h$-nef* if $B \cdot C \geq 0$ for every $h$-contracted curve $C$, and *$h$-numerically trivial*, written $B \equiv_h 0$, if $B \cdot C = 0$ for every $h$-contracted curve $C$.

An $h$-contracted curve lies in the fibre over a closed point $y$ with $\kappa(y) = k$, which is proper over $k$, so it is proper over $k$ and part 1 applies. Three facts are used. (a) Degrees are additive, $\deg_C (L \otimes M) = \deg_C L + \deg_C M$, and satisfy the projection formula: if $\mu \colon X' \to X$ is proper and $C' \subseteq X'$ is a proper curve, then $\mu^* D \cdot C' = 0$ if $\mu(C')$ is a point and $\mu^* D \cdot C' = \deg(C' \to \mu(C'))\, (D \cdot \mu(C'))$ otherwise (Lazarsfeld, Section 1.1.C; Debarre, Section 1.1). (b) If $L|_C$ has a section $s \neq 0$, then its zero scheme $Z$ is finite, because $C$ is integral of dimension 1, and $\deg_C L = \operatorname{length} \mathcal{O}_Z$: the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_C \xrightarrow{s} L|_C \to L|_C \otimes \mathcal{O}_Z \to 0$ is exact because $C$ is integral, and $L|_C \otimes \mathcal{O}_Z \cong \mathcal{O}_Z$ has Euler characteristic $\operatorname{length} \mathcal{O}_Z$. So an effective Cartier divisor $D$ that does not contain $C$ has $D \cdot C \geq 0$, with equality only if $D \cap C = \emptyset$, and a principal divisor has degree $0$ on every proper curve. (c) Part 2 does not depend on the expression of $B$. The relations $\sum_i r_i D_i = 0$ between finitely many Cartier divisors form the solution space of linear equations with integer coefficients, the coefficients of the $D_i$ along prime divisors, so they are real combinations of integer relations $\sum_i m_i D_i = 0$. For those, $\sum_i m_i D_i$ is a Cartier divisor whose Weil divisor is $0$, so it is $0$ as a Cartier divisor on the normal variety $X$ (Hartshorne, Remark II.6.11.2), and $\sum_i m_i (D_i \cdot C) = \deg_C \mathcal{O}_X = 0$ by (a).

**Lemma 1.5.20 (negativity on a surface over an affine base).** Let $S$ be a smooth quasi-projective surface, $T$ a normal affine surface, and $g \colon S \to T$ a projective birational morphism. Let $B$ be an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor on $S$ such that every component of $B$ with negative coefficient is $g$-exceptional and $B \cdot C \leq 0$ for every $g$-exceptional curve $C$. Then $B \geq 0$.

The surface $S$ is not projective, so Corollary P5.8.4 does not apply as stated, but its proof does. Every divisor on $S$ is Cartier by Corollary P1.9.26 and Theorem P3.9.10, so all intersection numbers with the proper exceptional curves are defined by Definition 1.5.19.

The proof of the negativity lemma cuts a variety down to a surface by general hyperplanes. A property holds for a *general* hyperplane of $\mathbb{P}^M$ if it holds for every hyperplane in a dense open subset of the dual projective space; finitely many such properties hold at once for a general hyperplane. An affine hyperplane of $\mathbb{A}^N$ is general if its closure in $\mathbb{P}^N$ is.

**Lemma 1.5.21 (cutting down by general hyperplanes).** Let $h \colon X \to Y$ be a projective morphism from a smooth quasi-projective variety $X$ of dimension $n$, with a locally closed embedding $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^M$, to an affine variety $Y \subseteq \mathbb{A}^N$. Let $0 \leq w \leq n - 2$. Choose in turn general affine hyperplanes $H_1, \dots, H_w$ of $\mathbb{A}^N$ and general hyperplanes $H'_1, \dots, H'_{n-2-w}$ of $\mathbb{P}^M$, and put
$$Y' = Y \cap H_1 \cap \dots \cap H_w, \qquad X' = h^{-1}(Y'), \qquad S = X' \cap H'_1 \cap \dots \cap H'_{n-2-w},$$
as closed subschemes. Then:

1. $S$ is smooth, and every irreducible component of $S$ has dimension 2.
2. For a closed subset $Z \subseteq Y$, every irreducible component of $Z \cap Y'$ has dimension at most $\dim Z - w$. If $Z$ is irreducible of dimension $w$, then $Z \cap Y'$ is finite and nonempty.
3. For a closed subset $V \subseteq X$, every irreducible component of $V \cap S$ has dimension at most $\dim V - n + 2$.
4. Let $F_1, \dots, F_s$ be irreducible closed subsets of $X'$, proper over $k$, of dimensions $d_i \geq n - 1 - w$. If the $H'_i$ are general also with respect to the $F_i$, then every $F_i \cap S$ is nonempty and all its irreducible components have dimension $d_i - (n - 2 - w) \geq 1$.

**Theorem 1.5.22 (the negativity lemma).** Let $h \colon X \to Y$ be a projective birational morphism of normal varieties, and $B$ an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor on $X$ such that $-B$ is $h$-nef. Then $B \geq 0$ if and only if $h_* B \geq 0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 1.5.23 (exceptional divisors and relative numerical triviality).** Let $h \colon X \to Y$ be as in Theorem 1.5.22 and $B$ an $h$-exceptional $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor. If $B$ is $h$-nef, then $-B \geq 0$. If $B \equiv_h 0$, then $B = 0$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 1.5.24 (an anti-ample exceptional divisor).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a projective birational morphism of normal varieties with $Y$ $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial. Then there is an effective $f$-exceptional Cartier divisor $F$ on $X$ with $F \cdot C < 0$ for every $f$-contracted curve $C$, and $\operatorname{Supp} F = \operatorname{Exc}(f)$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

Corollary 1.5.24 contains Theorem 1.5.12 for a normal $X$, since $\operatorname{Supp} F$ is a divisor. It is the form in which the program uses exceptional divisors over a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial base: $-F$ is positive on the curves contracted by $f$, and in Volume 4 this makes $f$ the contraction of the extremal face spanned by those curves.

### Proof in words

On a surface over an affine base, the negativity lemma is the argument of P5 Chapter 8. Mumford's argument with a function vanishing at a point of the base shows that the curves over that point have a negative definite intersection matrix, and the sign lemma for such matrices does the rest.

In higher dimension, take a component $E$ of $B$ with negative coefficient, chosen so that its image $h(E)$ has the largest dimension $w$ among such components. Cutting $Y$ by $w$ general hyperplanes leaves finitely many points of $h(E)$, and the images of all the negative components meet the cut in at most finitely many points. Cutting $X$ further by general hyperplanes leaves a smooth surface $S'$, which maps birationally to its image, and a proper curve $\Gamma \subseteq E \cap S'$ that lies on no other component of $B$. On $S'$, the restriction of $B$ still has negative intersection with the contracted curves, its negative components are all contracted, because their images lie in the finite set above, and its coefficient along $\Gamma$ is negative. This contradicts the surface case. The reduction to a smooth $X$, needed for Bertini's theorem, is a resolution of singularities and the projection formula.

For Corollary 1.5.24, take a relatively ample divisor $H$ on $X$, push it to $Y$, and pull a Cartier multiple back. The difference $F$ is exceptional with $F \cdot C = -m H \cdot C < 0$ on contracted curves, so the negativity lemma makes it effective, and a contracted curve with $F \cdot C < 0$ must lie in $\operatorname{Supp} F$.

### Examples

**Example 1.5.25 (the blow-up of a point of $\mathbb{P}^n$).** Let $\pi \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^n$ be as in Example 1.5.15. Every $\pi$-contracted curve $C$ lies in $E \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, and since $\mathcal{O}_E(E) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)$, fact (b) applied to a hyperplane of $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ not containing $C$ gives $E \cdot C = -\deg C < 0$, where $\deg C > 0$ is the degree of $C$ in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. So $-E$ is $\pi$-nef and $E$ is not. For $B = E$ the theorem gives $E \geq 0$, which is true. For $B = -E$ the pushforward $\pi_*(-E) = 0$ is effective but $-E$ is not, and the hypothesis fails because $-B = E$ is not $\pi$-nef. For $B = K_{Y/\mathbb{P}^n} = (n-1) E$, Corollary 1.5.23 confirms that the exceptional divisor $K_{Y/\mathbb{P}^n}$ is not $\pi$-nef unless it is $0$. The divisor $\pi^* H - E = q^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ satisfies $(\pi^* H - E) \cdot C = \deg C > 0$: it is $\pi$-nef, and $F = E$ is the divisor of Corollary 1.5.24.

**Example 1.5.26 (the Atiyah contraction).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be as in Example 1.5.16, with $C = \operatorname{Exc}(f)$. Here $f$ has no exceptional divisor, so $f_*$ is a bijection on prime divisors and $B \geq 0$ if and only if $f_* B \geq 0$ for every $B$: the negativity lemma says nothing. Example 1.5.16 gives a Cartier divisor $\tilde D$ with $\tilde D \cdot C = -1$. The plane $D'' = V(x, w)$ has strict transform $\tilde D'' = \{ s = 0 \} = D'' \times \{[0 : 1]\}$, since over $D'' \setminus \{0\}$ the ratio is $[s : t] = [x : z] = [w : y]$ with $x = w = 0$. It is $V(v)$ on the second chart, where $C = V(z, y)$, so it meets $C$ in one point with $\dim_k k[z, y, v]/(z, y, v) = 1$, and $\tilde D'' \cdot C = 1$ by fact (b). So both signs occur on $C$, but no exceptional divisor carries them, and Corollary 1.5.24 fails: the only exceptional divisor is $F = 0$, with $F \cdot C = 0$. This is a third proof that $Y$ is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial.

**Example 1.5.27 (a chain of blow-ups).** Let $f \colon S_{n+1} \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the composite of blow-ups of Example P5.8.6, and $B = K_{S_{n+1}/\mathbb{P}^2} = C_1 + 2 C_2 + \dots + (n+1) C_{n+1}$, as computed in Example P5.8.7 and in agreement with Theorem 1.5.13. Then $B \cdot C_j = 0$ for $j \leq n$ and $B \cdot C_{n+1} = -1$, so $-B$ is $f$-nef and $f_* B = 0$, and Theorem 1.5.22 predicts $B \geq 0$, as the formula shows. The divisor $B$ is not $f$-numerically trivial, as Corollary 1.5.23 requires of a nonzero exceptional divisor. Here $\mathbb{P}^2$ is smooth, so Corollary 1.5.24 applies, and $F = B$ is not a valid choice, because $F \cdot C_j = 0$ for $j \leq n$. The divisor $F = \sum_i a_i C_i$ with $F \cdot C_j = -1$ for all $j$ solves $M a = -(1, \dots, 1)$ for the intersection matrix $M$ of the chain, and by part 1 of Lemma P5.8.1 its solution $a$ has positive entries.

### Proof

*Lemma 1.5.20.* Let $U \subseteq T$ be as in Definition 1.5.5. By part 3 of Proposition 1.5.6, $T \setminus U$ has codimension 2 in the surface $T$, so it is a finite set of closed points. Fix $t \in T \setminus U$. The fibre $g^{-1}(t)$ is connected by Theorem P4.6.8, it is contained in the proper closed subset $\operatorname{Exc}(g)$ of $S$, so it has dimension at most 1, and by part 2 of Proposition 1.5.6 each of its closed points lies on a curve contained in it. So $g^{-1}(t) = C_1 \cup \dots \cup C_r$ for integral curves $C_i$, its irreducible components. Conversely a $g$-exceptional curve maps to a point with an infinite fibre, which lies in $T \setminus U$. So the $g$-exceptional curves are the components of the fibres over the finitely many points of $T \setminus U$, and curves over distinct points are disjoint.

For distinct curves $C \neq C'$ on $S$ with $C$ proper, fact (b) applied to the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}_S(C')$, whose zero scheme on $C$ is $C \cap C'$, gives
$$C' \cdot C = \sum_{P \in C \cap C'} \dim_k \mathcal{O}_{S, P} / (f, f'),$$
with $f$ and $f'$ local equations of $C$ and $C'$ at $P$. This is symmetric when $C'$ is proper too, nonnegative, and positive exactly when $C \cap C' \neq \emptyset$. So the intersection matrix $M = (C_i \cdot C_j)$ is symmetric with nonnegative entries off the diagonal, and its graph is connected: a splitting of $\{C_1, \dots, C_r\}$ into two nonempty sets with no intersections between them would write the connected set $g^{-1}(t)$ as a disjoint union of two nonempty closed sets.

The matrix $M$ is negative definite. Since $T$ is affine of dimension 2, there is $\varphi \in \mathcal{O}(T)$ with $\varphi \neq 0$ and $\varphi(t) = 0$. The function $g^* \varphi$ is regular on $S$ and vanishes on $g^{-1}(t)$, so
$$\operatorname{div}(g^* \varphi) = \sum_{i=1}^r a_i C_i + R, \qquad a_i \geq 1,$$
with $R$ effective and without any $C_i$ in its support. By fact (b), $\operatorname{div}(g^* \varphi) \cdot C_j = 0$ and $R \cdot C_j \geq 0$ for every $j$. By Proposition P3.7.5, $V(\varphi) \subseteq T$ has an irreducible component $\Gamma_0$ of dimension 1 through $t$. It is not contained in the finite set $T \setminus U$, so its strict transform $\Gamma$, the closure of $g^{-1}(\Gamma_0 \cap U)$, is a curve on which $g^* \varphi$ vanishes, not exceptional, hence a component of $R$. Its image $g(\Gamma)$ is closed, because $g$ is proper, and contains the dense subset $\Gamma_0 \cap U$ of $\Gamma_0$, so $t \in g(\Gamma)$, $\Gamma$ meets some $C_j$, and $R \cdot C_j \geq \Gamma \cdot C_j > 0$ for this $j$. So $a = (a_1, \dots, a_r)$ has positive entries, $M a = -(R \cdot C_j)_j \leq 0$ and $M a \neq 0$, and part 3 of Lemma P5.8.1 shows that $M$ is negative definite.

Write $B = N + \sum_C e_C C$, the sum over all $g$-exceptional curves, where $N$ has no exceptional component. By hypothesis $N \geq 0$, so $N \cdot C_j \geq 0$ by fact (b). For $t$ and $C_1, \dots, C_r$ as above, let $e = (e_{C_1}, \dots, e_{C_r})$. The exceptional curves over other points do not meet $C_j$, so
$$(M e)_j = B \cdot C_j - N \cdot C_j \leq 0 \qquad (1 \leq j \leq r).$$
Part 1 of Lemma P5.8.1 gives $e \geq 0$. This holds for every $t \in T \setminus U$, so $B \geq 0$. $\square$

*Lemma 1.5.21.* The basic step is the following. Let $\phi \colon V \to \mathbb{P}^K$ be a morphism from a scheme of finite type over $k$ with irreducible components $V_1, \dots, V_r$. The hyperplanes that contain a given point of $\phi(V_i)$ form a hyperplane in the dual space, so a general hyperplane $H$ contains none of the sets $\phi(V_i)$. Then on each affine open subset of $V_i$ meeting $\phi^{-1}(H)$, with its reduced structure, a local equation of $\phi^{-1}(H)$ is neither $0$ nor a unit, and by part 1 of Proposition P3.7.5 every irreducible component of $V_i \cap \phi^{-1}(H)$ has dimension $\dim V_i - 1$. $(\ast)$

(1) By Bertini's theorem (Hartshorne, Theorem III.10.8 and Corollary III.10.9), for a smooth variety $V$ and a morphism $\phi \colon V \to \mathbb{P}^K$, the scheme $\phi^{-1}(H)$ is smooth for a general hyperplane $H$. Apply this $n - 2$ times, to each connected component of the current smooth scheme, first with $\phi$ the composite $X \to Y \subseteq \mathbb{A}^N \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ and then with the embedding in $\mathbb{P}^M$. Each step is smooth, and by $(\ast)$ the components drop in dimension by exactly one, so $S$ is smooth of pure dimension $n - (n - 2) = 2$.

(2) Applying $(\ast)$ $w$ times to $Z \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ gives the bound. Let $Z$ be irreducible of dimension $w$ and $\bar Z$ its closure in $\mathbb{P}^N$. The linear space $\bar H_1 \cap \dots \cap \bar H_w$ has dimension $N - w$, so it meets $\bar Z$ by the projective dimension theorem (Hartshorne, Theorem I.7.2), in a finite set by $(\ast)$. The boundary $\bar Z \setminus Z$ has dimension at most $w - 1$, and by $(\ast)$ it does not meet $\bar H_1 \cap \dots \cap \bar H_w$. So $Z \cap Y'$ is finite and nonempty.

(3) By $(\ast)$, applied $n - 2$ times to $V$ and the same morphisms as in (1), each cut lowers the dimension of every component by one or removes it.

(4) Let $F$ be one of the $F_i$, of dimension $d \geq 1$, and $H'$ a hyperplane of $\mathbb{P}^M$. If $F \cap H' = \emptyset$, then $F$ is a closed subvariety of $\mathbb{P}^M \setminus H' \cong \mathbb{A}^M$, closed because it is proper over $k$, and by Theorem P3.4.21 the coordinate functions are constant on the connected, reduced, proper scheme $F$, so $F$ is a point, a contradiction. So $F \cap H' \neq \emptyset$, and by $(\ast)$, for a general $H'$, all its components have dimension $d - 1$. They are again irreducible, closed and proper over $k$. Repeating this $n - 2 - w$ times is possible, since the dimension before the last cut is $d - (n - 3 - w) \geq 2$, and it gives the claim. $\square$

*Theorem 1.5.22.* If $B \geq 0$, then $h_* B \geq 0$ by definition of $h_*$. Assume $h_* B \geq 0$, and write $B = \sum_j b_j B_j$ with distinct prime divisors $B_j$. Let $n = \dim X$.

*Step 1: reduction to a smooth $X$.* By Hironaka's theorem (Hir64; Kollár-Mori, Theorem 0.2) there is a projective birational morphism $\mu \colon \tilde X \to X$ with $\tilde X$ smooth. Write $B = \sum_i r_i D_i$ with Cartier divisors $D_i$, and put $\tilde B = \sum_i r_i \mu^* D_i$, an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor on $\tilde X$. For a non-exceptional prime divisor $\tilde P$ of $\tilde X$ with image $P$, $\mu$ is an isomorphism over a dense open subset of $P$ by part 3 of Proposition 1.5.6, so the coefficient of $\tilde P$ in $\tilde B$ is that of $P$ in $B$, and $\mu_* \tilde B = B$. The morphism $h \circ \mu$ is projective (Hartshorne, Exercise II.4.9) and birational, and $(h \circ \mu)_* = h_* \circ \mu_*$, because a prime divisor contracted by $\mu$ is contracted by $h \circ \mu$. So $(h \circ \mu)_* \tilde B = h_* B \geq 0$. Let $\tilde C$ be an $(h \circ \mu)$-contracted curve. If $\mu(\tilde C)$ is a point, then $\tilde B \cdot \tilde C = 0$ by fact (a). Otherwise $C = \mu(\tilde C)$ is $h$-contracted, and $\tilde B \cdot \tilde C = \deg(\tilde C \to C)\, (B \cdot C) \leq 0$. So $-\tilde B$ is $(h \circ \mu)$-nef. If the theorem holds for $h \circ \mu$, then $\tilde B \geq 0$, and $B = \mu_* \tilde B \geq 0$. So assume $X$ smooth.

*Step 2: reduction to an affine $Y$.* The coefficient $b_j$ can be read on $h^{-1}(V)$ for an affine open $V \subseteq Y$ meeting $h(B_j)$, and $h^{-1}(V) \to V$ satisfies the hypotheses: its contracted curves are $h$-contracted curves of $X$, and the pushforward commutes with restriction to $V$. So assume $Y \subseteq \mathbb{A}^N$ affine. Then $X$ is projective over an affine variety, hence quasi-projective, and fix a locally closed embedding $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^M$.

*Step 3: choice of a component.* Suppose that some $b_j < 0$. A non-exceptional $B_j$ has the same coefficient as $h(B_j)$ in $h_* B$, by part 3 of Proposition 1.5.6, so every $B_j$ with $b_j < 0$ is exceptional. Among those, choose $E = B_{j_0}$ with $w = \dim h(E)$ maximal. Then $w \leq n - 2$. Put $W = h(E)$, closed and irreducible, and $Z_j = E \cap B_j$ for $j \neq j_0$, closed of dimension at most $n - 2$.

*Step 4: cutting.* Choose $H_1, \dots, H_w$ general as in Lemma 1.5.21, and $Y'$, $X'$ as there. By part 2 of Lemma 1.5.21, $W \cap Y'$ is finite and nonempty, and $h(B_j) \cap Y'$ is finite for every $j$ with $b_j < 0$, since $\dim h(B_j) \leq w$. Let $y \in W \cap Y'$. By part 2 of Theorem P3.7.6, applied to $E \to W$, every irreducible component of $E \cap h^{-1}(y)$ has dimension at least $n - 1 - w$, and it is proper over $k$ and contained in $X'$. Choose the $H'_i$ general with respect to these components, and let $S$ be as in Lemma 1.5.21. By part 4 of Lemma 1.5.21, $E \cap S$ has an irreducible component of dimension at least 1, and by part 3, $\dim (E \cap S) \leq 1$. So there is a curve $\Gamma \subseteq E \cap S$, proper over $k$, which is an irreducible component of $E \cap S$. By part 3, every $Z_j \cap S$ is finite, so $\Gamma \not\subseteq B_j$ for $j \neq j_0$. Let $S'$ be the connected component of $S$ that contains $\Gamma$. By part 1 of Lemma 1.5.21 it is a smooth irreducible surface, quasi-projective and closed in $X$. Every irreducible component of $\operatorname{Exc}(h)$, and every $B_j$, has dimension at most $n - 1$, so its intersection with $S$ has dimension at most 1 by part 3. So $S' \not\subseteq \operatorname{Exc}(h)$ and $S' \not\subseteq B_j$ for all $j$.

*Step 5: the surface.* The morphism $h|_{S'} \colon S' \to Y$ is projective. Its image $h(S')$ is closed and irreducible, and since $S' \cap h^{-1}(U)$ is a nonempty open subset of $S'$ mapped isomorphically onto $h(S') \cap U$ by Corollary P4.6.10, $S' \to h(S')$ is birational and $h(S')$ is a surface. Let $\nu \colon T \to h(S')$ be the normalization, which is finite by Theorem P3.7.36, so $T$ is a normal affine surface. Since $S'$ is normal and $S' \to h(S')$ is dominant, Definition P3.7.35 gives $g \colon S' \to T$ with $h|_{S'} = \nu \circ g$. It is birational, and projective because $h|_{S'}$ is projective and $\nu$ is separated (Hartshorne, Exercises II.4.8 and II.4.9). If a curve $C \subseteq S'$ is $h$-contracted, then $g(C)$ is irreducible and contained in a fibre of $\nu$, which is finite, so $C$ is $g$-exceptional; conversely a $g$-exceptional curve is $h$-contracted.

*Step 6: the contradiction.* Every $B_j$ is Cartier on the smooth variety $X$ and does not contain $S'$, so $B_j|_{S'}$ is an effective Cartier divisor on $S'$ with support $B_j \cap S'$. Put $B_S = \sum_j b_j\, B_j|_{S'}$. For a curve $C \subseteq S'$, $\mathcal{O}_X(B_j)|_C = \mathcal{O}_{S'}(B_j|_{S'})|_C$, so $B_S \cdot C = B \cdot C$, and $B_S \cdot C \leq 0$ for every $g$-exceptional curve. A component $\Gamma'$ of $B_S$ with negative coefficient lies in some $B_j$ with $b_j < 0$, so $h(\Gamma') \subseteq h(B_j) \cap Y'$, a finite set. So $h(\Gamma')$ is a point and $\Gamma'$ is $g$-exceptional. Lemma 1.5.20 gives $B_S \geq 0$. But $\Gamma$ lies in $E$ and in no other $B_j$, so its coefficient in $B_S$ is $b_{j_0} \cdot \operatorname{ord}_\Gamma (E|_{S'})$, with $\operatorname{ord}_\Gamma(E|_{S'}) \geq 1$ because $\Gamma \subseteq E \cap S'$. This is negative, a contradiction. So $B \geq 0$. $\square$

*Corollary 1.5.23.* If $B$ is exceptional and $h$-nef, apply Theorem 1.5.22 to $-B$: then $-(-B) = B$ is $h$-nef and $h_*(-B) = 0$, so $-B \geq 0$. If $B \equiv_h 0$, then both $B$ and $-B$ are exceptional and $h$-nef, so $-B \geq 0$ and $B \geq 0$, and $B = 0$. $\square$

*Corollary 1.5.24.* Since $f$ is projective, $X$ is a closed subscheme of some $\mathbb{P}^K_Y$, and $\mathcal{O}_X(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(H)$ for a Cartier divisor $H$, because $X$ is integral (Hartshorne, Proposition II.6.15). Every $f$-contracted curve $C$ lies in a fibre $\mathbb{P}^K_k$ over a closed point, and a linear form not vanishing on $C$ is a section of $\mathcal{O}_X(1)|_C$ whose zero scheme is nonempty by Hartshorne, Theorem I.7.2, so $H \cdot C > 0$ by fact (b). Since $Y$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial there is $m \geq 1$ such that $m f_* H$ is Cartier. Put $F = f^*(m f_* H) - m H$, a Cartier divisor. For a non-exceptional prime divisor $P$ of $X$, $f$ is an isomorphism over a dense open subset of $f(P)$, so the coefficients of $P$ in $f^*(m f_* H)$ and in $m H$ both equal the coefficient of $f(P)$ in $m f_* H$. So $F$ is exceptional. For an $f$-contracted curve $C$, $f^*(m f_* H) \cdot C = 0$ by fact (a), so $F \cdot C = -m H \cdot C < 0$. So $-F$ is $f$-nef and $f_* F = 0$, and Theorem 1.5.22 gives $F \geq 0$. Its components are exceptional, so $\operatorname{Supp} F \subseteq \operatorname{Exc}(f)$ by part 4 of Proposition 1.5.6. Conversely, by part 2 of Proposition 1.5.6 every closed point $x \in \operatorname{Exc}(f)$ lies on an $f$-contracted curve $C$. If $C \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp} F$, then $F \cdot C \geq 0$ by fact (b), since $F$ is effective, which is false. So $x \in C \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} F$, and $\operatorname{Exc}(f) \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} F$ because closed points are dense in $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$. $\square$

### Lean

The project library states the cone and contraction theorems with `MinimalModel.IsExtremalContraction` and `MinimalModel.IsContraction`, and the relative nefness used there is a placeholder interface. Mathlib has no intersection numbers of divisors with curves on a variety and no Bertini theorem, so none of the statements of this section is in Lean. A formal proof would follow the reduction to Lemma 1.5.20 given here, with the matrix lemma of P5 Chapter 8 as its algebraic core.

### Notes and history

The negativity lemma is Kollár-Mori, Lemma 3.39 (KM98), where it is proved for proper birational morphisms by the same reduction to surfaces, and Kollár's book on singularities of the minimal model program, Section 1.1 (Kol13), gives the version for proper morphisms that is used in Volume 4. The surface case is Mumford's 1961 theorem on the negative definiteness of the intersection matrix of a resolution (Mum61), and the proof of Lemma 1.5.20 is his. Corollary 1.5.24 is Kollár-Mori, Lemma 2.62, and it is the source of the relatively anti-ample exceptional divisor used in the construction of divisorial contractions. In Volume 4 the negativity lemma compares discrepancies: if $\phi \colon X \dashrightarrow X^+$ is a flip, then for every divisor $E$ over $X$ the discrepancy $a(E, X^+) \geq a(E, X)$, with strict inequality over the flipping locus, and this inequality, applied on a common resolution, is a direct use of Theorem 1.5.22. The literature state is theorem.
