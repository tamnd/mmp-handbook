## Purity and the relative canonical divisor

### Statement

The notation of Section 1 is in force. A small contraction, one whose exceptional locus has codimension at least 2, is the input of a flip, and the minimal model program has to know when one can occur. Van der Waerden's purity theorem says that it cannot occur over a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial target. For a smooth target the theorem has a sharper form: the exceptional locus is the zero locus of the Jacobian, the support of the relative canonical divisor.

**Theorem 1.5.12 (van der Waerden purity).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a projective birational morphism of varieties with $Y$ normal and $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial. Then every irreducible component of $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ has codimension 1 in $X$. So $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ is the union of the $f$-exceptional prime divisors, and $f$ is either an isomorphism or not small.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a birational morphism of smooth varieties of dimension $n$, and let $\omega \neq 0$ be a rational $n$-form on $Y$. Since $K(X) = K(Y)$, the pullback $f^* \omega$ is a nonzero rational $n$-form on $X$. The *relative canonical divisor* is
$$K_{X/Y} = \operatorname{div}_X(f^* \omega) - f^* \operatorname{div}_Y(\omega),$$
where $\operatorname{div}$ is the divisor of a rational form of Definition P3.12.28 and $f^*$ on the right is the pullback of the Cartier divisor $\operatorname{div}_Y(\omega)$; every divisor on the smooth variety $Y$ is Cartier by Corollary P1.9.26 and Theorem P3.9.10. Replacing $\omega$ by $a \omega$ with $a \in K(Y)^*$ adds $\operatorname{div}(f^* a) = f^* \operatorname{div}(a)$ to both terms, so $K_{X/Y}$ does not depend on $\omega$. With $K_X = \operatorname{div}(f^* \omega)$ and $K_Y = \operatorname{div}(\omega)$ it reads $K_X = f^* K_Y + K_{X/Y}$.

**Theorem 1.5.13 (the relative canonical divisor over a smooth target).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a projective birational morphism of smooth varieties. Then $K_{X/Y}$ is an effective $f$-exceptional divisor, and $\operatorname{Supp} K_{X/Y} = \operatorname{Exc}(f)$. Near a closed point $x$, with local coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$ at $x$ and $y_1, \dots, y_n$ at $f(x)$, it is the divisor of the Jacobian $\det(\partial f^* y_i / \partial x_j)$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Proposition 1.5.14 (the blow-up of a smooth centre).** Let $X$ be a smooth variety, $Z \subseteq X$ a smooth closed subvariety of codimension $c \geq 2$, and $\pi \colon \tilde X = \operatorname{Bl}_Z X \to X$ the blow-up. Then $\tilde X$ is smooth, the exceptional divisor $E = \pi^{-1}(Z)$ is a prime divisor, $\operatorname{Exc}(\pi) = E$, and
$$K_{\tilde X} = \pi^* K_X + (c - 1) E, \qquad K_{\tilde X / X} = (c - 1) E.$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

The coefficient $c - 1$ is the *discrepancy* of $E$ over $X$, the number that Volume 4 attaches to every exceptional divisor. It grows with the codimension of the centre: blowing up a point of a smooth $n$-fold gives $n - 1$, and blowing up a smooth divisor changes nothing, since then $c = 1$ and $\pi$ is an isomorphism.

### Proof in words

For purity, take a closed point $x$ of the exceptional locus and an affine neighbourhood of $x$ embedded in affine space. Not every coordinate function can be regular at $y = f(x)$, since otherwise the embedding would be constant on the fibre $X_y$ and $x$ would be isolated in it. A coordinate $g$ with a pole along a divisor through $y$ is, after taking a power, a quotient $a/b$ of local equations of its zero and polar divisors, which exist because $Y$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial. On $X$ the equation $f^* a = g^m f^* b$ with $g$ regular shows that the hypersurface $f^* b = 0$ through $x$ maps into the zeros of both $a$ and $b$, a set of codimension at least 2. So its components are exceptional divisors through $x$.

For a smooth target the Jacobian does more. Where it does not vanish, the pullbacks of the coordinates at $y$ generate the maximal ideal at $x$, the fibre is a reduced point at $x$, and $x$ is not exceptional. Where $f$ is an isomorphism the Jacobian is a unit. So the Jacobian vanishes exactly on the exceptional locus.

For the blow-up of a smooth centre $V(x_1, \dots, x_c)$ the chart $x_j = x_1 t_j$ turns $dx_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n$ into $x_1^{c-1}$ times a nowhere vanishing form, and $x_1 = 0$ is the equation of $E$.

### Examples

**Example 1.5.15 (the blow-up of a point of $\mathbb{P}^n$).** This is the worked example of the chapter. Let $n \geq 2$, $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ with coordinates $x_0, \dots, x_n$, $p = [1 : 0 : \dots : 0]$, and
$$Y = \{ ([x_0 : \dots : x_n], [u_1 : \dots : u_n]) : x_i u_j = x_j u_i \text{ for } 1 \leq i < j \leq n \} \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^{n-1},$$
with $\pi \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^n$ and $q \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ the projections. By Example 1.5.11, $\pi$ is the blow-up of $p$, an isomorphism over $\mathbb{P}^n \setminus \{p\}$, with $\operatorname{Exc}(\pi) = E = \{p\} \times \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, and $\pi$ is a contraction.

*Charts.* Put $X_j = x_j / x_0$ on $\{x_0 \neq 0\} = \mathbb{A}^n$. For $1 \leq i \leq n$ let $W_i = \{x_0 \neq 0, u_i \neq 0\} \subseteq Y$. By Theorem P3.11.4, $W_i \cong \mathbb{A}^n$ with coordinates $a^{(i)}_i = X_i$ and $a^{(i)}_j = u_j / u_i$ for $j \neq i$, and $\pi$ is given on $W_i$ by $X_i = a^{(i)}_i$ and $X_j = a^{(i)}_i a^{(i)}_j$. In $W_i$ the divisor $E$ is $V(a^{(i)}_i)$, and the ideal of $E$ is generated by $X_i = a^{(i)}_i$, so $\pi^{-1}(p) = E$ as Cartier divisors, $E \cap W_i \cong \mathbb{A}^{n-1}$, and the $W_i$ cover $\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{A}^n)$.

*The canonical divisor.* Let $\omega = dX_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dX_n$, a rational $n$-form on $\mathbb{P}^n$. On $\{x_1 \neq 0\}$, with coordinates $z_0 = x_0 / x_1$ and $z_j = x_j / x_1$ for $j \geq 2$, one has $X_1 = z_0^{-1}$ and $X_j = z_j z_0^{-1}$, so $dX_1 = -z_0^{-2} dz_0$ and $dX_j = z_0^{-1} dz_j - z_j z_0^{-2} dz_0$. The terms with $dz_0$ in $dX_j$ for $j \geq 2$ die against $dX_1$, so
$$\omega = -z_0^{-(n+1)}\, dz_0 \wedge dz_2 \wedge \dots \wedge dz_n.$$
The same computation on every chart $\{x_i \neq 0\}$ gives $\operatorname{div}(\omega) = -(n+1) H_\infty$, with $H_\infty = V_+(x_0)$, so $K_{\mathbb{P}^n} = -(n+1)H$ for a hyperplane $H$. On $W_1$, write $a_j = a^{(1)}_j$. Then $dX_1 = da_1$ and $dX_j = a_j\, da_1 + a_1\, da_j$ for $j \geq 2$, and the terms $a_j\, da_1$ die against $da_1$:
$$\pi^* \omega = a_1^{n-1}\, da_1 \wedge da_2 \wedge \dots \wedge da_n.$$
The form $da_1 \wedge \dots \wedge da_n$ generates $\omega_{W_1}$, so $\operatorname{div}(\pi^* \omega) = (n-1) E$ on $W_1$, and the same holds on each $W_i$. Since $p \notin H_\infty$ and $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $\mathbb{P}^n \setminus \{p\}$, $\operatorname{div}_Y(\pi^* \omega) = (n-1) E - (n+1) \pi^* H_\infty$. So
$$K_Y = \pi^* K_{\mathbb{P}^n} + (n - 1) E = -(n+1) \pi^* H + (n-1) E,$$
and $K_{Y / \mathbb{P}^n} = (n-1) E$, as Theorem 1.5.13 and Proposition 1.5.14 with $c = n$ predict.

*The normal bundle of $E$.* The conormal sheaf $\mathcal{I}_E / \mathcal{I}_E^2 = \mathcal{O}_Y(-E)|_E$ is an invertible sheaf on $E \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ with generator $s_i$, the class of $X_i$, on $E \cap W_i$. On $W_i \cap W_j$ the equation $x_i u_j = x_j u_i$ gives $X_j = (u_j / u_i) X_i$, so $s_j = (u_j / u_i) s_i$. The sections $u_i$ of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(1)$ satisfy the same rule $u_j = (u_j / u_i) u_i$ on $\{u_i u_j \neq 0\}$. So $s_i \mapsto u_i$ glues to an isomorphism $\mathcal{O}_Y(-E)|_E \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(1)$, and
$$\mathcal{O}_E(E) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1).$$
For a line $\ell \subseteq E$, $E \cdot \ell = \deg \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1) = -1$.

*Intersection numbers.* Intersection numbers of Cartier divisors on a projective variety are multilinear and symmetric, $(H^n) = 1$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$, top intersection numbers of pullbacks along a birational morphism are unchanged, and for a prime Cartier divisor $E$ one has $(D_1 \cdots D_{n-1} \cdot E) = (D_1|_E \cdots D_{n-1}|_E)_E$ (Lazarsfeld, Section 1.1.C; Debarre, Section 1.1). Write $H$ for $\pi^* H$. A hyperplane not through $p$ has pullback disjoint from $E$, so $H|_E$ is trivial. Hence $(H^n) = 1$, $(H^k \cdot E^{n-k}) = 0$ for $0 < k < n$, and
$$(E^n) = \big( (E|_E)^{n-1} \big)_E = \big( \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)^{n-1} \big) = (-1)^{n-1}.$$
Two checks. First, $H - E = q^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(1)$: the pullback of the hyperplane $V_+(x_1)$ is $V(a^{(i)}_i a^{(i)}_1)$ on $W_i$ for $i \neq 1$, so it is $E + V_+(u_1)$, and $V_+(u_1) = q^* V_+(u_1)$. A divisor pulled back from $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ has vanishing $n$-th power, and indeed
$$\big( (H - E)^n \big) = (H^n) + (-1)^n (E^n) = 1 + (-1)^n (-1)^{n-1} = 0.$$
Second, the anticanonical volume is
$$\big( (-K_Y)^n \big) = \big( ((n+1)H - (n-1)E)^n \big) = (n+1)^n + (-1)^n (n-1)^n (-1)^{n-1} = (n+1)^n - (n-1)^n.$$
For $n = 2$ this is $9 - 1 = 8$, the degree of the del Pezzo surface of degree 8, and for $n = 3$ it is $64 - 8 = 56$.

**Example 1.5.16 (the Atiyah small contraction).** Let $Y = V(xy - zw) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^4$, the cone over $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, normal by Corollary P1.11.14, and let $f \colon X \to Y$ be the morphism of Example P4.6.13, with $X \subseteq Y \times \mathbb{P}^1$ given by $x t = z s$ and $w t = y s$. On $s \neq 0$, with $u = t/s$, the equations say $z = xu$ and $y = wu$, so this chart is $\mathbb{A}^3$ with coordinates $(x, w, u)$ and $f(x, w, u) = (x, wu, xu, w)$. On $t \neq 0$, with $v = s/t$, the chart is $\mathbb{A}^3$ with coordinates $(z, y, v)$ and $f(z, y, v) = (zv, y, z, yv)$. So $X$ is smooth. Over a point $(x, y, z, w) \neq 0$ of $Y$ the fibre is the single point $[s : t] = [x : z]$ if $(x, z) \neq 0$ and $[w : y]$ if $(w, y) \neq 0$, consistent by $xy = zw$. Over $0$ the fibre is $C = \{0\} \times \mathbb{P}^1$. So $U = Y \setminus \{0\}$, $\operatorname{Exc}(f) = C$ is a curve in a threefold, and $f$ is small. By Theorem 1.5.12, $Y$ is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, which is Example P1.8.23 again. The plane $D = V(x, z) \subseteq Y$ shows this directly. Its strict transform $\tilde D$ is $V(x)$ in the first chart and $V(z)$ in the second, and it contains $C$. On the overlap $x = zv$, so the generators $1/x$ and $1/z$ of $\mathcal{O}_X(\tilde D)$ satisfy $1/x = u \cdot (1/z)$ with $u = t/s$, and $\mathcal{O}_X(\tilde D)|_C \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)$. If $mD$ were Cartier near $0$, its pullback would be a Cartier divisor on $X$ equal to $m \tilde D$ off $C$, hence equal to $m \tilde D$, since $C$ has codimension 2 in the smooth variety $X$. But the pullback of a Cartier divisor restricts to a trivial bundle on the contracted curve $C$, whereas $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-m)$ is not trivial. So $D$ is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier.

**Example 1.5.17 (a divisorial resolution of the same cone).** Let $Y$ be as in Example 1.5.16, and let $\sigma \colon Y' \to Y$ be the strict transform of $Y$ in the blow-up of $0 \in \mathbb{A}^4$. On the chart $x = x$, $y = x y'$, $z = x z'$, $w = x w'$ of $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^4$, given by Theorem P3.11.4, the equation pulls back to $x^2 (y' - z' w')$, so $Y'$ is $V(y' - z'w') \cong \mathbb{A}^3$ there, with coordinates $(x, z', w')$, and $Y' \cap E_{\mathbb{A}^4} = V(x)$. The other charts are the same by symmetry. So $Y'$ is smooth. The blow-up is an isomorphism off $0$, so $\operatorname{Exc}(\sigma) = \sigma^{-1}(0) = G$, where on this chart $G = V(x) \cong \mathbb{A}^2$ with coordinates $(z', w')$ is the affine piece of the quadric $\{xy = zw\}$ in the exceptional $\mathbb{P}^3$ of $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^4$. So $G \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ is a prime divisor. The cone is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, but this resolution has a divisorial exceptional locus: the converse of Theorem 1.5.12 fails.

**Example 1.5.18 (the blow-up of a line in $\mathbb{P}^3$).** Let $L = V_+(x_2, x_3) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$ and $\pi \colon \tilde X \to \mathbb{P}^3$ its blow-up. Here $c = 2$, and Proposition 1.5.14 gives $K_{\tilde X} = \pi^* K_{\mathbb{P}^3} + E = -4 \pi^* H + E$. By Theorem P3.11.4 applied on the charts $x_i \neq 0$ with $i \in \{0, 1\}$, $E = L \times \mathbb{P}^1$ locally over $L$, and every fibre of $E \to L$ is a line $\ell$. On the chart $x_2 = x_2$, $x_3 = x_2 t$ the ideal of $E$ is $(x_2)$, and the computation of the normal bundle in Example 1.5.15, done on this chart, gives $\mathcal{O}_E(E)|_\ell \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)$, while $\pi^* \mathcal{O}(H)|_\ell$ is trivial. So $K_{\tilde X}$ has degree $-1$ on $\ell$, as Exercise 6 shows in general. In Theorem 1.5.13 the exceptional locus is the divisor $E$, and $K_{\tilde X/\mathbb{P}^3} = E$ is supported on it.

### Proof

*Theorem 1.5.12.* First let $x \in \operatorname{Exc}(f)$ be a closed point and $y = f(x)$, a closed point of $Y$. Choose an affine open neighbourhood $X_0$ of $x$ and a closed embedding $X_0 \subseteq \mathbb{A}^N$ with coordinate functions $g_1, \dots, g_N \in \mathcal{O}(X_0) \subseteq K(X) = K(Y)$.

Suppose every $g_i$ lies in $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$. Then there is an affine open $V \ni y$ on which all $g_i$ are regular, and they define a morphism $\varphi \colon V \to \mathbb{A}^N$. The two morphisms $\varphi \circ f$ and the embedding from $f^{-1}(V) \cap X_0$ to $\mathbb{A}^N$ are given by the functions $f^* g_i$ and $g_i$, which are equal in $K(X)$, so they are equal on the integral scheme $f^{-1}(V) \cap X_0$. Hence the embedding is constant on $X_y \cap X_0$, with value $\varphi(y)$, and $X_y \cap X_0 = \{x\}$. So the closed point $x$ is open in $X_y$, and $\{x\}$ is an irreducible component of $X_y$, which contradicts part 1 of Proposition 1.5.6. So some $g = g_i$ is not in $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$.

There is a prime divisor $Q$ of $Y$ through $y$ with $\operatorname{ord}_Q(g) < 0$. Otherwise choose an affine open $V \ni y$ that meets no polar divisor of $g$. Then $g$ is regular at the generic point of every prime divisor of $V$, so the open set of $V$ where $g$ is regular has complement of codimension at least 2, and Theorem P3.7.33 gives $g \in \mathcal{O}(V) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$, a contradiction.

Write $\operatorname{div}(g) = A - B$ with $A, B$ effective without common components, so $Q \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} B$. By Definition P3.9.18 there is $m \geq 1$ such that $mA$ and $mB$ are Cartier. Shrinking $V$ to an affine neighbourhood of $y$, $mA = \operatorname{div}(a)$ and $mB = \operatorname{div}(b)$ on $V$ with $a, b \in \mathcal{O}(V)$. Then $\operatorname{div}(g^m b / a) = 0$ on $V$, so $g^m b/a$ is a unit on $V$, and after replacing $a$ by its product with this unit, $g^m b = a$. Since $y \in Q \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} B$, $b(y) = 0$.

Let $X_1 = f^{-1}(V) \cap X_0$, an affine open neighbourhood of $x$, on which $g$ is regular. On $X_1$, $f^* a = g^m f^* b$, and $f^* b$ is neither $0$, since $f$ is dominant, nor a unit at $x$, since $b(y) = 0$. By part 1 of Proposition P3.7.5 every irreducible component of $V(f^* b) \subseteq X_1$ has dimension $n - 1$, and one of them, $P_1$, passes through $x$. On $P_1$, $f^* b = 0$ and hence $f^* a = 0$, so $f(P_1) \subseteq V(a) \cap V(b) = \operatorname{Supp} A \cap \operatorname{Supp} B \cap V$. Since $A$ and $B$ have no common components, every irreducible component of $\operatorname{Supp} A \cap \operatorname{Supp} B$ is a proper closed subset of a prime divisor and has dimension at most $n - 2$. So the closure $P$ of $P_1$ is a prime divisor of $X$ through $x$ with $\dim f(P) \leq n - 2$, exceptional, and $P \subseteq \operatorname{Exc}(f)$ by part 4 of Proposition 1.5.6.

So every closed point of $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ lies on an exceptional prime divisor. There are finitely many of them, contained in $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$, by part 4 of Proposition 1.5.6. Their union is a closed subset of $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ that contains every closed point of it, and closed points are dense in every closed subset of a variety, so the union is $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$. Its irreducible components are among the exceptional prime divisors. If $f$ is not an isomorphism, then $\operatorname{Exc}(f) \neq \emptyset$ by part 1 of Proposition 1.5.6 and Corollary P4.6.10, so $f$ is not small. $\square$

*Theorem 1.5.13.* Let $x \in X$ be a closed point and $y = f(x)$. Since $X$ and $Y$ are smooth, there are local coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathfrak{m}_x$ and $y_1, \dots, y_n \in \mathfrak{m}_y$, regular on neighbourhoods $X_0 \ni x$ and $V \ni y$ with $f(X_0) \subseteq V$, such that $dx_1, \dots, dx_n$ is a basis of $\Omega_{X_0}$ and $dy_1, \dots, dy_n$ is a basis of $\Omega_V$ (Proposition P1.12.9 and Definition P3.12.28). Take $\omega = dy_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dy_n$, so that $\operatorname{div}_Y(\omega) = 0$ on $V$. Write $d(f^* y_i) = \sum_j J_{ij}\, dx_j$ with $J_{ij} \in \mathcal{O}(X_0)$ and $J = \det(J_{ij})$. Then $f^* \omega = J\, dx_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n$, and $K_{X/Y} = \operatorname{div}(J)$ on $X_0$, an effective divisor because $J$ is regular. So $K_{X/Y} \geq 0$.

If $x \notin \operatorname{Exc}(f)$, then $f$ is an isomorphism near $x$ by part 1 of Proposition 1.5.6, so $f^* y_1, \dots, f^* y_n$ are local coordinates at $x$, the matrix $(J_{ij})$ is invertible near $x$, and $x \notin \operatorname{Supp} K_{X/Y}$. Conversely, suppose $J(x) \neq 0$. Under the isomorphism $\mathfrak{m}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x^2 \cong \Omega_{X, x} \otimes \kappa(x)$ of Proposition P1.12.9, the class of $f^* y_i \in \mathfrak{m}_x$ maps to $\sum_j J_{ij}(x)\, dx_j(x)$. These $n$ vectors are linearly independent, so the $f^* y_i$ span $\mathfrak{m}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x^2$, and by Nakayama's lemma they generate $\mathfrak{m}_x$. So $\mathfrak{m}_y \mathcal{O}_{X, x} = \mathfrak{m}_x$, and the local ring of the fibre $X_y$ at $x$ is $\mathcal{O}_{X, x} / \mathfrak{m}_y \mathcal{O}_{X, x} = k$, of dimension 0. So $x$ lies on no irreducible component of $X_y$ of positive dimension, $\{x\}$ is an irreducible component of $X_y$, and $x \notin \operatorname{Exc}(f)$ by part 1 of Proposition 1.5.6. Hence the closed points of $\operatorname{Supp} K_{X/Y}$ and of $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ coincide, and the two closed sets are equal. Every component of $K_{X/Y}$ is a prime divisor contained in $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$, hence exceptional by part 4 of Proposition 1.5.6. $\square$

*Proposition 1.5.14.* By Hartshorne, Theorem II.8.24, $\tilde X$ is smooth and $E$ is a prime divisor, a projective bundle over $Z$ with fibres $\mathbb{P}^{c-1}$. By Theorem P3.11.3, $\pi$ is projective, birational and an isomorphism over $X \setminus Z$, and $E = \pi^{-1}(Z)$ has $\dim \pi(E) = \dim Z < \dim E$, so $\operatorname{Exc}(\pi) = E$ by part 4 of Proposition 1.5.6. The formula is local over $X$, and both sides vanish over $X \setminus Z$. Let $z \in Z$ be a closed point. By Theorem P3.12.14 and Proposition P1.12.9 there is an affine open neighbourhood $X_0 = \operatorname{Spec} A$ of $z$ and $x_1, \dots, x_n \in A$ with $Z \cap X_0 = V(x_1, \dots, x_c)$ and $dx_1, \dots, dx_n$ a basis of $\Omega_A$. The blow-up of $X_0$ along $I = (x_1, \dots, x_c)$ is covered by the charts $\operatorname{Spec} A_i'$, where $A_1' = A[x_2/x_1, \dots, x_c/x_1] \subseteq K(X)$ and similarly for $i \leq c$, and on $\operatorname{Spec} A_1'$ the ideal $I A_1' = x_1 A_1'$ defines $E$, so $E = \operatorname{div}(x_1)$ there. Put $t_j = x_j / x_1$ for $2 \leq j \leq c$. The $k$-algebra $A_1'$ is generated by $A$ and the $t_j$, so $\Omega_{A_1'}$ is generated by $dA$ and the $dt_j$, hence by $dx_1, \dots, dx_n, dt_2, \dots, dt_c$. Since $x_j = x_1 t_j$,
$$dx_j = t_j\, dx_1 + x_1\, dt_j \quad (2 \leq j \leq c),$$
so $\Omega_{A_1'}$ is generated by the $n$ elements $dx_1, dt_2, \dots, dt_c, dx_{c+1}, \dots, dx_n$. It is locally free of rank $n$, because $\operatorname{Spec} A_1'$ is smooth of dimension $n$, and a surjection from a free module of rank $n$ onto a locally free module of rank $n$ is an isomorphism. So $\eta = dx_1 \wedge dt_2 \wedge \dots \wedge dt_c \wedge dx_{c+1} \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n$ generates $\omega_{A_1'}$. Let $\omega = dx_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n$, with $\operatorname{div}_X(\omega) = 0$ on $X_0$. Substituting and using $dx_1 \wedge dx_1 = 0$,
$$\pi^* \omega = x_1^{c-1}\, \eta,$$
so $\operatorname{div}(\pi^* \omega) = (c-1) E$ on $\operatorname{Spec} A_1'$, and by symmetry on every chart. So $K_{\tilde X} - \pi^* K_X = \operatorname{div}(\pi^* \omega) - \pi^* \operatorname{div}(\omega) = (c - 1)E$ over $X_0$. The divisor $K_{\tilde X/X}$ is supported on the prime divisor $E$ by Theorem 1.5.13, and its coefficient along $E$ has been computed on an open set meeting $E$, so $K_{\tilde X / X} = (c-1) E$. $\square$

Show by Theorem 1.5.12 that $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$ is a union of curves, and that $K_X \cdot C = K_{X/Y} \cdot C$ for every exceptional curve $C$. For the composite of two blow-ups in Example P5.8.7 with $n = 1$, check that $K_{X/Y} = C_1 + 2 C_2$ is supported on $\operatorname{Exc}(f)$.
5. (B) Let $Y = V(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3$ be the quadric cone, which is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial by Example P1.8.22, and $\sigma \colon Y' \to Y$ the blow-up of the vertex. Show that $\operatorname{Exc}(\sigma)$ is a curve, so that it has codimension 1 as Theorem 1.5.12 requires, and that $\mathcal{O}(2D)$ is principal near the vertex for the line $D = V(x, z)$.
6. (B) Let $Z \subseteq X$ be a smooth subvariety of codimension $c$ in a smooth variety, $\pi$ the blow-up of $Z$, and $\ell$ a line in a fibre $\mathbb{P}^{c-1}$ of $E \to Z$. Show that $E \cdot \ell = -1$, $\pi^* D \cdot \ell = 0$ for every divisor $D$ on $X$, and $K_{\tilde X} \cdot \ell = 1 - c$.

### Lean

Mathlib has Kähler differentials (`KaehlerDifferential`) and smooth morphisms of schemes, but no canonical divisor of a variety and no blow-up; the handbook's `MinimalModel.CanonicalDivisor` of Definition P3.12.28 is a placeholder interface. Purity, the Jacobian criterion for the exceptional locus and the blow-up formula are not in Lean.

### Notes and history

The purity theorem is due to van der Waerden (1927) for a smooth target, and the $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial form is Kollár-Mori, Corollary 2.63 (KM98) and Debarre, Proposition 1.40 (Deb01). The proof here is the classical one, with the $\mathbb{Q}$-factoriality used through local equations of $mA$ and $mB$. Zariski's version for a normal target says only that the exceptional locus has positive-dimensional fibres, and Example 1.5.16 shows that nothing more holds without $\mathbb{Q}$-factoriality. Atiyah found the contraction of Example 1.5.16 in 1958 in the study of families of surfaces: the two small resolutions $X$ and $X'$ of the cone, obtained from the two rulings of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, are not isomorphic over $Y$, and the birational map between them is the simplest flop. The blow-up formula of Proposition 1.5.14 is Hartshorne, Exercise II.8.5, where the Picard group of the blow-up is also computed. The literature state is theorem.
