## The Iitaka dimension and the growth of sections

### Statement

Throughout the chapter $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic 0, $X$ is a projective variety over $k$ of dimension $n$, that is an integral projective scheme, and $D$ is a Cartier divisor on $X$. Write $h^0(mD) = \dim_k H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))$. Since $X$ is integral, the product of two nonzero sections is nonzero, and a nonzero section $s$ of $\mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ and a nonzero section $t$ of $\mathcal{O}_X(m'D)$ have a ratio $s/t$ in the function field $k(X)$ when $m = m'$.

**Definition 1.4.1 (the semigroup, the exponent and the maps).** The *semigroup* of $D$ is $N(D) = \{m \ge 1 : h^0(mD) \ne 0\}$. If it is not empty, the *exponent* $e(D)$ is the greatest common divisor of its elements. For $m \in N(D)$ choose a basis $s_0, \dots, s_r$ of $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))$, with $s_0 \ne 0$. The rational map

$$\varphi_m = [s_0 : \dots : s_r] \colon X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^r$$

is defined on the open set where some $s_i$ does not vanish, and $Y_m \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r$ is the closure of its image, a projective variety. The field $k(\varphi_m) \subseteq k(X)$ is the subfield generated over $k$ by the ratios $s_i/s_0$. It is the set of the ratios $s/t$ of two sections of $\mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ with $t \ne 0$, together with the ratios of polynomials in them, and it does not depend on the basis.

The map $\varphi_m$ is dominant onto $Y_m$, and $\varphi_m^*$ identifies the function field $k(Y_m)$, generated by the ratios $y_i/y_0$ of the coordinates, with $k(\varphi_m)$. Another basis changes $\varphi_m$ by a linear automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^r$.

**Definition 1.4.2 (Iitaka dimension).** The *Iitaka dimension* of $D$ is

$$\kappa(D) = \kappa(X, D) = \max_{m \in N(D)} \dim Y_m,$$

and $\kappa(D) = -\infty$ if $N(D)$ is empty. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D$ on a normal $X$, $\kappa(D) = \kappa(aD)$ for any $a \ge 1$ with $aD$ Cartier; Corollary 1.4.7 shows that this does not depend on $a$. A Cartier divisor is *big* if $\kappa(D) = n$.

Lean: `MinimalModel.iitakaDim` and `MinimalModel.IsBig`, in the project library.

This is the definition of Definition 1.1.26, made precise, and on smooth curves and surfaces Corollary 1.4.7 shows that it agrees with Definition P5.11.1.

**Lemma 1.4.3 (the semigroup).** If $N(D)$ is not empty, then it is closed under addition, it is contained in $e(D)\mathbb{Z}$, and it contains $m \, e(D)$ for every large $m$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Proposition 1.4.4 (the stable field).** Let $N(D)$ be nonempty.

1. For $m \in N(D)$, $\dim Y_m$ is the transcendence degree of $k(\varphi_m)$ over $k$.
2. For $m, a \in N(D)$, $k(\varphi_m) \subseteq k(\varphi_{m + a})$.
3. The union $K_\infty = \bigcup_{m \in N(D)} k(\varphi_m)$ is a subfield of $k(X)$, finitely generated over $k$. There is $m_1 \in N(D)$ with $k(\varphi_{m_1}) = K_\infty$, and $k(\varphi_m) = K_\infty$ for every large $m \in N(D)$.
4. $\kappa(D)$ is the transcendence degree of $K_\infty$ over $k$, and $\kappa(D) = \dim Y_m$ for every large $m \in N(D)$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Lemma 1.4.5 (a polynomial bound).** Let $Z$ be a projective scheme over $k$ of dimension $d$, $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent sheaf on $Z$, and $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{M}$ invertible sheaves on $Z$. Then there is a constant $C$ with $h^0(Z, \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^m \otimes \mathcal{M}) \le C m^d$ for every $m \ge 1$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Theorem 1.4.6 (growth of sections).** Let $\kappa = \kappa(D) \ge 0$. There are constants $a, b > 0$ with

$$h^0(mD) \le b \, m^\kappa \quad \text{for all } m \ge 1, \qquad h^0(mD) \ge a \, m^\kappa \quad \text{for all large } m \in N(D).$$

So $\kappa(D)$ is the only integer $j$ for which $h^0(mD)/m^j$ is bounded above for all $m$ and bounded below by a positive constant for infinitely many $m$.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

**Corollary 1.4.7 (first properties).** Let $D$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$.

1. $\kappa(aD) = \kappa(D)$ for every $a \ge 1$, and $\kappa(D) = \kappa(D')$ if $D \sim D'$.
2. $\kappa(D + F) \ge \kappa(D)$ for every effective Cartier divisor $F$.
3. $\kappa(D) \in \{-\infty, 0, 1, \dots, n\}$, and $\kappa(D) = 0$ if and only if $N(D)$ is not empty and $h^0(mD) \le 1$ for every $m \ge 1$.
4. If $aD \sim A + F$ for some $a \ge 1$, an ample $A$ and an effective $F$, then $D$ is big.
5. If $X$ is a smooth curve or surface, $\kappa(D)$ is the Iitaka dimension of Definition P5.11.1.

Lean: not yet in Lean.

The converse of part 4 holds: a big divisor has a multiple of the form ample plus effective. It is proved in Section 3 as part of Theorem 1.4.24.

### Proof in words

A section of $mD$ and a section of $m'D$ multiply to a section of $(m + m')D$, so the multiples with sections form a semigroup, and the ratios of sections of $mD$ survive into $(m + a)D$ after multiplication by a fixed section of $aD$. So the fields $k(\varphi_m)$ increase along the semigroup. They live in the finitely generated field $k(X)$, so they stop growing, at a field $K_\infty$ of transcendence degree $\kappa$. The lower bound for the growth comes from one map $\varphi_{m_0}$ with image of dimension $\kappa$: the polynomials of degree $j$ in the sections of $m_0D$ that do not vanish on the image give at least as many sections of $jm_0D$ as the Hilbert function of $Y_{m_0}$, which grows like $j^\kappa$. The upper bound uses that every section of $mD$ is determined by its restriction to a subvariety $W$ of dimension $\kappa$, chosen so that it maps onto the image: two sections with the same restriction differ by a section whose ratio with a fixed section is a function pulled back from the image, and such a function cannot vanish on $W$ without vanishing everywhere. On $W$ the sections of a line bundle grow at most like $m^{\dim W}$ by the Hilbert polynomial.

### Examples

**Example 1.4.8 (curves).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ and $D$ a divisor of degree $d$. If $d < 0$, then $h^0(mD) = 0$ for $m \ge 1$, and $\kappa(D) = -\infty$. If $d > 0$, then $h^0(mD) = md + 1 - g$ for $md > 2g - 2$ by Corollary P5.1.11, so $N(D)$ contains every large $m$, $e(D) = 1$ and $\kappa(D) = 1$. If $d = 0$, then $h^0(mD)$ is $1$ when $mD \sim 0$ and $0$ otherwise, since a nonzero section of a degree 0 line bundle has no zeros. So $\kappa(D) = 0$ if the class of $D$ is torsion, of order $r$ say, with $N(D) = r\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ and $e(D) = r$, and $\kappa(D) = -\infty$ if the class has infinite order. On an elliptic curve with origin $O$, $D = p - O$ for a point $p$ of order $r$ is of the first kind, and Example P5.10.26 gives points of infinite order.

**Example 1.4.9 (the quadric).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $D$ of bidegree $(a, b)$, so $\mathcal{O}_X(D) = \mathcal{O}(a) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}(b)$. By Lemma P5.11.4, $h^0(mD) = h^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(ma)) \, h^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(mb))$, which is $(ma + 1)(mb + 1)$ when $a, b \ge 0$ and $0$ when $a < 0$ or $b < 0$. So $\kappa(D) = 2$ if $a, b > 0$, $\kappa(D) = 1$ if exactly one of $a, b$ is $0$ and the other is positive, $\kappa(0) = 0$, and $\kappa(D) = -\infty$ if $a < 0$ or $b < 0$. For $(a, b) = (1, 0)$ the map $\varphi_m$ is the first projection followed by the Veronese embedding of degree $m$, and $Y_m$ is a rational normal curve.

**Example 1.4.10 (the plane blown up at a point).** Let $\pi \colon X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2 \to \mathbb{P}^2$, with exceptional curve $E$ and $\ell$ the pullback of a line. By Corollary P5.6.5, part 3, $h^0(mE) = h^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}) = 1$ for $m \ge 0$, so $\kappa(E) = 0$, although $E$ is a nonzero effective divisor. By part 2 of the same corollary, $H^0(X, m(\ell - E))$ is the space of plane curves of degree $m$ with multiplicity at least $m$ at $p$. In coordinates with $p = [0 : 0 : 1]$ these are the binary forms of degree $m$ in $x, y$, so $h^0(m(\ell - E)) = m + 1$ and $\kappa(\ell - E) = 1$. The map $\varphi_1$ is the projection from $p$, a morphism $X \to \mathbb{P}^1$, and $Y_m$ is again a rational normal curve. Finally $\ell$ is the pullback of an ample divisor by a birational morphism, $h^0(m\ell) = \binom{m+2}{2}$ and $\kappa(\ell) = 2$.

**Example 1.4.11 (the lower bound only holds on the semigroup).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times E$ with $E$ an elliptic curve, and $D = \mathcal{O}(1) \boxtimes T$ with $T$ of degree 0 and order 2. By Lemma P5.11.4, $h^0(mD) = (m + 1) \, h^0(E, T^m)$, which is $m + 1$ for $m$ even and $0$ for $m$ odd. So $N(D) = 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, $e(D) = 2$ and $\kappa(D) = 1$. The upper bound of Theorem 1.4.6 holds with $b = 2$, but no bound $h^0(mD) \ge a m$ holds for all large $m$. This is why the theorem restricts the lower bound to $N(D)$.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 1.4.3.* If $s \in H^0(mD)$ and $t \in H^0(m'D)$ are nonzero, then $st \in H^0((m + m')D)$ is nonzero, since $X$ is integral. So $N = N(D)$ is closed under addition, and $N \subseteq e\mathbb{Z}$ with $e = e(D)$ by definition. Some finite subset $n_1, \dots, n_t$ of $N$ has greatest common divisor $e$, and by Bezout $e = \sum_i c_i n_i$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$. Let $P$ be the sum of the terms with $c_i > 0$ and $Q$ minus the sum of the terms with $c_i < 0$, so $e = P - Q$, and $P, Q$ are in $N \cup \{0\}$. If $Q = 0$, then $e \in N$ and every multiple of $e$ lies in $N$. Otherwise write $q = Q/e \ge 1$. Every integer $M \ge q(q - 1)$ can be written $M = \alpha q + \beta$ with $0 \le \beta < q$ and $\alpha \ge q - 1 \ge \beta$. Then

$$M e = \alpha Q + \beta (P - Q) = (\alpha - \beta) Q + \beta P,$$

a nonnegative combination of $P$ and $Q$, which lies in $N$ when $M \ge 1$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 1.4.4.* (1) The map $\varphi_m \colon X \dashrightarrow Y_m$ is dominant, so $\varphi_m^* \colon k(Y_m) \to k(X)$ is injective with image the field generated by the pullbacks $s_i/s_0$ of $y_i/y_0$, which is $k(\varphi_m)$. The dimension of the variety $Y_m$ is the transcendence degree of its function field (P3.7.4).

(2) Fix a nonzero $t \in H^0(aD)$. For sections $s, s'$ of $mD$ with $s' \ne 0$, $s/s' = ts/ts'$, a ratio of two sections of $(m + a)D$. So the generators of $k(\varphi_m)$ lie in $k(\varphi_{m + a})$.

(3) For $m, m' \in N$, part 2 puts $k(\varphi_m)$ and $k(\varphi_{m'})$ in $k(\varphi_{m + m'})$. So the family of subfields is directed, and its union $K = K_\infty$ is a field. It has finite transcendence degree $\kappa' \le n$, since it lies in $k(X)$. Choose a transcendence basis $t_1, \dots, t_{\kappa'}$ of $K$ over $k$ and extend it by $u_1, \dots, u_{n - \kappa'} \in k(X)$ to a transcendence basis of $k(X)$ over $k$. Then $k(X)$ is finite over $k(t, u)$, of degree $\delta$ say. The elements $u$ are algebraically independent over $K$. Let $c_1, \dots, c_p \in K$ be linearly independent over $k(t)$. If $\sum_i c_i g_i = 0$ with $g_i \in k(t, u)$, clear denominators so that each $g_i$ is a polynomial in $u$ with coefficients in $k(t)$. The coefficient of each monomial in $u$ gives $\sum_i c_i g_{i, \alpha} = 0$ with $g_{i, \alpha} \in k(t)$, because a polynomial in $u$ with coefficients in $K$ vanishes only if its coefficients do. So all $g_{i, \alpha} = 0$, and the $c_i$ stay linearly independent over $k(t, u)$. Hence $p \le \delta$, and $[K : k(t)] \le \delta$. So $K$ is finite over $k(t)$ and finitely generated over $k$. Each of finitely many generators lies in some $k(\varphi_{m})$, and by directedness all of them lie in $k(\varphi_{m_1})$ for one $m_1 \in N$. Then $k(\varphi_{m_1}) = K$. For $m \in N$ with $m - m_1 \in N$, part 2 gives $K = k(\varphi_{m_1}) \subseteq k(\varphi_m) \subseteq K$. By Lemma 1.4.3 every large $m \in N$ has this property, since $m - m_1$ is then a large multiple of $e$.

(4) Each $k(\varphi_m)$ lies in $K$, and equality holds for large $m \in N$. By part 1, $\dim Y_m \le \operatorname{trdeg}_k K$ with equality for large $m \in N$. $\square$

*Proof of Lemma 1.4.5.* Fix a very ample $\mathcal{O}_Z(1)$. By Serre's theorem P3.6.23 there is $c \ge 1$ with $\mathcal{L}^{-1}(c)$ and $\mathcal{M}^{-1}(c)$ globally generated. The sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ has finitely many associated points (Eis95, Theorem 3.1). For each of them the sections of $\mathcal{L}^{-1}(c)$ vanishing there form a proper subspace, because the sheaf is globally generated, and a vector space over the infinite field $k$ is not a finite union of proper subspaces. So there is a section $t$ of $\mathcal{L}^{-1}(c)$ that vanishes at no associated point of $\mathcal{F}$, and likewise a section $u$ of $\mathcal{M}^{-1}(c)$. Locally $t$ is a unit times a function outside every associated prime of $\mathcal{F}$, so multiplication by $t$ is injective on $\mathcal{F}$, and so is multiplication by $u$. Hence

$$\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^m \otimes \mathcal{M} \xrightarrow{\; t^m u \;} \mathcal{F}(c(m + 1))$$

is injective, and $h^0(\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^m \otimes \mathcal{M}) \le h^0(\mathcal{F}(c(m + 1)))$. By Theorem P4.3.4 the right side equals the Hilbert polynomial $P_{\mathcal{F}}(c(m + 1))$ for large $m$, of degree $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} \le d$. So the left side is at most $C' m^d$ for large $m$, and a larger constant $C$ covers the finitely many small $m$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 1.4.6.* Write $N = N(D)$, $e = e(D)$ and $\kappa = \kappa(D) \ge 0$.

*Lower bound.* Choose $m_0 \in N$ with $\dim Y_{m_0} = \kappa$ and a basis $s_0, \dots, s_r$ of $H^0(m_0D)$. Let $S = k[y_0, \dots, y_r]$. The map $S_j \to H^0(jm_0D)$, $P \mapsto P(s_0, \dots, s_r)$, has as kernel the forms of degree $j$ that vanish on $Y_{m_0}$: on the open set where $\varphi_{m_0}$ is defined, $P(s)$ vanishes at a point $x$ exactly when $P$ vanishes at $\varphi_{m_0}(x)$, and a section that vanishes on a dense open set is zero. So $h^0(jm_0 D)$ is at least the Hilbert function of $Y_{m_0}$ in degree $j$. By Theorem P4.3.4 and Definition P3.13.12 this is a polynomial of degree $\dim Y_{m_0} = \kappa$ with positive leading coefficient for large $j$. So there are $a_0 > 0$ and $j_0$ with $h^0(jm_0D) \ge a_0 j^\kappa$ for $j \ge j_0$.

The integer $m_0$ is a multiple of $e$. By Lemma 1.4.3, for each $\rho \in \{0, e, 2e, \dots, m_0 - e\}$ there is $r_\rho \in N$ with $r_\rho \equiv \rho \pmod{m_0}$; put $R = \max_\rho r_\rho$. Let $m \in N$ with $m \ge R + j_0 m_0$. Then $m \equiv \rho \pmod{m_0}$ for some $\rho$ as above, and $m = r_\rho + j m_0$ with $j \ge j_0$. Multiplication by a nonzero section of $r_\rho D$ embeds $H^0(jm_0D)$ in $H^0(mD)$, so $h^0(mD) \ge a_0 j^\kappa$. For $m \ge 2R$, $j \ge (m - R)/m_0 \ge m/(2m_0)$, and $h^0(mD) \ge a_0 (2m_0)^{-\kappa} m^\kappa$.

*Upper bound.* For $m \notin N$ there is nothing to prove. Take $m_1$ as in Proposition 1.4.4, part 3, so $k(\varphi_{m_1}) = K_\infty$, and fix a nonzero $s_0 \in H^0(m_1D)$. Let $U$ be the open set where $\varphi_{m_1}$ is defined, $\Gamma \subseteq X \times Y_{m_1}$ the closure of the graph of $\varphi_{m_1}|_U$, and $X_\infty$ the normalization of $\Gamma$ (Theorem P3.7.36). Then $X_\infty$ is a normal projective variety, the projection gives a birational morphism $\mu \colon X_\infty \to X$, and the other projection gives a surjective morphism $f_0 \colon X_\infty \to Y_{m_1}$, surjective because it is proper with dense image. Let $Y_\infty \to Y_{m_1}$ be the normalization. Since $X_\infty$ is normal and $f_0$ is dominant, $f_0$ factors through the normalization (Har77, Exercise II.3.8), as a surjective morphism $f \colon X_\infty \to Y_\infty$, and $f^* k(Y_\infty) = \mu^{-1} K_\infty$ inside $k(X_\infty) = k(X)$. Identify $k(X_\infty)$ with $k(X)$ by $\mu$.

Step 1: a subvariety. Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $Y_\infty$ and $X_\eta$ the generic fibre of $f$, of dimension $n - \kappa$. The effective Cartier divisor $\operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0)$ does not contain the generic point of $X_\infty$, so it meets $X_\eta$ in a proper closed subset. If $\kappa = n$, then $X_\eta$ is the generic point of $X_\infty$; put $W = X_\infty$. Otherwise choose a closed point $w$ of $X_\eta$ outside $\operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0)$, and let $W$ be its closure in $X_\infty$. Its function field $k(w)$ is finite over $k(\eta)$, so $W$ is a projective variety of dimension $\kappa$, it maps onto $Y_\infty$, and $W \not\subseteq \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0)$.

Step 2: the zeros of a section. Let $m \in N$ and $s \in H^0(mD)$ nonzero. The function $g = s^{m_1}/s_0^m$ is a ratio of two sections of $m m_1 D$, and $m m_1 \in N$, so $g \in K_\infty$ and $g = f^* h$ with $h \in k(Y_\infty)^*$. As Weil divisors on the normal variety $X_\infty$,

$$m_1 \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s) = m \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0) + \operatorname{div}(f^* h).$$

On the normal variety $Y_\infty$, $h$ and $h^{-1}$ are regular outside $Z = \operatorname{Supp} \operatorname{div}(h)$, by P3.8.4, so $f^*h$ is a unit outside $f^{-1}(Z)$. Hence $\operatorname{Supp} \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s) \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0) \cup f^{-1}(Z)$. Neither closed set contains $W$: the first by Step 1, the second because $W$ maps onto $Y_\infty$ and $Z$ is a proper closed subset. Since $W$ is irreducible, $\mu^* s$ does not vanish identically on $W$.

Step 3: the bound. By Step 2 the linear map $H^0(X, mD) \to H^0(W, \mu^* \mathcal{O}_X(mD)|_W)$, $s \mapsto \mu^* s|_W$, is injective for $m \in N$. Lemma 1.4.5 with $Z = W$, $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}_W$, $\mathcal{L} = \mu^* \mathcal{O}_X(D)|_W$ and $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{O}_W$ gives $h^0(mD) \le b \, m^\kappa$ for all $m \ge 1$.

The last sentence of the theorem follows: for $j > \kappa$ the lower bound makes $h^0(mD)/m^j$ go to infinity along $N$, and for $j < \kappa$ the upper bound makes it go to $0$. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 1.4.7.* (1) If $N(D)$ is empty, so is $N(aD)$. Otherwise $N(aD) = \{m : am \in N(D)\}$ is not empty. By Theorem 1.4.6 for $D$, $h^0(m \cdot aD) \le b a^\kappa m^\kappa$ for all $m$ and $h^0(m \cdot aD) \ge a' a^\kappa m^\kappa$ for all large $m \in N(aD)$, with $\kappa = \kappa(D)$. By the last sentence of Theorem 1.4.6 for $aD$, $\kappa(aD) = \kappa$. A linear equivalence $D \sim D'$ gives isomorphisms $H^0(mD) \cong H^0(mD')$ by multiplication with a rational function, which do not change the ratios, so the fields $k(\varphi_m)$ are the same.

(2) Let $s_F$ be the section of $\mathcal{O}_X(F)$ with divisor $F$. Multiplication by $s_F^m$ embeds $H^0(mD)$ in $H^0(m(D + F))$, so $N(D) \subseteq N(D + F)$ and $h^0(m(D + F)) \ge h^0(mD)$. If $\kappa(D) \ge 0$, the lower bound for $D$ and the upper bound for $D + F$ give $a m^{\kappa(D)} \le b \, m^{\kappa(D + F)}$ for infinitely many $m$, so $\kappa(D + F) \ge \kappa(D)$.

(3) Each $Y_m$ is the image of a variety of dimension $n$, so $\dim Y_m \le n$. The image $Y_m$ is a point if and only if all sections of $mD$ are proportional, that is $h^0(mD) = 1$.

(4) By part 1, $\kappa(D) = \kappa(aD) = \kappa(A + F)$, and by part 2 this is at least $\kappa(A)$. Some multiple $cA$ is very ample (Theorem P3.6.25), so $\varphi_c$ is a closed embedding of $X$, $Y_c \cong X$ has dimension $n$, and $\kappa(A) = n$.

(5) By Theorem 1.4.6, $h^0(mD) \le b m^\kappa$ for all $m$, and no bound $h^0(mD) \le c \, m^j$ with $j < \kappa$ holds, because of the lower bound. So $\kappa$ is the least $j$ of Definition P5.11.1. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $D$ a hyperplane. Show that $h^0(mD) = \binom{m + n}{n}$, and find the constants $a$ and $b$ of Theorem 1.4.6 for $m \ge 1$. Show that $\kappa(-D) = -\infty$.
2. (A) Let $D = p$ be a point on an elliptic curve. Show that $Y_1$ is a point, that $Y_2 = \mathbb{P}^1$, and that $\varphi_3$ is a closed embedding. So $\dim Y_m < \kappa(D)$ can happen for small $m \in N(D)$.
3. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times E$ and $D = \mathcal{O}(1) \boxtimes T$ with $T$ of degree 0 and order 3. Compute $N(D)$, $e(D)$ and $\kappa(D)$, and the integer $m_1$ of Proposition 1.4.4.
4. (B) Let $D_1, D_2$ be Cartier divisors with $\kappa(D_1), \kappa(D_2) \ge 0$. Show that $\kappa(D_1 + D_2) \ge \max(\kappa(D_1), \kappa(D_2))$. Give an example on $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ where $\kappa(D_1) = \kappa(D_2) = 1$ and $\kappa(D_1 + D_2) = 2$.
5. (B) Let $g \colon X' \to X$ be a birational morphism of projective varieties with $X$ normal. Use Theorem P4.6.8 and the projection formula P4.4.10 to show that $h^0(X', m g^*D) = h^0(X, mD)$ for all $m$, so $\kappa(X', g^*D) = \kappa(X, D)$. Check this for $\operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ and $D$ a line.
6. (C) Let $R(D) = \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} H^0(X, mD)$, a graded domain. Show that the field of degree 0 fractions $s/t$ of homogeneous elements of the same degree is $K_\infty$, and that the fraction field of $R(D)$ has transcendence degree $\kappa(D) + 1$ over $k$ when $\kappa(D) \ge 0$.

### Lean

`MinimalModel.iitakaDim` defines the Iitaka dimension of an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $D$ on a normal variety as $\bot$ when no $\lfloor mD \rfloor$ with $m \ge 1$ has a nonzero section, and otherwise as the largest $\kappa$ such that $h^0(\lfloor mD \rfloor) \ge c \, m^\kappa$ for some $c > 0$ and infinitely many $m$. For a Cartier divisor this is the characterization in the last sentence of Theorem 1.4.6, so the theorem says that the Lean definition agrees with Definition 1.4.2. The library defines `MinimalModel.IsBig` as ample plus effective up to $\mathbb{R}$-linear equivalence, which is the condition of Corollary 1.4.7, part 4. The theorem itself, the semigroup lemma and the stable field are not yet in Lean.

### Notes and history

Iitaka introduced the $D$-dimension in 1971, for line bundles on compact complex manifolds, as the largest dimension of the images of the maps $\varphi_m$, and proved the growth of $h^0(mD)$ and the fibration of Section 2. The proof of the growth here follows Laz04, Section 2.1, which works with the semigroup and the exponent in the same way. The upper bound through a subvariety that maps onto the image is a version of the argument in Laz04, Section 2.1, written so that it uses only the Hilbert polynomial. On surfaces Proposition P5.11.3 proved the upper bound, and the lower bound for $\kappa = 2$, directly.
