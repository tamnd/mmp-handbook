# Iitaka dimension and numerical dimension

A divisor is measured by the growth of its sections. If $D$ is a Cartier divisor on a projective variety $X$ of dimension $n$, the numbers $h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))$ either vanish for every $m \ge 1$ or grow like $m^\kappa$ along the multiples of a fixed integer, for one integer $\kappa$ between $0$ and $n$. This integer is the Iitaka dimension $\kappa(D)$. For the canonical divisor it is the Kodaira dimension, the first invariant of the classification and the one that decides how the minimal model program ends: a Mori fibre space when $\kappa = -\infty$, and, by the abundance conjecture, a model with $K$ semiample otherwise. The chapter works over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic 0.

Section 1 defines the semigroup $N(D)$ of the multiples with sections, its exponent $e(D)$, the maps $\varphi_m$ given by $|mD|$, and the Iitaka dimension as the largest dimension of their images. Its main theorem is the growth $a m^\kappa \le h^0(mD) \le b m^\kappa$, the upper bound for all $m$ and the lower bound for the large $m \in N(D)$. The proof runs through the fields $k(\varphi_m)$, which stabilize to a field $K_\infty$ of transcendence degree $\kappa$. Section 2 turns the stable field into a fibration. On a normal $X$ the maps $\varphi_m$ for large $m \in N(D)$ are, up to birational modifications, one contraction $f \colon X_\infty \to Y_\infty$ with $\dim Y_\infty = \kappa(D)$. This is the Iitaka fibration; the statement that $D$ has Iitaka dimension 0 on a very general fibre is cited from Laz04. The section defines the plurigenera and the Kodaira dimension and proves that the plurigenera of smooth projective varieties are birational invariants. Section 3 defines the numerical dimension $\kappa_\sigma$ of Nakayama, which perturbs $mD$ by a fixed ample divisor before counting sections. It proves $\kappa \le \kappa_\sigma$, that $D$ is big exactly when $\kappa_\sigma(D) = n$, and that for a nef divisor on a smooth surface $\kappa_\sigma$ is $0$, $1$ or $2$ according to $D \equiv 0$, $D^2 = 0$ or $D^2 > 0$.

The examples are curves, $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, the plane blown up at a point, products of curves, a fibre of an elliptic fibration, the surface $E \times E$ and smooth hypersurfaces. The failure examples are a point on an elliptic curve, where $\varphi_2$ is not the Iitaka fibration, and the cone over a plane quartic, whose dualizing sheaf is trivial although it is birational to a ruled surface. The required example is Theorem 1.4.26: on the ruled surface $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_E \oplus P)$ over an elliptic curve, with $P$ of degree 0 and of infinite order, the section $C_0$ with $C_0^2 = 0$ has $\kappa(C_0) = 0$ and $\kappa_\sigma(C_0) = 1$. So the two dimensions differ, and $\kappa$ is not determined by the numerical class, while $\kappa_\sigma$ is the invariant that the conjectures of the program compare with $\kappa$.

The chapter needs divisors, linear systems and projective bundles (P3), the Hilbert polynomial, the projection formula and Zariski's main theorem (P4), and the theory of curves and surfaces of P5, in particular Chapter P5.11 on the Kodaira dimension of surfaces. The cones of divisors and the volume are in Chapter 3, and Chapter 2 treats divisors over a base.
