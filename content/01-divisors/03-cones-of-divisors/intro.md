# The cones of divisors

A divisor class on a projective variety can be positive in four ways that the minimal model program uses all the time: it can be ample, nef, big or pseudoeffective. Each of these properties depends only on the numerical class, and each one cuts out a convex cone in the finite dimensional space $N^1(X/Z)$ of Definition 1.2.15. The ample cone is open, the nef cone is its closure, and the nef cone is dual to the closed cone of curves. The big cone is open, the pseudoeffective cone is its closure, and the volume of a divisor is a continuous function that is positive exactly on the big cone. This chapter proves these statements over a base $Z$, computes the cones in the examples that the rest of the handbook uses again, and shows that the cones can be polyhedral, as on rational surfaces, or round, as on $E \times E$.

Section 1 sets up the duality between nef classes and the closed cone of curves over $Z$. A lemma on dual cones turns the relative Kleiman criterion, Theorem 1.2.30, into the statement that the ample cone over $Z$ is the interior of the nef cone over $Z$, and that the closed cone of curves over $Z$ is the dual of the nef cone. The examples are the nef cones of $\operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2$, of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and of the Hirzebruch surfaces $\mathbb{F}_n$, both absolutely and over the base of their natural fibrations, and a class on $E \times E$ that is positive on every curve without being ample.

Section 2 defines the volume of a Cartier divisor, proves the growth bound $h^0(mD) = O(m^n)$, Kodaira's lemma and the homogeneity of the volume, and shows that a Cartier divisor has positive volume if and only if a multiple of it is ample plus effective. It then defines the big and pseudoeffective cones over $Z$ and proves that the big cone is the interior of the pseudoeffective cone. The continuity of the volume is quoted from Lazarsfeld. On a surface the volume is the square of the positive part of the Zariski decomposition, and this gives the volume functions of $\operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2$ and of $\mathbb{F}_n$ in closed form, together with the big divisor $\ell + 2E$ that is not nef.

Section 3 treats the abelian surface $E \times E$ for an elliptic curve $E$ without complex multiplication. There $\rho = 3$, and the nef cone, the closed cone of curves and the pseudoeffective cone all equal the circular cone $\{ st \ge c^2, \ s \ge 0, \ t \ge 0 \}$ in suitable coordinates. Every big class is ample, the volume is a quadratic form, and the rational boundary rays are exactly the rays of the elliptic curves $\{ (qP, pP) \}$. When $E$ has complex multiplication the Picard number jumps to $4$, and the curve $y^2 = x^3 - x$ shows it.

The chapter needs the intersection theory of surfaces of P5, the Zariski decomposition of Theorem P5.8.24, the cones of surfaces of P5 Chapter 12 and the spaces $N^1(X/Z)$ and $N_1(X/Z)$ of Definition 1.2.15. Chapter 4 uses the big cone and the volume for the Iitaka dimension and the numerical dimension, and Volume 2 uses the cones of surfaces computed here.
