# Singularities of pairs

The minimal model program runs on pairs $(X, \Delta)$ with mild singularities. This volume defines the classes of singularities, proves the tools that decide the class of a given pair, and works through the examples that a reader of the later volumes must know by hand. It is the volume of discrepancies.

> **The discrepancy box.** For a projective birational $f \colon Y \to X$ and a pair $(X, \Delta)$, write $K_Y + \Delta_Y = f^*(K_X + \Delta)$ with $f_*\Delta_Y = \Delta$. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the *discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) = -\operatorname{mult}_E \Delta_Y$, and the *log discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) + 1$. The pair is terminal or canonical if $a > 0$ or $a \ge 0$ for every exceptional $E$ over $X$. It is klt or lc if $a > -1$ or $a \ge -1$ for every prime divisor $E$ over $X$, exceptional or not. For a divisor $E$ on $X$ itself, $a(E, X, \Delta)$ is minus the coefficient of $E$ in $\Delta$, so klt asks for coefficients less than 1 and lc for coefficients at most 1. The Lean code states the same conditions with the log discrepancy, over all divisorial valuations.

The box is the one of Volume 0, Chapter 3. Two examples fix the signs: the blow-up of a point on a smooth surface has $a = 1$, and the pair $(\mathbb{A}^2, L)$ with $L$ a line has $a(L, \mathbb{A}^2, L) = -1$, so it is lc and not klt.

Chapter 1 defines the discrepancy on one model, proves that it depends only on the valuation and not on the model, and proves the bound $-1$: in dimension at least 2, a pair with one discrepancy below $-1$ has discrepancies that go to $-\infty$. Chapter 2 defines the six classes (terminal, canonical, klt, plt, dlt, lc), proves that one log resolution decides the class, and computes the classes of cones and of quotient singularities. Chapter 3 treats surfaces: the negativity lemma, the minimal resolution, the equality of du Val and canonical singularities, and the theorem that klt surface germs are quotients of $\mathbb{C}^2$. The later chapters of the volume treat terminal 3-fold singularities, adjunction, log canonical thresholds and minimal log discrepancies.

The spine is Reid's Young person's guide (Rei87), Kollár-Mori, Chapters 2, 4 and 5 (KM98), and Kollár's book on the singularities of the program, Chapters 2 and 3 (Kol13). Volume 3, Chapter 3, computes the discrepancies of toric varieties by the formula $a(v) = \langle m_\sigma, v \rangle - 1$. This volume uses those computations as examples and does not repeat them.
