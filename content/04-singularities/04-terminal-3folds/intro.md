# Terminal 3-fold singularities

Terminal singularities are the singularities of the minimal models of the program. On a surface they are smooth points, so the first new case is dimension 3, where the classification is complete. This chapter explains its structure: every terminal 3-fold point is a cyclic quotient of a smooth point or of an isolated compound Du Val point. The chapter proves the toric half of this picture in full, and states the deeper results with precise citations.

Section 1 proves again that terminal surface singularities are smooth, and computes the discrepancies of every cyclic quotient singularity $\frac1r(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ by the toric formula of Volume 3. The answer is a criterion in terms of the sums $S(k) = \sum_i \{k a_i / r\}$ of fractional parts. In dimension 3 the criterion gives the terminal lemma: $\frac1r(a_1, a_2, a_3)$ is terminal if and only if it is isomorphic to $\frac1r(1, -1, a)$ with $\gcd(a, r) = 1$. The proof reduces the lemma to a statement about sums of fractional parts, due to White, Frumkin, Morrison and Stevens, and cites that statement. Section 2 introduces the index one cover, proves that it preserves the classes, and defines the compound Du Val (cDV) points. It proves one direction of Reid's theorem, that an isolated cDV point is terminal of index 1, and sketches the converse. Section 3 states Mori's list of the terminal 3-fold points of index at least 2, as hyperquotients of isolated cDV points by cyclic groups, and Reid's theorem on the general elephant.

The worked examples are the terminal quotient $\frac12(1,1,1)$, with discrepancy $\frac12$, and the quotient $\frac13(1,1,1)$, which is canonical and not terminal. Both are computed by an explicit toric weighted blow-up, in coordinates. The hypersurface $xy + z^2 + t^3 = 0$ is a terminal point of type $cA_1$, and the chapter resolves it by two blow-ups and computes its discrepancy 1. The chapter ends with problems that test the criteria on explicit quotients and hypersurfaces.
