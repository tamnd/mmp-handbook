## Problems

Each problem decides the class of an explicit 3-fold singularity, by the fractional part criterion of Section 1, by an explicit blow-up, or by the results of Sections 2 and 3. Where a cyclic quotient is involved, list the vectors $v_k$ and the sums $S(k)$ in full.

1. (A) Compute $S(k)$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4$ for each of $\frac15(1,2,3)$, $\frac15(1,1,3)$, $\frac15(1,2,2)$ and $\frac15(1,1,2)$. Show that the first is terminal, the second and third are canonical of index 1 and not terminal, and the fourth is klt and not canonical, with a toric divisor of discrepancy $-\frac15$. Check the first three against the terminal lemma.
2. (A) Show that $\frac18(1,3,5)$ is terminal, and find $a$ with $\frac18(1,3,5) \cong \frac18(1,-1,a)$. Show that the toric divisor of $v_k$ has discrepancy $\frac{k}{8}$ for $k = 1, \dots, 7$, and compare with the list of Example 4.4.10 for $\frac18(1, -1, a)$, where the same values appear in another order.
3. (A) Show that $\frac14(1,1,2)$ has no pseudo-reflections, that $S(1) = S(2) = 1$ and $S(3) = 2$, and that it is canonical of index 1 and not terminal. Find the curve of points fixed by $\zeta^2$, and explain why the singular locus is not isolated.
4. (A) Show that $X = \{x^2 + y^3 + z^3 + t^3 = 0\}$ has an isolated singular point and that the section $t = 0$ is the $D_4$ singularity of Problem 3 of Chapter 3. Show that a general hyperplane section through the origin is also of type $D_4$, so that the point is of type $cD_4$, and deduce from Theorem 4.4.17 that it is terminal.
5. (B) Let $X_k = \{xy + z^2 + t^k = 0\}$ with $k \ge 2$. Show that $X_k$ is a cDV point of type $cA_1$. Show that the blow-up of the origin has exceptional divisor $E$ with $a(E, X_k) = 1$, that $E$ is a smooth quadric for $k = 2$ and a quadric cone for $k \ge 3$, and that the blow-up is a log resolution for $k = 2$. For $k = 3$, compare with Example 4.4.19.
6. (B) Compute $S(k)$ for $\frac16(1,2,3)$ and show that $S(k) = 1$ for $k \le 4$ and $S(5) = 2$. Deduce that the point is canonical of index 1 and not terminal, and find the two curves of points of $\mathbb{A}^3$ with a nontrivial stabilizer.
7. (B) Show that $\frac17(1,3,5)$ is klt and not canonical, with $S(3) = \frac67$, and that its index is 7. Find its index one cover.
8. (B) Let $\varphi = x^2 + y^2 + z^4 + u^4$ with weights $(1, 0, 1, 1)$ for $\mu_2$. Show that $\varphi$ is invariant, that $\{\varphi = 0\}$ has an isolated singular point, and that $\mu_2$ acts freely outside it. Show that the section $u = 0$ is an $A_3$ singularity, and that the hyperquotient has index 2 by Lemma 4.4.25. Which row of Mori's list does it belong to?
9. (B) Let $\varphi = u^2 + x^3 + y^3 + z^3$ with weights $(1, 2, 2, 0)$ for $\mu_3$. Show that $\varphi$ is invariant, that the fixed points of $\mu_3$ on $\{\varphi = 0\}$ are only the origin, and that the section $x = 0$ is a $D_4$ singularity. Show that the hyperquotient has index 3, with $w = 5 \equiv 2$.
10. (C) Let $X = \frac1r(1, -1, a)$ with $\gcd(a, r) = 1$, let $b$ satisfy $ab \equiv 1 \pmod r$ with $1 \le b \le r - 1$, and let $v = v_b = \frac1r(b, r - b, 1)$. Show that the star subdivision of $\sigma$ at $v$ has exceptional divisor of discrepancy $\frac1r$, and that its three cones have multiplicities $b$, $r - b$ and 1. Work out the case $\frac15(1, -1, 2)$, where $b = 3$, and show that the two singular points of the star subdivision are terminal cyclic quotients of index 3 and 2.
