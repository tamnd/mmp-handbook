## Problems

Each problem computes a different and uses it to decide the class of a pair, by the coefficient formula of Section 2, by adjunction or by inversion of adjunction. Where a resolution is involved, write down the crepant pullback $\Delta_Y$ in full and check one of the equations $(K_Y + \Delta_Y) \cdot E = 0$.

1. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2$, $S = \{y = 0\}$ and $B = b\{y = x^k\}$ with $k \ge 1$ and $b \ge 0$. Show that $\operatorname{Diff}_S(B) = kbP$, and deduce from Theorem 4.5.25 that $(X, S + B)$ is lc if and only if $b \le \frac1k$ and plt if and only if $b < \frac1k$. For $k = 2$, check the answer on the log resolution obtained by two blow-ups.
2. (A) Let $X = \frac15(1,2)$, so $\frac52 = [3, 2]$. Compute the continuants and the coefficients $c_i$ for a toric curve $S$ meeting the end with $E_1^2 = -3$, and for the other toric curve $T$, meeting the end with $E_2^2 = -2$. Show that both differents are $\frac45 P$. Deduce from Exercise 5 of Section 2 that the discrepancies of $E_1$ and $E_2$ are $-\frac25$ and $-\frac15$, and check them against $K_Y \cdot E_1 = 1$ and $K_Y \cdot E_2 = 0$.
3. (A) Let $X = \frac1n(1,1)$, $S$ a ruling and $B = b(L_1 + L_2)$ for two further rulings. Show that $\operatorname{Diff}_S(B) = (1 - \frac1n + \frac{2b}{n})P$, and that $(X, S + B)$ is lc if and only if $b \le \frac12$. Check the coefficient of $E$ in $\Delta_Y$ directly.
4. (B) Let $S = \{y^2 = x^2(x + 1)\} \subset \mathbb{A}^2$ be the nodal cubic, $L = \{y = 2x\}$, a line through the node transversal to both branches, and $b \ge 0$. Show that $\Delta_Y = S' + bL' + (1 + b)E$ on the blow-up, that $\operatorname{Diff}_{S^\nu}(bL) = (1 + b)(q_1 + q_2)$, and that $(\mathbb{A}^2, S + bL)$ is lc if and only if $b = 0$. Explain why it is never plt.
5. (B) Let $X = \{xy = z^2\}$ be the quadric cone, $S = \{x = z = 0\}$ and $H = X \cap \{x = y\}$, a hyperplane section through the vertex, which is the union of the two lines $x = y = \pm z$. Show that $f^*H = H' + E$ on the minimal resolution, that $\operatorname{Diff}_S(bH) = (\frac12 + b)P$, and that $(X, S + bH)$ is lc if and only if $b \le \frac12$ and plt if and only if $b < \frac12$.
6. (B) Let $P \in X$ be a $D_4$ singularity, with minimal resolution a central $(-2)$-curve $E_0$ meeting three $(-2)$-curves $E_1, E_2, E_3$. Let $S$ be a curve whose strict transform meets $E_1$ transversally at one point and no other $E_i$. Show that $\Delta_Y = S' + E_0 + E_1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_3$, so that $\operatorname{Diff}_S(0) = P$ and $(X, S)$ is lc and not plt. Explain how this agrees with Theorem 4.5.13.
7. (B) Let $X = \mathbb{A}^3$, $S = \{z = 0\}$ and $B = \{z = x^2 + y^4\}$. Show that $\operatorname{Diff}_S(tB) = tC$, where $C = \{x^2 + y^4 = 0\}$ is a tacnode on $S \cong \mathbb{A}^2$. Using Problem 1 of Chapter 2, show that $(\mathbb{A}^2, tC)$ is lc if and only if $t \le \frac34$, and deduce with Theorems 4.5.26 and 4.5.28 that $(\mathbb{A}^3, S + tB)$ is lc near $S$ if and only if $t \le \frac34$ and plt near $S$ if and only if $t < \frac34$.
8. (A) Let $X = \frac1{12}(1,5)$. Show that $\frac{12}{5} = [3, 2, 3]$, that the continuants are $0, 1, 3, 5, 12$, and that the coefficients are $c_1 = \frac{11}{12}$, $c_2 = \frac{9}{12}$, $c_3 = \frac{7}{12}$. Explain why the computation from the other end gives the same numbers, and relate this to $5 \cdot 5 \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$.
9. (B) Let $X = \mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$, with $n \ge 2$, and $S = \{x_0 = 0\}$, a curve through the singular point $[0 : 0 : 1]$, which is of type $\frac1n(1,1)$. Using $K_X \sim \mathcal{O}(-n - 2)$ and $S^2 = \frac1n$, show that $(K_X + S) \cdot S = -\frac{n+1}{n}$, and check that this is $\deg(K_{\mathbb{P}^1} + \operatorname{Diff}_S(0))$ with $\operatorname{Diff}_S(0) = (1 - \frac1n)P$, as Theorem 4.5.3 requires.
10. (C) Let $\bar X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be the projective cone over a smooth plane cubic $V$, and $S$ the ruling over a point $p \in V$. Show that $K_{\bar X} = -H$, that $S^2 = \frac13$ in Mumford's sense, and that $(K_{\bar X} + S) \cdot S = -\frac23$. Check this against $\deg(K_{\mathbb{P}^1} + \frac43 P)$ from Example 4.5.24. Show that $S$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier at the vertex if and only if $3p - H|_V$ is a torsion class in $\operatorname{Pic}^0(V)$, so that for a general $p$ only the surface version of the different, without a Cartier hypothesis, applies.
