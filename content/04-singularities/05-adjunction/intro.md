# Adjunction and inversion of adjunction

Adjunction compares a pair $(X, S + B)$ with a pair on the divisor $S$. For $X$ and $S$ smooth it is the formula $(K_X + S)|_S = K_S$. In general $K_X + S$ need not be Cartier along $S$ and $S$ need not be normal, and the formula needs a correction term, Shokurov's different. With it, adjunction reads $(K_X + S + B)|_{S^\nu} = K_{S^\nu} + \operatorname{Diff}_{S^\nu}(B)$ on the normalization $S^\nu$. The chapter defines the different, computes it, proves that adjunction passes singularities down to $S$, and proves the converse, inversion of adjunction, on surfaces.

Section 1 defines the different on a normal surface by Mumford's numerical pullback, with no Cartier hypothesis, proves that it does not depend on the resolution, and proves the adjunction formula and the effectivity of the different. It then defines the different in every dimension on a log resolution, and reduces its properties to surfaces by general hyperplane sections. Section 2 computes the different at a point where the resolution graph is a chain. The answer is the coefficient formula $1 - \frac1m + \sum_j \frac{m_j b_j}{m}$, proved with continuants, and the classification of plt surface pairs, cited, says that every plt surface pair is of this form. Section 3 proves adjunction: if $(X, S + B)$ is lc near $S$, then $(S^\nu, \operatorname{Diff}_{S^\nu}(B))$ is lc, and if it is plt, then the different is klt and $S$ is normal. Section 4 proves inversion of adjunction on surfaces, from a vanishing theorem for a resolution of a surface, through the connectedness lemma. It states the general lc case, a theorem of Kawakita (Kaw07), and the plt case (KM98, Theorem 5.50) as black boxes.

The worked examples are the line through the vertex of the quadric cone, with different $\frac12 P$, and the line through the vertex of $\frac1n(1,1)$, with different $(1 - \frac1n)P$. The chapter computes the different of $\frac17(1,3)$ from both ends of its chain. Two lines in the plane give a pair that is dlt and not plt, and the chapter explains why a pair that is plt and not dlt cannot exist. The nodal cubic is the failure without normality: the pair is lc, the curve is not normal, and the different is the conductor of the normalization. The cusp and the cone over a plane cubic have differents with coefficients greater than 1. Inversion of adjunction computes the lc threshold of a cusp with respect to its tangent line, and explains the lc threshold of a cone in $\mathbb{A}^N$ found in Chapter 2.
