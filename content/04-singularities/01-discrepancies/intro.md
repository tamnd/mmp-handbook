# Discrepancies

The discrepancy compares two canonical divisors. Let $f \colon Y \to X$ be a resolution of a singular variety. Then $K_Y$ and $f^* K_X$ agree outside the exceptional locus, and they differ by a combination of exceptional divisors. The coefficients of this combination are the discrepancies. They measure how far the singularity of $X$ is from a smooth point, and the minimal model program uses them to say which singularities it allows.

Section 1 defines the canonical divisor as the divisor of a rational top form, defines pairs and sub-pairs, and defines the discrepancy of a prime divisor on one model $Y$. Section 2 proves the theorem that the discrepancy depends only on the valuation of the divisor, by the formula $a(E) = \operatorname{ord}_E(\omega) - \sum_i r_i \operatorname{ord}_E(h_i)$. This formula is also the definition in the Lean library. Section 2 also proves that discrepancies do not change under a crepant pullback. Section 3 computes the discrepancy of the blow-up of a smooth centre and proves the bound $-1$: in dimension at least 2, either every discrepancy is at least $-1$, or the discrepancies are not bounded below.

The examples are the blow-up of a smooth point ($a = n - 1$), the quadric cone $xy = z^2$, the $A_1$ singularity ($a = 0$), the pairs $(\mathbb{A}^2, cL)$ and $(\mathbb{A}^2, c(L_1 + L_2))$, and the monomial valuations of $\mathbb{A}^n$. The failure is the cone $xz = yw$ with a plane through the vertex: the plane is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, so no discrepancy is defined. The chapter ends with problems that compute discrepancies by hand, among them the three valuations of the resolution of the cusp and the ordinary double point of dimension 3.
