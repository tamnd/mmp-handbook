## Monomial ideals and Howald's theorem

### Statement

For a monomial ideal, the log resolution of Theorem 4.6.3 can be chosen toric, and the minimum over its divisors becomes a problem of convex geometry. Identify $\mathbb{A}^n$ with the affine toric variety $U_\sigma$ of the positive orthant $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ in $N_\mathbb{R} = \mathbb{R}^n$, so that $M = \mathbb{Z}^n$ and the monomial $x^m$ is the character $\chi^m$. Write $\mathbf{1} = (1, \dots, 1)$.

**Definition 4.6.10 (the Newton polyhedron).** Let $\mathfrak{a} \subset k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a nonzero monomial ideal, generated by the monomials $x^m$ with $m$ in a finite set $G \subset \mathbb{Z}^n_{\ge 0}$. The *Newton polyhedron* of $\mathfrak{a}$ is
$$P(\mathfrak{a}) = \operatorname{conv}(G) + \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0} \subset \mathbb{R}^n.$$
For $v \in \sigma$ put $\varphi(v) = \min_{m \in G} \langle m, v \rangle$. The *diagonal number* of $\mathfrak{a}$ is $t_0(\mathfrak{a}) = \min\{ t \ge 0 : t \mathbf{1} \in P(\mathfrak{a}) \}$.

The polyhedron contains $m + \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0}$ for each $x^m \in \mathfrak{a}$, so it does not depend on the choice of $G$, and neither does $\varphi$: for $v \in \sigma$, the minimum of $\langle p, v \rangle$ over $P(\mathfrak{a})$ is attained at a point of $G$. The set of $t \ge 0$ with $t \mathbf{1} \in P(\mathfrak{a})$ is a closed half-line, since $P(\mathfrak{a})$ is closed and $P(\mathfrak{a}) + \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0} = P(\mathfrak{a})$, so the minimum exists. It is 0 if and only if $\mathfrak{a} = (1)$.

**Theorem 4.6.11 (Howald).** Let $\mathfrak{a} \ne (1)$ be a nonzero monomial ideal. Then
$$\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) = \operatorname{lct}_0(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) = \frac{1}{t_0(\mathfrak{a})} = \min\Big\{ \frac{\langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle}{\varphi(v)} : v \in \mathbb{Z}^n_{\ge 0}, \ \varphi(v) > 0 \Big\}.$$
The minimum is attained at a normal vector of a face of $P(\mathfrak{a})$ that contains $t_0 \mathbf{1}$.

**Corollary 4.6.12 (general members).** Let $\mathfrak{a} \ne (1)$ be a nonzero monomial ideal generated by $x^m$, $m \in G$. For $c = (c_m) \in k^G$ put $f_c = \sum_{m \in G} c_m x^m$. There is a nonempty open subset $V \subset k^G$ such that for $c \in V$,
$$\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \operatorname{div} f_c) = \min\{ 1, \operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) \}.$$

Lean: not yet in Lean.

### Proof in words

Every positive weight vector $v$ gives a monomial valuation, a toric divisor over $\mathbb{A}^n$ with log discrepancy $\langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle$ and order $\varphi(v)$ along the ideal. So the threshold is at most every ratio $\langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle / \varphi(v)$. Conversely, the function $\varphi$ is the minimum of finitely many linear functions, and the cones on which one of them wins form a fan. A smooth refinement of that fan is a toric log resolution of the ideal: on each chart the ideal is generated by its winning monomial. Theorem 4.6.3 then says that the threshold is the least ratio over the rays of the refinement, and this is at least the least ratio over all $v$. Finally, the least ratio is a statement about where the diagonal leaves the polyhedron: the face of $P(\mathfrak{a})$ through $t_0 \mathbf{1}$ has a normal vector $v$ with $\varphi(v) = t_0 \langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle$. For the corollary, a general combination of the generators has a strict transform that is a general member of a base point free linear system on the toric model, so by Bertini it is smooth and transverse to the toric boundary, and it adds only the ratio 1.

### Examples

**Example 4.6.13 (the curves $x^a + y^b$).** Let $a, b \ge 1$ and $\mathfrak{a} = (x^a, y^b)$, not both of $a, b$ equal to 1. The lower boundary of $P(\mathfrak{a})$ is the segment from $(a, 0)$ to $(0, b)$, on the line $\frac{p_1}{a} + \frac{p_2}{b} = 1$, and the diagonal meets it at $t_0 = \frac{ab}{a+b}$. So $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^2; \mathfrak{a}) = \frac1a + \frac1b$, attained at the normal vector $v = (b, a)$, with $\langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle = a + b$ and $\varphi(v) = ab$. For $c_1, c_2 \ne 0$, choose $\lambda, \mu$ with $\lambda^a = c_1$ and $\mu^b = c_2$. The automorphism $(x, y) \mapsto (\lambda x, \mu y)$ of $\mathbb{A}^2$ takes $\{c_1 x^a + c_2 y^b = 0\}$ to $\{x^a + y^b = 0\}$, and an automorphism preserves the threshold. The open set $V$ of the corollary meets $(k^*)^2$, so
$$\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^2; \{x^a + y^b = 0\}) = \min\Big\{ 1, \frac1a + \frac1b \Big\}.$$
For the cusp, $a = 2$ and $b = 3$, this is $\frac56$ again, without the three blow-ups of Example 4.6.7. The minimal vector $v = (3, 2)$ gives the monomial valuation with $v(x) = 3$ and $v(y) = 2$, log discrepancy 5 and order 6 along $x^2 + y^3$. After the exchange of $x$ and $y$ it has the same two numbers as the divisor $E_3$ of Example 4.6.7. The threshold is capped at 1 when $\frac1a + \frac1b \ge 1$, that is, when $\min\{a, b\} = 1$ or $a = b = 2$: the curve $\{x^2 + y^2 = 0\}$ is a node.

**Example 4.6.14 (an ideal with two edges).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^5, x^2 y, y^4)$. The point $(2, 1)$ lies below the segment from $(5, 0)$ to $(0, 4)$, since $\frac25 + \frac14 < 1$, so the lower boundary of $P(\mathfrak{a})$ has two edges: from $(5, 0)$ to $(2, 1)$, with normal vector $(1, 3)$, and from $(2, 1)$ to $(0, 4)$, with normal vector $(3, 2)$. The values are
$$\varphi(1, 3) = \min\{5, 5, 12\} = 5, \qquad \varphi(3, 2) = \min\{15, 8, 8\} = 8.$$
The diagonal meets the second edge at $(2 - 2s, 1 + 3s)$ with $2 - 2s = 1 + 3s$, that is $s = \frac15$ and $t_0 = \frac85$. So $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^2; \mathfrak{a}) = \frac58$, attained at $v = (3, 2)$ with ratio $\frac{5}{8}$, while the other edge gives the larger ratio $\frac45$. A general member $c_1 x^5 + c_2 x^2 y + c_3 y^4$ has threshold $\frac58$.

**Example 4.6.15 (powers of the maximal ideal).** Let $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and $k \ge 1$. Then $P(\mathfrak{m}^k) = \{ p \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0} : p_1 + \dots + p_n \ge k \}$, so $t_0 = \frac{k}{n}$ and $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{m}^k) = \frac{n}{k}$, attained at $v = \mathbf{1}$, the blow-up of the origin. A general member of $\mathfrak{m}^k$ is a general polynomial whose terms all have degree $k$, and its threshold is $\min\{1, \frac{n}{k}\}$. This agrees with Example 4.6.8 for $k = d$: a product of $d$ general linear forms is not a general member of $\mathfrak{m}^d$, and it has the same threshold.

### Proof

*The theorem.* Write $\lambda(v) = \langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle / \varphi(v)$ for $v \in \sigma$ with $\varphi(v) > 0$. The function $\varphi$ is a minimum of linear functions, so it is continuous, and $\lambda$ is continuous where it is defined and homogeneous of degree 0.

First, $\operatorname{lct}_0(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) \le \lambda(v)$. If $v$ is a primitive vector of positive integers, Example 4.1.17 gives a divisor $E_v$ over $\mathbb{A}^n$ whose valuation is the monomial valuation of weight $v$, with centre the origin and $a(E_v, \mathbb{A}^n, 0) = \langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle - 1$. By the definition of a monomial valuation, $\operatorname{ord}_{E_v}(x^m) = \langle m, v \rangle$, so $\operatorname{ord}_{E_v} \mathfrak{a} = \varphi(v)$. If $\varphi(v) > 0$ and $t > \lambda(v)$, then $a(E_v; \mathbb{A}^n, 0, \mathfrak{a}^t) = \langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle - 1 - t \varphi(v) < -1$ for a divisor with centre the origin, so the triple is not lc near the origin. By homogeneity and continuity, $\lambda(v) \ge \inf \lambda$ over positive integer vectors for every $v \in \sigma$ with $\varphi(v) > 0$, since the positive rational vectors are dense in $\sigma$ and $\lambda$ does not change under positive scaling. So $\operatorname{lct}_0 \le \inf\{ \lambda(v) \}$ over all $v \in \sigma$ with $\varphi(v) > 0$.

Next, a toric log resolution. Let $H$ be the finite set of linear forms $\langle m - m', \cdot \rangle$ for $m \ne m'$ in $G$. For each $s \colon H \to \{1, -1\}$ let $\sigma_s = \{ v \in \sigma : s(h) h(v) \ge 0 \text{ for all } h \in H \}$, a rational polyhedral cone, and let $\Sigma_0$ be the set of the faces of all the $\sigma_s$. A face of $\sigma_s$ is cut out by turning some of its defining inequalities, the coordinate inequalities of $\sigma$ and the inequalities $s(h) h \ge 0$, into equalities. The intersection of two members of $\Sigma_0$ is again cut out of each of them by turning inequalities into equalities, because for $h$ with $s(h) \ne s'(h)$ the two cones force $h = 0$. So it is a face of each, and $\Sigma_0$ is a fan with support $\sigma$. On a cone $\tau \in \Sigma_0$ each form $h \in H$ has a constant sign, so for any $m, m' \in G$ either $\langle m, v \rangle \le \langle m', v \rangle$ for all $v \in \tau$ or the reverse. Choose $m_\tau \in G$ minimal for this order. Then $\varphi(v) = \langle m_\tau, v \rangle$ for $v \in \tau$.

By Theorem 3.1.29, $\Sigma_0$ has a smooth refinement $\Sigma$. Each cone $\tau$ of $\Sigma$ lies in a cone of $\Sigma_0$, so it has an $m_\tau \in G$ with $\varphi = \langle m_\tau, \cdot \rangle$ on $\tau$. By Corollary 3.2.39, $f \colon Y = X_\Sigma \to U_\sigma = \mathbb{A}^n$ is proper, birational and toric, and $Y$ is smooth. On the chart $U_\tau$, for each $m \in G$, the vector $m - m_\tau$ is nonnegative on $\tau$, so it lies in $\tau^\vee \cap M$, and $\chi^m = \chi^{m_\tau} \chi^{m - m_\tau}$ with $\chi^{m - m_\tau}$ regular on $U_\tau$. Hence $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{U_\tau} = (\chi^{m_\tau})$. The prime divisors $D_\rho$ that meet $U_\tau$ are those with $\rho \in \tau(1)$ (Theorem 3.2.29), so Proposition 3.3.3 gives $\operatorname{div}(\chi^{m_\tau})|_{U_\tau} = \sum_{\rho \in \tau(1)} \langle m_\tau, u_\rho \rangle D_\rho = \sum_{\rho \in \tau(1)} \varphi(u_\rho) D_\rho$. These local descriptions agree, so
$$\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F), \qquad F = \sum_{\rho \in \Sigma(1)} \varphi(u_\rho) D_\rho.$$
The vector $m_\sigma$ of the orthant is $\mathbf{1}$, so Theorem 3.3.34 gives $K_Y = f^* K_{\mathbb{A}^n} + \sum_\rho (\langle \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle - 1) D_\rho$, where the rays $e_i$ of $\sigma$ have coefficient 0. So $\Delta_Y = \sum_\rho (1 - \langle \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle) D_\rho$. On each $U_\tau \cong \mathbb{A}^k \times (k^*)^{n-k}$ the divisors $D_\rho$ with $\rho \in \tau(1)$ are coordinate hyperplanes, so $\Delta_Y + F$ has snc support. By Theorem 4.6.3 in the form noted after it,
$$\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) = \min\{ \lambda(u_\rho) : \rho \in \Sigma(1), \ \varphi(u_\rho) > 0 \} \ge \inf\{ \lambda(v) \}.$$
Together with the first step, and $\operatorname{lct} \le \operatorname{lct}_0$, this gives $\operatorname{lct} = \operatorname{lct}_0 = \inf \lambda$, and the infimum is a minimum attained at a ray of $\Sigma$, an integer vector.

Last, $\inf \lambda = 1 / t_0$. The point $t_0 \mathbf{1}$ lies in $P(\mathfrak{a})$, so $\varphi(v) \le \langle t_0 \mathbf{1}, v \rangle = t_0 \langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle$ for every $v \in \sigma$, and $\lambda(v) \ge 1/t_0$ when $\varphi(v) > 0$. For the reverse, $P(\mathfrak{a})$ is the sum of a rational polytope and a rational cone, so by the theorem of Minkowski and Weyl it is the intersection of finitely many half-spaces $\{ p : \langle p, v_j \rangle \ge c_j \}$ with $v_j \in \mathbb{Z}^n \setminus \{0\}$. Since $P(\mathfrak{a}) + \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0} = P(\mathfrak{a})$, each $v_j$ is nonnegative, so $v_j \in \sigma$, and $c_j \le \varphi(v_j)$, so the half-spaces may be taken with $c_j = \varphi(v_j)$. If every inequality were strict at $t_0 \mathbf{1}$, a neighbourhood of it would lie in $P(\mathfrak{a})$, and so would $(t_0 - \epsilon) \mathbf{1}$ for small $\epsilon > 0$, which contradicts the choice of $t_0$. So some $v = v_j$ has $\varphi(v) = t_0 \langle \mathbf{1}, v \rangle > 0$, and $\lambda(v) = 1/t_0$. This $v$ is normal to the face $P(\mathfrak{a}) \cap \{\langle p, v \rangle = \varphi(v)\}$, which contains $t_0 \mathbf{1}$.

*The corollary.* Keep the toric model $f \colon Y \to \mathbb{A}^n$ of the proof. On $U_\tau$, $f^* f_c = \chi^{m_\tau} g_{c, \tau}$ with $g_{c, \tau} = \sum_m c_m \chi^{m - m_\tau}$. The functions $\chi^{m - m_\tau}$ for $m \in G$ have no common zero on $U_\tau$, since one of them is $\chi^0 = 1$. So the $g_{c, \tau}$ glue to the members $G_c$ of a base point free linear system on $Y$, of dimension at most $|G| - 1$, with $f^* \operatorname{div} f_c = G_c + F$. Let $S$ be a stratum of the toric boundary, an orbit of $Y$. It is smooth, and the restriction of the system to $S$ is base point free. By the theorem of Bertini in the form of Kleiman's transversality theorem (Har77, Chapter III, Section 10), applied to the morphism of $Y$ and of each orbit to projective space given by the system, there is a nonempty open set of $c$ for which $G_c$ is smooth and $G_c \cap S$ is smooth of codimension 1 in $S$ or empty for every orbit $S$. Also $G_c$ contains no $D_\rho$, since the members that contain a given $D_\rho$ form a proper linear subspace of $k^G$. For such $c$, $G_c$ is reduced, it is the strict transform of $\operatorname{div} f_c$, and $\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta_Y + F) + G_c$ is snc. The coefficient of $G_c$ in $\Delta_Y$ is 0 and in $f^* \operatorname{div} f_c$ is 1. Theorem 4.6.3 gives
$$\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \operatorname{div} f_c) = \min\Big\{ 1, \min_\rho \lambda(u_\rho) \Big\} = \min\{ 1, \operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) \},$$
where $G_c \ne 0$ because $f_c$ is a nonconstant polynomial for $c$ in a nonempty open set. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x_1^{a_1}, \dots, x_n^{a_n})$ with every $a_i \ge 1$. Show that $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) = \sum_i \frac{1}{a_i}$ and that $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \{x_1^{a_1} + \dots + x_n^{a_n} = 0\}) = \min\{1, \sum_i \frac{1}{a_i}\}$.
2. (A) Compute $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^2; (x^2 y, x y^2))$ by Howald's theorem, and compare it with the threshold of the three lines $xy(x + y) = 0$ from Example 4.6.8.
3. (A) Show that $\operatorname{ord}_E(\mathfrak{a}^k) = k \operatorname{ord}_E \mathfrak{a}$ for every divisor $E$ over $X$, and deduce $\operatorname{lct}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a}^k) = \frac1k \operatorname{lct}(X, \Delta; \mathfrak{a})$. For a monomial ideal, check this with $P(\mathfrak{a}^k) = k P(\mathfrak{a})$.
4. (B) Show that $\operatorname{lct}_0(\mathbb{A}^2; \{y^2 = x^{2k+1}\}) = \frac12 + \frac{1}{2k+1}$ for $k \ge 1$, and that these numbers decrease to $\frac12$.
5. (B) Let $f = \sum_m c_m x^m$ be a nonconstant polynomial with $f(0) = 0$, and $\mathfrak{a}_f = (x^m : c_m \ne 0)$ its term ideal. Show that $\operatorname{ord}_{E_v} f = \varphi(v)$ for every monomial valuation, and deduce $\operatorname{lct}_0(\mathbb{A}^n; \operatorname{div} f) \le \min\{1, \operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}_f)\}$. Show that the inequality is strict for $f = (x + y)^2$.
6. (C) Show that $\operatorname{lct}_0(\mathbb{A}^3; \{x^a + y^b + z^c = 0\}) = \min\{1, \frac1a + \frac1b + \frac1c\}$. Find all triples $2 \le a \le b \le c$ with $\frac1a + \frac1b + \frac1c > 1$, and identify the surfaces $\{x^a + y^b + z^c = 0\}$ for these triples in the list of Theorem 4.3.13, using Example 4.3.15 for $(2, 3, 3)$.

### Lean

The library has no monomial ideals, no Newton polyhedra and no thresholds of ideals. The thresholds of the curves $x^a + y^b$ are values of `MinimalModel.lct`, and Theorem 4.6.11 and Corollary 4.6.12 are not yet in Lean.

### Notes and history

Howald (How01) computed the multiplier ideals of a monomial ideal: $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ is generated by the monomials $x^m$ with $m + \mathbf{1}$ in the interior of $c P(\mathfrak{a})$. The threshold is the least $c$ for which $x^0 = 1$ fails this test, which is Theorem 4.6.11. Laz04, Chapter 9, gives the same proof by a toric log resolution. The corollary shows that a general polynomial with given terms has the threshold of its term ideal, capped at 1. Exercise 5 shows that a special polynomial can only have a smaller threshold. Volume 3, Chapter 3, has the toric discrepancy formula used in the proof.
