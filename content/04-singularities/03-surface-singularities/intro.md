# Surface singularities

On a surface the classes of Chapter 2 have a complete description. The reason is that a normal surface germ has a minimal resolution, and its exceptional curves have a negative definite intersection matrix. Linear algebra on that matrix then computes every discrepancy. This chapter proves the linear algebra in full, and states the deeper classification results with citations.

Section 1 proves that the intersection matrix of the exceptional curves of a resolution is negative definite, proves the negativity lemma for surfaces, and uses them for two results. First, Mumford's numerical pullback defines discrepancies on every normal surface, even when $K_X$ is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier. Second, on the minimal resolution every discrepancy is at most 0, so terminal surfaces are smooth. The cone over a curve of genus $g$ by a line bundle of degree $d$ has the discrepancy $-1 - \frac{2g-2}{d}$ for every line bundle. Section 2 proves that a surface germ is canonical if and only if its minimal resolution has only $(-2)$-curves, and that the graph of those curves is a Dynkin diagram of type $A$, $D$ or $E$. Section 3 sketches the proof that every klt surface germ is a quotient $\mathbb{C}^2 / G$, computes the discrepancies of the cyclic quotients $\frac1n(1,q)$ from the continued fraction of $\frac{n}{q}$, and states the classification of the lc surface germs.

The examples are the $A_n$ singularities, the cyclic quotients $\frac1n(1,1)$ and $\frac15(1,2)$, the cones over curves, and the cusp of a cycle of rational curves. Volume 3, Chapter 3, computes the toric cases by the formula $a(v) = \langle m_\sigma, v \rangle - 1$; this chapter computes the same numbers by intersection theory and checks them against Volume 3. The chapter ends with problems that resolve some surface singularities by hand.
