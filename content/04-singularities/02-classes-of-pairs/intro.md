# The classes of singularities

The minimal model program needs a class of singularities that is small enough to keep the theorems of the program true and large enough to contain every variety that the program makes. No single class does both jobs, and the program uses six: terminal, canonical, klt, plt, dlt and lc. This chapter defines them, proves that one log resolution decides which class a pair is in, and computes the classes of the two main families of examples: cones over smooth varieties and quotients of affine space by finite groups.

Section 1 gives the six definitions after KM98, Chapter 2, proves the implications between them, and proves that in dimension at least 2 terminal pairs have coefficients less than 1 and canonical, plt and lc pairs have coefficients at most 1. Section 2 proves the formula for the discrepancy of a pair with simple normal crossings and deduces that the discrepancies of the divisors of one log resolution decide every class. Section 3 proves that the cone over a smooth projective variety $V$ with an ample $L$ has a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier canonical divisor if and only if $K_V \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} rL$, and that then its discrepancy is $-1 - r$. For a curve of genus $g$ and a line bundle of degree $d$ this is $-1 - \frac{2g-2}{d}$. Section 4 proves that log discrepancies multiply by the ramification index under a finite morphism, and deduces that quotient singularities are $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial and klt.

The examples of the chapter are the pairs $(\mathbb{A}^2, L)$, $(\mathbb{A}^2, L_1 + L_2)$, the nodal cubic and the cusp, the cones over plane curves ($a = 2 - d$), the cone over a plane cubic (lc, not klt), the cone over a plane quartic (not lc), the quotients $\frac1n(1,1)$ ($a = -\frac{n-2}{n}$) and $\frac12(1,1,1)$ ($a = \frac12$), and the ordinary double point of dimension 3. Volume 3, Chapter 3, computes the same numbers for the toric examples by the formula $a(v) = \langle m_\sigma, v \rangle - 1$. The chapter ends with problems that compute the classes of some pairs by hand.
