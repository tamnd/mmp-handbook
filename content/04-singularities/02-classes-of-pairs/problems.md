## Problems

Each problem decides the class of a pair by hand: find a log resolution or a cover, compute the coefficients of the crepant pullback, and read off the class from Section 2.2.

1. (A) Let $C = \{y^2 = x^4\} \subset \mathbb{A}^2$, the tacnode, with two smooth branches $y = \pm x^2$. Show that two blow-ups give a log resolution, with exceptional curves $E_1$ and $E_2$ of weights $(1, 1)$ and $(1, 2)$ on $(x, y)$. Show that $\Delta_Y = tC' + (2t - 1)E_1 + (4t - 2)E_2$ for the pair $(\mathbb{A}^2, tC)$, and deduce that it is lc if and only if $t \le \frac34$.
2. (A) Let $\mu_2$ act on $\mathbb{A}^3$ by $(x, y, z) \mapsto (-x, -y, z)$, with quotient $X \cong \{uv = w^2\} \times \mathbb{A}^1$. Lift the action to the blow-up of the line $\{x = y = 0\}$, show that it fixes the exceptional divisor with $e = 2$, and deduce $a = 0$ for its image. Then show from a crepant resolution that $X$ is canonical and not terminal.
3. (B) Let $V \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth surface of degree $d$ and $X \subset \mathbb{A}^4$ its affine cone. Show that $a(E, X, 0) = 3 - d$. Deduce that the cone over a quadric is terminal, the cone over a cubic surface is canonical and not terminal, the cone over a quartic surface is lc and not klt, and the cone over a quintic surface is not lc.
4. (B) Let $X = \{xy = z^2\}$ and $D = \{x = z = 0\}$, a line on the cone. Show that $2D = \operatorname{div}(x)$, so that $K_X + D$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier. On the minimal resolution, show that $f^*D = D' + \frac12 E$ and that $D'$ meets $E$ once, transversally. Deduce that $\operatorname{discrep}(X, D) = -\frac12$, and that $(X, D)$ is plt, not klt and not canonical.
5. (C) Let $V = \{F = 0\} \subset \mathbb{P}^{N-1}$ be a smooth hypersurface of degree $d$ with $N \ge 3$, and $X = \{F = 0\} \subset \mathbb{A}^N$ its cone. Show that the blow-up of the origin is a log resolution of $(\mathbb{A}^N, cX)$, and that the pair is lc if and only if $c \le \min\{1, \frac{N}{d}\}$. Compare with Exercise 4 of Section 2.3: the pair $(\mathbb{A}^N, X)$ is lc if and only if $X$ is lc. Chapter 5 explains this agreement by inversion of adjunction.
