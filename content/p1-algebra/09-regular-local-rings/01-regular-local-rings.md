## Regular local rings

### Statement

Throughout, $(A, \mathfrak{m}, k)$ is a Noetherian local ring of dimension $d$. By Corollary P1.1.26 the least number of generators of $\mathfrak{m}$ is the embedding dimension $\dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$, and by Corollary P1.5.19 it is at least $d$.

**Definition P1.9.1 (regular local ring).** The ring $A$ is *regular* if $\dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 = d$, that is, if $\mathfrak{m}$ is generated by $d$ elements. A *regular system of parameters* of a regular local ring is a sequence $x_1, \dots, x_d$ that generates $\mathfrak{m}$. Such a sequence is a system of parameters in the sense of Definition P1.5.21, and by Corollary P1.1.26 a sequence $x_1, \dots, x_d \in \mathfrak{m}$ is a regular system of parameters if and only if its images form a basis of $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$.

Lean: Mathlib `IsRegularLocalRing` (definition: a Noetherian local ring with `(maximalIdeal R).spanFinrank = ringKrullDim R`), with `isRegularLocalRing_iff` and `IsRegularLocalRing.iff_finrank_cotangentSpace`, the form with $\dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ (proved in Mathlib).

A regular local ring of dimension $0$ is a field, since $\mathfrak{m}$ is generated by the empty set. By Theorem P1.7.12, a regular local ring of dimension $1$ is a discrete valuation ring, and a discrete valuation ring is regular of dimension $1$. Geometrically, $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ is the dual of the Zariski tangent space, and a point is regular when its tangent space is no larger than the variety.

The *associated graded ring* $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$ of Example P1.5.29 is a graded $k$-algebra generated by its degree one part $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$. If $x_1, \dots, x_e$ generate $\mathfrak{m}$, the $k$-algebra map
$$
\varphi : k[X_1, \dots, X_e] \longrightarrow \operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A), \qquad X_i \longmapsto x_i + \mathfrak{m}^2 ,
$$
is surjective and preserves degrees, since $\mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$ is spanned by the classes of the monomials of degree $n$ in the $x_i$.

**Theorem P1.9.2 (the associated graded ring).** The ring $A$ is regular if and only if $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A)$ is isomorphic, as a graded $k$-algebra, to a polynomial ring $k[X_1, \dots, X_e]$ with all $X_i$ of degree one. In that case $e = d$, and for every regular system of parameters $x_1, \dots, x_d$ the map $\varphi$ is an isomorphism.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Suppose $A$ is regular, and let $x_1, \dots, x_d$ be a regular system of parameters. If $d = 0$, then $\mathfrak{m} = 0$ and $\varphi : k \to k$ is the identity. Let $d \geq 1$ and suppose $\ker \varphi \neq 0$. Since $\varphi$ preserves degrees, the homogeneous components of an element of $\ker \varphi$ lie in $\ker \varphi$, so $\ker \varphi$ contains a nonzero homogeneous polynomial $F$ of some degree $e$. Write $P = k[X_1, \dots, X_d]$ and $P_n$ for its degree $n$ part, of dimension $\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}$. Since $P$ is a domain, multiplication by $F$ is injective, so for $n \geq e$
$$
\dim_k \mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1} \leq \dim_k P_n - \dim_k F P_{n-e} = \binom{n+d-1}{d-1} - \binom{n-e+d-1}{d-1} .
$$
By additivity of length (Proposition P1.3.28) along $A \supseteq \mathfrak{m} \supseteq \dots \supseteq \mathfrak{m}^n$, and since the length of a $k$-vector space is its dimension (Corollary P1.3.29),
$$
\ell_A(A/\mathfrak{m}^n) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \dim_k \mathfrak{m}^j/\mathfrak{m}^{j+1} .
$$
The identity $\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \binom{j+d-1}{d-1} = \binom{n+d-1}{d}$ gives, for $n \geq e$,
$$
\ell_A(A/\mathfrak{m}^n) \leq c + \binom{n+d-1}{d} - \binom{n-e+d-1}{d}
$$
for a constant $c$ that accounts for the terms with $j < e$. The two binomial coefficients are polynomials in $n$ of degree $d$ with the same leading coefficient $1/d!$, so the right side is a polynomial of degree $d - 1$ in $n$. Then the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of $A$ (Proposition P1.5.32), which has a positive leading coefficient, has degree at most $d - 1$. This contradicts the dimension theorem P1.5.35, which says that its degree is $d$. So $\varphi$ is injective, hence an isomorphism.

Conversely, suppose $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A) \cong k[X_1, \dots, X_e]$ as graded $k$-algebras with $\deg X_i = 1$. Comparing the degree one parts gives $e = \dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$. The formula above gives $\ell_A(A/\mathfrak{m}^n) = \sum_{j < n} \binom{j+e-1}{e-1} = \binom{n+e-1}{e}$, a polynomial of degree $e$. By the dimension theorem, $d = e$, so $A$ is regular. $\square$

For $a \in A$ with $a \neq 0$, Krull's intersection theorem P1.6.6 gives $\bigcap_n \mathfrak{m}^n = 0$, so there is a largest $i$ with $a \in \mathfrak{m}^i$. This $i$ is the *order* $\operatorname{ord}(a)$, and the class $a^* = a + \mathfrak{m}^{i+1} \in \mathfrak{m}^i/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}$ is the *initial form* of $a$. It is nonzero. For a point of a variety, $a^*$ is the lowest degree part of the Taylor expansion of $a$.

**Corollary P1.9.3 (regular local rings are domains).** A regular local ring is a domain, and $\operatorname{ord}(ab) = \operatorname{ord}(a) + \operatorname{ord}(b)$ for nonzero $a$ and $b$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Let $a, b \neq 0$ with $\operatorname{ord}(a) = i$ and $\operatorname{ord}(b) = j$. By the definition of the product in $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A)$, $a^* b^* = ab + \mathfrak{m}^{i+j+1}$ in $\mathfrak{m}^{i+j}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+j+1}$. By the theorem on the associated graded ring, $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A)$ is a polynomial ring, hence a domain, so $a^*b^* \neq 0$. Then $ab \notin \mathfrak{m}^{i+j+1}$. So $ab \neq 0$, and $ab \in \mathfrak{m}^{i+j}$ gives $\operatorname{ord}(ab) = i + j$. $\square$

**Proposition P1.9.4 (quotients by parameters).** Let $A$ be regular of dimension $d$, let $x_1, \dots, x_i \in \mathfrak{m}$ and $I = (x_1, \dots, x_i)$. Then $A/I$ is regular of dimension $d - i$ if and only if $x_1, \dots, x_i$ is part of a regular system of parameters, that is, their images in $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ are linearly independent. In this case:

1. $I$ is a prime ideal of height $i$;
2. for $0 \leq j < i$, the image of $x_{j+1}$ is a nonzerodivisor in $A/(x_1, \dots, x_j)$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Write $B = A/I$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n} = \mathfrak{m}/I$. Then $\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^2 = \mathfrak{m}/(\mathfrak{m}^2 + I)$ is the quotient of $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ by the span of the images of $x_1, \dots, x_i$. If $r$ is the dimension of this span, then $\dim_k \mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^2 = d - r$. By Corollary P1.5.22, $\dim B \geq d - i$.

If the images are independent, then $r = i$ and $d - i \leq \dim B \leq \dim_k \mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^2 = d - i$. So $B$ is regular of dimension $d - i$. Conversely, if $B$ is regular of dimension $d - i$, then $d - r = d - i$, so $r = i$ and the images are independent.

Suppose the images are independent. For each $j \leq i$, the images of $x_1, \dots, x_j$ are independent too, so $A_j = A/(x_1, \dots, x_j)$ is regular of dimension $d - j$, and by the corollary it is a domain. So $(x_1, \dots, x_j)$ is prime. The image of $x_{j+1}$ in $A_j$ is not zero, since its image in the cotangent space $\mathfrak{m}/(\mathfrak{m}^2 + (x_1, \dots, x_j))$ of $A_j$ is not zero. In the domain $A_j$ it is a nonzerodivisor, which proves (2). The chain
$$
0 \subsetneq (x_1) \subsetneq (x_1, x_2) \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq (x_1, \dots, x_i)
$$
consists of primes, and each inclusion is strict by the same remark. So $\operatorname{ht} I \geq i$. Krull's height theorem P1.5.18 gives $\operatorname{ht} I \leq i$. $\square$

Chapter 10 calls a sequence with property (2) a *regular sequence*. So a regular system of parameters is a regular sequence of length $\dim A$, and Chapter 10 deduces that a regular local ring is Cohen-Macaulay. The case $i = 1$ says: for $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$, the ring $A/xA$ is regular of dimension $d - 1$, and $x$ is a prime element of $A$. Section 9.3 uses this for an induction on the dimension.

**Proposition P1.9.5 (regularity and completion).** The ring $A$ is regular if and only if its $\mathfrak{m}$-adic completion $\hat{A}$ is regular. If $A$ is regular and contains a field, then $\hat{A} \cong k[[X_1, \dots, X_d]]$, and for a regular system of parameters $x_1, \dots, x_d$ the isomorphism can be chosen with $X_i \mapsto x_i$.

Lean: not in Mathlib in this form. Mathlib `AdicCompletion.spanFinrank_maximalIdeal_eq` gives the equality of the numbers of generators of the maximal ideals (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* By Theorem P1.6.18 (3), $A$ and $\hat{A}$ have the same dimension and the same embedding dimension. If $A$ contains a field, so does $\hat{A}$, and $\hat{A}$ is complete with $\dim \hat{A} = \dim_k \mathfrak{m}\hat{A}/\mathfrak{m}^2 \hat{A} = d$. Corollary P1.6.28 gives $\hat{A} \cong k[[X_1, \dots, X_d]]$. Its proof, through the Cohen structure theorem P1.6.27, sends the variables to a chosen set of $d$ generators of $\mathfrak{m}\hat{A}$, and the images of $x_1, \dots, x_d$ generate $\mathfrak{m}\hat{A}$. $\square$

So a regular local ring containing a field is, after completion, a power series ring: formally, every smooth point of a variety looks like the origin of affine space. In particular $\hat{A}$ is a domain. A regular point is analytically irreducible, unlike the node of Example P1.6.30.

### Proof in words

The dimension theorem identifies $\dim A$ with the growth rate of $\ell(A/\mathfrak{m}^n)$. If $\mathfrak{m}$ has $d$ generators and $\dim A = d$, then the monomials of degree $n$ in the generators must be independent modulo $\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$, since one relation would slow the growth to that of a ring of dimension $d - 1$. So the associated graded ring is a polynomial ring. A polynomial ring is a domain, and the product of the lowest order terms of two elements is the lowest order term of the product, so $A$ is a domain too. Cutting by part of a regular system of parameters lowers the dimension and the embedding dimension by the same amount, which gives a chain of prime ideals. Completion keeps both numbers, so regularity is a formal property, and Cohen's theorem identifies the completion with a power series ring.

### Examples

**Example P1.9.6 (affine space).** Let $R = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ and let $M$ be a maximal ideal. Then $R_M$ is regular of dimension $n$. By Corollary P1.5.42, $\operatorname{ht} M = n - \dim R/M = n$, so $\dim R_M = n$, and it remains to generate $M$ by $n$ elements. For $n = 0$ there is nothing to prove. Let $n \geq 1$, $R' = k[x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}]$ and $M' = M \cap R'$. By Zariski's lemma P1.4.28, $R/M$ is a finite extension of $k$, and $R'/M' \subseteq R/M$ is a domain of finite dimension over $k$, hence a field. So $M'$ is maximal, and by induction it is generated by $n - 1$ elements. Put $L = R'/M'$. Then $R/M'R \cong L[x_n]$ is a principal ideal domain, and $M/M'R$ is generated by one polynomial $\bar{g}$. A lift $g \in R$ gives $M = M'R + gR$, with $n$ generators. If $k$ is algebraically closed, then $M = (x_1 - a_1, \dots, x_n - a_n)$ by the Nullstellensatz, and the $n$ generators are visible.

**Example P1.9.7 (arithmetic regular rings).** The ring $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ is a discrete valuation ring, hence regular of dimension $1$, and so is $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]_{(p, x)}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (p, x)$. The chain $0 \subsetneq (p) \subsetneq (p, x)$ and Krull's height theorem give $\dim R = 2$, so $R$ is regular with regular system of parameters $p, x$. The element $x^2 - p$ is not in $\mathfrak{m}^2 = (p^2, px, x^2)$, so by the proposition on quotients $R/(x^2 - p)$ is regular of dimension $1$: it is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $x$, a localization of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{p}]$. The element $x^2 - p^2 = (x - p)(x + p)$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}^2$, and $R/(x^2 - p^2)$ is not a domain, so it is not regular.

**Example P1.9.8 (hypersurfaces).** Let $R$ be regular of dimension $n$, let $f \in \mathfrak{m}_R$ be nonzero and $B = R/(f)$. Then $\dim B = n - 1$: Corollary P1.5.22 gives $\dim B \geq n - 1$, and a chain of primes of $R$ containing $f$ can be extended by the prime $0$, since $R$ is a domain and $f \neq 0$. The cotangent space of $B$ is $\mathfrak{m}_R/(\mathfrak{m}_R^2 + (f))$, of dimension $n - 1$ if $f \notin \mathfrak{m}_R^2$ and $n$ if $f \in \mathfrak{m}_R^2$. So $B$ is regular if and only if $\operatorname{ord}(f) = 1$. For $R = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]_{(x_1, \dots, x_n)}$ this says: the hypersurface $f = 0$ is regular at the origin if and only if $f$ has a nonzero linear term. For a plane curve, $\operatorname{ord}(f)$ is the multiplicity of Example P1.5.37.

**Example P1.9.9 (the standard singular points).** By the example on hypersurfaces, each of the following local rings at the origin is a domain of dimension $n - 1$ that is not regular, since the equation has order $2$:

- the cusp $y^2 = x^3$ and the node $y^2 = x^2 + x^3$ in the plane, with $\dim = 1$ and embedding dimension $2$;
- the quadric cone $xy = z^2$ and the cones $xy = z^m$ in $3$-space, with $\dim = 2$ and embedding dimension $3$;
- the cone $xw = yz$ over a quadric surface in $4$-space, with $\dim = 3$ and embedding dimension $4$.

The coordinate cross $xy = 0$ is not regular at the origin either, and here the corollary gives a second reason: the ring is not a domain. The class groups computed in Section 8.2 for the cones are not zero, and Section 9.3 explains why: a regular local ring is factorial.

**Example P1.9.10 (the graded ring of a singular domain).** For the cusp $A = k[x, y]_{(x, y)}/(y^2 - x^3)$, the order of $y^2 - x^3$ is $2$ with initial form $y^2$, and $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A) = k[X, Y]/(Y^2)$. This ring is not a domain, while $A$ is a domain. So the associated graded ring of a domain need not be a domain, and the proof of the corollary needs regularity. Geometrically, the tangent cone of the cusp is the doubled line $Y^2 = 0$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that a Noetherian local ring is regular of dimension $0$ if and only if it is a field, and regular of dimension $1$ if and only if it is a discrete valuation ring.
2. (A) Let $A = k[x, y, z]_{(x, y, z)}/(xy, xz, yz)$, the local ring of the three coordinate axes at the origin. Compute $\dim A$ and the embedding dimension of $A$, and show that $A$ is not regular.
3. (A) Let $A = k[[t^2, t^3]] \subseteq k[[t]]$. Show that $A \cong k[[x, y]]/(y^2 - x^3)$, that $\dim A = 1$ and that the embedding dimension is $2$.
4. (B) Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring and $x \in \mathfrak{m}$ a nonzerodivisor such that $A/xA$ is regular. Show that $A$ is regular. (Hint: $x$ lies in no associated prime by Proposition P1.2.44, hence in no minimal prime, so $\dim A/xA \leq \dim A - 1$, and the embedding dimension drops by at most one.)
5. (B) Let $A$ be regular of dimension $d$. Show that $\ell_A(A/\mathfrak{m}^n) = \binom{n+d-1}{d}$ for all $n$, so that the multiplicity of $A$ is $1$.
6. (B) Compute $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{m}(A)$ for the node $A = k[x, y]_{(x, y)}/(y^2 - x^2 - x^3)$, with $k$ of characteristic not $2$, and use the theorem on the associated graded ring to show again that $A$ is not regular.
7. (C) Let $A$ be regular of dimension $d$, and let $I$ be an ideal such that $A/I$ is regular of dimension $d - i$. Show that $I$ is generated by part of a regular system of parameters of length $i$. (Hint: choose $x_1, \dots, x_i \in I$ whose images span $(I + \mathfrak{m}^2)/\mathfrak{m}^2$, and compare the domains $A/(x_1, \dots, x_i)$ and $A/I$, which have the same dimension.)
8. (C) Let $A$ be regular of dimension $d \geq 1$ and $x \in \mathfrak{m}$ with $\operatorname{ord}(x) = r$. Show that $xA \cap \mathfrak{m}^n = x\mathfrak{m}^{n-r}$ for $n \geq r$, and deduce that $\ell(A/(xA + \mathfrak{m}^n)) = \binom{n+d-1}{d} - \binom{n-r+d-1}{d}$. Conclude that the multiplicity of $A/xA$ is $r$, which extends Example P1.5.37.

### Lean

The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Krull introduced regular local rings in the 1930s, and Chevalley and Zariski studied them in the 1940s as the local rings of simple points, Zariski's term for smooth points. The theorem on the associated graded ring and the proof that a regular local ring is a domain follow Matsumura, Chapter 5, and Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapter 11. Cohen's structure theorem of 1946 identifies a complete regular local ring containing a field with a power series ring, and also describes the complete regular local rings of mixed characteristic. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Matsumura, Chapter 5, Eisenbud, Chapter 10, and Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapter 11.
