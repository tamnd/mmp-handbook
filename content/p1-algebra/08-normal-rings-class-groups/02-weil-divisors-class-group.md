## Weil divisors and the class group

### Statement

Throughout, $A$ is a Noetherian normal domain with fraction field $K$, and $v_\mathfrak{p}$ is the valuation of the DVR $A_\mathfrak{p}$ for a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of height $1$, as in Section 8.1.

**Definition P1.8.9 (Weil divisors and the class group).** The group of *Weil divisors* $\operatorname{Div}(A)$ is the free abelian group on the primes of height $1$ of $A$. Its elements are finite sums $D = \sum n_\mathfrak{p} [\mathfrak{p}]$ with $n_\mathfrak{p} \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $D$ is *effective*, $D \geq 0$, if all $n_\mathfrak{p} \geq 0$. For $f \in K^*$ the *divisor of $f$* is
$$
\operatorname{div}(f) = \sum_{\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1} v_\mathfrak{p}(f) [\mathfrak{p}] ,
$$
a finite sum by the remark after the algebraic Hartogs theorem in Section 8.1. The divisors $\operatorname{div}(f)$ are *principal*, and two divisors that differ by a principal divisor are *linearly equivalent*. The *class group* of $A$ is
$$
\operatorname{Cl}(A) = \operatorname{Div}(A) / \{\operatorname{div}(f) : f \in K^*\} .
$$

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.weilDivisors` and `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.IsWeilDivisor` (Weil divisors on a scheme, definitions). Principal divisors and this class group are not in Mathlib. The Mathlib `ClassGroup` is a different group, see the Lean part.

Since each $v_\mathfrak{p}$ is a homomorphism, $\operatorname{div}: K^* \to \operatorname{Div}(A)$ is a homomorphism. By the algebraic Hartogs theorem, $\operatorname{div}(f) \geq 0$ if and only if $f \in A$, and $\operatorname{div}(f) = 0$ if and only if $f \in A^*$. So there is an exact sequence
$$
1 \to A^* \to K^* \xrightarrow{\operatorname{div}} \operatorname{Div}(A) \to \operatorname{Cl}(A) \to 0 .
$$
For a normal affine variety $X$ with coordinate ring $A$, a prime of height one is a prime divisor of $X$, $v_\mathfrak{p} = \operatorname{ord}_D$, and $\operatorname{div}(f)$ records the zeros and poles of the rational function $f$ with multiplicities.

**Proposition P1.8.10 (class group of a factorial ring).** The class group $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ is zero if and only if $A$ is a unique factorization domain. More precisely, for a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of height $1$, $[\mathfrak{p}] = 0$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ if and only if $\mathfrak{p}$ is principal.

Lean: Mathlib `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_forall_isPrincipal_of_height_eq_one` (proved in Mathlib), the criterion of Proposition P1.5.23.

*Proof.* Suppose $\mathfrak{p} = \pi A$. Then $\mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p} = \pi A_\mathfrak{p}$, so $v_\mathfrak{p}(\pi) = 1$. If $\mathfrak{q}$ is another prime of height one and $\pi \in \mathfrak{q}$, then $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$ and $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{q}$, since both have height $1$. So $\operatorname{div}(\pi) = [\mathfrak{p}]$. Conversely, suppose $[\mathfrak{p}] = \operatorname{div}(f)$. Then $f \in A$, since $\operatorname{div}(f) \geq 0$, and the proposition on symbolic powers and principal ideals in Section 8.1 gives $fA = \mathfrak{p}^{(1)} = \mathfrak{p}$. The group $\operatorname{Div}(A)$ is generated by the $[\mathfrak{p}]$, so $\operatorname{Cl}(A) = 0$ if and only if every prime of height one is principal, if and only if $A$ is factorial by Proposition P1.5.23. $\square$

**Theorem P1.8.11 (Nagata's localization sequence).** Let $S \subseteq A$ be a multiplicative set with $0 \notin S$. Then $S^{-1}A$ is a Noetherian normal domain, and there is an exact sequence
$$
\bigoplus_{\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1,\ \mathfrak{p} \cap S \neq \emptyset} \mathbb{Z} [\mathfrak{p}] \longrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(A) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(S^{-1}A) \longrightarrow 0 ,
$$
where the first map sends $[\mathfrak{p}]$ to its class, and the second sends $[\mathfrak{p}]$ to $[S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}]$ if $\mathfrak{p} \cap S = \emptyset$ and to $0$ otherwise.

Lean: not in Mathlib in this form. The special case for a prime element $x$, that $A$ is factorial if and only if $A_x$ is, is `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_localizationAway_of_prime` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* The ring $S^{-1}A$ is Noetherian and normal by Proposition P1.4.20. By Theorem P1.2.6 its primes are the $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}$ with $\mathfrak{p} \cap S = \emptyset$, with the same inclusions, so its primes of height one are the $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}$ with $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$ and $\mathfrak{p} \cap S = \emptyset$. For these, $(S^{-1}A)_{S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}} = A_\mathfrak{p}$ inside $K$, so the valuation of $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}$ is $v_\mathfrak{p}$. Let $\varphi: \operatorname{Div}(A) \to \operatorname{Div}(S^{-1}A)$ be the homomorphism of the statement. It is surjective, its kernel is spanned by the $[\mathfrak{p}]$ with $\mathfrak{p} \cap S \neq \emptyset$, and $\varphi(\operatorname{div}_A(f)) = \operatorname{div}_{S^{-1}A}(f)$ by the equality of valuations. So $\varphi$ induces a surjection $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \to \operatorname{Cl}(S^{-1}A)$. If $\varphi(D) = \operatorname{div}_{S^{-1}A}(f)$, then $\varphi(D - \operatorname{div}_A(f)) = 0$, so $D$ is linearly equivalent to a combination of primes that meet $S$. This is exactness in the middle. $\square$

The sequence says that removing a closed subset $Z$ from a variety kills exactly the classes of the prime divisors inside $Z$. For $S = \{1, x, x^2, \dots\}$ the localization $A_x$ is the ring of the open set $D(x)$, and the primes of height one that meet $S$ are the components of the zero set of $x$, finitely many by Krull's theorem. So if $A_x$ is factorial, then $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ is generated by the classes of the components of $V(x)$. This is how the class groups below are computed.

**Theorem P1.8.12 (class group of a polynomial ring).** The ring $A[x]$ is a Noetherian normal domain, and $[\mathfrak{p}] \mapsto [\mathfrak{p}A[x]]$ induces an isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(A[x])$.

Lean: the instance that $A[x]$ is integrally closed when $A$ is, in `Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/IsIntegral.lean`, and `Polynomial.uniqueFactorizationMonoid` for the factorial case (proved in Mathlib). The isomorphism of class groups is not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* The ring $A[x]$ is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem (Theorem P1.3.10). For normality, recall Gauss's lemma: a polynomial ring over a factorial domain is factorial. So $K[x]$ and every $A_\mathfrak{p}[x]$, with $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$, are factorial, hence normal by Proposition P1.4.19. All these rings have fraction field $K(x)$. Let $F \in K(x)$ be integral over $A[x]$. Then $F$ is integral over $K[x]$ and over each $A_\mathfrak{p}[x]$, so $F \in K[x]$ and every coefficient of $F$ lies in every $A_\mathfrak{p}$. By Krull's theorem the coefficients lie in $A$, so $F \in A[x]$.

Let $S = A \setminus \{0\}$. Then $S^{-1}(A[x]) = K[x]$ is factorial, and Nagata's sequence shows that $\operatorname{Cl}(A[x])$ is generated by the primes $P$ of height one with $P \cap A \neq 0$. Such primes are of the form $\mathfrak{p}A[x]$. Indeed, let $\mathfrak{p} = P \cap A \neq 0$. The ideal $\mathfrak{p}A[x]$ is prime, since $A[x]/\mathfrak{p}A[x] = (A/\mathfrak{p})[x]$ is a domain, and $0 \neq \mathfrak{p}A[x] \subseteq P$, so $P = \mathfrak{p}A[x]$. Also $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$: a chain $0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}' \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}$ would give the chain $0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}'A[x] \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}A[x]$, strict because $\mathfrak{p}A[x] \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$.

Conversely, let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime of $A$ of height $1$. Then $\mathfrak{p}A[x]$ has height $1$. Suppose $0 \neq Q \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}A[x]$ is prime. If $Q \cap A \neq 0$, then $Q \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$, since $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$, and $\mathfrak{p}A[x] \subseteq Q$, which is false. So $Q \cap A = 0$. Let $t \in \mathfrak{p}$ be a uniformizer of $A_\mathfrak{p}$. The ring $A_\mathfrak{p}[x]$ is the localization of $A[x]$ at $A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$, so $QA_\mathfrak{p}[x]$ is a prime, and it lies in $\mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p}[x] = tA_\mathfrak{p}[x]$. Also $t \notin QA_\mathfrak{p}[x]$: otherwise $st \in Q \cap A = 0$ for some $s \in A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$. Take $h \neq 0$ in $Q$ and write $h = t^e h'$ in $A_\mathfrak{p}[x]$, where $h'$ has a coefficient that is a unit of $A_\mathfrak{p}$. Then $h' \in QA_\mathfrak{p}[x] \subseteq tA_\mathfrak{p}[x]$, which is false.

The local ring $A[x]_{\mathfrak{p}A[x]}$ contains $A_\mathfrak{p}$, and its maximal ideal is generated by $\mathfrak{p}$, hence by $t$. So $v_{\mathfrak{p}A[x]}$ restricts to $v_\mathfrak{p}$ on $K$. For a prime $Q$ of height one with $Q \cap A = 0$, every nonzero element of $A$ is a unit in $A[x]_Q$, so $v_Q$ is zero on $K^*$. Hence the injective map $\psi: \operatorname{Div}(A) \to \operatorname{Div}(A[x])$, $[\mathfrak{p}] \mapsto [\mathfrak{p}A[x]]$, satisfies $\psi(\operatorname{div}_A(f)) = \operatorname{div}_{A[x]}(f)$ for $f \in K^*$, and induces a map $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \to \operatorname{Cl}(A[x])$. It is surjective by the first part. For injectivity, suppose $\psi(D) = \operatorname{div}_{A[x]}(F)$ with $F \in K(x)^*$. Then $v_Q(F) = 0$ for every prime $Q$ of height one with $Q \cap A = 0$, so the divisor of $F$ on $K[x]$ is zero, since the primes of height one of $K[x]$ are the $S^{-1}Q$ with the same valuations (proof of Nagata's sequence), and $F$ is a unit of $K[x]$ by the algebraic Hartogs theorem. So $F \in K^*$, and $D = \operatorname{div}_A(F)$, since $\psi$ is injective. $\square$

To compute class groups of rings of invariants, one more tool is needed.

**Lemma P1.8.13 (graded pieces of a normal domain).** Let $G$ be an abelian group and $B = \bigoplus_{g \in G} B_g$ a $G$-graded domain, with $B_g B_h \subseteq B_{g + h}$. If $B$ is normal, then $B_0$ is a normal domain.

Lean: not in Mathlib in this form.

*Proof.* The ring $B_0$ is a subring of $B$, so a domain, and $\operatorname{Frac} B_0 \subseteq \operatorname{Frac} B$. Let $y \in \operatorname{Frac} B_0$ be integral over $B_0$. Then $y$ is integral over $B$, so $y \in B$. Write $y = f/g$ with $f, g \in B_0$ and $g \neq 0$, and $y = \sum_h y_h$ with $y_h \in B_h$. Then $\sum_h y_h g = f$, and $y_h g \in B_h$. Comparing components gives $y_h g = 0$ for $h \neq 0$, so $y_h = 0$ for $h \neq 0$, and $y = y_0 \in B_0$. $\square$

### Proof in words

A Weil divisor is a formal combination of prime divisors, and the class group measures how many of them are not cut out by a single function. A ring is factorial exactly when each prime divisor is the zero set of a function, with multiplicity one. Nagata's sequence localizes the problem: on an open set where the ring is factorial, every divisor is principal, so the class group is generated by the prime divisors in the complement. The relations among these generators come from the functions whose zeros and poles lie in the complement, and in the examples these functions are units of the open set.

The polynomial ring theorem says that $X \times \mathbb{A}^1$ has the same class group as $X$. A prime divisor of $X \times \mathbb{A}^1$ either dominates $X$, and then it is principal because $K[x]$ is factorial, or it is the pullback of a prime divisor of $X$.

### Examples

**Example P1.8.14 (the cone $xy = z^n$).** Let $n \geq 2$ and $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^n)$, the surface singularity of type $A_{n-1}$. Then $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$, generated by the class of the line $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$.

First, $A$ is a ring of invariants. Let $B = k[u, v]$, graded by $\mathbb{Z}/n$ with $\deg u = 1$ and $\deg v = -1$. Then $B_0$ is spanned by the monomials $u^a v^b$ with $a \equiv b \bmod n$, and such a monomial with $a \geq b$ is $(uv)^b (u^n)^{(a-b)/n}$, and similarly for $a < b$. So $B_0 = k[u^n, v^n, uv]$. The homomorphism $k[x, y, z] \to B_0$ with $x \mapsto u^n$, $y \mapsto v^n$, $z \mapsto uv$ is surjective, and its kernel $P$ contains $xy - z^n$. The ring $B$ is integral over $B_0$, so $\dim B_0 = 2$ (Corollary P1.4.14), and $\operatorname{ht} P = 1$ by the dimension formula (Corollary P1.5.42). The polynomial $xy - z^n$ is irreducible, since it has degree one in $x$ and $y$ is prime to $z^n$, so $(xy - z^n)$ is a prime of height one inside $P$, and $P = (xy - z^n)$. Hence $A \cong B_0$, and $A$ is a Noetherian normal domain by the lemma.

Second, the divisor of $x$. A prime of height one that contains $x$ contains $z^n = xy$, so it contains $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$, and it equals $\mathfrak{p}$. Since $A/\mathfrak{p} = k[y]$, the element $y$ is a unit in $A_\mathfrak{p}$, so $x = z^n/y$ and $\mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p} = (z)$. So $v_\mathfrak{p}(z) = 1$, $v_\mathfrak{p}(x) = n$ and $\operatorname{div}(x) = n[\mathfrak{p}]$.

Third, $A_x = k[x, x^{-1}, z]$, since $y = z^n/x$ in $A_x$, and $x$ and $z$ are algebraically independent, as $u^n$ and $uv$ are. This is a localization of the factorial ring $k[x, z]$, so it is factorial, and its units are the $c x^j$ with $c \in k^*$ and $j \in \mathbb{Z}$. By Nagata's sequence $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ is generated by $[\mathfrak{p}]$, and $n[\mathfrak{p}] = 0$.

Finally, suppose $m[\mathfrak{p}] = \operatorname{div}(g)$ with $g \in K^*$. The divisor of $g$ on $A_x$ is zero, so $g$ is a unit of $A_x$, $g = cx^j$, and $m = v_\mathfrak{p}(cx^j) = jn$. So $[\mathfrak{p}]$ has order exactly $n$, and $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$. For $n = 2$ this is the quadric cone of Example P1.5.24, and $2[\mathfrak{p}] = \operatorname{div}(x)$ is the relation $\mathfrak{p}^{(2)} = (x)$ of Section 8.1.

**Example P1.8.15 (the cone over a quadric surface).** Let $A = k[x, y, z, w]/(xw - yz)$. Then $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the class of the plane $\mathfrak{p} = (x, y)$.

Let $B = k[s, t, u, v]$, graded by $\mathbb{Z}$ with $\deg s = \deg t = 1$ and $\deg u = \deg v = -1$. A monomial $s^a t^b u^c v^d$ lies in $B_0$ when $a + b = c + d$, and then it is a product of $a + b$ factors from $su, sv, tu, tv$: pair each $s$ or $t$ with a $u$ or a $v$. So $B_0 = k[su, sv, tu, tv]$. The map $x \mapsto su$, $y \mapsto sv$, $z \mapsto tu$, $w \mapsto tv$ is a surjection $k[x, y, z, w] \to B_0$ that kills $xw - yz$. The fraction field of $B_0$ is $k(su, sv, tu)$, since $tv = (sv)(tu)/(su)$, and these three monomials are algebraically independent, since their exponent vectors are linearly independent. So $\dim B_0 = 3$ (Theorem P1.4.31), the kernel has height $1$, and as before it equals $(xw - yz)$, a prime because $xw - yz$ has degree one in $x$ and $w$ is prime to $yz$. So $A \cong B_0$ is a Noetherian normal domain of dimension $3$.

A prime that contains $x$ contains $yz$, so it contains $\mathfrak{p} = (x, y)$ or $\mathfrak{p}' = (x, z)$. Both are prime, with $A/\mathfrak{p} = k[z, w]$ and $A/\mathfrak{p}' = k[y, w]$, and neither contains the other. So they are the minimal primes of $xA$, of height one by the principal ideal theorem. In $A_\mathfrak{p}$ the element $z$ is a unit and $y = xw/z$, so $\mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p} = (x)$ and $v_\mathfrak{p}(x) = 1$. In the same way $v_{\mathfrak{p}'}(x) = 1$, and $\operatorname{div}(x) = [\mathfrak{p}] + [\mathfrak{p}']$.

In $A_x$ one has $w = yz/x$, so $A_x = k[x, x^{-1}, y, z]$ with $x, y, z$ algebraically independent. It is factorial with units $cx^j$. By Nagata's sequence, $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ is generated by $[\mathfrak{p}]$ and $[\mathfrak{p}'] = -[\mathfrak{p}]$. If $m[\mathfrak{p}] = \operatorname{div}(g)$, then $g$ is a unit of $A_x$, $g = cx^j$, and $m[\mathfrak{p}] = j[\mathfrak{p}] + j[\mathfrak{p}']$. So $j = 0$ and $m = 0$. Hence $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. The planes $V(\mathfrak{p})$ and $V(\mathfrak{p}')$ through the vertex belong to the two rulings of the quadric surface $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, and their classes are inverse to each other.

**Example P1.8.16 (Dedekind domains).** Let $A$ be a Dedekind domain. Its primes of height one are its nonzero primes, so $\operatorname{Div}(A)$ is the free abelian group on the maximal ideals. By unique factorization of ideals (Theorem P1.7.22), $I \mapsto \sum v_\mathfrak{p}(I) [\mathfrak{p}]$ is an isomorphism from the group of fractional ideals of Section 7.3 to $\operatorname{Div}(A)$, and it sends $fA$ to $\operatorname{div}(f)$. So $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ is the class group of Definition P1.7.24. For instance, $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, and the polynomial ring theorem gives $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}][x]) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove that $\operatorname{div}(fg) = \operatorname{div}(f) + \operatorname{div}(g)$ and the exactness of $1 \to A^* \to K^* \to \operatorname{Div}(A) \to \operatorname{Cl}(A) \to 0$ in detail.
2. (A) Let $f \in A$ be a prime element. Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(A) \to \operatorname{Cl}(A_f)$ is an isomorphism. Deduce that $\operatorname{Cl}(A[x, x^{-1}]) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(A)$.
3. (A) On the quadric cone $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$ let $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$ and $\mathfrak{q} = (y, z)$. Show that $\operatorname{div}(y) = 2[\mathfrak{q}]$ and $\operatorname{div}(z) = [\mathfrak{p}] + [\mathfrak{q}]$, and deduce that $[\mathfrak{q}] = [\mathfrak{p}]$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$.
4. (B) Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(A[x_1, \dots, x_n]) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(A)$. Find a prime of height one of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}][x]$ that is not principal.
5. (B) On $A = k[x, y, z, w]/(xw - yz)$, with $\mathfrak{p} = (x, y)$, $\mathfrak{p}' = (x, z)$ and $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y, z, w)$, show that $\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{p}' = x\mathfrak{m}$. Let $\mathfrak{q}' = (y, w)$. Show that $\operatorname{div}(y) = [\mathfrak{p}] + [\mathfrak{q}']$, and deduce that $[\mathfrak{q}'] = [\mathfrak{p}']$.
6. (B) Prove Nagata's criterion: if $A$ is a Noetherian domain, $x \in A$ is a prime element and $A_x$ is factorial, then $A$ is factorial. (Hint: by Proposition P1.5.23, take a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of height one with $x \notin \mathfrak{p}$, write $\mathfrak{p}A_x = fA_x$ with $f \in \mathfrak{p}$ not divisible by $x$, and show $\mathfrak{p} = fA$.)
7. (B) Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^n)$ with $n \geq 2$. Using the grading of $A = k[u^n, v^n, uv]$ by $\mathbb{Z}^2$, with $u^a v^b$ in degree $(a, b)$, show that a factor of a monomial in $A$ is a scalar times a monomial. Deduce that $x$, $y$ and $z$ are irreducible, and that $xy = z^n$ are two different factorizations.
8. (C) In the cone $xy = z^n$, let $B = k[u, v]$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$. Show that $\mathfrak{p} = uB \cap A$, and that the $u$-adic valuation of $\operatorname{Frac} B$ restricts to $v_\mathfrak{p}$ on $K$. (Hint: its valuation ring contains $A_\mathfrak{p}$, and Proposition P1.7.2 describes the rings between a DVR and its fraction field.) Deduce that $\mathfrak{p}^{(j)} = (x, z^j)$ for $1 \leq j \leq n$.

### Lean

Mathlib defines algebraic cycles on a scheme, `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle`, as functions with locally finite support, and the Weil divisors `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.weilDivisors` as the cycles supported in codimension one. It does not yet define the divisor of a rational function or the Weil class group. The Mathlib `ClassGroup R` of a domain is the group of invertible fractional ideals modulo principal ones, which `ClassGroup.equivPic` identifies with the Picard group. For a Dedekind domain it agrees with $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ here, and in general it is the subgroup of Section 8.3. The factoriality criteria `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_forall_isPrincipal_of_height_eq_one` and `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_localizationAway_of_prime` are in `Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/UFD.lean`. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Weil divisors are named after André Weil, and the divisor class group of a Krull domain was developed by Krull and later by Samuel, whose lecture notes of 1964 on unique factorization computed many examples. Nagata's sequence appears in his work on factoriality in the 1950s. The class group of the cones $xy = z^n$ is a standard example, and $\mathbb{Z}/n$ is the local class group of the Du Val singularity of type $A_{n-1}$ of Volume 4. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Matsumura, Chapter 4, Eisenbud, Chapter 11, and Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapter 9.
