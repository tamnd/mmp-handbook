## Hilbert series and dimension of graded rings

### Statement

Throughout, $k$ is a field and $A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_n$ is a graded ring with $A_0 = k$, generated as a $k$-algebra by homogeneous elements $x_1, \dots, x_s$ of degrees $d_1, \dots, d_s > 0$. Put $\mathfrak{m} = A_+$, a maximal ideal with $A/\mathfrak{m} = k$. The ring $A$ is *standard graded* if it is generated by $A_1$. For a finitely generated graded $A$-module $M$, each $M_n$ is a finite dimensional $k$-vector space, spanned by the products of monomials in the $x_i$ with finitely many homogeneous generators of $M$, and $M_n = 0$ for $n$ small enough.

**Definition P1.13.12 (Hilbert function and Hilbert series).** The *Hilbert function* of $M$ is $H_M(n) = \dim_k M_n$, and the *Hilbert series* is the formal Laurent series
$$
\operatorname{HS}_M(t) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} H_M(n)\, t^n \in \mathbb{Z}((t)) .
$$

Lean: not in Mathlib in this form; Mathlib has `Polynomial.hilbertPoly` (definition), the polynomial whose values are eventually the coefficients of $p(t)/(1 - t)^d$.

**Theorem P1.13.13 (Hilbert and Serre).** There is a Laurent polynomial $Q_M \in \mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]$ with
$$
\operatorname{HS}_M(t) = \frac{Q_M(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^s (1 - t^{d_i})} .
$$

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Induction on $s$. If $s = 0$, then $A = k$, $M$ is finite dimensional and $\operatorname{HS}_M$ is a Laurent polynomial. Let $s \geq 1$ and $d = d_s$. Multiplication by $x_s$ gives exact sequences of vector spaces
$$
0 \to K_n \to M_n \xrightarrow{\ x_s\ } M_{n+d} \to L_{n+d} \to 0 ,
$$
where $K$ and $L = M/x_s M$ are the kernel and the cokernel. They are finitely generated graded modules, since $A$ is Noetherian, and they are killed by $x_s$, so they are modules over $A/(x_s)$, which is generated by the images of $x_1, \dots, x_{s-1}$. Multiply the relation $\dim M_{n+d} - \dim M_n = \dim L_{n+d} - \dim K_n$ by $t^{n+d}$ and sum over $n$:
$$
(1 - t^d)\operatorname{HS}_M(t) = \operatorname{HS}_L(t) - t^d \operatorname{HS}_K(t) .
$$
The induction hypothesis applies to $K$ and $L$, and the formula follows. $\square$

**Corollary P1.13.14 (cutting by a nonzerodivisor).** Let $y \in A_e$, $e > 0$, be a nonzerodivisor on $M$. Then $\operatorname{HS}_{M/yM}(t) = (1 - t^e)\operatorname{HS}_M(t)$. If $y_1, \dots, y_c$ is a regular sequence on $M$ of homogeneous elements of degrees $e_j$, then $\operatorname{HS}_{M/(y_1, \dots, y_c)M} = \prod_j (1 - t^{e_j}) \operatorname{HS}_M$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* The sequence $0 \to M(-e) \xrightarrow{y} M \to M/yM \to 0$ is exact and graded, and $\operatorname{HS}_{M(-e)} = t^e \operatorname{HS}_M$. Induction gives the second statement. $\square$

**Lemma P1.13.15 (dimension at the vertex).** Every minimal prime of $A$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}$, and $\dim A = \dim A_\mathfrak{m}$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* The minimal primes of $A$ are the minimal primes of the homogeneous ideal $0$, so they are homogeneous by Proposition P1.13.3, and a proper homogeneous ideal lies in $\mathfrak{m}$ by Corollary P1.13.7. For a minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}$, the ring $A/\mathfrak{p}$ is a finitely generated $k$-domain, and its maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{p}$ has height $\dim A/\mathfrak{p}$ by Corollary P1.5.42. So $\dim A_\mathfrak{m} \geq \dim A/\mathfrak{p}$ for every minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}$, and $\dim A = \max_\mathfrak{p} \dim A/\mathfrak{p} \leq \dim A_\mathfrak{m} \leq \dim A$. $\square$

**Theorem P1.13.16 (dimension and the Hilbert polynomial).** Let $A$ be standard graded, and let $P_A$ be the Hilbert polynomial of Theorem P1.5.30, so that $H_A(n) = P_A(n)$ for large $n$. Then $\dim A = \deg P_A + 1$, where the zero polynomial has degree $-1$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* The ideal $\mathfrak{m}^n$ is spanned by the homogeneous elements of degree at least $n$, since $A$ is generated by $A_1$, so $\dim_k A/\mathfrak{m}^n = \sum_{j < n} H_A(j)$. The ring $A/\mathfrak{m}^n$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^n$, so it equals its localization at $\mathfrak{m}$, and $\ell(A_\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^n A_\mathfrak{m}) = \sum_{j < n} H_A(j)$. By the dimension theorem P1.5.35, $\dim A_\mathfrak{m}$ is the degree of the polynomial that agrees with this function for large $n$. If $P_A \neq 0$, its leading coefficient is positive, since its values are eventually nonnegative, and the sum of the values of a polynomial of degree $e$ is a polynomial of degree $e + 1$. If $P_A = 0$, the sum is eventually constant, of degree $0$. With the lemma, $\dim A = \dim A_\mathfrak{m} = \deg P_A + 1$. $\square$

**Theorem P1.13.17 (graded Noether normalization).** Let $k$ be infinite and $d = \dim A$. There are homogeneous elements $y_1, \dots, y_d \in A$ of the same positive degree, algebraically independent over $k$, such that $A$ is a finitely generated module over $k[y_1, \dots, y_d]$. If $A$ is standard graded, the $y_i$ can be chosen in $A_1$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Let $e$ be a common multiple of the degrees $d_i$, with $e = 1$ if $A$ is standard graded, and put $b_i = x_i^{e/d_i} \in A_e$. A homogeneous prime $\mathfrak{p} \neq \mathfrak{m}$ does not contain $\mathfrak{m}$, since $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal, so it misses some $x_i$ and hence some $b_i$.

Suppose homogeneous $y_1, \dots, y_r \in A_e$ are chosen, with $r \geq 0$. If $\mathfrak{m}$ is a minimal prime of $(y_1, \dots, y_r)$, then it is the only one, since all minimal primes are homogeneous and lie in $\mathfrak{m}$, so $\dim A/(y_1, \dots, y_r) = 0$, and the construction stops. Otherwise let $\mathfrak{p}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{p}_m$ be the minimal primes of $(y_1, \dots, y_r)$, all homogeneous and different from $\mathfrak{m}$. For each $j$ the set of $\lambda \in k^s$ with $\sum_i \lambda_i b_i \in \mathfrak{p}_j$ is a proper linear subspace of $k^s$, since $\mathfrak{p}_j \cap A_e$ is a subspace that misses some $b_i$. Over an infinite field, $k^s$ is not a finite union of proper subspaces, so some $y_{r+1} = \sum_i \lambda_i b_i \in A_e$ lies in no $\mathfrak{p}_j$. Then every prime that contains $(y_1, \dots, y_{r+1})$ strictly contains some $\mathfrak{p}_j$, and $\dim A/(y_1, \dots, y_{r+1}) \leq \dim A/(y_1, \dots, y_r) - 1$. So the construction stops after $r \leq d$ steps.

When it stops, $\bar{A} = A/(y_1, \dots, y_r)$ is a graded ring whose only prime is $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$, so $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$ is nilpotent and $\bar{A}$ is finite dimensional over $k$. Let $u_1, \dots, u_q$ be homogeneous elements of $A$ whose images span $\bar{A}$, and $N = \sum_j k[y_1, \dots, y_r] u_j$. By induction on $n$, $A_n \subseteq N$: indeed $A_n \subseteq N + \sum_i y_i A_{n - e}$, and $A_{n-e} \subseteq N$ by induction. So $A$ is finite over $R = k[y_1, \dots, y_r]$. By Corollary P1.4.14, $\dim R = \dim A = d$, while $R$ is a quotient of a polynomial ring in $r \leq d$ variables. A quotient of $k[Y_1, \dots, Y_r]$ by a nonzero ideal has dimension less than $r$ (Corollary P1.5.42), so $r = d$ and the $y_i$ are algebraically independent. $\square$

Over a finite field the theorem holds with homogeneous $y_i$ of possibly different positive degrees (Eisenbud, Chapter 13), but not always with $y_i \in A_1$ (Exercise 8). Section 10.2 uses the theorem in this form.

**Theorem P1.13.18 (graded regular rings).** If $A_\mathfrak{m}$ is a regular local ring, then $A$ is a polynomial ring: $A = k[z_1, \dots, z_d]$ for homogeneous elements $z_i$ of positive degrees that are algebraically independent, with $d = \dim A$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Since $A_\mathfrak{m}$ is a regular local ring, it is a domain (Corollary P1.9.3), and $A \to A_\mathfrak{m}$ is injective by Corollary P1.13.7, so $A$ is a domain. By Lemma P1.13.15, $\dim A = \dim A_\mathfrak{m} = d$. Since $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal, $\mathfrak{m}A_\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 A_\mathfrak{m} = \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$, of dimension $d$. Choose homogeneous $z_1, \dots, z_d \in \mathfrak{m}$ whose images form a basis of $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$. By the graded Nakayama lemma they generate the ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, and by induction on the degree, as in Proposition P1.13.5, they generate $A$ as a $k$-algebra. So $A = k[Z_1, \dots, Z_d]/P$ for a prime $P$, and $\dim k[Z]/P = d$ forces $P = 0$ by Corollary P1.5.42. $\square$

### Proof in words

Multiplication by a generator of degree $d$ has a finite dimensional kernel and cokernel in each degree, and they are modules over a ring with fewer generators. Counting dimensions gives a factor $1 - t^d$ in the Hilbert series, and induction makes the Hilbert series a rational function with denominator $\prod (1 - t^{d_i})$. For a standard graded ring the partial sums of the Hilbert function are the Hilbert-Samuel function at the vertex, so the degree of the Hilbert polynomial is $\dim A - 1$. A graded ring can be cut down to dimension zero by homogeneous elements of one degree, chosen outside finitely many homogeneous primes, and then it is finite over the polynomial ring that they generate. A graded ring that is regular at the vertex is a polynomial ring, because minimal homogeneous generators of the irrelevant ideal generate the ring.

### Examples

**Example P1.13.19 (polynomial rings and hypersurfaces).** For $A = k[x_1, \dots, x_s]$ with the standard grading, $\operatorname{HS}_A = 1/(1 - t)^s$ and $H_A(n) = \binom{n + s - 1}{s - 1}$. For a hypersurface $A = k[x_1, \dots, x_s]/(f)$ with $f$ homogeneous of degree $e$, the corollary gives $\operatorname{HS}_A = (1 - t^e)/(1 - t)^s$. For a plane curve, $s = 3$, and for $n \geq e - 2$,
$$
H_A(n) = \binom{n + 2}{2} - \binom{n - e + 2}{2} = en + 1 - \frac{(e-1)(e-2)}{2} .
$$
The leading coefficient is the degree $e$, and the constant term is $1 - g$ with $g = (e-1)(e-2)/2$ the genus of a smooth plane curve of degree $e$.

**Example P1.13.20 (the twisted cubic and the quadric cone).** The Veronese ring $A = k[s, t]^{(3)}$, with $A_n$ spanned by the monomials of degree $3n$, has $H_A(n) = 3n + 1$ and $\operatorname{HS}_A = (1 + 2t)/(1 - t)^2$. It has dimension $2$, as Theorem P1.13.16 says. It is generated by the four elements of $A_1$ but it is not a polynomial ring, and its vertex is not regular, in agreement with Theorem P1.13.18. Similarly the quadric cone $k[s, t]^{(2)}$ has $\operatorname{HS} = (1 + t)/(1 - t)^2 = (1 - t^2)/(1 - t)^3$.

**Example P1.13.21 (a weighted hypersurface).** Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(x^2 + y^3 + z^5)$ with weights $15, 10, 6$. The equation has degree $30$ and is a nonzerodivisor on $k[x, y, z]$, so
$$
\operatorname{HS}_A(t) = \frac{1 - t^{30}}{(1 - t^{15})(1 - t^{10})(1 - t^{6})} .
$$

**Example P1.13.22 (an embedded point).** Let $A = k[x, y]/(x^2, xy)$. Then $A_0 = k$, $A_1 = kx \oplus ky$ and $A_n = k y^n$ for $n \geq 2$, so $H_A(n) = 1$ for $n \geq 2$ and $\operatorname{HS}_A = (1 + t - t^2)/(1 - t)$. So $\dim A = 1$, the dimension of the line $x = 0$. The embedded point at the origin changes only finitely many values of the Hilbert function.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute the Hilbert series and the Hilbert polynomial of $k[x, y, z, w]/(xw - yz)$, and check Theorem P1.13.16.
2. (A) Let $A = k[x, y, z, w]/(q_1, q_2)$, where $q_1, q_2$ are quadrics forming a regular sequence. Show that $\operatorname{HS}_A = (1 + t)^2/(1 - t)^2$ and $H_A(n) = 4n$ for $n \geq 1$.
3. (A) Let $A = k[s, t]^{(r)}$. Compute $\operatorname{HS}_A$ and show that $H_A(n) = rn + 1$.
4. (B) Let $A$ be standard graded and $\operatorname{HS}_A = Q(t)/(1 - t)^\delta$ with $Q(1) \neq 0$. Show that $\delta = \dim A$, and that $Q(1)$ is the multiplicity of $A_\mathfrak{m}$ in the sense of Section 5.3.
5. (B) Let $A$ be standard graded and Cohen-Macaulay at the vertex, with $k$ infinite and $y_1, \dots, y_d \in A_1$ as in Theorem P1.13.17. Show that $\operatorname{HS}_A = \operatorname{HS}_{A/(y)}(t)/(1 - t)^d$, so that the numerator has nonnegative coefficients.
6. (B) Let $k$ be infinite and $A = k[s^4, s^3t, st^3, t^4]$, graded with the four generators in degree one. Show that $\operatorname{HS}_A = (1 + 2t + 2t^2 - t^3)/(1 - t)^2$, and deduce from Exercise 5 that $A$ is not Cohen-Macaulay at the vertex, as Example P1.10.20 shows by another argument.
7. (B) Let $A$ be as in the section and $y \in A_e$, $e > 0$, a homogeneous element. Show that $\dim A/yA \geq \dim A - 1$.
8. (C) Let $k = \mathbb{F}_2$ and $A = k[x, y]/(xy(x + y))$. Show that $A$ is standard graded of dimension $1$, that no $y \in A_1$ makes $A$ finite over $k[y]$, and that $x^2 + xy + y^2 \in A_2$ does.

### Notes and history

Hilbert proved in 1890 that the Hilbert function of a graded module over a polynomial ring is eventually a polynomial, with his syzygy theorem as the main tool. Serre's work on coherent sheaves, in the 1950s, connected the Hilbert polynomial with the Euler characteristic of a sheaf on projective space. Noether normalization in the graded form goes back to Hilbert's work on invariants, where homogeneous systems of parameters appear. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 11, Matsumura, Chapter 5, and Eisenbud, Chapter 13.
