## The homogeneous spectrum

### Statement

Throughout, $A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_n$ is a graded ring and $A_+$ its irrelevant ideal. For a homogeneous $f \in A_e$ with $e > 0$, the localization $A_f$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-graded, with $\deg(a/f^m) = \deg a - me$ for homogeneous $a$ (Exercise 4 of Section 13.1), and $A_{(f)}$ denotes its degree zero part, the ring of fractions $a/f^m$ with $a \in A_{me}$.

**Definition P1.13.23 (homogeneous spectrum).** The *homogeneous spectrum* $\operatorname{Proj} A$ is the set of homogeneous primes $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$ with $A_+ \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, with the topology induced from $\operatorname{Spec} A$. For a homogeneous ideal $I$, put $V_+(I) = \{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Proj} A : \mathfrak{p} \supseteq I\}$, and for homogeneous $f$, put $D_+(f) = \{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Proj} A : f \notin \mathfrak{p}\}$.

Lean: Mathlib `ProjectiveSpectrum` (definition), with the scheme `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` (definition).

The closed sets of $\operatorname{Proj} A$ are the sets $V_+(I)$: a closed set $V(J) \cap \operatorname{Proj} A$ equals $V_+(I)$ for the ideal $I$ generated by the homogeneous components of the elements of $J$, because a homogeneous prime contains $J$ if and only if it contains these components. The sets $D_+(f)$ with $f \in A_+$ homogeneous cover $\operatorname{Proj} A$, since a prime in $\operatorname{Proj} A$ misses some homogeneous element of $A_+$, and they form a basis of the topology, since the complement of $V_+(I)$ is the union of the $D_+(f)$ with $f \in I$ homogeneous. Also $D_+(f) \cap D_+(g) = D_+(fg)$.

**Theorem P1.13.24 (affine charts).** Let $f \in A_e$ with $e > 0$. The map
$$
\varphi : D_+(f) \to \operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}, \qquad \mathfrak{p} \mapsto \mathfrak{p}A_f \cap A_{(f)} ,
$$
is a homeomorphism. Its inverse sends $\mathfrak{q}$ to the ideal $\psi(\mathfrak{q})$ generated by the homogeneous $a \in A_n$, for all $n$, with $a^e/f^n \in \mathfrak{q}$.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.basicOpenIsoSpec`, an isomorphism of schemes from the open set $D_+(f)$ of `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` to the spectrum of `HomogeneousLocalization.Away` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* For $\mathfrak{p} \in D_+(f)$, the ideal $\mathfrak{p}A_f$ is a prime of $A_f$, so $\varphi(\mathfrak{p})$ is a prime of the subring $A_{(f)}$. For a homogeneous $a \in A_n$, the element $a^e/f^n$ lies in $A_{(f)}$, and
$$
a^e/f^n \in \varphi(\mathfrak{p}) \iff a^e \in \mathfrak{p} \iff a \in \mathfrak{p}, \tag{$*$}
$$
since an equation $a^e/f^n = x/f^m$ with $x \in \mathfrak{p}$ gives $f^N(a^e f^m - x f^n) = 0$ for some $N$, and $f \notin \mathfrak{p}$.

Let $\mathfrak{q}$ be a prime of $A_{(f)}$, and let $S$ be the set of homogeneous $a \in A_n$, for all $n$, with $a^e/f^n \in \mathfrak{q}$. If $a, b \in S \cap A_n$, then every term of $(a + b)^{2e}/f^{2n}$ has the form $\binom{2e}{i} (a^e/f^n)(a^{i-e} b^{2e-i}/f^n)$ with $i \geq e$ or the same with $a$ and $b$ exchanged, and $a^{i-e}b^{2e-i}/f^n$ lies in $A_{(f)}$, since its degree is $0$. So $((a + b)^e/f^n)^2 \in \mathfrak{q}$, and $a + b \in S$. If $a \in S \cap A_n$ and $c \in A_m$, then $(ca)^e/f^{n+m} = (c^e/f^m)(a^e/f^n) \in \mathfrak{q}$. So the elements of $A$ all of whose homogeneous components lie in $S$ form a homogeneous ideal, and this ideal is $\psi(\mathfrak{q})$, with $\psi(\mathfrak{q}) \cap A_n = S \cap A_n$. It does not contain $f$, since $f^e/f^e = 1 \notin \mathfrak{q}$. For homogeneous $a \in A_n$ and $b \in A_m$ with $ab \in \psi(\mathfrak{q})$, $(a^e/f^n)(b^e/f^m) \in \mathfrak{q}$, so $a$ or $b$ lies in $\psi(\mathfrak{q})$. By Lemma P1.13.2, $\psi(\mathfrak{q})$ is a prime, and it lies in $D_+(f)$.

By $(*)$, $\psi(\varphi(\mathfrak{p}))$ and $\mathfrak{p}$ have the same homogeneous elements, so they are equal. An element of $A_{(f)}$ has the form $a/f^m$ with $a \in A_{me}$, and $a/f^m \in \mathfrak{q}$ if and only if $(a/f^m)^e = a^e/f^{me} \in \mathfrak{q}$, that is, $a \in \psi(\mathfrak{q})$. By $(*)$ for $\mathfrak{p} = \psi(\mathfrak{q})$, this holds if and only if $a/f^m \in \varphi(\psi(\mathfrak{q}))$. So $\varphi(\psi(\mathfrak{q})) = \mathfrak{q}$.

Both maps preserve inclusions. For continuity, let $I$ be a homogeneous ideal. By $(*)$, $\varphi$ maps $V_+(I) \cap D_+(f)$ onto $V(J)$, where $J$ is the ideal of $A_{(f)}$ generated by the elements $a^e/f^n$ with $a \in I \cap A_n$: a prime $\mathfrak{p} \in D_+(f)$ contains the homogeneous elements of $I$ if and only if $\varphi(\mathfrak{p})$ contains these elements $a^e/f^n$. Conversely, for an ideal $J$ of $A_{(f)}$, let $I$ be the ideal generated by the homogeneous $a \in A_n$ with $a^e/f^n \in J$. Then $\psi(\mathfrak{q}) \supseteq I$ if $\mathfrak{q} \supseteq J$, and if $\mathfrak{p} \supseteq I$ and $b/f^m \in J$, then $b^e/f^{me} \in J$, so $b \in I \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ and $b/f^m \in \varphi(\mathfrak{p})$. So $\psi$ maps $V(J)$ onto $V_+(I) \cap D_+(f)$. Both maps send closed sets to closed sets, and they are inverse bijections, so they are homeomorphisms. $\square$

The rings $A_{(f)}$ glue along $A_{(fg)}$ (Exercise 3), and this makes $\operatorname{Proj} A$ a scheme covered by the affine schemes $\operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}$. The construction is in Hartshorne, *Algebraic geometry*, Section II.2, and the later volumes use it.

**Proposition P1.13.25 (Veronese subrings).** Let $r \geq 1$ and $f \in A_e$ with $e > 0$. Then $A_{(f)} = (A^{(r)})_{(f^r)}$ as subrings of $A_f$, and $\mathfrak{p} \mapsto \mathfrak{p} \cap A^{(r)}$ is a homeomorphism $\operatorname{Proj} A \to \operatorname{Proj} A^{(r)}$ that is compatible with these charts.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* An element of $A_{(f)}$ is $a/f^m$ with $a \in A_{me}$, and $a/f^m = af^{m(r-1)}/f^{mr}$, where $af^{m(r-1)}$ has degree $mer$, a multiple of $r$. So $a/f^m \in (A^{(r)})_{(f^r)}$. The other inclusion is clear, since $A_{f^r} = A_f$. For a homogeneous prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$ and homogeneous $a$, $a \in \mathfrak{p}$ if and only if $a^r \in \mathfrak{p} \cap A^{(r)}$. So $\mathfrak{p}$ is determined by $\mathfrak{p} \cap A^{(r)}$, and $\mathfrak{p} \in D_+(f)$ if and only if $\mathfrak{p} \cap A^{(r)} \in D_+(f^r)$. On $D_+(f)$, the map is the composition of the homeomorphisms of the theorem for $A$ and for $A^{(r)}$ with the identity of $A_{(f)} = (A^{(r)})_{(f^r)}$. The sets $D_+(f^r)$ cover $\operatorname{Proj} A^{(r)}$: a point $\mathfrak{q}$ misses some homogeneous $g \in A^{(r)}_+$, which is a homogeneous element of $A$ of positive degree, and then $\mathfrak{q}$ misses $g^r$, so $\mathfrak{q} \in D_+(g^r)$. So the map is a bijection that restricts to homeomorphisms $D_+(f) \to D_+(f^r)$ on an open cover, and it is a homeomorphism. $\square$

**Proposition P1.13.26 (the projective Nullstellensatz).** Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $S = k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ and $I \subseteq S$ a homogeneous ideal. The set of points $[a] \in \mathbb{P}^n(k)$ with $g(a) = 0$ for all homogeneous $g \in I$ is empty if and only if $\sqrt{I} \supseteq S_+$, and when the set is nonempty, the forms that vanish on it generate the homogeneous ideal $\sqrt{I}$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Let $C = V(I) \subseteq k^{n+1}$ be the affine zero set. Since $I$ is generated by homogeneous elements, $C$ is a cone: $a \in C$ implies $\lambda a \in C$ for $\lambda \in k$. The projective zero set is empty if and only if $C \subseteq \{0\}$, which by the Nullstellensatz (P0, Chapter 3, and Theorem P1.4.30) means $\sqrt{I} \supseteq (x_0, \dots, x_n) = S_+$. Let the projective zero set be nonempty, so that $I \neq S$ and no nonzero constant lies in $\sqrt{I}$. A form $g$ of positive degree vanishes on the projective zero set if and only if it vanishes on $C \setminus \{0\}$, which is the union of the lines through its points, hence if and only if it vanishes on $C$, since $g(0) = 0$. By the Nullstellensatz the polynomials that vanish on $C$ form $\sqrt{I}$, which is homogeneous by Proposition P1.13.1. So the forms that vanish on the projective zero set are the homogeneous elements of $\sqrt{I}$, and they generate $\sqrt{I}$. $\square$

### Proof in words

The homogeneous primes not containing the irrelevant ideal are the points of a projective scheme. Inverting a homogeneous element $f$ of positive degree and taking the degree zero part gives a ring whose primes correspond to the homogeneous primes that miss $f$: in one direction one extends and contracts, and in the other one takes the homogeneous $a$ with $a^e/f^n$ in the prime. These charts cover $\operatorname{Proj} A$. A Veronese subring gives the same charts, so it gives the same homogeneous spectrum. Over an algebraically closed field, the projective zero set of a homogeneous ideal is empty exactly when its radical contains the irrelevant ideal, because the affine zero set is a cone.

### Examples

**Example P1.13.27 (projective space).** Let $S = k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ with the standard grading. Then $S_{(x_i)} = k[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$, a polynomial ring in the $n$ elements $x_j/x_i$ with $j \neq i$: an element of degree zero is $a/x_i^m$ with $a$ a form of degree $m$, which is a polynomial in the $x_j/x_i$, and the $x_j/x_i$ are algebraically independent, since setting $x_i = 1$ maps them to the variables of $k[x_j : j \neq i]$. So $\mathbb{P}^n = \operatorname{Proj} S$ is covered by $n + 1$ copies of $\mathbb{A}^n$, glued as in Example P1.12.28.

**Example P1.13.28 (a conic).** Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(xz - y^2) \cong k[s, t]^{(2)}$, with $x, y, z \mapsto s^2, st, t^2$. By Proposition P1.13.25, $\operatorname{Proj} A \cong \operatorname{Proj} k[s, t] = \mathbb{P}^1$. This is the parametrization $[s : t] \mapsto [s^2 : st : t^2]$ of the smooth conic. The affine cone $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is the quadric cone, singular at the vertex, while $\operatorname{Proj} A$ is smooth.

**Example P1.13.29 (a weighted projective plane).** Let $A = k[x, y, z]$ with $\deg x = \deg y = 1$ and $\deg z = 2$, so that $\operatorname{Proj} A = \mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$. The chart $A_{(x)}$ is spanned by the elements $x^{-a-b-2c} x^a y^b z^c$, so $A_{(x)} = k[y/x, z/x^2]$ is a polynomial ring, and similarly for $A_{(y)}$. The chart $A_{(z)}$ is spanned by the monomials $x^a y^b z^{-(a+b)/2}$ with $a + b$ even, so it is generated by $u = x^2/z$, $v = xy/z$ and $w = y^2/z$, and it is isomorphic to $k[x, y]^{(2)} \cong k[u, v, w]/(uw - v^2)$, the quadric cone. So $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ is the projective cone over a conic, with one singular point of type $A_1$ at $[0 : 0 : 1]$, although $A$ is a polynomial ring. A weighted projective space can be singular, and in the minimal model program such spaces give examples of varieties with quotient singularities.

**Example P1.13.30 (the blow-up of the plane at a point).** Let $R = k[x, y]$, $I = (x, y)$ and $B = R[It] = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} I^n t^n$, the Rees algebra of Example P1.13.10, generated over $R$ by $xt$ and $yt$ in degree one. An element of $B_{(xt)}$ is $at^n/(xt)^n = a/x^n$ with $a \in I^n$, a combination of the $x^i y^{n-i}$, so $B_{(xt)} = R[y/x] = k[x, y/x]$, a polynomial ring in $x$ and $u = y/x$. Similarly $B_{(yt)} = k[x/y, y]$. These are the two charts of the blow-up of the origin, and the chart $k[x, u]$ with $y = xu$ is the one of Example P1.5.49. In general $\operatorname{Proj} R[It] \to \operatorname{Spec} R$ is the blow-up of $\operatorname{Spec} R$ along $V(I)$.

**Example P1.13.31 (section rings and the canonical ring).** For a projective variety $X$ and a line bundle $L$, the *section ring* is $R(X, L) = \bigoplus_{m \geq 0} H^0(X, L^{\otimes m})$. If $L$ is ample, $R(X, L)$ is finitely generated and $\operatorname{Proj} R(X, L) \cong X$. For a smooth complex projective variety the *canonical ring* $R(X, K_X) = \bigoplus_{m \geq 0} H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mK_X))$ is finitely generated. Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan proved this for varieties of general type, Siu gave an analytic proof in that case, and the general case reduces to it by a result of Fujino and Mori. If $X$ is of general type, $\operatorname{Proj} R(X, K_X)$ is the canonical model of $X$ (Volume 9). The ring need not be generated in degree one, and Proposition P1.13.25 shows that $\operatorname{Proj}$ does not change when $R(X, K_X)$ is replaced by a Veronese subring that is generated in degree one (Exercise 8).

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Proj} A = \emptyset$ if and only if every element of $A_+$ is nilpotent.
2. (A) Let $A = k[x, y]$ with the standard grading. Show that $\operatorname{Proj} A$ has one point that is not closed, the prime $0$, and that the other points are the primes $(g)$ with $g$ an irreducible form, which correspond to the points of $\mathbb{P}^1$ over $k$ up to the Galois action when $k$ is not algebraically closed.
3. (B) Let $f \in A_e$ and $g \in A_c$ with $e, c > 0$. Show that $A_{(fg)}$ is the localization of $A_{(f)}$ at the element $g^e/f^c$. (Hint: $a/(fg)^n = (a g^{n(e-1)}/f^{n(c+1)}) \cdot (g^e/f^c)^{-n}$ for $a \in A_{n(e+c)}$.)
4. (B) Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(f)$ with $f$ a form of degree $3$ that defines a smooth plane cubic, and $k$ algebraically closed. Compute the chart $A_{(z)}$ and show that it is the coordinate ring of an affine plane cubic.
5. (B) Show that $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ is isomorphic to the image of the map $[x : y : z] \mapsto [x^2 : xy : y^2 : z]$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$, the projective cone over a conic. (Hint: use the Veronese subring $A^{(2)}$.)
6. (B) Let $A = k[x, y, z]$ with weights $1, 1, n$ for $n \geq 2$. Show that $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$ has one singular point, and that its chart $A_{(z)}$ is the cone $k[x, y]^{(n)}$ over the rational normal curve of degree $n$.
7. (B) In the blow-up example, show that the two charts glue along $k[x, y/x, x/y]$, and that the inverse image of the origin is $\operatorname{Proj}$ of $\operatorname{gr}_I(R) = k[x, y]$, a projective line.
8. (C) Let $A$ be a graded ring generated over $A_0$ by finitely many homogeneous elements of positive degree. Show that some Veronese subring $A^{(r)}$ is generated over $A_0$ by $A_r$. (Hint: let $r$ be a suitable multiple of the least common multiple of the degrees of the generators, and count exponents of monomials.)

### Notes and history

The homogeneous spectrum is due to Grothendieck, in EGA II of 1961, as the scheme version of the classical projective variety of a homogeneous ideal. Weighted projective spaces were studied by Dolgachev and others around 1980, and they are a standard source of examples in the minimal model program. The finite generation of the canonical ring was proved by Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan in 2010, and by Siu for varieties of general type with analytic methods. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Eisenbud, Chapter 13, Matsumura, Chapter 5, and Hartshorne, Section II.2, for the construction of $\operatorname{Proj}$.
