## Graded rings and homogeneous ideals

### Statement

A graded ring $A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_n$ and a graded module $M = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} M_n$ are as in Definition P1.5.27. Every $a \in A$ is a finite sum $a = \sum_n a_n$ of its *homogeneous components* $a_n \in A_n$. The *irrelevant ideal* is $A_+ = \bigoplus_{n > 0} A_n$, and $A/A_+ \cong A_0$. For a graded module $M$ and $e \in \mathbb{Z}$, the *shifted module* $M(e)$ is $M$ with $M(e)_n = M_{n+e}$. A *Veronese subring* is $A^{(r)} = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_{rn}$ for $r \geq 1$.

**Proposition P1.13.1 (homogeneous ideals).** For an ideal $I$ of a graded ring $A$ the following are equivalent:

1. $I$ is generated by homogeneous elements;
2. for every $a \in I$, all homogeneous components of $a$ lie in $I$;
3. $I = \bigoplus_n (I \cap A_n)$.

An ideal with these properties is *homogeneous*. Then $A/I = \bigoplus_n A_n/(I \cap A_n)$ is a graded ring. Sums, products, intersections and radicals of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.IsHomogeneous` and `HomogeneousIdeal` (definitions), and `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.radical` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* (1) implies (2): let $I$ be generated by homogeneous $h_j$ of degrees $e_j$ and $a = \sum_j r_j h_j \in I$. Then $a_n = \sum_j (r_j)_{n - e_j} h_j \in I$. (2) and (3) say the same thing. (2) implies (1): the homogeneous components of the elements of $I$ lie in $I$ and generate $I$. The statement on $A/I$ follows from (3).

A sum or a product of ideals generated by homogeneous elements is generated by the sums or the products of the generators, so (1) holds. An intersection satisfies (2) if each ideal does. Let $I$ be homogeneous and $a \in \sqrt{I}$, with top component $a_t$. If $a^N \in I$, then the component of $a^N$ of degree $Nt$ is $a_t^N$, which lies in $I$ by (2), so $a_t \in \sqrt{I}$. Then $a - a_t \in \sqrt{I}$ has fewer components, and induction on the number of components shows that all components of $a$ lie in $\sqrt{I}$. $\square$

**Lemma P1.13.2 (primality test).** Let $I \neq A$ be a homogeneous ideal such that for all homogeneous $a, b$, $ab \in I$ implies $a \in I$ or $b \in I$. Then $I$ is prime.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_of_homogeneous_mem_or_mem` and `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_iff` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* Let $ab \in I$ with $a, b \notin I$. Removing from $a$ and from $b$ the homogeneous components that lie in $I$ changes $ab$ by an element of $I$, so one may assume that no component of $a$ or $b$ lies in $I$, and $a, b \neq 0$. Let $a_s$ and $b_t$ be the top components. The component of $ab$ of degree $s + t$ is $a_s b_t$, which lies in $I$ by (2) of the proposition. So $a_s \in I$ or $b_t \in I$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Proposition P1.13.3 (homogeneous primes).** Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime of a graded ring $A$, and let $\mathfrak{p}^*$ be the ideal generated by the homogeneous elements of $\mathfrak{p}$. Then $\mathfrak{p}^*$ is a homogeneous prime, the largest homogeneous ideal in $\mathfrak{p}$. Every minimal prime of a homogeneous ideal is homogeneous.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.homogeneousCore` (definition), with `Ideal.IsPrime.homogeneousCore` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* The ideal $\mathfrak{p}^*$ is homogeneous and lies in $\mathfrak{p}$, so it is not $A$. If $a, b$ are homogeneous with $ab \in \mathfrak{p}^* \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, then $a$ or $b$ lies in $\mathfrak{p}$, hence in $\mathfrak{p}^*$, since it is homogeneous. By Lemma P1.13.2, $\mathfrak{p}^*$ is prime. Every homogeneous ideal in $\mathfrak{p}$ is generated by homogeneous elements of $\mathfrak{p}$, so it lies in $\mathfrak{p}^*$. If $\mathfrak{p}$ is minimal over a homogeneous ideal $I$, then $I \subseteq \mathfrak{p}^* \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, and $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{p}^*$ by minimality. $\square$

**Proposition P1.13.4 (associated primes of graded modules).** Let $A$ be a graded ring and $M$ a graded $A$-module. Every associated prime of $M$ is homogeneous.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak{p} = \operatorname{ann}(x)$ with $x = x_1 + \dots + x_m$, where $x_l \in M_{d_l}$ and $d_1 < \dots < d_m$. Let $a = a_1 + \dots + a_r \in \mathfrak{p}$ with $a_k \in A_{e_k}$, $e_1 < \dots < e_r$. It is enough to show $a_1 \in \mathfrak{p}$: then $a - a_1 \in \mathfrak{p}$ has fewer components, and induction gives all $a_k \in \mathfrak{p}$.

The claim is that $a_1^i x_l = 0$ for all $l \leq i$, by induction on $i$. The component of $ax = 0$ of degree $e_1 + d_1$ is $a_1 x_1$, which gives $i = 1$. For the step, the component of degree $e_1 + d_{i+1}$ is $\sum a_k x_l$ over the pairs with $e_k + d_l = e_1 + d_{i+1}$. Each such pair has $l \leq i + 1$, and $l = i + 1$ only for $k = 1$. Multiplying by $a_1^i$ kills the terms with $l \leq i$, by the induction hypothesis, and leaves $a_1^{i+1} x_{i+1} = 0$. So $a_1^m x = 0$, that is $a_1^m \in \mathfrak{p}$, and $a_1 \in \mathfrak{p}$ since $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime. $\square$

So the primary decomposition of a homogeneous ideal $I$ in a Noetherian graded ring has homogeneous associated primes, and one can choose the primary components homogeneous as well (Eisenbud, Chapter 3).

**Proposition P1.13.5 (graded Noetherian rings).** A graded ring $A$ is Noetherian if and only if $A_0$ is Noetherian and $A$ is a finitely generated $A_0$-algebra. In that case $A$ is generated over $A_0$ by finitely many homogeneous elements of positive degree.

Lean: Mathlib `GradedRing.GradeZero.isNoetherianRing` and `GradedAlgebra.exists_finset_adjoin_eq_top_and_homogeneous` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* If $A_0$ is Noetherian and $A$ is finitely generated, then $A$ is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem. Conversely, let $A$ be Noetherian. Then $A_0 \cong A/A_+$ is Noetherian, and $A_+$ is generated by finitely many homogeneous elements $x_1, \dots, x_s$ of degrees $d_i > 0$. By induction on $n$, $A_n \subseteq A_0[x_1, \dots, x_s]$: this holds for $n = 0$, and for $n > 0$, $A_n = (A_+)_n = \sum_i x_i A_{n - d_i}$ by (1) implies (2) of the first proposition. $\square$

**Lemma P1.13.6 (graded Nakayama).** Let $A$ be a graded ring, $I \subseteq A_+$ a homogeneous ideal and $M$ a graded module with $M_n = 0$ for $n$ small enough. If $IM = M$, then $M = 0$. Homogeneous elements of $M$ whose images generate $M/IM$ generate $M$.

Lean: not in Mathlib in this form.

*Proof.* If $M \neq 0$, let $n$ be least with $M_n \neq 0$. Then $(IM)_n = \sum_{j > 0} I_j M_{n - j} = 0$, so $M_n = (IM)_n = 0$, a contradiction. For the second statement apply the first to $M/N$, where $N$ is the graded submodule generated by the given elements; $M/N$ is graded, bounded below, and $I(M/N) = M/N$. $\square$

**Corollary P1.13.7 (graded free modules).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian graded ring with $A_0 = k$ a field, and $\mathfrak{m} = A_+$. Let $M$ be a finitely generated graded $A$-module.

1. Every homogeneous element of $A$ outside $\mathfrak{m}$ is a unit, every element outside $\mathfrak{m}$ is a nonzerodivisor on $M$, so $M \to M_\mathfrak{m}$ is injective, and every proper homogeneous ideal lies in $\mathfrak{m}$.
2. A minimal set of homogeneous generators of $M$ has $\dim_k M/\mathfrak{m}M$ elements.
3. If $M_\mathfrak{m}$ is a free $A_\mathfrak{m}$-module, then $M$ is free, with a basis of homogeneous elements, so $M \cong \bigoplus_i A(-c_i)$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

*Proof.* (1) A homogeneous element outside $\mathfrak{m}$ has degree $0$, so it is a nonzero element of $k$. Let $s \notin \mathfrak{m}$, so $s_0 \neq 0$, and $x \in M$ with $sx = 0$. If $x \neq 0$, let $x_j$ be its lowest component. The component of $sx$ of degree $j$ is $s_0 x_j$, so $x_j = 0$, a contradiction. A proper homogeneous ideal $I$ has $I \cap A_0 \neq k$, so $I \cap A_0 = 0$ and $I \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$.

(2) A finitely generated graded module is bounded below. By Lemma P1.13.6, homogeneous elements generate $M$ if and only if their images span $M/\mathfrak{m}M$, and a homogeneous basis of $M/\mathfrak{m}M$ lifts to homogeneous elements of $M$.

(3) Let $m_1, \dots, m_r$ be minimal homogeneous generators of degrees $c_i$, with $r = \dim_k M/\mathfrak{m}M$. The map $F = \bigoplus_i A(-c_i) \to M$ is surjective and graded, and its kernel $N$ is a finitely generated graded module. The fibre of $M_\mathfrak{m}$ at the closed point has dimension $r$, so $M_\mathfrak{m}$ is free of rank $r$, so the surjection $F_\mathfrak{m} \to M_\mathfrak{m}$ splits and $F_\mathfrak{m} \cong M_\mathfrak{m} \oplus N_\mathfrak{m}$. Comparing the dimensions of the fibres at the closed point gives $N_\mathfrak{m} \otimes \kappa = 0$, and $N_\mathfrak{m} = 0$ by Nakayama's lemma. By (1), $N \to N_\mathfrak{m}$ is injective, so $N = 0$. $\square$

### Proof in words

An ideal is homogeneous when it is generated by homogeneous elements, and then it contains the components of its elements. A homogeneous ideal is prime as soon as the prime condition holds for homogeneous elements, because the top component of a product is the product of the top components. Every prime contains a largest homogeneous ideal, which is again prime, so the minimal primes and the associated primes of graded objects are homogeneous. When $A_0$ is a field, the irrelevant ideal plays the part of the maximal ideal of a local ring: Nakayama's lemma holds for graded modules, and a graded module that is free at the vertex is free.

### Examples

**Example P1.13.8 (homogeneous and non-homogeneous ideals).** In $k[x, y]$ the ideal $(x^2, xy)$ is homogeneous, with primary decomposition $(x) \cap (x^2, y)$ and associated primes $(x)$ and $(x, y)$, both homogeneous. The ideal $(x - 1)$ of $k[x]$ is a prime that is not homogeneous, and its homogeneous core is $0$. The ideal $(y - x^2)$ of $k[x, y]$ is not homogeneous for the standard grading, but it is homogeneous of degree $2$ if $\deg x = 1$ and $\deg y = 2$.

**Example P1.13.9 (weighted gradings).** The ring $A = k[x, y, z]/(x^2 + y^3 + z^5)$ of the $E_8$ singularity is graded with $\deg x = 15$, $\deg y = 10$ and $\deg z = 6$, since each monomial of the equation has degree $30$. Similarly $xy - z^n$ is homogeneous for $\deg x = \deg y = n$ and $\deg z = 2$. These gradings come from the action of the multiplicative group, and they make the du Val singularities quasi-homogeneous.

**Example P1.13.10 (Rees algebra and associated graded ring).** For an ideal $I$ of a ring $R$, the Rees algebra $R[It] = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} I^n t^n \subseteq R[t]$ is graded with degree zero part $R$, and $R[It]/I R[It] = \operatorname{gr}_I(R)$ is the associated graded ring of Example P1.5.29. If $R$ is Noetherian, both are Noetherian by Proposition P1.13.5, since they are generated over $R$ and over $R/I$ by the images of generators of $I$ in degree one. Section 13.3 uses the Rees algebra to describe blow-ups.

**Example P1.13.11 (Veronese subrings).** The Veronese subring $k[s, t]^{(2)} = k[s^2, st, t^2]$ is isomorphic to $k[x, y, z]/(xz - y^2)$, the quadric cone, with all generators in degree one. The Veronese subring $k[s, t]^{(3)}$ is the cone over the twisted cubic. For a Noetherian graded ring $A$, each $A^{(r)}$ is Noetherian, and $A$ is a finitely generated $A^{(r)}$-module (Exercise 6).

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the ideal $(x^2 - y, xy)$ of $k[x, y]$ is not homogeneous for the standard grading, and find its homogeneous core.
2. (A) Let $A$ be a graded domain. Show that a unit of $A$ is homogeneous of degree $0$. Give an example of a graded ring that is not a domain with a unit that is not homogeneous.
3. (A) Show that the annihilator of a homogeneous element of a graded module is a homogeneous ideal.
4. (B) Let $A$ be a graded ring and $f \in A_e$ with $e > 0$. Show that $A_f$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-graded, with $\deg(a/f^n) = \deg a - ne$ for homogeneous $a$, and that its degree zero part $A_{(f)}$ is a subring.
5. (B) Let $A$ be a Noetherian graded ring and $M$ a finitely generated graded module. Show that every associated prime of $M$ is the annihilator of a homogeneous element of $M$. (Hint: if $\mathfrak{p} = \operatorname{ann}(x)$ is homogeneous, show that $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \operatorname{ann}(x_l)$ for every component $x_l$ of $x$, and use that $\bigcap_l \operatorname{ann}(x_l) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$.)
6. (B) Let $A$ be a Noetherian graded ring and $r \geq 1$. Show that $A$ is a finitely generated module over $A^{(r)}$, and that $A^{(r)}$ is Noetherian.
7. (B) Let $A = k[x, y]$ and $M = A/(x)$. Show that $M$ is a graded module whose localization at $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$ is not free, and compare with part 3 of Corollary P1.13.7.
8. (C) Let $A$ be graded with $A_0 = k$ a field and $\mathfrak{m} = A_+$. Show that $A$ has a unique homogeneous maximal ideal, and that a finitely generated graded projective $A$-module is free.

### Notes and history

Homogeneous ideals of polynomial rings are the ideals of projective varieties, and they are as old as projective geometry in algebraic form. Hilbert's work of 1890 on the theory of algebraic forms studied graded modules over polynomial rings systematically. The statements on homogeneous primes and associated primes are standard, and they are in Eisenbud, Chapter 3. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 10, and Eisenbud, Chapter 3.
