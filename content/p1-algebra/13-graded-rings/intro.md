# Graded rings

This chapter studies graded rings, the algebra behind projective geometry, as Section 5.3 promised. Section 13.1 proves that homogeneous ideals are closed under the usual operations, that minimal and associated primes of graded objects are homogeneous, that a graded ring is Noetherian exactly when it is a finitely generated algebra over a Noetherian degree zero part, and the graded form of Nakayama's lemma. Section 13.2 proves that the Hilbert series of a finitely generated graded module is rational, that the dimension of a standard graded algebra is one more than the degree of its Hilbert polynomial, the graded Noether normalization used in Section 10.2, and that a graded algebra which is regular at the vertex is a polynomial ring. Section 13.3 constructs the homogeneous spectrum $\operatorname{Proj} A$, proves that it is covered by the affine charts $\operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}$, and proves the projective Nullstellensatz.

A projective variety $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ is $\operatorname{Proj}$ of its homogeneous coordinate ring, and the affine cone over $X$ is the spectrum of the same ring. So statements about graded rings are statements about cones and about projective varieties at the same time. The Hilbert polynomial records the degree and the genus, a weighted grading makes the du Val singularities quasi-homogeneous, a weighted projective space is $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a polynomial ring with unequal weights and can be singular, and $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a Rees algebra is a blow-up.

In the minimal model program graded rings appear as section rings. The canonical ring $R(X, K_X) = \bigoplus_m H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mK_X))$ of a smooth projective variety is finitely generated, and for a variety of general type its $\operatorname{Proj}$ is the canonical model (Volume 9). Flips are also constructed as $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a finitely generated graded ring of sections (Volume 8). The chapter follows Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapters 10 and 11, Matsumura, Chapter 5, and Eisenbud, Chapters 3 and 13. It uses Chapters 1 to 12.
