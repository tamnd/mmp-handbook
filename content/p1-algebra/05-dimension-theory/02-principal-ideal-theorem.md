## Krull's principal ideal theorem

### Statement

**Definition P1.5.14 (symbolic power).** Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime of a ring $A$ and $n \geq 1$. The $n$-th *symbolic power* of $\mathfrak{p}$ is
$$
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)} = \mathfrak{p}^n A_{\mathfrak{p}} \cap A = \{a \in A : sa \in \mathfrak{p}^n \text{ for some } s \notin \mathfrak{p}\} .
$$
The ideal $\mathfrak{p}^n A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is primary, since its radical is the maximal ideal of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ (Proposition P1.2.28), and the contraction of a primary ideal is primary. So $\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}$ is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary. It contains $\mathfrak{p}^n$, and $\mathfrak{p}^{(n)} A_{\mathfrak{p}} = \mathfrak{p}^n A_{\mathfrak{p}}$. Geometrically, $\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}$ is the ideal of the functions that vanish to order at least $n$ at the general point of $V(\mathfrak{p})$, while $\mathfrak{p}^n$ may have embedded components at special points.

Lean: not in Lean.

**Example P1.5.15.** In $k[x, y]$ every power of a maximal ideal is primary, so $\mathfrak{m}^{(n)} = \mathfrak{m}^n$. For a prime $(f)$ generated by a prime element of a factorial ring, $(f)^{(n)} = (f^n)$, since $(f^n)$ is $(f)$-primary.

**Example P1.5.16.** Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$, the quadric cone of Example P1.4.25, and $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$, the ideal of the line $x = z = 0$ on the cone. Then $\mathfrak{p}^2 = (x^2, xz, z^2) = (x^2, xz, xy)$, since $z^2 = xy$. The element $y$ is not in $\mathfrak{p}$ and $xy = z^2 \in \mathfrak{p}^2$, so $x \in \mathfrak{p}^{(2)}$. But $x \notin \mathfrak{p}^2$, because $\mathfrak{p}^2 \subseteq \mathfrak{m}^2$ for $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y, z)$ while $x \notin \mathfrak{m}^2$ by degree. So $\mathfrak{p}^2 \neq \mathfrak{p}^{(2)}$. Exercise 5 shows $\mathfrak{p}^{(2)} = (x)$.

**Theorem P1.5.17 (Krull's principal ideal theorem).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $x \in A$. Every prime $\mathfrak{p}$ that is minimal over $(x)$ has height at most $1$.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.height_le_one_of_isPrincipal_of_mem_minimalPrimes` and `Ideal.height_span_singleton_le_one` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* The primes of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ are the primes of $A$ inside $\mathfrak{p}$, so $\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is minimal over $xA_{\mathfrak{p}}$ and has the same height as $\mathfrak{p}$. So assume that $A$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, and that $\mathfrak{p}$ is minimal over $(x)$. Then $\mathfrak{p}$ is the only prime that contains $x$: such a prime lies in $\mathfrak{p}$ and contains a minimal prime of $(x)$, which can only be $\mathfrak{p}$. So $A/(x)$ is a Noetherian ring with one prime, and it is Artinian by the Akizuki-Hopkins theorem (Theorem P1.3.35).

Let $\mathfrak{q} \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}$ be a prime. It suffices to show that $\mathfrak{q}$ is a minimal prime of $A$. The images of the ideals $\mathfrak{q}^{(n)} + (x)$ form a descending chain in the Artinian ring $A/(x)$, so there is an $N$ with $\mathfrak{q}^{(n)} + (x) = \mathfrak{q}^{(n+1)} + (x)$ for all $n \geq N$. Fix $n \geq N$ and let $y \in \mathfrak{q}^{(n)}$. Write $y = z + ax$ with $z \in \mathfrak{q}^{(n+1)}$ and $a \in A$. Then $ax = y - z \in \mathfrak{q}^{(n)}$. Since $x \notin \mathfrak{q}$ (the only prime that contains $x$ is $\mathfrak{p}$) and $\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}$ is $\mathfrak{q}$-primary, $a \in \mathfrak{q}^{(n)}$. Hence
$$
\mathfrak{q}^{(n)} = \mathfrak{q}^{(n+1)} + x \, \mathfrak{q}^{(n)} .
$$
The module $\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}/\mathfrak{q}^{(n+1)}$ is finitely generated and equals $x$ times itself, with $x$ in the maximal ideal. By Nakayama's lemma (Theorem P1.1.25) it is zero, so $\mathfrak{q}^{(n)} = \mathfrak{q}^{(n+1)}$. Localizing at $\mathfrak{q}$ gives $\mathfrak{q}^n A_{\mathfrak{q}} = \mathfrak{q}^{n+1} A_{\mathfrak{q}} = (\mathfrak{q}A_{\mathfrak{q}}) \cdot \mathfrak{q}^n A_{\mathfrak{q}}$, and Nakayama's lemma in the local ring $A_{\mathfrak{q}}$ gives $\mathfrak{q}^n A_{\mathfrak{q}} = 0$. So the maximal ideal of $A_{\mathfrak{q}}$ is nilpotent, $A_{\mathfrak{q}}$ has only one prime, and $\mathfrak{q}$ contains no smaller prime. Every chain of primes that ends at $\mathfrak{p}$ therefore has length at most $1$. $\square$

**Theorem P1.5.18 (Krull's height theorem).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $x_1, \dots, x_r \in A$. Every prime $\mathfrak{p}$ that is minimal over $(x_1, \dots, x_r)$ has height at most $r$.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.height_le_card_of_mem_minimalPrimes_span_finset` and `Ideal.height_le_spanFinrank` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* Induction on $r$. For $r = 0$, $\mathfrak{p}$ is a minimal prime of $A$ and has height $0$, and $r = 1$ is the principal ideal theorem. Let $r \geq 2$. As in the last proof, localize at $\mathfrak{p}$: assume that $A$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, and that $\mathfrak{p}$ is the only prime that contains $I = (x_1, \dots, x_r)$.

Step 1. Let $\mathfrak{q} \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}$ be a prime such that no prime lies strictly between $\mathfrak{q}$ and $\mathfrak{p}$. The claim is $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{q} \leq r - 1$. Since $\mathfrak{q}$ does not contain $I$, some $x_i$ is not in $\mathfrak{q}$, say $x_1$. The only prime that contains $\mathfrak{q} + (x_1)$ is $\mathfrak{p}$, by the choice of $\mathfrak{q}$, so $\mathfrak{p} = \sqrt{\mathfrak{q} + (x_1)}$. For $i = 2, \dots, r$ choose $n \geq 1$ and $a_i \in A$, $y_i \in \mathfrak{q}$ with
$$
x_i^n = a_i x_1 + y_i .
$$
Let $J = (y_2, \dots, y_r) \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$. Every $x_i$ lies in $\sqrt{J + (x_1)}$, so $\sqrt{J + (x_1)} = \mathfrak{p}$. In the ring $A/J$, the prime $\mathfrak{p}/J$ is therefore minimal over the principal ideal generated by the image of $x_1$, and it has height at most $1$ by the principal ideal theorem. The prime $\mathfrak{q}/J$ lies strictly inside $\mathfrak{p}/J$, so it is a minimal prime of $A/J$: a prime strictly inside it would give a chain of length $2$ below $\mathfrak{p}/J$. So $\mathfrak{q}$ is minimal over $J$, which has $r - 1$ generators, and $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{q} \leq r - 1$ by induction.

Step 2. Let $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_m = \mathfrak{p}$ be a chain with $m \geq 1$. The set of primes $\mathfrak{q}'$ with $\mathfrak{p}_{m-1} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}' \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}$ is not empty, and since $A$ is Noetherian it has a maximal element $\mathfrak{q}$. No prime lies strictly between $\mathfrak{q}$ and $\mathfrak{p}$. The chain $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_{m-1}$ lies in $\mathfrak{q}$, so $m - 1 \leq \operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{q} \leq r - 1$ by Step 1. Hence $m \leq r$. $\square$

**Corollary P1.5.19 (finiteness of dimension).** In a Noetherian ring every prime has finite height, and the primes satisfy the descending chain condition. A Noetherian local ring $(A, \mathfrak{m}, k)$ has finite dimension, and
$$
\dim A \leq \dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 .
$$

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.height_le_spanFinrank`; the finite dimension of a Noetherian local ring is an instance of the class `FiniteRingKrullDim` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* A prime $\mathfrak{p}$ is generated by finitely many elements, say $r$, and it is minimal over itself, so $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} \leq r$. A strictly descending chain starting at $\mathfrak{p}$ has length at most $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p}$. For a local ring, $\dim A = \operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{m}$, and $\mathfrak{m}$ is generated by $\dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ elements by Corollary P1.1.26. $\square$

The number $\dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ is the *embedding dimension* of $A$ (Example P1.1.28). The local rings for which equality holds are the regular local rings of Chapter 9.

**Theorem P1.5.20 (converse of the height theorem).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $\mathfrak{p}$ a prime of height $r$. There are $x_1, \dots, x_r \in \mathfrak{p}$ such that $\operatorname{ht}(x_1, \dots, x_i) = i$ for $i = 1, \dots, r$, and $\mathfrak{p}$ is minimal over $(x_1, \dots, x_r)$.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.exists_finset_card_eq_height_of_isNoetherianRing` (proved in Mathlib), which gives a $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideal with $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p}$ generators.

*Proof.* Choose the $x_i$ by induction, so that every minimal prime of $I_i = (x_1, \dots, x_i)$ has height at least $i$. Suppose $x_1, \dots, x_{i-1}$ are chosen, with $i \leq r$. The ideal $I_{i-1}$ has finitely many minimal primes (Theorem P1.3.20). Let $\mathfrak{q}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{q}_s$ be those of height $i - 1$. None of them contains $\mathfrak{p}$, since $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = r > i - 1$. By prime avoidance (Proposition P1.1.11) there is $x_i \in \mathfrak{p}$ outside $\mathfrak{q}_1 \cup \dots \cup \mathfrak{q}_s$. Let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a minimal prime of $I_i$. It contains a minimal prime $\mathfrak{q}$ of $I_{i-1}$. If $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{q} \geq i$, then $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{P} \geq i$. Otherwise $\mathfrak{q}$ is one of the $\mathfrak{q}_j$, and $x_i \in \mathfrak{P} \setminus \mathfrak{q}$ gives $\mathfrak{q} \subsetneq \mathfrak{P}$ and $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{P} \geq i$. So all minimal primes of $I_i$ have height at least $i$, and $\operatorname{ht} I_i = i$ by the height theorem. For $i = r$, the ideal $I_r \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ lies in a minimal prime $\mathfrak{P} \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ of $I_r$ of height $r$. A prime strictly inside $\mathfrak{p}$ has height less than $r$, so $\mathfrak{P} = \mathfrak{p}$. $\square$

**Definition P1.5.21 (system of parameters).** Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring of dimension $d$. A *system of parameters* of $A$ is a sequence $x_1, \dots, x_d \in \mathfrak{m}$ such that $(x_1, \dots, x_d)$ is $\mathfrak{m}$-primary, that is, $\sqrt{(x_1, \dots, x_d)} = \mathfrak{m}$.

Lean: not in Lean as a definition.

**Corollary P1.5.22 (systems of parameters).** A Noetherian local ring $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ of dimension $d$ has a system of parameters, and $d$ is the least number of elements that generate an $\mathfrak{m}$-primary ideal. For $x_1, \dots, x_r \in \mathfrak{m}$,
$$
\dim A/(x_1, \dots, x_r) \geq d - r ,
$$
with equality if and only if $x_1, \dots, x_r$ is part of a system of parameters. If $x \in \mathfrak{m}$ lies in no minimal prime of $A$, in particular if $x$ is a nonzerodivisor, then $\dim A/xA = d - 1$.

Lean: Mathlib `ringKrullDim_le_ringKrullDim_quotSMulTop_succ` and, for a nonzerodivisor, `ringKrullDim_quotient_span_singleton_succ_eq_ringKrullDim` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* The converse of the height theorem, applied to $\mathfrak{m}$, gives $d$ elements with $\mathfrak{m}$ minimal over the ideal they generate; since $\mathfrak{m}$ is the only maximal ideal, it is then the only prime over this ideal, which is therefore $\mathfrak{m}$-primary. If $n$ elements generate an $\mathfrak{m}$-primary ideal, then $\mathfrak{m}$ is minimal over it and $d = \operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{m} \leq n$ by the height theorem.

Let $\bar{A} = A/(x_1, \dots, x_r)$, of dimension $s$, and let $y_1, \dots, y_s \in \mathfrak{m}$ map to a system of parameters of $\bar{A}$. Then $\mathfrak{m}$ is the only prime over $(x_1, \dots, x_r, y_1, \dots, y_s)$, so $d \leq r + s$. If $s = d - r$, the $x_i$ and $y_j$ form a system of parameters. Conversely, if $x_1, \dots, x_r, x_{r+1}, \dots, x_d$ is a system of parameters, then the images of $x_{r+1}, \dots, x_d$ generate an ideal of $\bar{A}$ that is primary for the maximal ideal, so $s \leq d - r$.

For the last claim, $\dim A/xA \geq d - 1$ by the above. Let $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_m$ be a chain of primes that contain $x$. Since $x$ lies in no minimal prime, $\mathfrak{p}_0$ is not minimal and contains a prime $\mathfrak{p}_{-1} \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_0$. This gives a chain of length $m + 1$ in $A$, so $m \leq d - 1$. A nonzerodivisor lies in no associated prime (Proposition P1.2.44), and minimal primes are associated (Theorem P1.2.48). $\square$

**Proposition P1.5.23 (factorial Noetherian domains).** A Noetherian domain $A$ is a unique factorization domain if and only if every prime of height $1$ is principal.

Lean: Mathlib `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_forall_isPrincipal_of_height_eq_one` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* Let $A$ be factorial and $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$. Choose $a \neq 0$ in $\mathfrak{p}$. Some prime factor $f$ of $a$ lies in $\mathfrak{p}$. Then $0 \subsetneq (f) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ is a chain of primes, and $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$ forces $(f) = \mathfrak{p}$. Conversely, suppose every height one prime is principal. In a Noetherian domain every nonzero nonunit is a product of irreducible elements: otherwise choose $a$ without such a product with $(a)$ maximal among such principal ideals; $a$ is not irreducible, so $a = bc$ with $(a) \subsetneq (b)$ and $(a) \subsetneq (c)$, and $b$ and $c$ are products of irreducibles, which is a contradiction. It remains to show that an irreducible $f$ is prime. Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a minimal prime over $(f)$. By the principal ideal theorem, $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$, so $\mathfrak{p} = (g)$ for some $g$. Then $g$ divides $f$, and $g$ is not a unit, so $f = ug$ with $u$ a unit since $f$ is irreducible. So $(f) = \mathfrak{p}$ is prime. $\square$

### Proof in words

The principal ideal theorem says that one equation cuts down the dimension by at most one. The proof works in the local ring at the prime $\mathfrak{p}$, where $x$ defines a zero dimensional, hence Artinian, quotient. A prime $\mathfrak{q}$ below $\mathfrak{p}$ gives the descending chain of symbolic powers $\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}$, which stops modulo $x$; Nakayama's lemma then makes $\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}$ stop altogether, and this forces $\mathfrak{q}$ to be minimal. The height theorem follows by an induction that replaces $r$ equations by $r - 1$ equations in a smaller prime. The converse builds $r$ equations one at a time, each avoiding the minimal primes of the previous ones, by prime avoidance.

For a variety $X$ and a function $f$, the theorem says that every irreducible component of the zero set $V(f)$ has codimension at most $1$, and exactly $1$ when $f$ is not a zero divisor. This is why the zero set of a function on a normal variety is a sum of prime divisors, the starting point of Weil divisors and of the class group in Chapter 8. The height theorem gives codimension at most $r$ for $r$ equations. The converse fails globally: a codimension two subvariety need not be cut out by two equations, even up to radical, but it is a component of the zero set of two equations. A system of parameters of the local ring at a point $p$ of a $d$-dimensional variety is a set of $d$ functions whose common zero set near $p$ is $p$ alone.

### Examples

**Example P1.5.24 (a height one prime that is not principal).** Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$. If $x = 0$ on the cone then $z^2 = 0$, so $\sqrt{(x)} = \mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{p}$ is minimal over $(x)$. Since $A$ is a domain and $\mathfrak{p} \neq 0$, $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$. But $\mathfrak{p}$ is not principal. The ring is graded with $x, y, z$ in degree $1$, and $\mathfrak{p}$ is generated by elements of degree $1$. Let $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y, z)$. If $\mathfrak{p} = (f)$, then $\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{p}$ is spanned by the image of $f$, so it has dimension at most $1$. But $\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{p}$ lies in degrees at least $2$, and $x$, $z$ are linearly independent in the degree one part of $A$, which is spanned by $x, y, z$. So no combination $ax + bz$ with $a, b \in k$ not both zero lies in $\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{p}$, and $\dim_k \mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{p} = 2$. By the proposition on factorial domains, $A$ is not factorial, which is the claim of Example P1.4.25. Chapter 8 computes the class group of the cone: it is $\mathbb{Z}/2$, generated by the line $\mathfrak{p}$, and $2[\mathfrak{p}] = 0$ because $\mathfrak{p}^{(2)} = (x)$.

**Example P1.5.25 (a plane on the cone over a quadric surface).** Let $A = k[x, y, z, w]/(xw - yz)$, a domain of dimension $3$, the cone over $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$. On the zero set of $x$ the equation becomes $yz = 0$, so the minimal primes of $(x)$ are $\mathfrak{p} = (x, y)$ and $\mathfrak{p}' = (x, z)$, two planes through the vertex. Both have height $1$. As in the last example, $\mathfrak{p}$ needs two generators at the vertex. Here the class group is $\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $[\mathfrak{p}]$, and no multiple of $\mathfrak{p}$ is principal (Chapter 8). This is the local picture of a variety that is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial. The blow-ups of $\mathfrak{p}$ and of $\mathfrak{p}'$ are the two small resolutions of the vertex, and they differ by the Atiyah flop of Volume 8.

**Example P1.5.26 (the theorem fails without the Noetherian hypothesis).** Let $B = k(x)[y]_{(y)}$, a local ring with maximal ideal $yB$, and let
$$
V = \{ f \in B : f(0) \in k[x]_{(x)} \} ,
$$
where $f(0) \in k(x)$ is the value at $y = 0$. The map $V \to k[x]_{(x)}$, $f \mapsto f(0)$, is a surjective ring map with kernel $P = yB$, so $P$ is a prime of $V$, and its preimage $\mathfrak{m} = \{f : f(0) \in x k[x]_{(x)}\}$ is a maximal ideal. The chain $0 \subsetneq P \subsetneq \mathfrak{m}$ shows $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{m} \geq 2$. Every element $yg$ of $P$ equals $x \cdot (yg/x)$ with $yg/x \in P \subseteq V$, so $P \subseteq xV$. Let $\mathfrak{Q}$ be a prime that contains $x$. Then $\mathfrak{Q} \supseteq P$, and $\mathfrak{Q}/P$ is a prime of the discrete valuation ring $k[x]_{(x)}$ that contains $x$, so $\mathfrak{Q} = \mathfrak{m}$. So $\mathfrak{m}$ is minimal over $(x)$ and has height at least $2$. In fact $\mathfrak{m} = xV$: for $f \in \mathfrak{m}$, the quotient $f/x$ lies in $V$, since $f(0)/x \in k[x]_{(x)}$. So even a principal maximal ideal can have height $2$. The ring $V$ is a valuation ring of rank $2$ (Chapter 7) and is not Noetherian.

### Exercises

1. (A) Find the minimal primes of $(xy, xz)$ and of $(x^2, xy)$ in $k[x, y, z]$, and their heights. Explain why the embedded prime $(x, y)$ of $(x^2, xy)$ is not bounded by the height theorem.
2. (A) Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring and $x \in \mathfrak{m}$ a nonzerodivisor. Show that $\dim A/xA = \dim A - 1$. Show that $\dim k[[x, y, z]]/(f) = 2$ for every $f \neq 0$ in the maximal ideal (use Section 5.3 for $\dim k[[x, y, z]] = 3$).
3. (B) Show that a Noetherian ring satisfies the descending chain condition on primes. Show that $k[x_1, x_2, \dots]$ does not: $(x_1, x_2, \dots) \supsetneq (x_2, x_3, \dots) \supsetneq \cdots$.
4. (B) Let $A$ be Noetherian and $\mathfrak{p}$ a prime of height at least $2$. Show that $\mathfrak{p}$ contains infinitely many primes of height $1$. (Hint: if $\mathfrak{q}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{q}_s$ were all of them, choose $x \in \mathfrak{p}$ outside the $\mathfrak{q}_j$ and outside the minimal primes of $A$, and look at a minimal prime of $(x)$ inside $\mathfrak{p}$.) Interpret this for a point on a surface.
5. (B) In $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$ with $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$, show that $A/(x) \cong k[y, z]/(z^2)$ and that $(x)$ is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary. Show that $\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}} = zA_{\mathfrak{p}}$ and deduce $\mathfrak{p}^{(2)} = (x)$.
6. (B) Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring and $x_1, \dots, x_d$ a system of parameters. Show that $x_1^{a_1}, \dots, x_d^{a_d}$ is a system of parameters for all $a_i \geq 1$. Give a system of parameters of $k[x, y]_{(x, y)}$ that does not generate the maximal ideal.
7. (B) Let $A = k[x, y, z, w]$ and let $\mathfrak{p}$ be the prime of the affine cone over the twisted cubic, the kernel of $k[x, y, z, w] \to k[s^3, s^2 t, s t^2, t^3]$. Show that $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 2$, and that $\mathfrak{p}$ is generated by the three $2 \times 2$ minors of $\begin{pmatrix} x & y & z \\ y & z & w \end{pmatrix}$ but not by two elements. (Use the grading and Nakayama's lemma. The radical of an ideal with two generators can still equal $\mathfrak{p}$, but this is harder.)

### Notes and history

Krull proved the principal ideal theorem, his Hauptidealsatz, in 1928, and the height theorem soon after. The proof with symbolic powers given here is the standard modern one, and it is the proof of Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapter 11, and of Matsumura, Chapter 5. Chevalley introduced systems of parameters in 1943 in his work on local rings. The criterion for factorial domains in terms of height one primes is classical, and Chapter 8 recasts it as the vanishing of the class group. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapter 11, Matsumura, Chapter 5, and Eisenbud, Chapter 10.
