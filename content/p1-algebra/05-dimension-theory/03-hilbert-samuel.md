## Hilbert functions and the dimension theorem

### Statement

**Definition P1.5.27 (graded ring and graded module).** A *graded ring* is a ring $A$ with a decomposition $A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_n$ into additive subgroups such that $A_m A_n \subseteq A_{m+n}$. Then $A_0$ is a subring and each $A_n$ is an $A_0$-module. A *graded module* over $A$ is a module $M = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} M_n$ with $A_m M_n \subseteq M_{m+n}$. The elements of $A_n$ and of $M_n$ are *homogeneous of degree* $n$. Chapter 13 studies graded rings in detail. Here they are a tool for counting.

Lean: Mathlib `GradedRing` and `GradedAlgebra` (definitions).

**Example P1.5.28.** The polynomial ring $k[x_1, \dots, x_s]$ is graded by total degree, with $A_n$ the forms of degree $n$. A quotient by an ideal generated by homogeneous elements is graded, for example the coordinate ring $k[x, y, z]/(f)$ of a plane projective curve with $f$ a form.

**Example P1.5.29.** Let $A$ be a ring and $I$ an ideal. The *associated graded ring* is $\operatorname{gr}_I(A) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} I^n/I^{n+1}$, with the product $(a + I^{m+1})(b + I^{n+1}) = ab + I^{m+n+1}$ for $a \in I^m$ and $b \in I^n$. For an $A$-module $M$, $\operatorname{gr}_I(M) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} I^n M/I^{n+1} M$ is a graded module over it. For the local ring of a variety at a point $p$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, $\operatorname{Spec} \operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)$ is the *tangent cone* at $p$. For $A = k[x, y]_{(x, y)}/(y^2 - x^2 - x^3)$, the node, $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A) = k[x, y]/(y^2 - x^2)$, the two tangent lines.

**Theorem P1.5.30 (Hilbert).** Let $A = A_0[x_1, \dots, x_s]$ be a graded ring with $A_0$ Artinian and $x_1, \dots, x_s \in A_1$, and let $M$ be a finitely generated graded $A$-module. Then each $M_n$ has finite length over $A_0$, and there is a polynomial $P_M \in \mathbb{Q}[t]$ of degree at most $s - 1$ with
$$
\ell_{A_0}(M_n) = P_M(n) \quad \text{for all large } n .
$$
Here the zero polynomial has degree $-1$.

Lean: not in Lean in this form; Mathlib has `Polynomial.hilbertPoly`, the polynomial whose values are the coefficients of $p(t)/(1 - t)^d$ (definition).

*Proof.* The ring $A_0$ is Noetherian (Theorem P1.3.35), so $A$ is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem. Let $M$ be generated by homogeneous $m_1, \dots, m_r$ of degrees $d_1, \dots, d_r$. Then $M_n$ is generated over $A_0$ by the elements $x^\alpha m_j$ with $|\alpha| + d_j = n$, which are finitely many, so $M_n$ is a finitely generated module over an Artinian ring. Such a module is Noetherian and Artinian, so it has finite length (Proposition P1.3.28).

Induction on $s$. If $s = 0$, then $A = A_0$, and $M_n = 0$ for $n > \max d_j$, so $P_M = 0$. Let $s \geq 1$. Multiplication by $x_s$ gives exact sequences of $A_0$-modules
$$
0 \to K_n \to M_n \xrightarrow{x_s} M_{n+1} \to L_{n+1} \to 0 ,
$$
where $K = \bigoplus K_n$ is the kernel and $L = M/x_s M$ the cokernel of multiplication by $x_s$ on $M$. Both are finitely generated graded $A$-modules, since $A$ is Noetherian, and both are killed by $x_s$. So they are graded modules over $A/(x_s) = A_0[x_1, \dots, x_{s-1}]$, and by induction $\ell(K_n)$ and $\ell(L_n)$ are polynomials in $n$ of degree at most $s - 2$ for large $n$. By additivity of length,
$$
\ell(M_{n+1}) - \ell(M_n) = \ell(L_{n+1}) - \ell(K_n),
$$
a polynomial of degree at most $s - 2$ for large $n$. It remains to show: if $f : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ and $\Delta f(n) = f(n+1) - f(n)$ agrees with a polynomial of degree at most $e$ for large $n$, then $f$ agrees with a polynomial of degree at most $e + 1$ for large $n$. Write that polynomial as $\sum_{i=0}^{e} c_i \binom{t}{i}$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{Q}$, which is possible since the binomial polynomials $\binom{t}{i}$ form a basis of the polynomials of degree at most $e$. Let $g(t) = \sum_i c_i \binom{t}{i+1}$. Since $\binom{t+1}{i+1} - \binom{t}{i+1} = \binom{t}{i}$, the function $f - g$ has $\Delta(f - g)(n) = 0$ for large $n$, so it is constant for large $n$, and $f = g + c$ for large $n$. $\square$

**Definition P1.5.31 (Hilbert-Samuel function).** Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring, $\mathfrak{q}$ an $\mathfrak{m}$-primary ideal and $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module. The *Hilbert-Samuel function* is
$$
\chi^M_{\mathfrak{q}}(n) = \ell_A(M/\mathfrak{q}^n M) .
$$
It is finite, since $M/\mathfrak{q}^n M$ is a finitely generated module over the Artinian ring $A/\mathfrak{q}^n$ (a Noetherian ring with one prime).

Lean: not in Lean.

**Proposition P1.5.32 (the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial).** In this situation, let $\mathfrak{q}$ be generated by $s$ elements. There is a polynomial of degree at most $s$ that agrees with $\chi^M_{\mathfrak{q}}(n)$ for large $n$. Its degree $d(M)$ does not depend on $\mathfrak{q}$. Write $d(A)$ for $d(M)$ with $M = A$.

Lean: not in Lean.

*Proof.* The ring $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{q}}(A)$ has degree zero part $A/\mathfrak{q}$, which is Artinian, and it is generated over $A/\mathfrak{q}$ by the images in $\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}^2$ of the $s$ generators of $\mathfrak{q}$. The module $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{q}}(M)$ is generated by its degree zero part $M/\mathfrak{q}M$, so it is finitely generated. By Hilbert's theorem, $\ell(\mathfrak{q}^n M/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1} M)$ is a polynomial of degree at most $s - 1$ for large $n$. Lengths over $A/\mathfrak{q}$ and over $A$ agree for modules killed by $\mathfrak{q}$. By additivity,
$$
\chi^M_{\mathfrak{q}}(n) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \ell(\mathfrak{q}^i M/\mathfrak{q}^{i+1} M),
$$
and the step from $\Delta f$ to $f$ in the last proof shows that it is a polynomial of degree at most $s$ for large $n$. If $\mathfrak{q}$ is $\mathfrak{m}$-primary, then $\mathfrak{m}^r \subseteq \mathfrak{q} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ for some $r$ (Proposition P1.3.21), so $\mathfrak{m}^{rn} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}^n \subseteq \mathfrak{m}^n$ and
$$
\chi^M_{\mathfrak{m}}(n) \leq \chi^M_{\mathfrak{q}}(n) \leq \chi^M_{\mathfrak{m}}(rn) .
$$
Polynomials that satisfy these inequalities for large $n$ have the same degree. $\square$

If $d = d(A)$, the leading term of $\chi^A_{\mathfrak{q}}$ is $e(\mathfrak{q}) n^d/d!$ with $e(\mathfrak{q})$ a positive integer (Exercise 5), the *multiplicity* of $\mathfrak{q}$. The multiplicity of $A$ is $e(A) = e(\mathfrak{m})$.

**Lemma P1.5.33 (Artin-Rees).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $I$ an ideal, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module and $N \subseteq M$ a submodule. There is an integer $c \geq 0$ with
$$
I^n M \cap N = I^{n-c}(I^c M \cap N) \quad \text{for all } n \geq c .
$$

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.exists_pow_inf_eq_pow_smul` (proved in Mathlib), with the Rees algebra `reesAlgebra`.

*Proof.* Let $A^* = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} I^n$, the *Rees algebra*, a graded ring that is the subring $A[It]$ of $A[t]$. If $I = (a_1, \dots, a_s)$, then $A^* = A[a_1 t, \dots, a_s t]$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra, so it is Noetherian. The graded $A^*$-module $M^* = \bigoplus_n I^n M$ is generated by the generators of $M$ in degree $0$, so it is finitely generated. The subgroup $N^* = \bigoplus_n (I^n M \cap N)$ is a graded submodule, since $I (I^n M \cap N) \subseteq I^{n+1} M \cap N$. Since $A^*$ is Noetherian, $N^*$ is finitely generated, by homogeneous elements $g_j \in I^{d_j} M \cap N$ of degrees $d_j \leq c$. For $n \geq c$, every element of $I^n M \cap N$ is a sum of terms $a g_j$ with $a \in I^{n - d_j}$. Since $I^{n-d_j} = I^{n-c} I^{c - d_j}$ and $I^{c-d_j} g_j \subseteq I^c M \cap N$, this gives $I^n M \cap N \subseteq I^{n-c}(I^c M \cap N)$. The other inclusion is clear. $\square$

**Proposition P1.5.34 (cutting by a nonzerodivisor).** Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring, $M$ a finitely generated module and $x \in \mathfrak{m}$ a nonzerodivisor on $M$. Then $d(M/xM) \leq d(M) - 1$.

Lean: not in Lean.

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak{q}$ be $\mathfrak{m}$-primary, $N = xM$ and $M' = M/xM$. For each $n$ there is an exact sequence
$$
0 \to N/(N \cap \mathfrak{q}^n M) \to M/\mathfrak{q}^n M \to M'/\mathfrak{q}^n M' \to 0 .
$$
By the Artin-Rees lemma there is $c$ with $N \cap \mathfrak{q}^n M = \mathfrak{q}^{n-c}(N \cap \mathfrak{q}^c M) \subseteq \mathfrak{q}^{n-c} N$ for $n \geq c$. Multiplication by $x$ is an isomorphism $M \to N$ that maps $\mathfrak{q}^{n-c} M$ onto $\mathfrak{q}^{n-c} N$. So
$$
\ell(N/(N \cap \mathfrak{q}^n M)) \geq \ell(N/\mathfrak{q}^{n-c} N) = \chi^M_{\mathfrak{q}}(n - c),
$$
and $\chi^{M'}_{\mathfrak{q}}(n) \leq \chi^M_{\mathfrak{q}}(n) - \chi^M_{\mathfrak{q}}(n - c)$ for $n \geq c$. The right side is a polynomial of degree at most $d(M) - 1$ for large $n$, since the leading terms cancel. $\square$

**Theorem P1.5.35 (the dimension theorem).** Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring, and let $\delta(A)$ be the least number of elements of $\mathfrak{m}$ that generate an $\mathfrak{m}$-primary ideal. Then
$$
\dim A = d(A) = \delta(A) .
$$

Lean: not in Lean; Mathlib has the height theorem, which gives $\dim A \leq \delta(A)$, but not the Hilbert-Samuel function.

*Proof.* Section 5.2 gives $\dim A = \delta(A)$: the height theorem gives $\dim A \leq \delta(A)$ and a system of parameters gives $\delta(A) \leq \dim A$. The proposition on the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial, applied to an $\mathfrak{m}$-primary ideal with $\delta(A)$ generators, gives $d(A) \leq \delta(A)$.

It remains to prove $\dim A \leq d(A)$, by induction on $d(A)$. If $d(A) = 0$, then $\ell(A/\mathfrak{m}^n)$ is constant for large $n$, so $\mathfrak{m}^n = \mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$, and Nakayama's lemma gives $\mathfrak{m}^n = 0$. Every prime contains $\mathfrak{m}^n$, hence $\mathfrak{m}$, so $\dim A = 0$. Let $d(A) > 0$ and let $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_r$ be a chain of primes with $r \geq 1$. Choose $x \in \mathfrak{p}_1 \setminus \mathfrak{p}_0$, and let $A' = A/\mathfrak{p}_0$, a local domain, with $x'$ the image of $x$. Then $x' \neq 0$ is a nonzerodivisor on $A'$. The Hilbert-Samuel function of $A'$ with respect to its maximal ideal is $\ell(A/(\mathfrak{p}_0 + \mathfrak{m}^n)) \leq \ell(A/\mathfrak{m}^n)$, so $d(A') \leq d(A)$, and by the last proposition
$$
d(A'/x'A') \leq d(A') - 1 \leq d(A) - 1 .
$$
By induction $\dim A'/x'A' \leq d(A) - 1$. The primes $\mathfrak{p}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{p}_r$ contain $\mathfrak{p}_0$ and $x$, so they give a chain of length $r - 1$ in $A'/x'A' = A/(\mathfrak{p}_0 + xA)$. Hence $r - 1 \leq d(A) - 1$, and $r \leq d(A)$. $\square$

**Corollary P1.5.36 (power series).** The ring $k[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$ is a Noetherian local ring of dimension $n$, and its multiplicity is $1$.

Lean: Mathlib `MvPowerSeries.isNoetherianRing` (instance, proved in Mathlib); the dimension is not in Lean in this form.

*Proof.* The ring is Noetherian by Remark P1.3.13 and induction, and local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$, since a power series with nonzero constant term is a unit. The quotient $A/\mathfrak{m}^j$ has the monomials of degree less than $j$ as a $k$-basis, so
$$
\chi^A_{\mathfrak{m}}(j) = \binom{n + j - 1}{n} = \frac{j^n}{n!} + \dots ,
$$
a polynomial of degree $n$ with leading coefficient $1/n!$. $\square$

### Proof in words

There are three ways to measure the size of a local ring: the longest chain of primes, the least number of equations that cut out the closed point, and the growth of the lengths $\ell(A/\mathfrak{m}^n)$. The dimension theorem says that they agree. The second and the first agree by Krull's theorems of Section 5.2. The third is at most the second because the associated graded ring is generated by the images of the equations, and Hilbert's theorem bounds the growth of a graded module by the number of generators of the ring. The first is at most the third because cutting by a nonzerodivisor lowers the degree of the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial, a consequence of the Artin-Rees lemma, and a chain of primes can be shortened by one such cut.

Geometrically, $\ell(A/\mathfrak{m}^n)$ counts the conditions that the Taylor expansion of order $n - 1$ at a point imposes, and it grows like $e \, n^d/d!$. At a smooth point of a $d$-dimensional variety this is the count of monomials, with $e = 1$. At a singular point $e > 1$. For a hypersurface $e$ is the order of the equation, and for a cone over a projective variety $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ it is the degree of $Y$. The multiplicity is the first invariant of a singularity. It bounds the singularities that the MMP allows: the multiplicity of a terminal 3-fold singularity, for example, is at most $2$ when it is a hypersurface, and Volume 4 meets such bounds.

### Examples

**Example P1.5.37 (plane curve singularities).** Let $R = k[x, y]_{(x, y)}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n}$, let $f \in \mathfrak{n}$ have order $r$, that is, $f \in \mathfrak{n}^r \setminus \mathfrak{n}^{r+1}$, and let $A = R/(f)$. The associated graded ring of $R$ is the domain $k[x, y]$, so the lowest degree form of a product is the product of the lowest degree forms, and the order is additive: $fg \in \mathfrak{n}^n$ if and only if $g \in \mathfrak{n}^{n - r}$. So $(f) \cap \mathfrak{n}^n = f \mathfrak{n}^{n-r}$ for $n \geq r$, and since multiplication by $f$ is injective,
$$
\chi^A(n) = \ell(R/\mathfrak{n}^n) - \ell\big((f)/f\mathfrak{n}^{n-r}\big) = \binom{n+1}{2} - \binom{n - r + 1}{2} = rn - \frac{r(r-1)}{2} .
$$
So $\dim A = 1$ and $e(A) = r$. A smooth point of a curve has $e = 1$, the node $y^2 = x^2 + x^3$ and the cusp $y^2 = x^3$ have $e = 2$, and the ordinary triple point $y^3 = x^3 + x^4$ has $e = 3$. The node and the cusp have the same multiplicity, and the tangent cone tells them apart: two lines against a double line.

**Example P1.5.38 (the quadric cone).** Let $A = k[x, y, z]_{(x, y, z)}/(xy - z^2)$. The same argument as in the last example, with $R = k[x, y, z]_{(x, y, z)}$ and $r = 2$, gives, for $n \geq 2$,
$$
\chi^A(n) = \binom{n + 2}{3} - \binom{n}{3} = n^2 .
$$
A direct count agrees: the forms of degree $j$ in $k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$ have dimension $2j + 1$, and $\sum_{j < n} (2j + 1) = n^2$. So $\dim A = 2$ and $e(A) = 2$, since $n^2 = 2 \cdot n^2/2!$. This is the degree of the conic $xy = z^2$ in $\mathbb{P}^2$. Here the multiplicity is $2$, but the singularity is mild: it is the quotient singularity $\frac{1}{2}(1, 1)$, a canonical singularity in the sense of Volume 4.

**Example P1.5.39 (the theorem fails without the Noetherian hypothesis).** Let $V$ be the valuation ring of rank $2$ of Section 5.2, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = xV$ and the prime $P = yB$. Then $P \subseteq x^n V$ for every $n$, so $V/x^n V \cong k[x]_{(x)}/(x^n)$, and $\ell(V/\mathfrak{m}^n) = n$. So the Hilbert-Samuel function is a polynomial of degree $1$, and $\delta(V) = 1$, but $\dim V = 2$. The proof of the dimension theorem breaks at the Artin-Rees lemma and at Nakayama's lemma, which need finitely generated modules, and $P$ is not finitely generated.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy, xz, yz)$, the three coordinate axes, graded by degree. Show that $\ell(A_n) = 3$ for $n \geq 1$. Compute the Hilbert-Samuel function of the local ring at the origin and show $e = 3$.
2. (A) Let $A = k[s^3, s^2 t, s t^2, t^3]$, graded so that the four generators have degree $1$. Show that $\dim_k A_n = 3n + 1$. Compare with the Hilbert polynomial of $\mathbb{P}^1$ embedded by cubics.
3. (A) Let $f \in k[x, y, z]$ be a form of degree $e$ and $A = k[x, y, z]/(f)$. Show that $\dim_k A_n = en - e(e - 3)/2$ for $n \geq e - 2$. With $g = (e-1)(e-2)/2$, this is $en + 1 - g$, the Riemann-Roch count of Volume P5 for a plane curve of degree $e$ and genus $g$.
4. (B) Let $\mathfrak{q} = (x^a, y^b)$ in $k[x, y]_{(x, y)}$. Show that $e(\mathfrak{q}) = ab$. Show that $e(\mathfrak{q}) \geq e(\mathfrak{m})$ for every $\mathfrak{m}$-primary $\mathfrak{q}$ in a Noetherian local ring.
5. (B) Show that a polynomial $P \in \mathbb{Q}[t]$ with $P(n) \in \mathbb{Z}$ for all large $n$ is an integer combination of the $\binom{t}{i}$. Deduce that $e(\mathfrak{q})$ is a positive integer.
6. (B) Let $f \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ have order $r \geq 1$ at the origin and let $A$ be the local ring of $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]/(f)$ at the origin. Show that $\dim A = n - 1$ and $e(A) = r$.
7. (C) Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring and $M$ a finitely generated module. Show that $d(M) = \dim M$, the dimension of $\operatorname{Supp} M$. (Hint: for $\dim M \leq d(M)$ use the proof of the theorem with $M$ in place of $A$; for $d(M) \leq \dim M$ use a system of parameters of $A/\operatorname{Ann} M$.)

### Lean

Mathlib proves the Artin-Rees lemma as `Ideal.exists_pow_inf_eq_pow_smul`, through the Rees algebra `reesAlgebra` and stable filtrations. It defines graded rings by `GradedRing` and has the polynomial `Polynomial.hilbertPoly` attached to a rational power series $p(t)/(1 - t)^d$, but it does not yet have the Hilbert function of a graded module, the Hilbert-Samuel function or the dimension theorem. Its file on Hilbert polynomials lists the case of graded modules as future work. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Hilbert proved that the Hilbert function of a graded module is eventually a polynomial in 1890, in the same paper as the basis theorem and the syzygy theorem. Samuel defined the Hilbert-Samuel function of a local ring and the multiplicity of a primary ideal in 1951, and Chevalley had developed the dimension theory of local rings through systems of parameters in the 1940s. Artin and Rees found their lemma independently in the 1950s. The proof here is the one of Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapters 10 and 11. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapters 10 and 11, Matsumura, Chapter 5, and Eisenbud, Chapters 5 and 12.
