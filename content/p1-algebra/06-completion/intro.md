# Completion

This chapter studies the completion of a ring along an ideal, the algebra of formal neighbourhoods and Taylor series. Section 6.1 defines inverse limits and the $I$-adic completion of a module, gives the main examples $k[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$ and $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and proves the Krull intersection theorem with the Artin-Rees lemma of Section 5.3. Section 6.2 proves the main facts for a Noetherian ring $A$: completion is exact on finitely generated modules, $\hat{M} = M \otimes_A \hat{A}$, the completion $\hat{A}$ is flat over $A$ and Noetherian, and for a local ring it is faithfully flat with the same dimension. Section 6.3 proves Hensel's lemma by Newton's method and the Cohen structure theorem for complete local rings that contain a field of characteristic zero.

The completion of the local ring of a variety $X$ at a point $p$ sees an infinitesimal neighbourhood of $p$ of every order at once. Two varieties with isomorphic completions at $p$ have the same singularity at $p$ in the analytic sense, even when no Zariski open sets of them are isomorphic. The node $y^2 = x^2 + x^3$ is irreducible, but in the completion its equation factors into two smooth branches, and so it is analytically the same as the pair of axes $xy = 0$. At a smooth point every variety of dimension $d$ is analytically the same as $\mathbb{A}^d$: the completion is $k[[x_1, \dots, x_d]]$.

The minimal model program classifies singularities in this analytic sense. The du Val singularities of Volume 4, the terminal singularities of threefolds and the cDV points all have normal forms that hold only after completion, such as $xy = z^{n+1}$ for the $A_n$ singularity. Over a field of characteristic zero the classes terminal, canonical, klt and lc do not change under analytic isomorphism, so they can be studied on the completion. Faithful flatness of $A \to \hat{A}$ is the tool that carries properties back and forth: depth, the Cohen-Macaulay property and regularity are the same for $A$ and $\hat{A}$ (Chapters 9 and 10).

The chapter follows Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapter 10, Matsumura, Chapter 3, and Eisenbud, Chapter 7. It uses Chapters 1 to 5, in particular flatness from Section 1.4 and the Artin-Rees lemma and the dimension theorem from Section 5.3.
