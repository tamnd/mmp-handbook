# Serre's criterion and reflexive modules

This chapter turns normality into a statement about depth and codimension, and studies the modules that behave like the sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ of Weil divisors. Section 11.1 defines Serre's conditions $R_k$ and $S_k$, proves that depth at least two in an ideal is the same as the extension of homomorphisms across the zero set of the ideal, and proves the algebraic Hartogs property $A = \bigcap_{\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1} A_\mathfrak{p}$ for domains that satisfy $S_2$. Section 11.2 proves Serre's criteria: a Noetherian ring is reduced if and only if it satisfies $R_0$ and $S_1$, and a Noetherian domain is normal if and only if it satisfies $R_1$ and $S_2$. Section 11.3 defines reflexive modules, characterizes them over a normal domain by their localizations at height one primes, and identifies the class group with the set of reflexive modules of rank one.

For a variety $X$, the condition $R_1$ says that $X$ is smooth outside a closed subset of codimension at least two, and $S_2$ says that functions defined outside a closed subset of codimension at least two extend. So a normal variety is one whose singularities are small and whose functions satisfy Hartogs's theorem. Two consequences are used again and again: a normal surface is Cohen-Macaulay, as Chapter 10 promised, and a hypersurface is normal exactly when its singular locus has codimension at least two. This keeps the promise of Exercise 8 of Section 8.1.

In the minimal model program a Weil divisor $D$ on a normal variety $X$ gives the reflexive sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ of rank one, and the canonical sheaf $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}_X(K_X)$ is the main example. The divisor $D$ is Cartier exactly when $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ is invertible, and $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier when some reflexive power $\mathcal{O}_X(rD)$ is invertible (Volume 4). A flip or a flop is an isomorphism outside closed subsets of codimension at least two, and by $S_2$ it does not change the sections of reflexive sheaves of rank one (Volume 8). The same property makes the canonical ring of Volume 9 a ring of sections of reflexive sheaves.

The chapter follows Matsumura, Chapters 4 and 8, and Eisenbud, Chapter 11. It uses Chapters 1 to 10, in particular Krull's theorem and the class group of Chapter 8 and depth from Section 10.1.
