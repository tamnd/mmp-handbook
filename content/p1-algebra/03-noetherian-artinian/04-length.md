## Length and the Jordan-Hölder theorem

### Statement

**Definition P1.3.26 (composition series and length).** A module $M \neq 0$ is *simple* if its only submodules are $0$ and $M$. A simple $A$-module is isomorphic to $A/\mathfrak{m}$ for a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$: it is generated by any nonzero element $x$, and the kernel of $a \mapsto ax$ is a maximal ideal. A *composition series* of $M$ is a chain
$$
0 = M_0 \subsetneq M_1 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq M_n = M
$$
with every quotient $M_i/M_{i-1}$ simple. Its length is $n$, and the quotients are its *composition factors*. The *length* $\ell(M) = \ell_A(M)$ is the least length of a composition series of $M$, or $\infty$ if $M$ has none. A module has *finite length* if $\ell(M) < \infty$.

Lean: Mathlib `CompositionSeries` and `Module.length` (definitions). Mathlib defines `Module.length` as the Krull dimension of the lattice of submodules, that is, the supremum of the lengths of chains of submodules. The theorem below shows that this agrees with the definition here.

**Theorem P1.3.27 (Jordan-Hölder).** Let $M$ have a composition series of length $n$. Then every chain of submodules of $M$ has length at most $n$, and every chain can be refined to a composition series. All composition series of $M$ have length $n$, and they have the same composition factors, counted with multiplicity, up to isomorphism and order.

Lean: Mathlib `CompositionSeries.jordan_holder` and `Module.length_compositionSeries` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* Step 1: if $N \subsetneq M$, then $\ell(N) < \ell(M)$. Take a composition series $(M_i)$ of $M$ of length $n = \ell(M)$ and put $N_i = N \cap M_i$. The map $N_i/N_{i-1} \to M_i/M_{i-1}$ is injective, so $N_i/N_{i-1}$ is $0$ or simple. Deleting repeated terms of $(N_i)$ gives a composition series of $N$ of length at most $n$. If its length is $n$, no term repeats, so $N_i/N_{i-1} \cong M_i/M_{i-1}$ for all $i$. Then $N_1 = M_1$, since $N_1 \subseteq M_1$ and both are simple. Inductively $N_i = M_i$ for all $i$: from $N_{i-1} = M_{i-1}$ and $N_i \subseteq M_i$ with $N_i/M_{i-1}$ nonzero in the simple module $M_i/M_{i-1}$, it follows that $N_i = M_i$. So $N = M$, a contradiction.

Step 2: a chain $0 = N_0 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq N_k = M$ gives $0 = \ell(N_0) < \ell(N_1) < \dots < \ell(N_k) = \ell(M)$ by Step 1, so $k \leq \ell(M)$. A composition series of length $k$ has $k \leq \ell(M)$ by Step 2 and $\ell(M) \leq k$ by definition, so $k = \ell(M)$. A chain of length less than $\ell(M)$ is not a composition series, so it has a step $N_{i-1} \subsetneq N_i$ with a proper submodule strictly between them, and it can be refined. Since the length of a chain is bounded, refinement ends at a composition series.

Step 3, the factors, by induction on $n = \ell(M)$. Let $(M_i)$ and $(M'_i)$ be two composition series of length $n$. If $M_{n-1} = M'_{n-1}$, apply induction to $M_{n-1}$. If not, $M_{n-1} + M'_{n-1} = M$, since it strictly contains $M_{n-1}$ and $M/M_{n-1}$ is simple. Let $K = M_{n-1} \cap M'_{n-1}$. Then
$$
M_{n-1}/K \cong M/M'_{n-1}, \qquad M'_{n-1}/K \cong M/M_{n-1},
$$
and both are simple. Choose a composition series of $K$, of length $n - 2$ by Step 2. Adding $M_{n-1}$ to it gives a composition series of $M_{n-1}$, and by induction its factors are those of $(M_i)_{i \leq n-1}$. So the factors of $(M_i)$ are the factors of $K$ together with $M/M'_{n-1}$ and $M/M_{n-1}$. The same argument for $M'_{n-1}$ gives the same list for $(M'_i)$. $\square$

**Proposition P1.3.28 (additivity of length).** For an exact sequence $0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$, $\ell(M) = \ell(M') + \ell(M'')$. A module has finite length if and only if it is Noetherian and Artinian.

Lean: Mathlib `Module.length_eq_add_of_exact` and `isFiniteLength_iff_isNoetherian_isArtinian` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* If $M'$ and $M''$ have composition series, the series of $M'$ followed by the preimages in $M$ of the series of $M''$ is a composition series of $M$, of length $\ell(M') + \ell(M'')$. If $M$ has finite length, so do $M'$ and $M''$, by intersecting and projecting a composition series as in Step 1. If $M$ has finite length, chains have bounded length, so both chain conditions hold. Conversely, let $M$ be Noetherian and Artinian. Choose a maximal proper submodule $M^{(1)}$ of $M$, then a maximal proper submodule $M^{(2)}$ of $M^{(1)}$, and so on, using Noetherian submodules. The chain $M \supsetneq M^{(1)} \supsetneq \dots$ has simple quotients, and by the descending chain condition it ends, which can only happen at $0$. $\square$

**Corollary P1.3.29 (length over a field).** Over a field $k$, $\ell_k(V) = \dim_k V$. If $A$ is a $k$-algebra and every composition factor of $M$ is isomorphic to $A/\mathfrak{m}$ with $A/\mathfrak{m} = k$, then $\ell_A(M) = \dim_k M$.

Lean: Mathlib `Module.length_eq_finrank` and `Module.length_eq_rank` (proved in Mathlib).

*Proof.* The simple $k$-modules are the lines. In the second case each factor of a composition series has $k$-dimension $1$, and dimension is additive. $\square$

### Proof in words

A composition series cuts a module into simple pieces, the residue fields $A/\mathfrak{m}$. The Jordan-Hölder theorem says that the pieces and their number do not depend on the cut. The proof compares two series through the intersection of their next to last terms. The number of pieces is the length, which is additive in exact sequences, like dimension.

For a $k$-algebra whose relevant residue fields are $k$, length is the $k$-dimension. This is how length enters geometry: a zero dimensional subscheme of a variety is a finite set of points with a nilpotent structure, and the length of its local ring at a point is the multiplicity of the point. The intersection number of two curves at a point of a smooth surface is the length of $\mathcal{O}_{S,p}/(f, g)$. Volume 2 builds the intersection form $D \cdot C$ on surfaces from these lengths, and the order of vanishing of a function along a prime divisor (Chapter 7) is a length too.

### Examples

**Example P1.3.30 (the integers).** For $n = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_r$ with primes $p_i$, not necessarily distinct, the chain $0 \subset p_1 \cdots p_{r-1}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \subset \dots \subset p_1\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a composition series with factors $\mathbb{Z}/p_i$. So $\ell_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n) = r$, and the Jordan-Hölder theorem gives the uniqueness of prime factorization. The $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\mathbb{Z}$ has infinite length.

**Example P1.3.31 (a tangent line).** Let $A = k[x, y]_{(x, y)}$ and consider the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 0$. Then
$$
A/(y - x^2, y) \cong k[x]_{(x)}/(x^2),
$$
with $k$-basis $1, x$. Its length is $2$: the line meets the parabola at the origin with multiplicity $2$, as a tangent line should. The line $y = x$ gives $A/(y - x^2, y - x) \cong k[x]_{(x)}/(x - x^2) = k[x]_{(x)}/(x) = k$, since $1 - x$ is a unit in $k[x]_{(x)}$. Its length is $1$: a transversal intersection.

**Example P1.3.32 (fat points).** The ring $k[x, y]/(x, y)^n$ has $k$-basis the monomials of degree less than $n$, so its length is $n(n + 1)/2$. The ring $k[x, y]/(x^2, y)$ has length $2$; it is a point with a tangent direction.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute the length of $k[x, y]/(x^2, y^3)$, of $k[x, y]/(x^2, xy, y^2)$ and of $\mathbb{Z}/360$.
2. (A) Let $A = \mathbb{R}[x]$ and $M = \mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2 + 1)$. Show that $\ell_A(M) = 1$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} M = 2$. Which hypothesis of the corollary fails?
3. (B) Compute the intersection multiplicity $\ell(A/(y^2 - x^3, y - \lambda x))$ at the origin, with $A = k[x, y]_{(x, y)}$, for every $\lambda \in k$. Explain the answer with the tangent cone of the cusp.
4. (B) Let $A$ be Noetherian, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module and $\mathfrak{p}$ a minimal element of $\operatorname{Supp} M$. Show that $M_{\mathfrak{p}}$ has finite length over $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$. (Hint: a prime filtration of $M$ from Section 2.4.)
5. (B) Show that $\ell(M_1 \oplus M_2) = \ell(M_1) + \ell(M_2)$ and that, for a module of finite length, every composition factor is $A/\mathfrak{m}$ for some $\mathfrak{m} \in \operatorname{Supp} M$.
6. (C) Let $M$ have finite length. Show that the natural map $M \to \bigoplus_{\mathfrak{m}} M_{\mathfrak{m}}$ over the maximal ideals in $\operatorname{Supp} M$ is an isomorphism, and that $\ell_A(M) = \sum_{\mathfrak{m}} \ell_{A_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})$.

### Notes and history

Jordan proved the invariance of the length of a composition series of a finite group in 1870, and Hölder the invariance of the factors in 1889. The version for modules is the same theorem. The use of lengths of local rings as intersection multiplicities goes back to Chevalley and Weil; Serre later showed that in higher dimension the length must be corrected by Tor terms. The literature state of every result here is theorem. The section follows Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapter 6.
