# Depth, Cohen-Macaulay and Gorenstein rings

This chapter studies the rings between regular local rings and arbitrary Noetherian local rings that occur as singularities in the minimal model program. Section 10.1 defines regular sequences and the depth of a module, proves Rees's theorem that all maximal regular sequences in an ideal have the same length, computed by the vanishing of Ext, and proves that the depth is at most the dimension of $A/\mathfrak{p}$ for every associated prime $\mathfrak{p}$. Section 10.2 defines Cohen-Macaulay rings, where depth equals dimension, proves that their systems of parameters are regular sequences and that they have no embedded primes, and proves the criterion that a local ring finite over a regular local ring of the same dimension is Cohen-Macaulay exactly when it is a free module. Section 10.3 defines the type of a Cohen-Macaulay ring and the Gorenstein rings, the Cohen-Macaulay rings of type one, and proves that complete intersections are Gorenstein.

For a variety $X$, depth measures how far a function defined outside a closed subset extends, and the Cohen-Macaulay property says that $X$ is equidimensional at each point in a strong sense: cutting by a general hypersurface through the point lowers the dimension by one, and keeps the property. A finite map from a Cohen-Macaulay variety to a smooth variety of the same dimension is flat, which explains the failure of flatness for the two planes meeting in a point of Example P1.5.50. Regular rings are Cohen-Macaulay, as Chapter 9 promised, and so are complete intersections and normal surfaces.

In the minimal model program the Gorenstein condition says that the canonical divisor $K_X$ of a normal Cohen-Macaulay variety is Cartier, and the index of Chapter 8 measures the failure of this condition. The Du Val singularities of Volume 4 are hypersurface singularities, hence Gorenstein, and the canonical surface singularities are exactly the Du Val singularities. Klt and rational singularities are Cohen-Macaulay, a theorem of Volume 4 that is used in the vanishing theorems of Volume 5. Depth, the Cohen-Macaulay property and the Gorenstein property are the same for $A$ and its completion $\hat{A}$, as Chapter 6 promised.

The chapter follows Matsumura, Chapter 6, Eisenbud, Chapters 17, 18 and 21, and Atiyah-Macdonald for the background on modules. It uses Chapters 1 to 9, in particular associated primes from Section 2.4, the dimension theory of Chapter 5, completion from Section 6.2 and the regular local rings of Chapter 9.
