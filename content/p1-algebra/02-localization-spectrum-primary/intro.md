# Localization, the prime spectrum and primary decomposition

This chapter turns a ring into a space and then cuts the space into pieces. Section 2.1 constructs the ring of fractions $S^{-1}A$ and the localized module $S^{-1}M$. Localization is exact, its prime ideals are the primes of $A$ that miss $S$, and many properties of a module can be tested one prime at a time. Section 2.2 makes the set $\operatorname{Spec} A$ of prime ideals a topological space, the prime spectrum, and proves its basic properties. Sections 2.3 and 2.4 decompose an ideal into primary ideals and identify the primes that occur as the associated primes of a module.

For an affine variety $X$ with coordinate ring $A = k[X]$, the localization $A_f$ is the coordinate ring of the open set where $f \neq 0$, and the local ring $A_\mathfrak{m}$ at a maximal ideal is the ring of rational functions defined near the point $\mathfrak{m}$. The space $\operatorname{Spec} A$ contains the points of $X$ as its closed points, and it adds one point for each irreducible closed subvariety: the generic point, which lies in every nonempty open subset of that subvariety. A primary decomposition of an ideal $I$ splits the closed set $V(I)$ into its irreducible components, and it also records the embedded points, where the ideal carries more structure than its radical. The associated primes are these components and embedded points.

The minimal model program needs all of this. Discrepancies are computed in the local ring at the generic point of a prime divisor, which is a discrete valuation ring by P1 Ch. 7. Serre's condition $S_2$ in P1 Ch. 11 is a condition on the associated primes of quotients, and it is part of the definition of the slc pairs of Volume 13. Flatness of a family over a smooth curve is a condition on associated primes, as Section 2.4 shows.

The chapter follows Atiyah-Macdonald, Chapters 3 and 4, with associated primes from Matsumura, Chapter 2, and Eisenbud, Chapter 3. It uses Chapter 1 throughout.
