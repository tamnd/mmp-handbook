# Kähler differentials

This chapter gives the algebraic theory of differential forms. Section 12.1 defines derivations and the module of Kähler differentials $\Omega_{B/k}$ by a universal property, computes it for polynomial rings, and proves the two fundamental exact sequences. They give a presentation of $\Omega_{A/k}$ by the Jacobian matrix for every finitely generated algebra $A$, and they identify the cotangent space at a rational point with the fibre of $\Omega_{A/k}$. Section 12.2 computes the differentials of field extensions: they vanish for finite separable extensions, and their dimension is the transcendence degree for separably generated extensions. This keeps the promise of Section 9.2 that the Jacobian matrix of an affine domain over a perfect field has rank $n - d$ at the generic point. Section 12.3 proves that a closed point of an affine domain over a perfect field is regular exactly when the differentials are free of rank $d$ near it, and defines the canonical module and the canonical divisor of a normal affine domain.

The differentials are the cotangent bundle of a variety in algebraic form. A smooth variety $X$ of dimension $d$ has the line bundle $\Omega^d_X = \bigwedge^d \Omega_X$ of $d$-forms, and its divisor class is the canonical class $K_X$. For a normal variety the smooth locus has a complement of codimension at least two, and the reflexive sheaf of Chapter 11 that extends $\Omega^d$ across it defines $K_X$ as a Weil divisor. The minimal model program studies the sign of $K_X$, so this definition stands behind every later volume. The examples of the chapter compute $K_{\mathbb{P}^n} = -(n+1)H$ and show that the canonical divisor of a normal surface hypersurface singularity, such as a du Val singularity, is Cartier and trivial.

The chapter follows Matsumura, Chapter 9, and Eisenbud, Chapter 16. It uses Chapters 1 to 11, in particular the Jacobian criterion of Section 9.2 and the reflexive modules of Section 11.3.
