# Plane curves and elliptic curves

This chapter studies curves in $\mathbb{A}^2$ and $\mathbb{P}^2$. The field $k$ is algebraically closed of any characteristic, except where a section says otherwise. The chapter uses projective varieties (Chapter 4) and singular points (Chapter 6). It gives the intersection multiplicity of two plane curves, Bézout's theorem, the two singular cubics, and the smooth cubics with their group law.

Two curves of this chapter come back in later volumes. The cuspidal cubic $y^2 = x^3$ is resolved by blow-ups in P8 Ch. 4, and Volume 4 computes its log canonical threshold $\frac56$. The smooth cubic is the first curve of genus 1. It is not rational, and P5 proves this with the genus.
