# Rational maps and the quadratic transformation of the plane

The field $k$ is algebraically closed, of any characteristic. A variety is an irreducible affine or quasi-projective variety over $k$, as in Chapters 3 and 4.

Birational geometry studies varieties up to maps that are isomorphisms on dense open sets. This chapter gives the first tools: the function field, rational maps, the blow-up of a point, and the standard quadratic transformation of $\mathbb{P}^2$. The minimal model program is a way to choose a good model in a birational class, so these objects are present in every later volume.

The chapter uses the Nullstellensatz of Chapter 3 and projective varieties from Chapter 4. Section 5.3 calls a point nonsingular in the sense of Section 6.2, but no proof in this chapter depends on Chapter 6. Self-intersection numbers of curves on surfaces appear only as a preview of P5. The quadratic transformation returns in P5 Ch. 7, where Castelnuovo's criterion is proved, and the blow-up of a point is the first example of a divisorial contraction in Volume 1.
