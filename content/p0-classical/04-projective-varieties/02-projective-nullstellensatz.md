## Projective algebraic sets and the projective Nullstellensatz

### Statement

Let $S = k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ and let $S_d$ be the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $d$, so that $S = \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} S_d$. Every $f \in S$ is a unique sum $f = \sum_d f_d$ of homogeneous components $f_d \in S_d$. The ideal $S_+ = (x_0, \dots, x_n) = \bigoplus_{d > 0} S_d$ is the irrelevant ideal.

A polynomial does not give a function on $\mathbb{P}^n$, because its value depends on the choice of homogeneous coordinates. For $F \in S_d$ the identity $F(\lambda a) = \lambda^d F(a)$ shows that the condition $F(a) = 0$ depends only on the point $[a]$.

**Definition P0.4.10 (homogeneous ideal).** An ideal $J \subseteq S$ is homogeneous if it is generated by homogeneous polynomials. Equivalently, for each $f \in J$ all the homogeneous components $f_d$ are in $J$.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.IsHomogeneous` (a definition for graded rings, in `Mathlib.RingTheory.GradedAlgebra.Homogeneous.Ideal`).

**Example P0.4.11.** The ideal $(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2) \subset k[x_0, x_1, x_2]$ is homogeneous, and so is $S_+$. The ideal $(x_1 - 1)$ is not homogeneous: it contains $x_1 - 1$ but not its components $x_1$ and $-1$.

**Example P0.4.12.** A sum, a product, an intersection and the radical of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous (Exercise 1 and the lemma below). The ideal $(x_0^2, x_0 x_1)$ is homogeneous, and its radical is $(x_0)$.

The two forms of the definition agree. If $J$ is generated by homogeneous $g_i$ and $f = \sum h_i g_i \in J$, then the component $f_d$ is $\sum_i (h_i)_{d - \deg g_i}\, g_i$, which is in $J$. Conversely, if $J$ contains the components of its elements, then these components generate $J$.

**Lemma P0.4.13 (radical and prime).** Let $J \subseteq S$ be a homogeneous ideal.

1. The radical $\sqrt{J}$ is homogeneous.
2. If $J \neq S$ and, for all homogeneous $f$ and $g$, $fg \in J$ implies $f \in J$ or $g \in J$, then $J$ is prime.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.radical` and `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_of_homogeneous_mem_or_mem` (proved in Mathlib, for graded rings).

*Proof.* For 1 let $f \in \sqrt{J}$, with $f \neq 0$, and let $f_e$ be its component of highest degree. If $f^m \in J$, then the component of $f^m$ of degree $me$ is $f_e^m$, and it is in $J$ because $J$ is homogeneous. So $f_e \in \sqrt{J}$, and $f - f_e \in \sqrt{J}$ has fewer components. By induction on the number of components all $f_d$ are in $\sqrt{J}$. For 2 suppose $fg \in J$ with $f, g \notin J$. Remove from $f$ the components that are in $J$, and the same for $g$; the product stays in $J$. Now let $f_a$ and $g_b$ be the components of highest degree of $f$ and $g$, so $f_a, g_b \notin J$. The component of degree $a + b$ of $fg$ is $f_a g_b$, and it is in $J$. This contradicts the hypothesis. $\square$

**Definition P0.4.14 (projective algebraic set).** For a set $T$ of homogeneous polynomials, $V(T) = \{[a] \in \mathbb{P}^n : F(a) = 0 \text{ for all } F \in T\}$. For a homogeneous ideal $J$, $V(J)$ is the set of points where all homogeneous elements of $J$ vanish. A projective algebraic set is a set $V(T)$. The Zariski topology on $\mathbb{P}^n$ has the projective algebraic sets as closed sets. A projective variety is an irreducible projective algebraic set. For a subset $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$, the ideal $I(X)$ is the ideal generated by the homogeneous $F$ with $F(a) = 0$ for all $[a] \in X$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated; the scheme version is `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` with its closed sets `ProjectiveSpectrum.zeroLocus`.

**Example P0.4.15.** A hypersurface of degree $d$ is $V(F)$ for $F \in S_d$, $d \geq 1$. The conic $V(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2) \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ is a projective variety. A linear subspace $\mathbb{P}(W)$ is $V$ of the linear forms that vanish on $W$.

**Example P0.4.16.** The empty set is $V(1)$ and also $V(S_+)$, because no point has all coordinates $0$. A finite set of points is a projective algebraic set, as in the affine case.

The properties of $V$ are the same as in the affine case: $V(0) = \mathbb{P}^n$, $V(J_1) \cup V(J_2) = V(J_1 J_2)$ and $\bigcap_\alpha V(T_\alpha) = V(\bigcup_\alpha T_\alpha)$. The proofs of P0 Chapter 3 apply. So the Zariski topology is a topology, and the ideal $I(X)$ is homogeneous by definition.

The link with the affine case is the affine cone. Let $\pi \colon \mathbb{A}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{P}^n$ be the map $a \mapsto [a]$. For $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ the affine cone is $C(X) = \pi^{-1}(X) \cup \{0\}$. Write $V_a$ and $I_a$ for the affine zero set and ideal in $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$.

**Lemma P0.4.17 (affine cone).** Let $J \subsetneq S$ be a homogeneous ideal and $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ nonempty. Then $V_a(J) = C(V(J))$ and $I_a(C(X)) = I(X)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

*Proof.* A homogeneous ideal $J \neq S$ contains no nonzero constant, so $J \subseteq S_+$ and $0 \in V_a(J)$. For $a \neq 0$, $a \in V_a(J)$ if and only if all homogeneous elements of $J$ vanish at $a$, because $J$ contains the components of its elements. This is the condition $[a] \in V(J)$. For the second statement, a homogeneous $F$ of degree $d \geq 1$ vanishes on $C(X)$ if and only if it vanishes on $X$, since $F(0) = 0$. A nonzero constant vanishes on neither, since $X \neq \emptyset$. So the homogeneous elements of $I(X)$ and of $I_a(C(X))$ agree. The ideal $I_a(C(X))$ is homogeneous, because $C(X)$ is stable under scalars: if $f(\lambda a) = \sum_d \lambda^d f_d(a) = 0$ for all $\lambda \in k$, with $k$ infinite, then each $f_d(a) = 0$. So both ideals are generated by the same homogeneous elements. $\square$

**Theorem P0.4.18 (projective Nullstellensatz).** Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $J \subseteq S$ a homogeneous ideal.

1. $V(J) = \emptyset$ if and only if $\sqrt{J} \supseteq S_+$, that is, if and only if $S_d \subseteq J$ for some $d$.
2. If $V(J) \neq \emptyset$, then $I(V(J)) = \sqrt{J}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated; Mathlib has `ProjectiveSpectrum.zeroLocus` and `ProjectiveSpectrum.vanishingIdeal` for the projective spectrum of a graded ring, and the affine form `MvPolynomial.vanishingIdeal_zeroLocus_eq_radical` that the proof uses.

*Proof.* If $J = S$ both statements are clear, so let $J \neq S$. For 1, by the lemma $V(J) = \emptyset$ if and only if $V_a(J) \subseteq \{0\}$. By the affine Nullstellensatz this holds if and only if $\sqrt{J} = I_a(V_a(J)) \supseteq I_a(\{0\}) = S_+$. If $\sqrt{J} \supseteq S_+$, then $x_i^{m} \in J$ for all $i$ and some $m$, and every monomial of degree $d = (n+1)(m-1) + 1$ contains some $x_i^m$, so $S_d \subseteq J$. Conversely $S_d \subseteq J$ gives $x_i^d \in J$, so $x_i \in \sqrt{J}$. For 2, by the lemma and the affine Nullstellensatz, $I(V(J)) = I_a(C(V(J))) = I_a(V_a(J)) = \sqrt{J}$. $\square$

**Corollary P0.4.19 (projective dictionary).** The maps $V$ and $I$ are inclusion reversing bijections between the homogeneous radical ideals $J \neq S_+$ and the projective algebraic sets in $\mathbb{P}^n$. Under these bijections, the homogeneous prime ideals $\mathfrak{p} \neq S_+$ correspond to the projective varieties.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

*Proof.* For a projective algebraic set $X = V(T)$, $T \subseteq I(X)$ gives $V(I(X)) \subseteq X$, and $X \subseteq V(I(X))$ is clear. For a homogeneous radical $J \neq S_+$: if $J = S$, then $I(V(J)) = I(\emptyset) = S$. If $J \neq S$, then $V(J) \neq \emptyset$, since otherwise $S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{J} = J \subsetneq S$ forces $J = S_+$ because $S_+$ is maximal. So $I(V(J)) = \sqrt{J} = J$ by the theorem. The irreducible case follows as in P0 Chapter 3, with the lemma on homogeneous primes in place of primality. $\square$

### Proof in words

A point of $\mathbb{P}^n$ is a line in $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$, and a projective algebraic set is a union of lines through $0$, the affine cone. Its equations are homogeneous, and the homogeneous ideals are the ideals of cones. So the projective Nullstellensatz is the affine Nullstellensatz for cones. The only new feature is the irrelevant ideal: its affine zero set is the origin alone, which is not a point of $\mathbb{P}^n$. So $S_+$ cuts out the empty set, and it has to be removed from the dictionary.

### Examples

The ideal $J = (x_0^2, x_1^2) \subset k[x_0, x_1]$ is homogeneous and $V(J) = \emptyset$ in $\mathbb{P}^1$. Here $\sqrt{J} = S_+$, and $S_3 \subseteq J$: every monomial $x_0^a x_1^b$ with $a + b = 3$ has $a \geq 2$ or $b \geq 2$. The ideal $J$ is not $S$, so the weak Nullstellensatz of the affine case does not hold literally in projective space.

Let $X = \{[1 : 0], [0 : 1]\} \subset \mathbb{P}^1$. Then $I(X) = (x_0 x_1)$. Its affine cone is the union of the two coordinate axes in $\mathbb{A}^2$, with ideal $(x_0 x_1)$, as the lemma says.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the sum, product and intersection of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous.
2. (A) Show that $V(x_0 x_1 - x_2^2, x_2) = \{[1 : 0 : 0], [0 : 1 : 0]\}$ in $\mathbb{P}^2$, and find $I$ of this set.
3. (A) Show that a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{P}^n$ is dense, and that $\mathbb{P}^n$ is irreducible.
4. (B) Let $F \in S_d$ be a nonconstant homogeneous polynomial with factorization $F = c F_1^{m_1} \cdots F_r^{m_r}$ into distinct irreducible factors. Show that each $F_i$ is homogeneous, and that the irreducible components of $V(F)$ are the $V(F_i)$.
5. (B) Show that the Zariski topology on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is Noetherian, and that every projective algebraic set is a finite union of projective varieties.
6. (B) Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be a projective variety. Show that the homogeneous coordinate ring $S(X) = S/I(X)$ is a graded domain, and that $X$ is a point if and only if $\dim_k S(X)_d = 1$ for all $d$.

### Lean

Mathlib has homogeneous ideals of a graded ring as `Ideal.IsHomogeneous`, with the radical and primality lemmas used above. It has the projective spectrum `ProjectiveSpectrum` of a graded ring, with `ProjectiveSpectrum.zeroLocus` and `ProjectiveSpectrum.vanishingIdeal`, and the scheme `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj`. The classical form for points of $\mathbb{P}^n$ over an algebraically closed field is not in Lean as stated. The handbook uses these Mathlib names and adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The projective Nullstellensatz is Hilbert's theorem applied to cones, and the name irrelevant ideal records that $S_+$ cuts out nothing. The literature state is theorem. In the language of P3, a projective variety $X$ is the scheme $\operatorname{Proj} S(X)$, and two graded ideals that agree in high degree define the same subscheme. Exercise 6 is the first case of the Hilbert function $d \mapsto \dim_k S(X)_d$. Hilbert showed that it is a polynomial for large $d$, and the degree of this polynomial is the dimension of $X$.
