## Affine charts and projective closure

### Statement

This section works with the chart $U_0 = \{x_0 \neq 0\}$ and the bijection $\varphi_0 \colon U_0 \to \mathbb{A}^n$, $[a_0 : \dots : a_n] \mapsto (a_1/a_0, \dots, a_n/a_0)$ of Section 1. The other charts $U_i$ are the same up to a change of the names of the coordinates.

**Definition P0.4.20 (homogenization).** Let $f \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be nonzero of degree $e$. The homogenization of $f$ is

$$f^h(x_0, \dots, x_n) = x_0^e\, f(x_1/x_0, \dots, x_n/x_0),$$

a homogeneous polynomial of degree $e$. For a homogeneous $F \in k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ the dehomogenization is $F_* = F(1, x_1, \dots, x_n)$. For an ideal $I \subseteq k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, $I^h$ is the ideal generated by all $f^h$ with $f \in I$, $f \neq 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated; Mathlib has the homogeneous components `MvPolynomial.homogeneousComponent` but no homogenization map.

**Example P0.4.21.** For $f = y - x^2$ in $k[x, y]$, with $x = x_1$ and $y = x_2$, the homogenization is $f^h = x_0 x_2 - x_1^2$. For $f = x^3 + x + 1$ in one variable, $f^h = x_1^3 + x_0^2 x_1 + x_0^3$.

**Example P0.4.22.** For $F = x_0 x_1^2$, $F_* = x_1^2$ and $(F_*)^h = x_1^2$. So $F = x_0 \,(F_*)^h$: dehomogenization loses the powers of $x_0$ that divide $F$.

The basic rules are direct from the definitions. Let $f \neq 0$ and let $F$ be homogeneous, with $F = x_0^r G$ where $x_0$ does not divide $G$.

1. $(f^h)_* = f$, and $f^h$ is not divisible by $x_0$.
2. $F = x_0^r\, (F_*)^h$.
3. $(fg)^h = f^h g^h$, and $f^h(1, a) = f(a)$, $F(1, a) = F_*(a)$ for $a \in \mathbb{A}^n$.

For 2, note that $F_* = G_*$. Since $x_0 \nmid G$, some monomial of $G$ has no $x_0$, so $G_*$ has degree $\deg G$. Then $(G_*)^h = x_0^{\deg G}\, G(1, x_1/x_0, \dots, x_n/x_0) = G$, because $G$ is homogeneous.

**Proposition P0.4.23 (charts are affine).** The bijection $\varphi_0 \colon U_0 \to \mathbb{A}^n$ is a homeomorphism, where $U_0$ has the topology induced from the Zariski topology of $\mathbb{P}^n$. So $\mathbb{P}^n$ is covered by $n + 1$ open sets, each homeomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^n$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated; the scheme version is the open cover of `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` by the affine opens `D_+(x_i)`.

*Proof.* The set $U_0 = \mathbb{P}^n \setminus V(x_0)$ is open. A closed subset of $U_0$ is $U_0 \cap V(T)$ for homogeneous $T$. By rule 3, $\varphi_0(U_0 \cap V(T)) = V(T_*)$ with $T_* = \{F_* : F \in T\}$, which is closed in $\mathbb{A}^n$. Conversely, a closed set $V(S) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ has $\varphi_0^{-1}(V(S)) = U_0 \cap V(S^h)$ by rule 3, which is closed in $U_0$. So $\varphi_0$ and its inverse are continuous. $\square$

**Definition P0.4.24 (projective closure).** Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an algebraic set. Identify $\mathbb{A}^n$ with $U_0$ by $\varphi_0$. The projective closure $\overline{X}$ of $X$ is the closure of $X$ in $\mathbb{P}^n$. The points of $\overline{X} \setminus X$, which lie in the hyperplane $V(x_0)$, are the points at infinity of $X$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Example P0.4.25.** The closure of a line $\{a + tb : t \in k\}$ in $\mathbb{A}^n$ is the line in $\mathbb{P}^n$ through $[1 : a]$ and $[0 : b]$. It has one point at infinity, $[0 : b]$, the direction of the line.

**Example P0.4.26.** The closure of $\mathbb{A}^n$ is $\mathbb{P}^n$, because a nonempty open subset of the irreducible space $\mathbb{P}^n$ is dense.

**Theorem P0.4.27 (equations of the closure).** Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an algebraic set. Then

$$\overline{X} = V(I(X)^h), \qquad I(\overline{X}) = I(X)^h, \qquad \overline{X} \cap U_0 = X.$$

If $X$ is an affine variety, then $\overline{X}$ is a projective variety.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

*Proof.* Put $Y = V(I(X)^h)$, a closed set. For $a \in X$ and $f \in I(X)$, $f^h(1, a) = f(a) = 0$, so $X \subseteq Y$ and $\overline{X} \subseteq Y$. Now let $F$ be a homogeneous polynomial that vanishes on $X$. Then $F_*(a) = F(1, a) = 0$ for $a \in X$, so $F_* \in I(X)$, and $F = x_0^r (F_*)^h \in I(X)^h$ by rule 2. The ideal $I(\overline{X})$ is generated by such $F$, because a homogeneous polynomial vanishes on $\overline{X}$ if and only if it vanishes on $X$. So $I(\overline{X}) \subseteq I(X)^h$, and the reverse inclusion holds because $I(X)^h$ vanishes on $Y \supseteq \overline{X}$. Then $Y = V(I(X)^h) = V(I(\overline{X})) = \overline{X}$, by the corollary of Section 2. Since $X$ is closed in $U_0 \cong \mathbb{A}^n$, $X = \overline{X} \cap U_0$. The closure of an irreducible subspace is irreducible, which gives the last statement. $\square$

### Proof in words

A homogeneous equation of $\overline{X}$ restricts on the chart to an equation of $X$, and an equation $f$ of $X$ gives back the homogeneous equation $f^h$. Powers of $x_0$ do not matter on the chart. So the homogeneous ideal of the closure is spanned by the homogenizations of all equations of $X$. The warning is in the word "all": the homogenizations of a set of generators of $I(X)$ can cut out a larger set, with extra components at infinity.

### Examples

The parabola $X = V(y - x^2)$ has $I(X)^h = (x_0 x_2 - x_1^2)$, since homogenization is multiplicative and $I(X)$ is principal. So $\overline{X}$ is the conic $V(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2)$. Its points at infinity have $x_0 = 0$ and then $x_1 = 0$, so there is one point $[0 : 0 : 1]$, the direction of the $y$-axis. The hyperbola $V(xy - 1)$ has closure $V(x_1 x_2 - x_0^2)$ with two points at infinity, $[0 : 1 : 0]$ and $[0 : 0 : 1]$, the directions of its two asymptotes.

The twisted cubic $X = \{(t, t^2, t^3)\} \subset \mathbb{A}^3$ has $I(X) = (y - x^2, z - x^3)$. In $\mathbb{P}^3$ with coordinates $[w : x : y : z]$, the homogenized generators $wy - x^2$ and $w^2 z - x^3$ vanish on the whole line $\{w = x = 0\}$. So $V(wy - x^2, w^2 z - x^3)$ is larger than $\overline{X}$. The correct equations are the three $2 \times 2$ minors

$$wy - x^2, \qquad wz - xy, \qquad xz - y^2.$$

They lie in $I(X)^h$, since they are the homogenizations of $y - x^2$, $z - xy$ and $xz - y^2$, which vanish on $X$. Their common zeros with $w = 1$ are the points $y = x^2$, $z = xy = x^3$, that is, $X$. With $w = 0$ they give $x^2 = 0$ and then $y^2 = xz = 0$, so only the point $p = [0 : 0 : 0 : 1]$. So the zero set of the three minors is $X \cup \{p\}$. The point $p$ is in $\overline{X}$: if a homogeneous $F$ vanishes on $X$, then $F(s^3, s^2 t, s t^2, t^3) = 0$ for all $s \neq 0$, so this polynomial in $s, t$ is $0$, and $s = 0$, $t = 1$ gives $F(p) = 0$. So $\overline{X} = X \cup \{p\}$ is the image of $\mathbb{P}^1$ under $[s : t] \mapsto [s^3 : s^2 t : s t^2 : t^3]$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Find the projective closure and the points at infinity of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ and of the cubic $y^2 = x^3 - x$.
2. (A) Show that the closure of $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ meets $V(x_0)$ in the set of $[0 : b]$ with $f_e(b) = 0$ for all $f \in I(X)$, where $f_e$ is the component of highest degree of $f$.
3. (A) Show that a principal ideal $(f)$ satisfies $(f)^h = (f^h)$, so the projective closure of the hypersurface $V(f)$ is $V(f^h)$.
4. (B) Show that $wz - xy \notin (wy - x^2, w^2 z - x^3)$, and find the irreducible components of $V(wy - x^2, w^2 z - x^3) \subset \mathbb{P}^3$.
5. (B) Let $Z \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be a projective variety not contained in $V(x_0)$. Show that $Z \cap U_0$ is an affine variety whose projective closure is $Z$. Deduce a bijection between affine varieties in $\mathbb{A}^n$ and projective varieties in $\mathbb{P}^n$ not contained in $V(x_0)$.

### Lean

Mathlib has homogeneous components of multivariate polynomials (`MvPolynomial.homogeneousComponent`) but no projective closure of affine varieties. The chart statement is the scheme fact that `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` is covered by the affine opens $D_+(x_i) \cong \operatorname{Spec}$ of the degree zero part of the localization. The handbook uses these Mathlib names and adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Homogenization goes back to the projective geometry of the nineteenth century, where a plane curve $f(x, y) = 0$ was always read as $f^h(x_0, x_1, x_2) = 0$ together with its points at infinity. The twisted cubic is the standard example that the homogenized generators do not suffice: for this one needs a Gröbner basis for a graded order, a result of Buchberger's theory (1965). The literature state is theorem. P3 replaces the charts $U_i$ by the open affine subschemes $D_+(x_i)$ of $\operatorname{Proj}$, and the projective closure by the scheme theoretic closure.
