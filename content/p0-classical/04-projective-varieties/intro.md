# Projective varieties

Affine varieties are not compact in any useful sense: two parallel lines in $\mathbb{A}^2$ do not meet, and a curve can have points that go off to infinity. Projective space adds the missing points at infinity. This chapter defines projective space, the projective algebraic sets cut out by homogeneous polynomials, and the projective Nullstellensatz. Throughout, $k$ is an algebraically closed field of any characteristic, except in the classification of quadrics, where the characteristic is not 2.

The chapter uses the results of Chapter 3 on affine varieties, with no other algebra. Section 3 shows that projective space is covered by copies of affine space, and that every affine variety has a projective closure. Section 4 gives the first morphisms between projective varieties: the Veronese and Segre embeddings and the projection from a point. It ends with the classification of quadrics and the identification of a nondegenerate conic with $\mathbb{P}^1$ and of a nondegenerate quadric surface with $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$. The minimal model program works with projective varieties throughout, and these examples come back in every later volume.
