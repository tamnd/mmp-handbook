# Cubic surfaces and the 27 lines

This chapter studies smooth cubic surfaces in $\mathbb{P}^3$ over an algebraically closed field $k$. It proves that every cubic surface contains a line, finds the 27 lines of the Fermat cubic by hand, and describes the configuration of the lines on any smooth cubic surface. The last section shows the smooth cubic surface as the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at six points, where the 27 lines are the curves with self-intersection $-1$. The chapter uses projective varieties (Chapter 4), rational maps (Chapter 5), dimension and tangent planes (Chapter 6) and Bézout's theorem (Chapter 7).

The cubic surface is the running example of a del Pezzo surface in the handbook. The curves with self-intersection $-1$ of Section 8.4 are the curves that Castelnuovo's theorem contracts, and they give the first examples of extremal rays in the minimal model program. Volumes 2, 6, 7, 12 and 14 come back to the cubic surface.
