# Dimension, tangent spaces and the Jacobian criterion

The field $k$ is algebraically closed. The characteristic is arbitrary, except in Section 6.3, which needs $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, and the chapter says where else it matters.

The chapter defines the dimension of a variety, the Zariski tangent space at a point, and nonsingular and singular points. The Jacobian criterion turns nonsingularity into a rank condition on a matrix of partial derivatives, so it can be computed. The quadric cone $uw = v^2$ is the first singular surface of the handbook: it is the quotient of the plane by $\pm 1$, the singularity $A_1 = \frac12(1,1)$. The last section measures how singular a point of a hypersurface is, by its multiplicity and its tangent cone.

The chapter uses Chapters 3 to 5. It cites two results of commutative algebra: the dimension theory of P1 Ch. 5 (Krull dimension equals transcendence degree) and the inequality $\dim_k \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 \geq \dim A$ for a local ring $A$ from P1 Ch. 9. Later volumes use the quadric cone as a running example, and the multiplicity of a point is the first measure of a singularity before the discrepancies of Volume 4.
