## The program in higher dimension

In dimension 3 and higher, a smooth projective variety $X$ has no simple analogue of a $(-1)$-curve. Mori's idea is to look at the curves on which $K_X$ is negative. The minimal model program runs a finite sequence of steps. Each step makes $X$ simpler, and the program ends at a variety $X'$ birational to $X$ of one of two kinds:

- a *minimal model*: $K_{X'}$ is nef;
- a *Mori fibre space*: a morphism $X' \to Z$ with connected fibres, $\dim Z < \dim X'$, and $-K_{X'}$ ample on the fibres.

**One step.** Suppose that $K_X$ is not nef. The *cone theorem* says that the curves $C$ with $K_X \cdot C < 0$ lie in a small part of the cone of curves, spanned by countably many *extremal rays*, each spanned by a rational curve. The *contraction theorem* gives, for each such ray $R$, a morphism $\varphi_R \colon X \to Z$ that contracts exactly the curves in $R$. Both theorems hold for klt pairs, a class of mildly singular varieties with a boundary divisor that Volume 4 defines.

The contraction $\varphi_R$ is of one of three types.

1. *Fibre type*: $\dim Z < \dim X$. Then $X \to Z$ is a Mori fibre space, and the program stops.
2. *Divisorial*: $\varphi_R$ is birational and contracts a divisor. Then $Z$ replaces $X$, and the program goes on.
3. *Small*: $\varphi_R$ is birational and contracts only a set of codimension at least 2. Then $K_Z$ is not even $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, and $Z$ is a bad place to go on. The program replaces $X$ by the *flip* $X^+$ of $\varphi_R$: a variety with a small contraction $X^+ \to Z$ such that $K_{X^+}$ is positive on the curves that it contracts. The map $X \dashrightarrow X^+$ is an isomorphism outside a set of codimension at least 2.

The flips of klt pairs exist in all dimensions. This is a theorem of Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan (BCHM10), after Mori in dimension 3 and Shokurov in dimension 4.

**Singular varieties.** Even when $X$ is smooth, a divisorial contraction can give a singular $Z$. For example, let $X$ be a smooth 3-fold that contains a surface $E \cong \mathbb{P}^2$ with normal bundle $\mathcal{O}(-2)$. The contraction of $E$ gives a point of type $\frac12(1,1,1)$, the quotient of $\mathbb{A}^3$ by $x \mapsto -x$. So the program must work with singular varieties from the start. The classes of singularities (terminal, canonical, klt, lc) are in Volume 0 Chapter 3 and in Volume 4.

**Termination.** The program stops if no infinite sequence of flips exists. Divisorial contractions lower the Picard number, so they cannot go on for ever, but a flip does not change the Picard number. **What is open.** Two conjectures remain. The *minimal model conjecture* says that each klt pair with $K_X + \Delta$ pseudo-effective has a minimal model. *Abundance* says that on a minimal model, $K_X + \Delta$ is semiample: a multiple of it is the pull-back of an ample divisor under a morphism. Both are theorems for surfaces and 3-folds, and both are open in general from dimension 4 on.
