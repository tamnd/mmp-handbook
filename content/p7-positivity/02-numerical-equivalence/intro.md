# Numerical equivalence and the spaces $N^1$ and $N_1$

Two line bundles are numerically equivalent if they have the same degree on every curve. This chapter divides the Picard group by this relation and tensors with $\mathbb{R}$. The result is a real vector space $N^1(X)$, and the dual construction on $1$-cycles gives a second space $N_1(X)$. Intersection numbers give a pairing between them that is nondegenerate by construction. The theorem of the base says that the two spaces are finite-dimensional. Their common dimension is the Picard number $\rho(X)$, and the pairing is then perfect.

Section 2.1 defines numerical equivalence for line bundles on a projective scheme and shows that a numerically trivial line bundle has zero intersection numbers in every dimension, not just with curves. It defines $\mathbb{R}$-divisor classes and $1$-cycles, the pairing, and the maps $f^*$ and $f_*$ for a morphism, and it compares the result with the numerical equivalence of surfaces in P5. Section 2.2 proves the theorem of the base over $\mathbb{C}$ through the first Chern class, cites the theorem in arbitrary characteristic, and draws the consequences: $N^1(X)_\mathbb{Z}$ is a lattice, $N^1(X)$ has a rational structure, and the Picard number goes down under surjective morphisms. Section 2.3 computes $N^1$ and $N_1$, with explicit dual bases, for projective spaces and their products, for the blow-up of a point, and for the Hirzebruch surfaces.

The cones of Chapter 4 live in $N^1(X)$ and $N_1(X)$, and the minimal model program contracts the curves whose classes span an extremal ray of a cone in $N_1(X)$. The numerical point of view is what makes this possible: the space of all line bundles is infinite-dimensional in general, for instance on an elliptic curve, while $N^1(X)$ is finite-dimensional, and the positivity of a line bundle depends only on its numerical class (Chapter 3).

The chapter follows Lazarsfeld, Section 1.1.C (Laz04), Debarre, Chapter 1 (Deb01), and Kleiman's paper (Kle66), which introduced the numerical point of view.
