## Kollár's injectivity and torsion-freeness

### Statement

Throughout, $k$ has characteristic $0$. A line bundle $L$ is *semiample* if $L^m$ is generated by global sections for some $m \geq 1$. A coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on a variety $Z$ is *torsion free* if no nonzero local section is killed by a nonzero function, that is, if every nonzero coherent subsheaf of $\mathcal{F}$ has support $Z$.

**Theorem P7.9.13 (Kollár's torsion-freeness and vanishing, black box).** Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a surjective morphism from a smooth projective variety to a projective variety, and put $d = \dim X - \dim Z$.

1. The sheaves $R^j f_* \omega_X$ are torsion free for all $j \geq 0$.
2. $R^j f_* \omega_X = 0$ for $j > d$.
3. (Kollár vanishing.) For every ample line bundle $A$ on $Z$, $H^i(Z, R^j f_* \omega_X \otimes A) = 0$ for all $i > 0$ and $j \geq 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.9.14 (Kollár's injectivity theorem, black box).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety, $L$ a semiample line bundle on $X$, and $s \in H^0(X, L^k)$ a nonzero section for some $k \geq 1$. Then for every $l \geq 1$ and every $i$ multiplication by $s$,
$$H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes L^l) \to H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes L^{l+k}),$$
is injective.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.9.15 (Kollár's decomposition, black box).** In the situation of Theorem P7.9.13 there is an isomorphism in the derived category of $Z$,
$$Rf_* \omega_X \cong \bigoplus_{j = 0}^{d} R^j f_* \omega_X [-j].$$
In particular the Leray spectral sequence $H^i(Z, R^j f_* \omega_X) \Rightarrow H^{i+j}(X, \omega_X)$ degenerates at $E_2$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.9.16 (vanishing above the relative dimension).** Part 2 of Theorem P7.9.13 follows from part 1. In particular part 1 implies the Grauert-Riemenschneider theorem for morphisms of projective varieties.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The injectivity theorem is the deepest of the four statements, and Kollár proves it by Hodge theory: for a smooth divisor $D \in |L^k|$, the map $H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes L^l) \to H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes L^l(D))$ is dual to a restriction map on cohomology with coefficients in a local system, and the degeneration of the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence on the cyclic cover branched along $D$ makes it injective. Esnault and Viehweg give a proof through logarithmic de Rham complexes (EV92, Lecture 5). The torsion-freeness follows from injectivity: a torsion section of $R^j f_* \omega_X$ is killed by a section of a large power of an ample line bundle $A$ on $Z$, and the Leray spectral sequence for $f$ and the line bundle $L = f^* A$, which is semiample, turns this into a nonzero element in the kernel of a multiplication map. Kollár vanishing then follows from torsion-freeness and Theorem P7.8.18 by induction on $\dim Z$, cutting by general members of $|A^m|$. The decomposition theorem is proved in the second paper (Kol86b) by Hodge theory.

Of the four, only the step from torsion-freeness to vanishing above $d$ is proved here, and it is a short argument. Over the open set where $f$ is smooth, the fibres have dimension $d$, so $R^j f_* \omega_X$ vanishes there for $j > d$. A sheaf supported on a proper closed subset is torsion, so torsion-freeness kills it everywhere. For $d = 0$ this is the Grauert-Riemenschneider theorem.

### Examples

**Example P7.9.17 (injectivity and its sharpness).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g \geq 1$, $X = C \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with projections $p$, $q$, and $L = q^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)$, which is generated by global sections and not big. Let $s \in H^0(X, L)$ be the pullback of a nonzero linear form. Since $\omega_X = p^* \omega_C \otimes q^* \mathcal{O}(-2)$, the Künneth formula gives, for $l \geq 1$,
$$H^1(X, \omega_X \otimes L^l) = H^1(C, \omega_C) \otimes H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(l - 2)) \oplus H^0(C, \omega_C) \otimes H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(l-2)) = H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(l-2)),$$
and multiplication by $s$ is multiplication by a linear form on $H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(l-2))$, which is injective, as Theorem P7.9.14 says. For $l = 0$ the statement fails: $H^1(X, \omega_X) = H^0(C, \omega_C) \otimes H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-2)) = k^g$, while $H^1(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = 0$, so multiplication by $s$ has kernel $k^g$. The hypothesis $l \geq 1$ cannot be dropped. Note that $H^1(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = 0$ although $L$ is not big, so the injectivity theorem is not a vanishing theorem.

**Example P7.9.18 (a product).** Let $f = p \colon X = C \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to C$, with $d = 1$. By flat base change, $f_* \omega_X = \omega_C \otimes H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-2)) = 0$ and $R^1 f_* \omega_X = \omega_C \otimes H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-2)) = \omega_C$, a line bundle, so torsion free and nonzero in the top degree $j = d$. Kollár vanishing says $H^1(C, \omega_C \otimes A) = 0$ for $A$ ample, which is Kodaira vanishing on $C$. The decomposition is $Rf_* \omega_X = \omega_C[-1]$, and $H^2(X, \omega_X) = H^1(C, \omega_C) = k$, as Serre duality requires. This is the failure of Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing in degree $d$ seen in Example P7.9.7.

**Example P7.9.19 (torsion for the structure sheaf).** Let $f \colon Y \to C$ be the resolution of the cone over an elliptic curve of Example P7.9.11, and compactify it: $\bar C$ is the projective cone, $\operatorname{Proj}$ of $S[t]$ with $t$ of degree $1$, and $\bar Y$ is the $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over $E$ obtained from the line bundle $Y$ by adding a section at infinity, a smooth projective surface with a birational morphism $\bar f \colon \bar Y \to \bar C$ which is $f$ over $C$ and an isomorphism near the section at infinity. Then $R^1 \bar f_* \mathcal{O}_{\bar Y}$ is $k$ at the vertex and zero elsewhere, by Example P7.9.11. It is a nonzero torsion sheaf, so the torsion-freeness of Theorem P7.9.13 fails for $\mathcal{O}$ in place of $\omega$. On the other hand $R^1 \bar f_* \omega_{\bar Y} = 0$, as Theorem P7.9.13 predicts for $d = 0$.

### Proof

*Theorem P7.9.13.* This is Kollár, Theorem 2.1 (Kol86): part (i) is torsion-freeness, part (ii) is the vanishing above $d$, and part (iii) is Kollár vanishing. It is a black box here. Part 2 is also proved below from part 1. For $Z$ smooth and $f$ birational, $f_* \omega_X = \omega_Z$ by Lemma P4.9.19 and $R^j f_* \omega_X = 0$ for $j > 0$ by Theorem P7.9.8, so part 1 holds and part 3 is Kodaira vanishing on $Z$, Theorem P7.8.2.

*Theorem P7.9.14.* This is Kollár, Theorem 2.2 (Kol86); Fujino gives a proof with a boundary (Fuj11). It is a black box here. When $L$ is ample, both sides vanish for $i > 0$ by Theorem P7.8.2, and for $i = 0$ the map is injective because $X$ is integral.

*Theorem P7.9.15.* This is Kollár, Theorem 3.1 (Kol86b), a black box. Degeneration at $E_2$ follows from the decomposition, since the Leray spectral sequence is the hypercohomology spectral sequence of $Rf_* \omega_X$ with respect to its cohomology sheaves, and for a direct sum of shifted sheaves its differentials vanish.

*Proposition P7.9.16.* By Theorem P3.12.12 there is a dense open set $V \subseteq Z$ over which $f$ is smooth, of relative dimension $d$, since the general fibre has dimension $d$. Every fibre of $f|_{f^{-1}(V)}$ has dimension $d$, so $R^j f_* \omega_X|_V = 0$ for $j > d$ (Har77, III.11.2). So for $j > d$ the coherent sheaf $R^j f_* \omega_X$ is supported on the proper closed subset $Z \setminus V$. If it were nonzero, it would be a nonzero coherent subsheaf of itself whose support is not $Z$, contradicting part 1. So it is zero. If $f$ is generically finite, then $d = 0$, and $R^j f_* \omega_X = 0$ for $j > 0$, which is Theorem P7.9.8 for projective $X$ and $Z$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Check the three parts of Theorem P7.9.13 for a finite surjective morphism $f \colon X \to Z$ of smooth projective varieties, using Corollary P4.4.3 and the trace splitting of Lemma P7.8.9.
2. (A) In Example P7.9.17, compute the kernel of multiplication by $s^2$ from $H^1(X, \omega_X)$ to $H^1(X, \omega_X \otimes L^2)$.
3. (B) Deduce from Theorem P7.9.14, applied with $L$ ample, $k = 1$ and a section $s$ defining a smooth divisor $D$, that the restriction $H^0(X, \omega_X \otimes L^{l+1}) \to H^0(D, \omega_D \otimes L^l|_D)$ is surjective for $l \geq 1$.
4. (B) Let $f \colon X \to Z$ be a surjective morphism of smooth projective varieties with connected fibres of dimension $d$. Show that $R^d f_* \omega_X \cong \omega_Z$, by relative duality on the smooth locus and torsion-freeness.
5. (B) Show that Theorem P7.9.15 and Theorem P7.9.13 together give $h^i(X, \omega_X \otimes f^* A) = \sum_j h^{i-j}(Z, R^j f_* \omega_X \otimes A)$, and that for $A$ ample the right side is $h^0(Z, R^i f_* \omega_X \otimes A)$.
6. (C) Prove part 1 of Theorem P7.9.13 from Theorem P7.9.14. Let $\mathcal{T} \subseteq R^j f_* \omega_X$ be the torsion subsheaf and $A$ a very ample line bundle on $Z$ with $R^j f_* \omega_X \otimes A^m$ generated by global sections and without higher cohomology for $m \geq 1$, and $H^i(Z, R^q f_* \omega_X \otimes A^m) = 0$ for $i > 0$, all $q$ and $m \geq 1$. Choose $s \in H^0(Z, A^k)$ vanishing on the support of $\mathcal{T}$ to high order, and show that multiplication by $f^* s$ on $H^j(X, \omega_X \otimes f^* A^m)$ has a nonzero kernel if $\mathcal{T} \neq 0$.

### Notes and history

Kollár proved the injectivity, torsion-freeness and vanishing theorems in the first paper on higher direct images of dualizing sheaves (Kol86) and the decomposition in the second (Kol86b). The literature state is theorem. Esnault and Viehweg give algebraic proofs of the injectivity theorem through logarithmic de Rham complexes and the degeneration theorem of Deligne and Illusie (EV92, Lectures 5 and 6), and Lazarsfeld, Section 4.3 (Laz04) treats the case used most often. Fujino extended all four statements to simple normal crossing pairs (Fuj11), which is the form used in the theory of quasi-log schemes. Kollár's vanishing theorem contains the vanishing on the base in Theorem P7.9.3. The theorem numbers for Kol86 are checked against later papers that cite it, not against the paper.
