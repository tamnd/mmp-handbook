# Vanishing II: Grauert-Riemenschneider and Kollár

The minimal model program works over a base. A contraction $f \colon X \to Z$ is built from sections of $f$-ample or $f$-nef divisors, the singularities it produces are studied through a resolution $Y \to X$, and in both cases the vanishing that is needed is the vanishing of higher direct images $R^j f_*$, not of cohomology on a projective variety. This chapter turns the absolute theorem of Chapter 8 into relative statements. The relative theorems are the ones the minimal model program uses: relative Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing in the cone and contraction theorems, Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing in the theory of rational singularities, and Kollár's torsion-freeness in the study of fibrations.

Section 9.1 defines $f$-nef and $f$-ample divisors, proves that a nef and big class stays big after adding a nef class, and deduces relative Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for a morphism of projective varieties from the absolute theorem and the comparison lemma of Chapter 8; it states the relative klt form of Kollár and Mori, which is what the minimal model program cites. Section 9.2 proves the Grauert-Riemenschneider theorem $R^j f_* \omega_X = 0$ for a generically finite morphism from a smooth variety, first for projective varieties and then for quasi-projective ones by compactification and resolution. This closes the black box that P4 Ch. 9 uses in Kempf's criterion for rational singularities. The cone over an elliptic curve shows that $\omega_X$ cannot be replaced by $\mathcal{O}_X$. Section 9.3 states Kollár's injectivity, torsion-freeness and vanishing theorems and his decomposition theorem, with their sources, and proves that torsion-freeness forces $R^j f_* \omega_X = 0$ above the relative dimension.

Throughout the chapter $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$.

The chapter follows Kollár and Mori, Sections 2.4 and 2.5 (KM98), Lazarsfeld, Section 4.3 (Laz04), Kollár's papers on higher direct images of dualizing sheaves (Kol86a, Kol86b), and Esnault and Viehweg, Lecture 5 (EV92).
