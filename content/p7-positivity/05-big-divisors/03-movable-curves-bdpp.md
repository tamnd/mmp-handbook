## Movable curves and the theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell

### Statement

Throughout, $X$ is a projective variety over $k$ of dimension $n \geq 2$. By Theorem P7.2.12 the intersection pairing identifies $N_1(X)$ with the dual of $N^1(X)$, so a linear form on $N^1(X)$ is a class in $N_1(X)$. For line bundles $A_1, \dots, A_{n-1}$ write $A_1 \cdots A_{n-1} \in N_1(X)$ for the class of the form $\xi \mapsto (\xi \cdot A_1 \cdots A_{n-1})$, and the same for classes $h_1, \dots, h_{n-1} \in N^1(X)$. Dual cones are taken with respect to this pairing (Definition P7.4.1).

**Definition P7.5.19 (covering classes and the movable cone).** A class $\gamma \in N_1(X)$ is a *covering class* if for every closed subset $Z \subsetneq X$ there is an effective $1$-cycle $\sum_i a_i C_i$, with $a_i > 0$ and $C_i$ curves, such that $\sum_i a_i [C_i] = \gamma$ and no $C_i$ is contained in $Z$. The *movable cone* $\operatorname{Mov}(X) \subseteq N_1(X)$ is the closed convex cone generated by the covering classes.

For example, if a family of curves of class $\gamma$ sweeps out $X$, that is, if every point of a dense open subset of $X$ lies on a curve of the family, then $\gamma$ is a covering class, since a curve through a point outside $Z$ is not contained in $Z$.

**Proposition P7.5.20 (movable classes).**

1. $\operatorname{Mov}(X) \subseteq \overline{NE}(X)$.
2. $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Mov}(X)^\vee$: every pseudo-effective class has nonnegative degree on every movable class.
3. If $A_1, \dots, A_{n-1}$ are ample line bundles, $A_1 \cdots A_{n-1}$ is a covering class. Consequently $h_1 \cdots h_{n-1} \in \operatorname{Mov}(X)$ for all nef classes $h_1, \dots, h_{n-1}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.5.21 (Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$. Then
$$\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X) = \operatorname{Mov}(X)^\vee, \qquad \operatorname{Mov}(X) = \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee.$$
The cone $\operatorname{Mov}(X)$ is also the closed convex cone generated by the classes $\mu_* (A_1 \cdots A_{n-1})$, where $\mu \colon X' \to X$ runs over the birational morphisms from smooth projective varieties and $A_1, \dots, A_{n-1}$ over the ample line bundles on $X'$. So a class $\xi$ is pseudo-effective if and only if $\xi \cdot \gamma \geq 0$ for every covering class $\gamma$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P7.5.22 (uniruledness).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$. Then $X$ is uniruled, that is, covered by rational curves, if and only if $K_X$ is not pseudo-effective.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.5.23 (surfaces).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface over $k$, of any characteristic, and identify $N_1(X)$ with $N^1(X)$ by Proposition P7.2.8. Then $\operatorname{Mov}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X)$ and $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X) = \operatorname{Mov}(X)^\vee$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A movable curve can be moved off any given divisor, and a curve not contained in an effective divisor meets it nonnegatively. So the pseudo-effective cone lies in the dual of the movable cone. Complete intersections of ample divisors move, since general members avoid any given subvariety, so the movable cone contains all products of $n - 1$ nef classes.

The theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell is the converse: a class that is nonnegative on every movable curve is pseudo-effective. Their proof is analytic. A class outside the pseudo-effective cone is separated from it by a hyperplane, and they show, using the approximation of positive currents and a Morse-type inequality for the volume, that the separating hyperplane can be chosen to be given by a strongly movable class $\mu_*(A_1 \cdots A_{n-1})$. The handbook states the theorem and only deduces the form with covering classes from their form. On a surface there is nothing to prove: movable curves are nef and the statement is the duality of Proposition P7.4.15.

The theorem characterizes uniruled varieties. A covering family of rational curves has negative degree on $K_X$, by deformation theory, so $K_X$ is not pseudo-effective. Conversely, if $K_X$ is not pseudo-effective, the theorem gives a movable class with $K_X \cdot \gamma < 0$, and the bend and break method of Miyaoka and Mori produces rational curves through a general point.

### Examples

**Example P7.5.24 (the blow-up of a point).** Let $X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^n$ with $\ell$, $e$ and $\tilde\ell_p = \ell - e$ as in Example P7.2.25. For $\gamma = a \ell + b e$, $H \cdot \gamma = a$ and $E \cdot \gamma = -b$. Since $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X) = \langle E, H - E \rangle$ (Proposition P7.4.18), $\gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee$ if and only if $b \leq 0$ and $a + b \geq 0$, that is,
$$\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee = \langle \ell, \tilde\ell_p \rangle.$$
Both generators are covering classes: the preimages of lines not through $p$ sweep out $X$, and so do the strict transforms of lines through $p$, since these lines cover $\mathbb{P}^n$. By part 2 of Proposition P7.5.20, $\operatorname{Mov}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee$, so $\operatorname{Mov}(X) = \langle \ell, \tilde\ell_p \rangle$, in every characteristic and without Theorem P7.5.21. The movable cone is strictly smaller than $\overline{NE}(X) = \langle e, \tilde\ell_p \rangle$: the class $e$ is not movable, since $E \cdot e = -1 < 0$ while $E$ is effective. For $n = 2$, $\ell = H$ and $\tilde\ell_p = H - E$ under Proposition P7.2.8, and $\operatorname{Mov}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X)$, as Proposition P7.5.23 says.

**Example P7.5.25 (canonical classes that are not pseudo-effective).** On $\mathbb{P}^n$, $K = \mathcal{O}(-n-1)$ and lines are covering curves with $K \cdot \ell = -n - 1 < 0$, so $K_{\mathbb{P}^n}$ is not pseudo-effective by part 2 of Proposition P7.5.20. On $\mathbb{F}_n$, the fibres $F \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ of the ruling cover the surface and $K \cdot F = -2$ by part 2 of Proposition P5.9.9, since $K \sim -2 C_- - (n + 2) F$, $C_- \cdot F = 1$ and $F^2 = 0$. So $K_{\mathbb{F}_n}$ is not pseudo-effective. Both varieties are rational, so these are instances of the easy direction of Corollary P7.5.22. In both cases no multiple of $K$ has a section, which Chapter 6 records as $\kappa = -\infty$.

### Proof

*Proposition P7.5.20.* Part 1. A covering class is the class of an effective $1$-cycle, so it lies in $NE(X)$, and the closed convex cone it generates lies in $\overline{NE}(X)$.

Part 2. Let $\gamma$ be a covering class and $E$ an effective Cartier divisor. Take $Z = \operatorname{Supp} E$ and an effective cycle $\sum_i a_i C_i$ of class $\gamma$ with no $C_i$ in $Z$. By Lemma P7.4.14, $E \cdot C_i \geq 0$, so $[E] \cdot \gamma \geq 0$. By linearity this holds for $\xi \in \operatorname{Eff}(X)$, and by continuity of the pairing for $\xi \in \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$. Since $\operatorname{Mov}(X)$ is the closure of the set of nonnegative combinations of covering classes, the same continuity gives $\xi \cdot \gamma \geq 0$ for $\gamma \in \operatorname{Mov}(X)$.

Part 3. Replacing each $A_i$ by a very ample power $A_i^{m_i}$ multiplies the class by the positive number $m_1 \cdots m_{n-1}$, and the covering classes form a cone, so assume the $A_i$ very ample. Let $Z \subsetneq X$ be closed. Put $\mathcal{F}_0 = \mathcal{O}_X$. Suppose $\mathcal{F}_{j-1}$ is constructed, $1 \leq j \leq n - 1$, with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}_{j-1} \leq n - j + 1$ and $\dim (Z \cap \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}_{j-1}) \leq n - j$. By part 1 of Lemma P7.1.1 there is $\sigma_j \in H^0(X, A_j)$ that vanishes neither at the finitely many associated points of $\mathcal{F}_{j-1}$ nor at the generic points of the irreducible components of $Z \cap \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}_{j-1}$. Let $\mathcal{F}_j = (\mathcal{F}_{j-1})_{\sigma_j}$ be the cokernel of multiplication by $\sigma_j$, as in part 1 of Lemma P7.1.2. By parts 2 and 3 of Lemma P7.1.2, $\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}_j = \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}_{j-1} \cap V(\sigma_j)$ has dimension at most $n - j$, and $Z \cap \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}_j$ meets each component of $Z \cap \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}_{j-1}$ in a proper closed subset, so it has dimension at most $n - j - 1$. After $n - 1$ steps, $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{F}_{n-1}$ has support of dimension at most $1$ and $Z \cap \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}$ is finite. For every line bundle $L$, part 6 of Theorem P7.1.6, applied $n - 1$ times, and Theorem P7.1.13 give
$$(L \cdot A_1 \cdots A_{n-1}) = (L \cdot \mathcal{F}) = (L \cdot [\mathcal{F}]_1).$$
So $A_1 \cdots A_{n-1}$ is the class of the effective $1$-cycle $[\mathcal{F}]_1$, whose components lie in $\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}$ and so are not contained in $Z$. For the last statement, ample classes are positive combinations of classes of ample line bundles (part 3 of Definition P7.3.8), so by multilinearity $h_1 \cdots h_{n-1}$ is a positive combination of covering classes when the $h_i$ are ample. The map $(h_1, \dots, h_{n-1}) \mapsto h_1 \cdots h_{n-1}$ is continuous, $\operatorname{Mov}(X)$ is closed, and $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ is the closure of $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ by part 4 of Theorem P7.3.12.

*Theorem P7.5.21.* This is Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell, Theorem 2.2 (BDPP13), also Lazarsfeld, Theorem 11.4.19 (Laz04), which is not proved here. They prove that $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$ is the dual of the closed convex cone $\overline{SME}(X)$ generated by the classes $\mu_* (A_1 \cdots A_{n-1})$. These classes are covering classes. Indeed, let $Z \subsetneq X$ be closed and let $U \subseteq X$ be a dense open set over which $\mu$ is an isomorphism. By part 3 of Proposition P7.5.20 on $X'$, applied to the proper closed set $\mu^{-1}(Z \cup (X \setminus U))$, the class $A_1 \cdots A_{n-1}$ is represented by an effective cycle $\sum_i a_i C'_i$ with no $C'_i$ in that set. Each $C'_i$ meets $\mu^{-1}(U)$, so $\mu(C'_i)$ is a curve, not contained in $Z$, and $\mu_* C'_i = [\mu(C'_i)]$. By the projection formula of part 2 of Proposition P7.2.7, $\mu_*(A_1 \cdots A_{n-1}) = \sum_i a_i [\mu(C'_i)]$. Hence $\overline{SME}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Mov}(X)$, and with part 2 of Proposition P7.5.20,
$$\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee = \overline{SME}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Mov}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee,$$
where the first equality is the dual form of their theorem, by part 2 of Proposition P7.4.3. So all three cones agree, and dualizing once more gives $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X) = \operatorname{Mov}(X)^\vee$.

*Corollary P7.5.22.* This is Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell, Corollary 0.3 (BDPP13), not proved here. If $X$ is uniruled, then through a general point passes a rational curve $C$ with $K_X \cdot C < 0$, and such curves form a family sweeping out $X$ (Kollár, Chapter IV, Section 1, in Kol96). Their class is a covering class with negative degree on $K_X$, so $K_X$ is not pseudo-effective by part 2 of Proposition P7.5.20. The converse uses Theorem P7.5.21 to produce a movable class $\gamma$ with $K_X \cdot \gamma < 0$ and then the bend and break theorem of Miyaoka and Mori.

*Proposition P7.5.23.* For $n = 2$, the class $A_1$ of part 3 of Proposition P7.5.20 is the image of $[A_1]$ under Proposition P7.2.8, so that part gives $\operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Mov}(X)$. By part 2 of Proposition P7.5.20 and part 3 of Proposition P7.4.15, $\operatorname{Mov}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee = \operatorname{Nef}(X)$. So $\operatorname{Mov}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X) = \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee$, and $\operatorname{Mov}(X)^\vee = (\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)^\vee)^\vee = \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.4.3.

### Exercises

1. (A) On $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, show that the two rulings give covering classes and that $\operatorname{Mov} = \overline{NE} = \operatorname{Nef}$.
2. (A) On $\mathbb{F}_n$, $n \geq 1$, compute $\operatorname{Mov}(\mathbb{F}_n) = \langle F, C_+ \rangle$ directly from the families of fibres and of curves in $|C_+|$, and check Proposition P7.5.23.
3. (B) Let $X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^3$. Using Proposition P7.1.23, show that $H \cdot H = \ell$, $(H - E) \cdot (H - E) = \tilde\ell_p$, $H \cdot E = 0$ and $E \cdot E = -e$ in $N_1(X)$. Which of these classes are covering classes?
4. (B) Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface and $C$ an irreducible curve with $C^2 < 0$. Show that $[C]$ is not in $\operatorname{Mov}(X)$, and that a covering class of the form $[C]$ with $C$ irreducible has $C^2 \geq 0$.
5. (B) Show that a smooth projective variety with $K_X \sim 0$, and a product $C_1 \times C_2$ of curves of genus at least $1$, have pseudo-effective canonical class, and deduce from the easy direction of Corollary P7.5.22 that they are not uniruled over $\mathbb{C}$.
6. (C) Let $X$ be a smooth projective threefold and $\mu \colon X' = \operatorname{Bl}_C X \to X$ the blow-up of a smooth curve, with $A$ ample on $X'$. Show that $\mu_*(A \cdot A)$ is a covering class on $X$, and compare with the classes $h_1 \cdot h_2$ for $h_i$ nef on $X$.

### Lean

The project library has no movable cone, no covering classes and no notion of uniruled variety. It defines the cone of curves `MinimalModel.IntersectionData.coneOfCurves`, the nef cone `MinimalModel.nefCone` and the effective cone `MinimalModel.effCone`, and the predicate `MinimalModel.IsPseudoEffective`. Theorem P7.5.21 and Corollary P7.5.22 are not stated there. Mathlib has no cones of curves. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The theorem is due to Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell (BDPP13), whose preprint circulated from 2004; Lazarsfeld, Section 11.4.C (Laz04), gives an account with the same analytic proof. The literature state is theorem over $\mathbb{C}$, and in characteristic $0$ by the Lefschetz principle. In positive characteristic the duality of Theorem P7.5.21 is not known in general, while Proposition P7.5.20 and Proposition P7.5.23 hold over any algebraically closed field. Later proofs of the duality replace the analytic step by the differentiability of the volume function on the big cone. Corollary P7.5.22 answered a question going back to Mumford and is the reason the pseudo-effectivity of $K_X$ is the dividing line in the minimal model program: by Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan (BCHM10), a smooth projective variety of general type has a minimal model, and one whose canonical class is not pseudo-effective is birational to a Mori fibre space.
