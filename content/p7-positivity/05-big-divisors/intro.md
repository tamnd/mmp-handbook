# Big and pseudo-effective divisors

A line bundle is big when its sections grow as fast as the dimension allows, that is, when $h^0(L^m)$ grows like $m^n$ on a variety of dimension $n$. Ampleness asks for an embedding by some multiple, while bigness asks only for a birational map, and the minimal model program needs this weaker notion: the canonical class of a variety of general type is big, the boundary of a pair is perturbed by big divisors, and the program with scaling stops when a divisor leaves the big cone. This chapter proves that bigness is a numerical property, that the big classes form an open convex cone, and that this cone is the interior of the closed cone spanned by effective divisors, the pseudo-effective cone.

Section 5.1 proves Kodaira's lemma, that a big line bundle has a multiple that is an ample line bundle plus an effective divisor, and derives from it the first properties of bigness. Section 5.2 introduces the pseudo-effective cone, proves that its interior is the big cone, and computes both cones on the blow-up of projective space at a point, the Hirzebruch surfaces and the plane blown up at two points. Section 5.3 introduces covering families of curves and the movable cone, proves that it is contained in the dual of the pseudo-effective cone and contains the complete intersections of ample classes, and states the theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell that the two cones are dual, with its corollary on uniruled varieties.

On a surface curves and divisors live in the same space, and the movable cone is the nef cone. In higher dimension the duality theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell is the replacement for this coincidence, and its corollary, that a smooth projective variety is uniruled exactly when its canonical class is not pseudo-effective, is the starting point of the minimal model program for varieties that are not uniruled.

The chapter follows Lazarsfeld, Sections 2.2 and 11.4 (Laz04), Debarre, Chapter 1 (Deb01), Nakayama's memoir (Nak04), and the paper of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell (BDPP13).
