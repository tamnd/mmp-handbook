# Intersection numbers

On a smooth projective surface the intersection number of two divisors is $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) - \chi(-D) - \chi(-E) + \chi(-D - E)$ (Definition P5.5.4). This chapter extends that definition to any number of line bundles $L_1, \dots, L_r$ on a projective scheme $X$ and a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ whose support has dimension at most $r$. The number $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{F})$ is the coefficient of $m_1 \cdots m_r$ in the Euler characteristic $\chi(\mathcal{F} \otimes L_1^{m_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes L_r^{m_r})$, which by a theorem of Snapper is a polynomial in $m_1, \dots, m_r$. No smoothness, no normality and no moving lemma is needed, and the number depends only on the isomorphism classes of the $L_i$.

Section 1.1 proves Snapper's theorem and the formal properties of the intersection number: it is an integer, symmetric and multilinear in the $L_i$, additive in $\mathcal{F}$, and it can be computed by cutting $\mathcal{F}$ with a section of one of the $L_i$. Section 1.2 expresses the number through the cycle of $\mathcal{F}$, proves that it is positive for ample line bundles, proves the projection formula for a morphism, and proves asymptotic Riemann-Roch: $\chi(X, L^m) = \frac{(L^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1})$. Section 1.3 computes the number on the blow-up of a point of a smooth variety, where $E^n = (-1)^{n-1}$, on projective bundles over $\mathbb{P}^1$, and on the Hirzebruch surfaces and products of projective spaces, which are the examples of the rest of the volume.

The intersection number is the input of everything that follows. Numerical equivalence (Chapter 2) identifies line bundles with the same intersection numbers with all curves, Kleiman's and Nakai's criteria (Chapter 3) characterize ampleness by the signs of intersection numbers, and the cones of Chapter 4 live in the vector spaces spanned by the numerical classes. In the minimal model program the number $K_X \cdot C$ decides which curves are contracted, and $L^n$ is the volume of a nef divisor.

The chapter follows Kleiman, *Toward a numerical theory of ampleness*, Chapter I, Section 2 (Kle66), Lazarsfeld, Section 1.1 (Laz04), and Debarre, Section 1.1 (Deb01).
