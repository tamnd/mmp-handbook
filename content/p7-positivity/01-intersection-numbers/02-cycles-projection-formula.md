## Cycles, positivity and the projection formula

### Statement

As in Section 1.1, $X$ is a projective scheme over the algebraically closed field $k$. An *$r$-cycle* on $X$ is a finite formal sum $\sum_j n_j [V_j]$ of integral closed subschemes $V_j$ of dimension $r$ with $n_j \in \mathbb{Z}$; it is *effective* if all $n_j \geq 0$. Intersection numbers are extended to cycles by linearity: $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \sum_j n_j [V_j]) = \sum_j n_j (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V_j)$.

**Definition P7.1.12 (the cycle of a sheaf).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a coherent sheaf with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} \leq r$. For each irreducible component $V$ of $\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}$ of dimension $r$, with generic point $\eta_V$ and the reduced structure, the stalk $\mathcal{F}_{\eta_V}$ is a module of finite length over $\mathcal{O}_{X, \eta_V}$. The *$r$-cycle of $\mathcal{F}$* is
$$[\mathcal{F}]_r = \sum_{V} \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}_{X, \eta_V}} (\mathcal{F}_{\eta_V}) \, [V].$$
For a closed subscheme $Z$ of dimension at most $r$, $[Z]_r = [\mathcal{O}_Z]_r$. The multiplicity of $V$ in $[Z]_r$ is the length of the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{Z, \eta_V}$.

**Theorem P7.1.13 (the cycle formula).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be coherent with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} \leq r$ and $L_1, \dots, L_r$ line bundles. Then
$$(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{F}) = (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot [\mathcal{F}]_r).$$
In particular, if $V$ is integral of dimension $r$ and $\mathcal{G}$ is a coherent $\mathcal{O}_V$-module of generic rank $\rho$, then $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{G}) = \rho \, (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.1.14 (positivity).** Let $V \subseteq X$ be an integral closed subscheme of dimension $r$ and $L_1, \dots, L_r$ line bundles.

1. If every $L_i$ is generated by global sections, then $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V) \geq 0$.
2. If every $L_i$ is ample, then $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V) > 0$.
3. If $L_1, \dots, L_r$ are very ample, there are effective Cartier divisors $D_i \in |L_i|$ such that $V \cap D_1 \cap \dots \cap D_r$ is a finite nonempty scheme of length $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V)$.

The same conclusions hold for a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ in place of $\mathcal{O}_V$, with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} = r$, by Theorem P7.1.13.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.1.15 (the projection formula).** Let $f \colon Y \to X$ be a morphism of projective schemes over $k$, $L_1, \dots, L_r$ line bundles on $X$ and $\mathcal{G}$ a coherent sheaf on $Y$ with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{G} \leq r$. Then each $R^q f_* \mathcal{G}$ is coherent with support of dimension at most $r$, and
$$(f^* L_1 \cdots f^* L_r \cdot \mathcal{G}) = \sum_{q \geq 0} (-1)^q (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot R^q f_* \mathcal{G}).$$
If $V \subseteq Y$ is integral of dimension $r$ and $W = f(V)$ with its reduced structure, then
$$(f^* L_1 \cdots f^* L_r \cdot V) = \begin{cases} [K(V) : K(W)] \, (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot W) & \text{if } \dim W = r, \\ 0 & \text{if } \dim W < r. \end{cases}$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P7.1.16 (surjective morphisms).** Let $f \colon Y \to X$ be a surjective morphism of projective varieties, $n = \dim Y$, and $L_1, \dots, L_n$ line bundles on $X$. If $\dim X = n$, then $f$ is generically finite and $(f^* L_1 \cdots f^* L_n) = \deg(f) \, (L_1 \cdots L_n)$, with $\deg f = [K(Y) : K(X)]$; if $f$ is birational, the two numbers are equal. If $\dim X < n$, then $(f^* L_1 \cdots f^* L_n) = 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.1.17 (asymptotic Riemann-Roch).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be coherent with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} \leq d$, and $L_1, \dots, L_r$ line bundles.

1. The homogeneous part of degree $d$ of the polynomial $P_{\mathcal{F}}$ of Theorem P7.1.4 is
$$P_{\mathcal{F}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{m}) = \frac{1}{d!} \bigl( (m_1 L_1 + \dots + m_r L_r)^d \cdot \mathcal{F} \bigr) = \sum_{|\mathbf{a}| = d} \frac{(L_1^{a_1} \cdots L_r^{a_r} \cdot \mathcal{F})}{a_1! \cdots a_r!} \, m_1^{a_1} \cdots m_r^{a_r},$$
where $m_1 L_1 + \dots + m_r L_r$ denotes $L^{\mathbf{m}}$ and $L_1^{a_1} \cdots L_r^{a_r}$ the list in which $L_i$ appears $a_i$ times.
2. For one line bundle $L$, $\chi(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^m) = \frac{(L^d \cdot \mathcal{F})}{d!} m^d + O(m^{d-1})$. In particular, if $\dim X = n$, then $\chi(X, L^m) = \frac{(L^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1})$.
3. If $L$ is ample and $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} = d$, then $h^0(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^m) = \frac{(L^d \cdot \mathcal{F})}{d!} m^d + O(m^{d-1})$ for $m \geq 0$, and $(L^d \cdot \mathcal{F}) > 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P7.1.18 (the degree).** Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_k$ be a closed subscheme, $H = \mathcal{O}_X(1)$, and $\mathcal{F}$ coherent with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} = d$. Then $(H^d \cdot \mathcal{F}) = \deg \mathcal{F}$, the degree of Definition P4.3.9. In particular $(H^n \cdot X) = \deg X$ for $n = \dim X$, and $\deg X = \sum_V \operatorname{length}(\mathcal{O}_{X, \eta_V}) \deg V$, the sum over the components $V$ of dimension $n$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The cycle formula says that an intersection number only sees the top-dimensional part of $\mathcal{F}$, counted with multiplicity. Both sides are additive in $\mathcal{F}$, so it suffices to peel $\mathcal{F}$ apart: the subsheaf of sections killed by the ideal of one component $V$ is an $\mathcal{O}_V$-module, and removing it lowers the length at the generic point of $V$. For an $\mathcal{O}_V$-module of generic rank $\rho$, a general map $\mathcal{O}_V^\rho \to \mathcal{G} \otimes A^m$ is an isomorphism at the generic point, so it differs from $\mathcal{O}_V^\rho$ by sheaves with smaller support, which do not count.

Positivity is proved by cutting. A globally generated $L_r$ has a section that does not vanish at the generic point of $V$, so $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V)$ is the intersection number of $L_1, \dots, L_{r-1}$ with the effective cycle of $V \cap D$, which is nonnegative by induction. For very ample bundles the divisor $D$ also meets $V$, which gives strict positivity, and ample bundles reduce to very ample ones by multilinearity.

The projection formula is the Leray spectral sequence: $\chi(Y, \mathcal{G} \otimes f^* M) = \sum_q (-1)^q \chi(X, R^q f_* \mathcal{G} \otimes M)$ for every line bundle $M$ on $X$, and the coefficients of $m_1 \cdots m_r$ on the two sides agree. For an integral $V$ that maps generically finitely onto $W$, the higher direct images are supported on a smaller set, and $f_* \mathcal{O}_V$ has generic rank $[K(V) : K(W)]$ on $W$, so the cycle formula finishes the proof. Asymptotic Riemann-Roch is part 1 of Theorem P7.1.6 read in one variable: if $L_1 = \dots = L_d = L$, the polynomial in $d$ variables is a polynomial in $t_1 + \dots + t_d$, and its coefficient of $t_1 \cdots t_d$ is $d!$ times the leading coefficient.

### Examples

**Example P7.1.19 (the Veronese embedding).** Let $\nu_d \colon \mathbb{P}^n \to \mathbb{P}^N$, $N = \binom{n + d}{d} - 1$, be the Veronese embedding, with $\nu_d^* \mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(d)$. By Theorem P7.1.15 with $f = \nu_d$, a closed immersion onto $V = \nu_d(\mathbb{P}^n)$, $\deg V = (\mathcal{O}(1)^n \cdot V) = (\mathcal{O}(d)^n \cdot \mathbb{P}^n) = d^n$ by Example P7.1.8. For $n = 1$ this is the rational normal curve of degree $d$, and for $n = d = 2$ the Veronese surface of degree $4$ in $\mathbb{P}^5$.

**Example P7.1.20 (products of projective spaces).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^a \times \mathbb{P}^b$ with projections $p_1$, $p_2$, $H_1 = p_1^* \mathcal{O}(1)$, $H_2 = p_2^* \mathcal{O}(1)$, and $\mathcal{O}(m_1, m_2) = H_1^{m_1} \otimes H_2^{m_2}$. Regard $X = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}^{b+1})$ over $\mathbb{P}^a$ (Example P3.6.36), with $\mathcal{O}_X(1) = H_2$. By part 4 of Theorem P4.4.14 with $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{O}(m_1)$, $H^p(X, \mathcal{O}(m_1, m_2)) \cong H^p(\mathbb{P}^a, \mathcal{O}(m_1)) \otimes \operatorname{Sym}^{m_2} k^{b+1}$ for $m_2 \geq 0$, so
$$\chi(\mathcal{O}(m_1, m_2)) = \binom{m_1 + a}{a} \binom{m_2 + b}{b}$$
for $m_2 \geq 0$ and then, both sides being polynomials by Theorem P7.1.4, for all $m_1, m_2$. The homogeneous part of degree $a + b$ is $m_1^a m_2^b / (a! \, b!)$. Comparing with part 1 of Theorem P7.1.17 gives
$$(H_1^i \cdot H_2^j) = \begin{cases} 1 & (i, j) = (a, b), \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad i + j = a + b.$$
The geometry is visible: $H_1^a$ is represented by a fibre $\{x\} \times \mathbb{P}^b$ of $p_1$, and $H_1^{a+1} = 0$ because $a + 1$ general hyperplanes of $\mathbb{P}^a$ do not meet. The Segre embedding $\sigma \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^{ab + a + b}$ has $\sigma^* \mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(1, 1)$, so its image has degree $(H_1 + H_2)^{a+b} = \binom{a + b}{a}$. For $a = b = 1$ this is the quadric of Example P7.1.9.

**Example P7.1.21 (Bézout's theorem).** Let $f_1, \dots, f_n$ be homogeneous of degrees $d_1, \dots, d_n \geq 1$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$, with $Z = V_+(f_1, \dots, f_n)$ finite. Then $\sum_{x \in Z} \operatorname{length} \mathcal{O}_{Z, x} = d_1 \cdots d_n$. Let $Z_j = V_+(f_1, \dots, f_j)$. Each irreducible component of $Z_j$ has dimension at least $n - j$ by part 4 of Proposition P3.7.5. Suppose a component $W$ had dimension $e > n - j$. Its affine cone $\hat{W} \subseteq \mathbb{A}^{n+1}$, the zero set of the homogeneous ideal of $W$, is integral of dimension $e + 1$ and contains the origin. By part 2 of Proposition P3.7.5, every component of $\hat{W} \cap V(f_{j+1}, \dots, f_n)$ through the origin has dimension at least $e + 1 - (n - j) \geq 2$, so this intersection contains a line through the origin, and $W \cap V_+(f_{j+1}, \dots, f_n) \subseteq Z$ has a component of positive dimension. That contradicts the finiteness of $Z$. So $Z_j$ has pure dimension $n - j$. Its local rings are quotients of the regular local rings of $\mathbb{P}^n$ by $j$ elements cutting the dimension by $j$, so they are Cohen-Macaulay by Corollary P1.10.15 and have no embedded primes by part 2 of Theorem P1.10.14. The associated points of $\mathcal{O}_{Z_j}$ are therefore the generic points of its components, and $f_{j+1}$ vanishes at none of them, because $Z_{j+1}$ has no component of dimension $n - j$. Applying part 6 of Theorem P7.1.6 $n$ times,
$$\sum_{x \in Z} \operatorname{length} \mathcal{O}_{Z, x} = (\cdot \mathcal{O}_Z) = (\mathcal{O}(d_1) \cdots \mathcal{O}(d_n) \cdot \mathbb{P}^n) = d_1 \cdots d_n.$$

**Example P7.1.22 (a cover of the line).** Let $f \colon C \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be a finite morphism of degree $e$ from an integral projective curve. By Theorem P7.1.15, $\deg f^* \mathcal{O}(1) = (f^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cdot C) = e \, (\mathcal{O}(1) \cdot \mathbb{P}^1) = e$. So the fibre over a general point, a divisor in $|f^* \mathcal{O}(1)|$, has $e$ points counted with multiplicity, which recovers the degree of a finite morphism of curves of P5 Ch. 2 from the projection formula.

### Proof

*Theorem P7.1.13.* Both sides are additive on exact sequences of coherent sheaves with support of dimension at most $r$: the left side by part 3 of Theorem P7.1.6, and the right side because localization at $\eta_V$ is exact and length is additive (Proposition P1.3.28). Both sides vanish if $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} < r$, the left by part 2 of Theorem P7.1.6.

First let $\mathcal{F} = i_* \mathcal{G}$ for an integral closed subscheme $i \colon V \to X$ of dimension $r$ and $\mathcal{G}$ coherent on $V$ of generic rank $\rho = \dim_{K(V)} \mathcal{G}_{\eta}$. The length of $\mathcal{G}_\eta$ over $\mathcal{O}_{X, \eta}$ is $\rho$, because $\mathcal{G}_\eta$ is killed by the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{X, \eta}$, whose residue field is $K(V)$; so the right side is $\rho \, (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V)$. Let $A$ be very ample on $V$. By Theorem P3.6.23, $\mathcal{G} \otimes A^m$ is generated by global sections for $m$ large, so its sections span the fibre $\mathcal{G}_\eta \otimes A_\eta$ over $K(V)$, and $\rho$ of them, $s_1, \dots, s_\rho$, give a basis. The map $\varphi \colon \mathcal{O}_V^\rho \to \mathcal{G} \otimes A^m$ defined by the $s_i$ is an isomorphism at $\eta$. Its kernel is a subsheaf of $\mathcal{O}_V^\rho$ that vanishes at $\eta$, so it is zero, because the only associated point of $\mathcal{O}_V^\rho$ is $\eta$. Its cokernel $\mathcal{Q}$ vanishes at $\eta$, so $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{Q} < r$. By parts 2, 3 and 5 of Theorem P7.1.6,
$$(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{G}) = (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{G} \otimes A^m) = \rho \, (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{O}_V) + (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{Q}) = \rho \, (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V),$$
where the twist by $A^m$ and the computation take place on $V$, which is allowed by part 7 of Theorem P7.1.6.

In general, use induction on $\lambda(\mathcal{F}) = \sum_V \operatorname{length}(\mathcal{F}_{\eta_V})$, the sum over the components of dimension $r$ of $\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}$. If $\lambda(\mathcal{F}) = 0$, then $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} < r$ and both sides are $0$. Otherwise let $V$ be a component of dimension $r$, $\mathcal{I}$ its ideal sheaf, and $\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{I}] \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ the subsheaf of local sections killed by $\mathcal{I}$, a coherent $\mathcal{O}_V$-module. At $\eta = \eta_V$, $\mathcal{I}_\eta$ is the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{X, \eta}$ and $\mathcal{F}_\eta$ is a nonzero module of finite length, so $\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{I}]_\eta$, its socle, is nonzero. In the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{F}[\mathcal{I}] \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}' \to 0$ the quotient has $\lambda(\mathcal{F}') < \lambda(\mathcal{F})$, and its support lies in that of $\mathcal{F}$. The case above applies to $\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{I}]$ and the induction hypothesis to $\mathcal{F}'$, and additivity of both sides gives the formula for $\mathcal{F}$. $\square$

*Proposition P7.1.14.* Part 1, by induction on $r$. If $r = 0$, then $V$ is a closed point and $(\cdot V) = \chi(\mathcal{O}_V) = 1$. Let $r \geq 1$. Since $L_r$ is generated by global sections, some section $\sigma$ does not vanish at the generic point $\eta$ of $V$, the only associated point of $\mathcal{O}_V$. By part 6 of Theorem P7.1.6 and Theorem P7.1.13,
$$(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V) = (L_1 \cdots L_{r-1} \cdot \mathcal{O}_{V \cap D}) = \sum_W \operatorname{length}(\mathcal{O}_{V \cap D, \eta_W}) \, (L_1 \cdots L_{r-1} \cdot W),$$
with $D$ the divisor of $\sigma$ and the sum over the components $W$ of $V \cap D$ of dimension $r - 1$. By induction every term is nonnegative.

Part 3. Put $V_r = V$. Choose sections $\sigma_r, \sigma_{r-1}, \dots, \sigma_1$ one after the other: once $V_j$ is defined, let $\sigma_j \in H^0(L_j)$ be a section that does not vanish at any associated point of $\mathcal{O}_{V_j}$, which exists by part 1 of Lemma P7.1.1, and let $V_{j-1} = V_j \cap D_j$ with $D_j$ the divisor of $\sigma_j$. The claim is that $\dim V_j = j$ for every $j$. It holds for $j = r$. Let $j \geq 1$ and $W$ a component of $V_j$ of dimension $j$. Then $W \cap D_j$ is nonempty: otherwise $W$ would be a closed subscheme of the affine space $\mathbb{P}^N \setminus H$, where $H$ is the hyperplane that defines $\sigma_j$ in the embedding by $L_j$, all coordinates would be global functions on $W$, constant by Example P3.4.27, and $W$ would be a point. Locally $W \cap D_j$ is the zero set of one function on the integral scheme $W$, which is not zero because $\sigma_j$ does not vanish at the generic point of $W$, an associated point of $\mathcal{O}_{V_j}$ by Theorem P1.2.48. So by part 1 of Proposition P3.7.5 its components have dimension $j - 1$, and $\dim V_{j-1} \geq j - 1$. By part 3 of Lemma P7.1.2, $\dim V_{j-1} \leq j - 1$. So $V_0 = V \cap D_1 \cap \dots \cap D_r$ is finite and nonempty. Applying part 6 of Theorem P7.1.6 $r$ times, to $\mathcal{O}_{V_r}, \mathcal{O}_{V_{r-1}}, \dots$, and then part 1 of Proposition P7.1.7, its length is $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V)$, which is therefore positive.

Part 2. By Theorem P3.6.25 there is $a \geq 1$ such that every $L_i^a$ is very ample. By multilinearity, $(L_1^a \cdots L_r^a \cdot V) = a^r (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot V)$, and the left side is positive by part 3. $\square$

*Theorem P7.1.15.* Since $Y$ is projective over $k$, the morphism $(i, f) \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^N_k \times X = \mathbb{P}^N_X$, with $i$ a closed immersion, is a closed immersion, so $f$ is projective and $R^q f_* \mathcal{G}$ is coherent by Theorem P4.4.8, zero for $q > N$. Its support is contained in $f(\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{G})$, a closed set of dimension at most $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{G} \leq r$: the image of an integral closed subscheme $Z$ is integral with function field contained in $K(Z)$, so its dimension is at most $\dim Z$ by part 1 of Theorem P3.7.4.

For a line bundle $M$ on $X$, the projection formula (Proposition P4.4.10) gives $R^q f_* (\mathcal{G} \otimes f^* M) \cong R^q f_* \mathcal{G} \otimes M$, and the Leray spectral sequence (Corollary P2.4.26) is $E_2^{p, q} = H^p(X, R^q f_* \mathcal{G} \otimes M) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(Y, \mathcal{G} \otimes f^* M)$. All terms are finite-dimensional and only finitely many are nonzero. The alternating sum $\sum_{p, q} (-1)^{p+q} \dim E_s^{p,q}$ does not change from one page to the next, because $E_{s+1}$ is the cohomology of the differential $d_s$ of total degree $1$, and on the last page it is $\chi(Y, \mathcal{G} \otimes f^* M)$, because the abutment has a finite filtration with these graded pieces. So
$$\chi(Y, \mathcal{G} \otimes f^* M) = \sum_q (-1)^q \chi(X, R^q f_* \mathcal{G} \otimes M).$$
Apply this with $M = L_I^{-1}$ for every $I \subseteq \{1, \dots, r\}$ and take the alternating sum of Definition P7.1.5. This proves the first formula.

For the second, take $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{O}_V$. If $\dim W < r$, every $R^q f_* \mathcal{O}_V$ is supported on $W$, and the right side is zero by part 2 of Theorem P7.1.6. Let $\dim W = r$, with generic point $\xi$. Since $K(W) \subseteq K(V)$ and both have transcendence degree $r$ over $k$ (part 1 of Theorem P3.7.4), the extension $K(V)/K(W)$ is finitely generated and algebraic, hence finite. By part 2 of Corollary P4.5.2, applied to $V \to W$, the stalk $(R^q f_* \mathcal{O}_V)_\xi$ is $H^q(V_\xi, \mathcal{O})$ for the generic fibre $V_\xi$. This is an integral scheme of finite type over $K(W)$ with function field $K(V)$, of dimension $\operatorname{trdeg}_{K(W)} K(V) = 0$, so $V_\xi = \operatorname{Spec} K(V)$. Hence $(R^q f_* \mathcal{O}_V)_\xi = 0$ for $q > 0$, and $(f_* \mathcal{O}_V)_\xi = K(V)$, of length $[K(V) : K(W)]$ over $\mathcal{O}_{X, \xi}$. For $q > 0$, $R^q f_* \mathcal{O}_V$ is supported on a proper closed subset of $W$, of dimension less than $r$, and contributes $0$. By Theorem P7.1.13, $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot f_* \mathcal{O}_V) = [K(V) : K(W)] \, (L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot W)$. $\square$

*Corollary P7.1.16.* Apply Theorem P7.1.15 with $V = Y$. If $\dim X = n$, $f(Y) = X$ and the degree is $[K(Y) : K(X)]$, which is $1$ if $f$ is birational. If $\dim X < n$, the second case applies. $\square$

*Theorem P7.1.17.* Part 1. Fix $\mathbf{m} \in \mathbb{Z}^r$ and put $M = L^{\mathbf{m}}$. The function $u \mapsto \chi(\mathcal{F} \otimes M^u) = P_{\mathcal{F}}(u \mathbf{m})$ is a polynomial $p(u)$ of degree at most $d$ with leading coefficient $c_d = P^{(d)}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{m})$. Apply Theorem P7.1.4 to $d$ copies of $M$: the polynomial $Q(t_1, \dots, t_d)$ with $Q(\mathbf{t}) = \chi(\mathcal{F} \otimes M^{t_1 + \dots + t_d})$ agrees with $p(t_1 + \dots + t_d)$ on $\mathbb{Z}^d$, so they are equal as polynomials. The coefficient of $t_1 \cdots t_d$ in $p(t_1 + \dots + t_d)$ comes only from $c_d (t_1 + \dots + t_d)^d$, and equals $d! \, c_d$. By part 1 of Theorem P7.1.6 it is $(M^d \cdot \mathcal{F})$. So $P^{(d)}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{m}) = (M^d \cdot \mathcal{F}) / d!$ for every integer vector $\mathbf{m}$. Expanding $(M^d \cdot \mathcal{F}) = ((m_1 L_1 + \dots + m_r L_r)^d \cdot \mathcal{F})$ by multilinearity and symmetry gives the multinomial formula, with coefficient $\frac{d!}{a_1! \cdots a_r!} (L_1^{a_1} \cdots L_r^{a_r} \cdot \mathcal{F})$ for $m^{\mathbf{a}}$. Two polynomials that agree on $\mathbb{Z}^r$ are equal, which proves part 1. Part 2 is the case $r = 1$. For part 3, $H^p(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^m) = 0$ for $p > 0$ and $m$ large by Theorem P4.2.10, so $h^0 = \chi$ for $m$ large, and the difference for the finitely many remaining $m \geq 0$ is absorbed in $O(m^{d-1})$. Positivity is part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14 together with Theorem P7.1.13: $[\mathcal{F}]_d$ is a nonzero effective cycle. $\square$

*Corollary P7.1.18.* With $L = H$, part 2 of Theorem P7.1.17 and Theorem P4.3.4 say that the Hilbert polynomial of $\mathcal{F}$ has leading term $\frac{(H^d \cdot \mathcal{F})}{d!} n^d$. By Definition P4.3.9 the degree is $d!$ times the leading coefficient. The last formula is Theorem P7.1.13. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ be a curve of bidegree $(a, b)$, the zero scheme of a nonzero section of $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$. Compute the degree of its image under the Segre embedding in $\mathbb{P}^3$.
2. (A) Show that the twisted cubic $\nu_3(\mathbb{P}^1) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$ meets a general plane in three points, and that $\nu_3^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ has degree $3$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$.
3. (B) Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ be a projective variety of dimension $n$ and degree $d$, and $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ a hypersurface of degree $e$ not containing $X$. Show that $\sum_W \operatorname{length}(\mathcal{O}_{X \cap Y, \eta_W}) \deg W = de$, the sum over the components of $X \cap Y$.
4. (B) Give an example of a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $\mathbb{P}^2$ with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} = 1$ and a line bundle $L$ with $(L \cdot \mathcal{F}) < 0$. Show that this cannot happen if $L$ is generated by global sections.
5. (B) Let $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the quotient by the involution that swaps the factors, given by the symmetric functions, so that $f^* \mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(1, 1)$. Use Corollary P7.1.16 to compute $\deg f$, and check the answer geometrically.
6. (C) Let $f \colon Y \to X$ be a surjective morphism of projective varieties with $\dim Y = n$, $\dim X = s < n$, and $L_1, \dots, L_s$ on $X$, $M_1, \dots, M_{n-s}$ on $Y$. Show that $(f^* L_1 \cdots f^* L_s \cdot M_1 \cdots M_{n-s}) = (L_1 \cdots L_s) \, (M_1 \cdots M_{n-s} \cdot Y_x)$ for a general closed point $x \in X$, when the $L_i$ are very ample. Cut by divisors pulled back from $X$ and use part 3 of Proposition P7.1.14.

### Lean

Mathlib has `Module.length`, which is the multiplicity in Definition P7.1.12, and the Leray spectral sequence is not in Mathlib. None of the results of this section is in Mathlib or in the project library. The project library records only the case of curves: `MinimalModel.IntersectionData` packages the numbers $D \cdot C$, and its value on a Cartier divisor is the degree on the normalization, as in part 2 of Proposition P7.1.7. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The cycle formula and the projection formula in this generality are in Kleiman's 1966 paper, Chapter I, Section 2, with the same proofs; Lazarsfeld, Section 1.1.C, states them for Cartier divisors and subvarieties. The literature state is theorem. Asymptotic Riemann-Roch in the form $\chi(L^m) = \frac{L^n}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1})$ is the reason the top self-intersection is the right measure of the size of a line bundle, and Chapter 7 defines the volume of an arbitrary divisor as the leading coefficient of $h^0(L^m)$. Bézout's theorem for plane curves is classical; its form for $n$ hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{P}^n$ with lengths as multiplicities is due to the school of Macaulay and to Weil and Samuel. In the minimal model program the projection formula is used every time a divisor is pulled back along a contraction, for instance to show that $f^* A \cdot C = 0$ for a curve $C$ contracted by $f$, which is how extremal faces of the cone of curves are attached to morphisms in Chapter 4.
