## Monomial ideals and Howald's theorem

### Statement

Throughout, $k$ has characteristic $0$, $X = \mathbb{A}^n = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, $\sigma = \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0}$, and $\mathbf{1} = (1, \dots, 1)$. For a nonzero monomial ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, generated by the $x^m$ with $m$ in a finite set $G \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^n_{\geq 0}$, $P(\mathfrak{a})$ is the Newton polyhedron, $\varphi(v) = \min_{m \in G} \langle m, v \rangle$ for $v \in \sigma$, and $t_0(\mathfrak{a})$ is the diagonal number, all as in Definition 4.6.10. For $c > 0$, $c P(\mathfrak{a}) = \{ c p : p \in P(\mathfrak{a}) \}$, and $\operatorname{Int}$ is the interior in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Theorem P7.10.17 (Howald).** Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nonzero monomial ideal and $c > 0$ rational. Then $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ is the monomial ideal
$$\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \big( x^v : v \in \mathbb{Z}^n_{\geq 0}, \ v + \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int}(c P(\mathfrak{a})) \big).$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P7.10.18 (the threshold again).** Let $\mathfrak{a} \neq (1)$ be a nonzero monomial ideal. Then $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{O}_X$ if and only if $c < 1 / t_0(\mathfrak{a})$. So $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb{A}^n; \mathfrak{a}) = 1 / t_0(\mathfrak{a})$, which is the second equality of Theorem 4.6.11.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A monomial ideal is invariant under the torus, and so is a toric log resolution of it. The proof of Theorem 4.6.11 builds one: the function $\varphi$ is linear on the cones of a fan, a smooth refinement of the fan gives a toric variety $Y$ over $\mathbb{A}^n$, and on $Y$ the ideal becomes the invertible sheaf of the torus-invariant divisor $F = \sum_\rho \varphi(u_\rho) D_\rho$. The relative canonical divisor is torus-invariant as well, with coefficient $\langle \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle - 1$ along $D_\rho$. So the multiplier ideal is the push-forward of the sheaf of a torus-invariant divisor, and its sections are spanned by the monomials that satisfy one inequality for each ray. The inequality for the ray $u_\rho$ is a round-down inequality, and the elementary fact that an integer $N$ satisfies $N - 1 \geq \lfloor x \rfloor$ if and only if $N > x$ turns it into the strict inequality $\langle v + \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle > c \varphi(u_\rho)$. The rays cut out $c P(\mathfrak{a})$ by these inequalities, made weak, because $\varphi$ is linear on each cone of the fan, so the strict inequalities cut out its interior.

The shift by $\mathbf{1}$ is the relative canonical divisor, and the interior is the round-down. The threshold is the first $c$ at which $\mathbf{1}$ leaves the interior of $c P(\mathfrak{a})$, which is the description through the diagonal in Volume 4.

### Examples

**Example P7.10.19 (the ideal $(x^2, y^3)$).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^2, y^3) \subseteq k[x, y]$. The Newton polyhedron is $P(\mathfrak{a}) = \{ p \in \mathbb{R}^2_{\geq 0} : \frac{p_1}{2} + \frac{p_2}{3} \geq 1 \}$, and its interior scaled by $c$ is $\{ p_1 > 0, p_2 > 0, \frac{p_1}{2} + \frac{p_2}{3} > c \}$. So $x^a y^b \in \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ if and only if $\frac{a + 1}{2} + \frac{b + 1}{3} > c$. The values of the left side are $\frac56$ at $(0, 0)$, $\frac76$ at $(0, 1)$, $\frac43$ at $(1, 0)$ and $\frac32$ at $(0, 2)$ and $(2, 0)$, and $\frac53$ at $(1, 1)$. Hence
$$\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \begin{cases} (1) & c < \frac56, \\ (x, y) & \frac56 \leq c < \frac76, \\ (x, y^2) & \frac76 \leq c < \frac43, \\ (x^2, xy, y^2) & \frac43 \leq c < \frac32. \end{cases}$$
The first jump is at $\frac56 = \frac12 + \frac13$, as in Example 4.6.13. The cusp $\{x^2 = y^3\}$ is a general member of $\mathfrak{a}$ up to scaling, and for $c < 1$ its multiplier ideal, computed in Example P7.10.7 after exchanging $x$ and $y$, is also $\mathcal{O}$ below $\frac56$ and $\mathfrak{m}$ from $\frac56$ to $1$.

**Example P7.10.20 (powers of the maximal ideal again).** Let $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}^k$ with $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$. Then $P(\mathfrak{m}^k) = \{ p \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0} : |p| \geq k \}$ with $|p| = p_1 + \dots + p_n$, by Example 4.6.15, and $v + \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int}(c P(\mathfrak{m}^k))$ if and only if $|v| + n > ck$, that is, $|v| \geq \lfloor ck \rfloor - n + 1$. So $\mathcal{J}((\mathfrak{m}^k)^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor ck \rfloor - n + 1}$, which is $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^{ck})$ of Example P7.10.8, and the threshold is $\frac{n}{k}$, the reciprocal of $t_0 = \frac{k}{n}$.

### Proof

*Theorem P7.10.17.* By the proof of Theorem 4.6.11 there is a fan $\Sigma_0$ with support $\sigma$ such that $\varphi$ is linear on each of its cones, and by Theorem P8.8.4 a smooth fan $\Sigma$ refining $\Sigma_0$, so that $\varphi$ is linear on each cone of $\Sigma$. Let $f \colon Y = X_\Sigma \to \mathbb{A}^n$ be the toric morphism, proper and birational by Proposition P8.8.2, with $Y$ smooth. The rays $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$ have primitive generators $u_\rho \in \sigma \cap \mathbb{Z}^n$, and they include the coordinate rays, whose generators are the standard basis vectors $e_i$, since every cone of $\Sigma$ lies in $\sigma$ and $\Sigma$ covers $\sigma$. The proof of Theorem 4.6.11 shows that
$$\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F), \qquad F = \sum_{\rho \in \Sigma(1)} \varphi(u_\rho) D_\rho,$$
and that the torus-invariant divisors $D_\rho$ form an snc divisor. For the relative canonical divisor, $K_{Y/\mathbb{A}^n}$ is the divisor on $Y$ of the pullback of the generator $dx_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n = x_1 \cdots x_n \, \omega$ of $\omega_{\mathbb{A}^n}$, where $\omega = \frac{dx_1}{x_1} \wedge \dots \wedge \frac{dx_n}{x_n}$. On $Y$, $\operatorname{div}(\omega) = -\sum_\rho D_\rho$ by Theorem P8.7.17, and $\operatorname{div}(x_i) = \sum_\rho \langle e_i, u_\rho \rangle D_\rho$ by part 3 of Lemma P8.7.2, so
$$K_{Y/\mathbb{A}^n} = \sum_{\rho \in \Sigma(1)} \big( \langle \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle - 1 \big) D_\rho.$$
By part 1 of Proposition P7.10.4, $f$ computes the multiplier ideal: $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = f_* \mathcal{O}_Y(B)$ with
$$B = K_{Y/\mathbb{A}^n} - \lfloor c F \rfloor = \sum_\rho a_\rho D_\rho, \qquad a_\rho = \langle \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle - 1 - \lfloor c \varphi(u_\rho) \rfloor.$$
Since $\mathbb{A}^n$ is affine, the ideal is determined by its global sections $H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(B)) \subseteq k(x_1, \dots, x_n)$. By Proposition P8.7.3 this space is spanned by the monomials $\chi^v = x^v$ with $v \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and $\langle v, u_\rho \rangle \geq -a_\rho$ for every $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$. For $\rho$ a coordinate ray, $-a_{\rho} = \lfloor c \varphi(e_i) \rfloor \geq 0$, so these $v$ lie in $\mathbb{Z}^n_{\geq 0}$, in agreement with $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$. The condition for $\rho$ is
$$\langle v + \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle - 1 \geq \lfloor c \varphi(u_\rho) \rfloor,$$
and since the left side is an integer $N - 1$, it holds if and only if $N > c \varphi(u_\rho)$, that is,
$$\langle v + \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle > c \varphi(u_\rho) \quad \text{for every } \rho \in \Sigma(1). \tag{$*$}$$
It remains to show that $(*)$ describes $\operatorname{Int}(c P(\mathfrak{a}))$. First,
$$c P(\mathfrak{a}) = \{ w \in \mathbb{R}^n : \langle w, u_\rho \rangle \geq c \varphi(u_\rho) \text{ for every } \rho \in \Sigma(1) \}.$$
If $w = c p$ with $p \in P(\mathfrak{a})$, then $\langle p, u \rangle \geq \varphi(u)$ for every $u \in \sigma$, since the minimum of $\langle \cdot, u \rangle$ on $P(\mathfrak{a})$ is attained on $G$ (Definition 4.6.10). Conversely, suppose $w$ satisfies the inequalities for the rays. Every $u \in \sigma$ lies in a cone $\tau$ of $\Sigma$, so $u = \sum_{\rho \in \tau(1)} \lambda_\rho u_\rho$ with $\lambda_\rho \geq 0$, and $\varphi$ is linear on $\tau$, so $\langle w, u \rangle = \sum \lambda_\rho \langle w, u_\rho \rangle \geq c \sum \lambda_\rho \varphi(u_\rho) = c \varphi(u)$. The proof of Theorem 4.6.11 writes $P(\mathfrak{a})$ as the intersection of finitely many half-spaces $\{ p : \langle p, v_j \rangle \geq \varphi(v_j) \}$ with $v_j \in \sigma$, so $w / c \in P(\mathfrak{a})$. Second, the interior of a finite intersection of closed half-spaces $\{ \langle w, u_\rho \rangle \geq b_\rho \}$ with $u_\rho \neq 0$ is the set where all the inequalities are strict: that set is open and contained in the intersection, and if $w$ is interior with $\langle w, u_\rho \rangle = b_\rho$, then $w - \epsilon u_\rho$ lies in the intersection for small $\epsilon > 0$ and violates the inequality for $\rho$. So $(*)$ says $v + \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int}(c P(\mathfrak{a}))$, and the theorem follows.

*Corollary P7.10.18.* By the theorem, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{O}_X$ if and only if $1 = x^0$ lies in it, that is, $\mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int}(c P(\mathfrak{a}))$, that is, $\frac1c \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Int} P(\mathfrak{a})$. If $t > t_0$, then $t \mathbf{1} \in t_0 \mathbf{1} + \operatorname{Int} \sigma$, an open set contained in $P(\mathfrak{a})$ because $P(\mathfrak{a}) + \sigma = P(\mathfrak{a})$, so $t \mathbf{1}$ is interior. If $t \leq t_0$, then $t \mathbf{1}$ is not interior: otherwise $(t - \epsilon) \mathbf{1} \in P(\mathfrak{a})$ for small $\epsilon > 0$, against the minimality of $t_0$, since $P(\mathfrak{a}) + \sigma = P(\mathfrak{a})$ puts $t' \mathbf{1}$ in $P(\mathfrak{a})$ for every $t' \geq t - \epsilon$. So $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathcal{O}_X$ if and only if $\frac1c > t_0$. By Proposition P7.10.5 the threshold is the least $c$ with nontrivial multiplier ideal, which is $\frac{1}{t_0}$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ for $\mathfrak{a} = (x^5, x^2 y, y^4)$ and $c \leq 1$, and check that the first jump is at $\frac58$, as in Example 4.6.14.
2. (A) For $\mathfrak{a} = (x_1 \cdots x_n)$, a principal monomial ideal, check that Theorem P7.10.17 gives $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = (x_1 \cdots x_n)^{\lfloor c \rfloor}$, in agreement with part 2 of Proposition P7.10.4.
3. (B) Show from Theorem P7.10.17 that $\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil c \rceil} \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ for a monomial ideal, using $t P(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq s P(\mathfrak{a})$ for $t \geq s > 0$. Then prove the same inclusion for every nonzero ideal from Definition P7.10.2.
4. (B) Call $c > 0$ a *jumping number* of $\mathfrak{a}$ if $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c - \epsilon}) \neq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ for every $\epsilon > 0$. Show from the proof of Theorem P7.10.17 that every jumping number of a monomial ideal has the form $(\langle \mathbf{1}, u_\rho \rangle + j) / \varphi(u_\rho)$ with $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$, $\varphi(u_\rho) > 0$ and $j \geq 0$ an integer, so that the jumping numbers are rational and form a discrete set.
5. (B) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x^a, y^b)$. Find all the jumping numbers of $\mathfrak{a}$ in $(0, 1)$, and show that their number is the number of lattice points $(i, j)$ with $i, j \geq 1$ and $\frac{i}{a} + \frac{j}{b} < 1$ when $\gcd(a, b) = 1$.
6. (C) Prove Skoda's theorem, Theorem P7.10.13, for monomial ideals from Theorem P7.10.17: show that for $c \geq n$ every lattice point of $\operatorname{Int}(c P(\mathfrak{a})) - \mathbf{1}$ is a point of $G$ plus a lattice point of $\operatorname{Int}((c - 1) P(\mathfrak{a})) - \mathbf{1}$, using Carathéodory's theorem on a face of $P(\mathfrak{a})$.

### Notes and history

Howald proved the theorem (How01) by the toric resolution used here. The literature state is theorem. Lazarsfeld, Section 9.3.C (Laz04), gives the same proof, and Cox, Little and Schenck (CLS11) treat the toric log resolution. The threshold formula of Volume 4, Theorem 4.6.11, is the case of the first jump. Monomial ideals are the main test case for the general theory: the jumping numbers, subadditivity, and the Briançon-Skoda theorem can all be checked by convex geometry, and the multiplier ideals of a general member of a monomial ideal agree with those of the ideal below the coefficient $1$.
