# Multiplier ideals

The proof of Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing in Chapter 8 passes to a log resolution $\mu \colon X' \to X$, rounds down the pulled back boundary, and absorbs the round-down into the relative canonical divisor $K_{X'/X}$. When the boundary is not small, the absorption fails, and what is left over is the ideal $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor)$. This is the multiplier ideal of $D$. It measures how far the pair $(X, D)$ is from being klt, it is computed on any log resolution, and Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing on the resolution becomes Nadel vanishing on $X$ with the multiplier ideal as a correction. In the minimal model program multiplier ideals appear through the log canonical threshold, which is the first value of $c$ at which the multiplier ideal of $c D$ is nontrivial, and through their analytic counterpart in the work of Siu and of Hacon and McKernan on the extension of pluricanonical forms.

Section 10.1 defines the multiplier ideal of an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and of an ideal with a rational exponent, proves that it does not depend on the log resolution, and proves that it is trivial exactly below the log canonical threshold of Volume 4, Chapter 6. The cusp and the powers of the maximal ideal are computed. Section 10.2 proves local vanishing and Nadel vanishing from the relative and absolute Kawamata-Viehweg theorems, states the restriction, subadditivity and Skoda theorems, and proves the easy half of Skoda's theorem. Three concurrent lines in the plane show the vanishing at work. Section 10.3 proves Howald's theorem that the multiplier ideals of a monomial ideal are monomial and are read off from the Newton polyhedron, using the toric log resolution of Volume 4, Chapter 6, and recovers the threshold formula proved there.

Throughout the chapter $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$.

The chapter follows Lazarsfeld, Chapters 9, 10 and 11 (Laz04), with Kollár and Mori, Section 2.4 (KM98), for the vanishing theorems, and Howald's paper (How01) for monomial ideals.
