## Nadel vanishing and the local theory

### Statement

Throughout, $k$ has characteristic $0$, $X$ is a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$, and the multiplier ideals are those of Section 10.1. Log resolutions are taken projective, as Theorem 4.2.10 allows.

**Theorem P7.10.9 (local vanishing).** Let $D$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with a log resolution $\mu \colon X' \to X$, and $\mathfrak{a}$ a nonzero ideal with a log resolution $\mu$ and $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$. Then for $j > 0$ and rational $c \geq 0$,
$$R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor) = 0, \qquad R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor c F \rfloor) = 0.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.10.10 (Nadel vanishing).** Let $D$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $L$ a Cartier divisor on $X$.

1. If $L - D$ is nef and big, then $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(D)) = 0$ for $i > 0$.
2. Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nonzero ideal, $M$ a Cartier divisor with $\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(M)$ generated by global sections, and $c \geq 0$ rational. If $L - cM$ is nef and big, then $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)) = 0$ for $i > 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.10.11 (restriction, black box).** Let $H \subseteq X$ be a smooth prime divisor and $D$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor whose support does not contain $H$. Then
$$\mathcal{J}(H, D|_H) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(X, D) \cdot \mathcal{O}_H.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.10.12 (subadditivity, black box).** Let $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathfrak{b}$ be nonzero ideals and $c, d \geq 0$ rational, and $D_1$, $D_2$ effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors. Then
$$\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c \mathfrak{b}^d) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{b}^d), \qquad \mathcal{J}(D_1 + D_2) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(D_1) \cdot \mathcal{J}(D_2),$$
where $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c \mathfrak{b}^d) = \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor c F_{\mathfrak{a}} + d F_{\mathfrak{b}} \rfloor)$ on a common log resolution. In particular $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{mc}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)^m$ for every integer $m \geq 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.10.13 (Skoda, black box).** Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nonzero ideal. For every integer $m \geq n$,
$$\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^m) = \mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{m - 1}).$$
In particular $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^m) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}^{m - n + 1}$ for $m \geq n$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.10.14 (the easy inclusion).** For every nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ and rational $c \geq 1$, $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1}) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Local vanishing is relative Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for the log resolution. The divisor $K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor$ is $K_{X'}$ plus the round-up of $-\mu^* D$, up to the pullback of $K_X$, and $-\mu^* D$ is the pullback of a divisor from $X$, so it is nef and big over $X$ after adding the pullback of an ample divisor. For an ideal, $-F$ is the same as the pullback of $-M$ plus a globally generated divisor. Nadel vanishing then follows by the Leray spectral sequence: the higher direct images vanish, the direct image is the multiplier ideal twisted by $K_X + L$, and the cohomology upstairs vanishes by Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, since $\mu^*(L - D)$ is nef and big.

So Nadel vanishing is Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing without the snc hypothesis and with any coefficients, and the price is the multiplier ideal. When $D$ has snc support with coefficients less than $1$, the ideal is trivial and the theorem is Theorem P7.8.18. The restriction theorem is proved by the same method, with the vanishing used to compare the multiplier ideals on $X$ and on $H$ through a log resolution on which the strict transform of $H$ is one of the snc components, and subadditivity follows from restriction applied to the diagonal of $X \times X$. Skoda's theorem uses the Koszul complex of a reduction of $\mathfrak{a}$ by $n$ elements together with local vanishing. These are black boxes here. The easy inclusion of Skoda's theorem is a computation of orders.

### Examples

**Example P7.10.15 (three concurrent lines).** Let $L_1, L_2, L_3 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be distinct lines through a point $p$, $D = c(L_1 + L_2 + L_3)$ with $0 < c < 1$, and $\mu \colon X' \to \mathbb{P}^2$ the blow-up of $p$, as in Example P7.8.22. Then $\mu$ is a log resolution of $D$, $K_{X'/X} = E$ and $\mu^* D = c(L_1' + L_2' + L_3') + 3cE$, so $K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor = (1 - \lfloor 3c \rfloor) E$. By Proposition P4.4.16,
$$\mathcal{J}(D) = \begin{cases} \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2} & 0 < c < \tfrac23, \\ \mathfrak{m}_p & \tfrac23 \leq c < 1. \end{cases}$$
The first jump is at $\frac23 = \operatorname{lct}_p(\mathbb{P}^2; L_1 + L_2 + L_3)$, which is $\frac2d$ for $d = 3$ lines, as in Example 4.6.8. Take $\frac23 \leq c < 1$ and $L = kH$. If $k \geq 3$, then $L - D \equiv (k - 3c)H$ is ample, and Nadel vanishing gives $H^i(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathfrak{m}_p(k - 3)) = 0$ for $i > 0$. This can be checked directly: $H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(k - 3)) \to k(p)$ is surjective for $k \geq 3$, and $H^i(\mathcal{O}(k-3)) = 0$ for $i > 0$. On $X'$ the computation is the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing of $K_{X'} + kH - 2E = (k - 3)H - E$. For $k = 2$ the hypothesis fails, since $(2 - 3c) H$ is not nef, and so does the conclusion: the sequence $0 \to \mathfrak{m}_p(-1) \to \mathcal{O}(-1) \to k(p) \to 0$ gives $H^1(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathfrak{m}_p(-1)) = k$.

**Example P7.10.16 (Skoda for the maximal ideal).** By Example P7.10.8, $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^c) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}$ on $\mathbb{A}^n$. For an integer $m \geq n$, $\mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^{m-1}) = \mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathfrak{m}^{m - n} = \mathfrak{m}^{m - n + 1} = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^m)$, as Theorem P7.10.13 says. For $m = n - 1$ the equality fails: $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^{n-1}) = \mathcal{O}$, while $\mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{m}^{n-2}) = \mathfrak{m}$. So the bound $m \geq n$ in Skoda's theorem is sharp, and only the inclusion of Proposition P7.10.14 survives below it.

### Proof

*Theorem P7.10.9.* Let $H$ be an ample divisor on $X$ with $H - D$ ample. Put $N = -\lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor$ and $\Delta = \{\mu^* D\}$, which has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$. Then $N - \Delta = -\mu^* D$ and $N - \Delta + \mu^* H = \mu^*(H - D)$, which is nef by part 3 of Proposition P7.3.9 and big since $(\mu^*(H - D))^n = ((H - D)^n) > 0$ by Theorem P7.1.15 and Definition P7.8.1. Theorem P7.9.3 gives $R^j \mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor) = 0$ for $j > 0$, and $K_{X'} = \mu^* K_X + K_{X'/X}$ with the projection formula gives the first statement. For the ideal, choose $H$ very ample with $\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(H)$ generated by global sections, by Theorem P4.2.7. Pulling back generating sections gives sections of $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(\mu^* H - F)$ without common zero, so $\mu^* H - F$ is nef by part 1 of Proposition P7.3.9. With $N = -\lfloor c F \rfloor$, $\Delta = \{c F\}$ and the ample divisor $(c + 1) H$,
$$N - \Delta + \mu^*((c + 1) H) = c(\mu^* H - F) + \mu^* H$$
is a nef divisor plus a nef and big one, so nef and big by Lemma P7.9.2, and Theorem P7.9.3 applies again.

*Theorem P7.10.10.* For part 1, let $\mu$ be a log resolution of $D$, and put $N' = \mu^* L - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor$ and $\Delta' = \{\mu^* D\}$. Then $N' - \Delta' = \mu^*(L - D)$ is nef and big, as in the proof of Theorem P7.10.9, so Theorem P7.8.18 on $X'$ gives $H^i(X', \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'} + N')) = 0$ for $i > 0$. By the projection formula,
$$\mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'} + N') = \mu^* \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor \mu^* D \rfloor),$$
so $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'} + N') = \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(D)$, and $R^j \mu_* = 0$ for $j > 0$ by Theorem P7.10.9. The Leray spectral sequence (Theorem P2.4.26) gives $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(D)) = H^i(X', \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'} + N')) = 0$. For part 2, let $\mu$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak{a}$. As in the proof of Theorem P7.10.9, $\mu^* M - F$ is nef, so $N' - \Delta' = \mu^* L - c F = \mu^*(L - cM) + c(\mu^* M - F)$, with $N' = \mu^* L - \lfloor c F \rfloor$ and $\Delta' = \{cF\}$, is nef and big by Lemma P7.9.2, and the same argument applies.

*Theorem P7.10.11.* This is Lazarsfeld's restriction theorem (Laz04, Section 9.5), proved there from local vanishing; it is a black box here.

*Theorem P7.10.12.* This is the subadditivity theorem of Demailly, Ein and Lazarsfeld (DEL00), in the algebraic form of Lazarsfeld, Section 9.5 (Laz04); it is a black box here. The last statement is the case $\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{a}$ iterated, since a log resolution of $\mathfrak{a}$ computes $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c} \mathfrak{a}^{d}) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c+d})$.

*Theorem P7.10.13.* This is the algebraic form of Skoda's theorem (Sko72) in Lazarsfeld, Section 9.6 (Laz04), proved there from local vanishing and the Koszul complex; it is a black box here. The last statement follows by induction on $m$ from $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{n-1}) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$.

*Proposition P7.10.14.* Let $\mu$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak{a}$ and $F = \sum_i e_i F_i$, $K_{X'/X} = \sum_i k_i F_i$. Let $g$ be a local section of $\mathfrak{a}$ and $h$ one of $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{c-1})$ over an open set $U$. Then $\operatorname{ord}_{F_i}(\mu^* g) \geq e_i$, since $\mathfrak{a} \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$, and $\operatorname{ord}_{F_i}(\mu^* h) \geq \lfloor (c-1) e_i \rfloor - k_i$ on $\mu^{-1}(U)$. Since $e_i$ is an integer, $\lfloor (c - 1) e_i \rfloor + e_i = \lfloor c e_i \rfloor$, so
$$\operatorname{ord}_{F_i}(\mu^*(g h)) \geq \lfloor c e_i \rfloor - k_i$$
for every $i$, and $gh$ is a section of $\mu_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}(K_{X'/X} - \lfloor c F \rfloor) = \mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ over $U$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $D$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $\mathbb{P}^n$ of degree $d$, that is, $D \equiv dH$. Show that $H^i(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{J}(D)(m)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and every integer $m > d - n - 1$.
2. (A) Deduce from Exercise 1 that if $D \equiv dH$ on $\mathbb{P}^2$ with $d < 3$ and $\mathcal{J}(D) = \mathfrak{m}_p$ for a point $p$, then $H^1(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathfrak{m}_p(m)) = 0$ for $m \geq 0$, and check this directly.
3. (B) Let $D$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $X$ and $L$ a Cartier divisor with $L - D$ nef and big. Suppose that $\mathcal{J}(D)$ is the ideal of a finite set $Z$ of reduced points. Show that $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L)) \to H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z)$ is surjective, so that $K_X + L$ separates the points of $Z$.
4. (B) Let $\mathfrak{a} = (x, y^2) \subseteq k[x, y]$. Using the log resolution obtained by two blow-ups, compute $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c)$ for $0 \leq c < 3$ and check Theorem P7.10.13 for $m = 2$.
5. (B) Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be the ideal of a smooth subvariety $W \subseteq X$ of codimension $r \geq 2$. Show that $\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \mathfrak{a}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - r + 1}$, with $\mathfrak{a}^j = \mathcal{O}_X$ for $j \leq 0$, using the blow-up of $W$ and $K_{X'/X} = (r - 1)E$, and compare with Example P7.10.8 and Theorem P7.10.13.
6. (C) Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface, $A$ an ample divisor and $x \in X$ a point. Suppose that $A^2 > 4$ and $A \cdot C \geq 2$ for every curve $C$ through $x$. Using Riemann-Roch, find an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D \equiv c A$ with $c < 1$ and $\operatorname{mult}_x D > 2$. Assuming that $\mathcal{J}(D)$ is $\mathfrak{m}_x$ near $x$, deduce from Nadel vanishing that $K_X + A$ has a section not vanishing at $x$. This is the first step of the proof of Reider's theorem by multiplier ideals (Laz04, Section 10.4).

### Lean

The project library has no multiplier ideals and no form of Nadel vanishing. The relative vanishing used for Theorem P7.10.9 is the projective snc case of `MinimalModel.KawamataViehweg`, which is stated without proof. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Nadel proved the vanishing theorem for analytic multiplier ideals on compact Kähler manifolds (Nad90), and the algebraic form with the proof given here is Lazarsfeld, Section 9.4 (Laz04), following Esnault and Viehweg. The literature state is theorem. The restriction theorem and its inversion of adjunction form are in Lazarsfeld, Section 9.5. Demailly, Ein and Lazarsfeld proved subadditivity (DEL00) and used it for the asymptotic theory of base loci. Skoda's theorem (Sko72) was analytic, and the algebraic form is due to Lazarsfeld and Lipman; it gives the Briançon-Skoda theorem on integral closures, Lazarsfeld, Section 9.6. In the minimal model program Nadel vanishing is the vanishing theorem behind the extension theorems of Siu, Kawamata, and Hacon and McKernan.
