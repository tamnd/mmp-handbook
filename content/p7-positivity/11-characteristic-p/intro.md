# Characteristic p

Every proof of a vanishing theorem in Chapters 8 to 10 rests on Kodaira's theorem, and Kodaira's theorem rests on Hodge theory, through the Lefschetz principle. In characteristic $p > 0$ there is no Hodge theory, and Kodaira's theorem is false. This chapter explains what fails and what survives. The failure is the reason why the minimal model program in positive characteristic, for threefolds and beyond, is built on different tools: Frobenius splitting, its singular counterparts, and the trace of Frobenius in place of multiplier ideals.

Section 11.1 constructs the curves of Tango, on which Frobenius kills a cohomology class of a negative line bundle, works out a plane curve of this kind, and outlines Raynaud's construction of a smooth projective surface with an ample line bundle that violates Kodaira vanishing. Section 11.2 states the theorem of Deligne and Illusie that the de Rham complex of a variety of dimension less than $p$ that lifts to the Witt vectors of length $2$ decomposes, and deduces Kodaira and Akizuki-Nakano vanishing for such varieties by iterating Frobenius. Raynaud's surface therefore does not lift. Section 11.3 defines Frobenius splitting, proves that it gives Kodaira vanishing by the same iteration, proves that toric varieties are Frobenius split, and decides which curves are: the projective line, ordinary elliptic curves, and no curve of genus at least $2$.

Throughout the chapter $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p > 0$.

The chapter follows Raynaud's paper (Ray78), Deligne and Illusie (DI87), Esnault and Viehweg, Lectures 8 to 11 (EV92), and Brion and Kumar, Chapter 1 (BK05), with Mehta and Ramanathan's paper (MR85) for Frobenius splitting.
