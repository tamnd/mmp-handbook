# Nef and ample cones and the closed cone of curves

By Chapter 3, ampleness and nefness of a line bundle depend only on its class in $N^1(X)$, and the ample classes form an open convex cone whose closure is the nef cone. This chapter studies the cones themselves. The nef cone in $N^1(X)$ and the closed cone of curves $\overline{NE}(X)$ in $N_1(X)$ are dual to each other, so either one determines the other, and the shape of $\overline{NE}(X)$, its faces and extremal rays, records the morphisms from $X$ to other projective varieties: a morphism $f$ cuts out the face of the classes it contracts, and the face is zero exactly when $f$ is finite.

Section 4.1 collects the convex geometry: dual cones, the bipolar theorem, salient cones and the compact slice, faces and extremal rays, polyhedral cones, and Minkowski's theorem that a closed salient cone is spanned by its extremal rays. Section 4.2 proves the duality of $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ and $\overline{NE}(X)$, identifies the ample cone and the interior of $\overline{NE}(X)$, attaches a face to every morphism, compares it under Stein factorization, and introduces the effective cone with its bound by covering families of curves. Section 4.3 computes the three cones $\overline{NE}$, $\operatorname{Nef}$ and $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}$ for the blow-up of projective space at a point, the Hirzebruch surfaces, the plane blown up at two points and the del Pezzo surfaces, where the cones are polyhedral, and for the plane blown up at nine points, $E \times E$ and Mumford's surface, where they are not, or where $NE(X)$ is not closed.

The examples show the three phenomena that the minimal model program must handle: cones with infinitely many extremal rays, round cones, and nef classes on the boundary that come from no morphism. The cone theorem of P5 Ch. 12 for surfaces, and its higher-dimensional form in the volumes on the minimal model program, controls the $K_X$-negative part of $\overline{NE}(X)$, where none of these phenomena occur.

The chapter follows Lazarsfeld, Sections 1.4 and 1.5 (Laz04), Debarre, Chapter 1 (Deb01), Kleiman's paper (Kle66), and Fulton, Section 1.2 (Ful93), for convex cones.
