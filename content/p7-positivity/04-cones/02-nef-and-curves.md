## The nef cone, the closed cone of curves and their faces

### Statement

Throughout, $X$ is a projective scheme over $k$ and $N_1(X)$ is identified with the dual of $N^1(X)$ by the perfect pairing of Theorem P7.2.12. The cones $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$, $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$, $NE(X)$ and $\overline{NE}(X)$ are those of Definition P7.3.8, and the language of cones is that of Definition P7.4.1. To avoid trivial cases, $\dim X \geq 1$, so that $\rho(X) \geq 1$.

**Theorem P7.4.9 (duality of the nef cone and the closed cone of curves).**

1. $\operatorname{Nef}(X) = \overline{NE}(X)^\vee$ and $\overline{NE}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X)^\vee$.
2. Both cones are closed, salient and full-dimensional.
3. $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$, and it is the set of classes positive on $\overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$. The interior of $\overline{NE}(X)$ is the set of $z$ with $\delta \cdot z > 0$ for every $\delta \in \operatorname{Nef}(X) \setminus \{0\}$.
4. For $\delta \in \operatorname{Nef}(X)$, $F_\delta = \overline{NE}(X) \cap \delta^\perp$ is a face of $\overline{NE}(X)$, and $F_\delta = \{0\}$ if and only if $\delta$ is ample. Every nef class that is not ample lies on the boundary of $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ and cuts out a nonzero face of $\overline{NE}(X)$.

Lean: `MinimalModel.nefCone`, in the project library, is the set of classes of nef $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisors, and `MinimalModel.IntersectionData.coneOfCurves` is $\overline{NE}(X)$; the duality of part 1 is not stated there.

**Definition P7.4.10 (contracted curves and the face of a morphism).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a morphism of projective schemes. A curve $C \subseteq X$ is *contracted* by $f$ if $f(C)$ is a point. The *face of $f$* is
$$F_f = \{ z \in \overline{NE}(X) : f_* z = 0 \},$$
with $f_* \colon N_1(X) \to N_1(Y)$ the map of part 2 of Proposition P7.2.7.

**Proposition P7.4.11 (the face of a morphism).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a morphism of projective schemes and $A$ an ample line bundle on $Y$.

1. $f_*$ maps $NE(X)$ into $NE(Y)$ and $\overline{NE}(X)$ into $\overline{NE}(Y)$.
2. $F_f = \overline{NE}(X) \cap (f^* A)^\perp$. So $F_f$ is a face of $\overline{NE}(X)$, cut out by the nef class $f^* A$, and it does not depend on $A$.
3. A curve $C$ is contracted by $f$ if and only if $[C] \in F_f$, if and only if $f^* A \cdot C = 0$.
4. $F_f = \{0\}$ if and only if $f$ is finite. If $f$ contracts a curve, $f^* A$ is nef and not ample.
5. If $h \colon Y \to Z$ is a morphism of projective schemes, then $F_f \subseteq F_{h \circ f}$. If $f = g \circ f'$ is the Stein factorization of Theorem P4.6.15, with $f' \colon X \to Y'$ a contraction and $g$ finite, then $Y'$ is projective and $F_f = F_{f'}$.
6. If $L$ is a line bundle generated by global sections and $\varphi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^N$ is the morphism it defines, with $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong L$, then $F_\varphi = \overline{NE}(X) \cap L^\perp$, and $\varphi$ contracts exactly the curves $C$ with $L \cdot C = 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.4.12 (surjective morphisms and the cones).** Let $f \colon Y \to X$ be a surjective morphism of projective schemes. Then $f_* NE(Y) = NE(X)$, the closure of $f_* \overline{NE}(Y)$ is $\overline{NE}(X)$, and $\operatorname{Nef}(X) = \{ \delta : f^* \delta \in \operatorname{Nef}(Y) \}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P7.4.13 (the effective cone).** The *effective cone* $\operatorname{Eff}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)$ is the convex cone spanned by the classes of effective Cartier divisors. Its closure $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$ is the *pseudo-effective cone*, studied in Chapter 5.

**Lemma P7.4.14 (effective divisors and curves).** Let $D$ be an effective Cartier divisor on $X$ and $C$ a curve not contained in $\operatorname{Supp} D$. Then $D \cdot C \geq 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.4.15 (the effective cone and covering curves).**

1. $\operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Eff}(X)$, so $\operatorname{Eff}(X)$ is full-dimensional, and $\operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$.
2. Let $X$ be a variety and $\Sigma$ a set of curves on $X$ with the same class $\gamma \in N_1(X)$, whose union is not contained in any proper closed subset of $X$. Then $\delta \cdot \gamma \geq 0$ for every $\delta \in \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$.
3. Let $S$ be a smooth projective surface. Under the isomorphism $N_1(S) \cong N^1(S)$ of Proposition P7.2.8, $\operatorname{Eff}(S) = NE(S)$, $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(S) = \overline{NE}(S)$ and $\operatorname{Nef}(S) = \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(S)^\vee \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(S)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Kleiman's work in Chapter 3 shows that a class is nef exactly when it is nonnegative on $\overline{NE}(X)$, and that a class is ample exactly when it is positive on $\overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$. Read through the duality of Section 4.1, these say that the nef cone is the dual of the closed cone of curves, that ample classes are the interior points of the nef cone, and that the closed cone of curves is the dual of the nef cone. A nef class that is not ample vanishes on a nonzero part of $\overline{NE}(X)$, and this part is a face. A morphism $f$ produces such a class, the pullback of an ample class, and the face it cuts out consists of the classes killed by $f_*$, which contain the curves contracted by $f$. The face is zero exactly when $f$ is finite, and the Stein factorization shows that only the contraction part of $f$ matters. This is the dictionary between faces of $\overline{NE}(X)$ and morphisms that the minimal model program inverts: the contraction theorem constructs, for suitable faces, the morphism that contracts them.

On the side of divisors, effective classes are nonnegative on every curve that moves enough to escape any given divisor, which bounds the effective cone from outside. On a surface curves and divisors are the same, and the effective cone is the cone of curves.

### Examples

**Example P7.4.16 (a projective space and a curve).** On $\mathbb{P}^n$, $N^1 = \mathbb{R} H$ and $N_1 = \mathbb{R} [\ell]$ with $H \cdot \ell = 1$, and every curve of degree $d$ has class $d [\ell]$ (Proposition P7.2.21). So $NE = \overline{NE} = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [\ell]$, $\operatorname{Nef} = \operatorname{Eff} = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} H$ and $\operatorname{Amp} = \mathbb{R}_{> 0} H$. The only faces of $\overline{NE}$ are $\{0\}$ and $\overline{NE}$ itself: the first is the face of the identity, and the second is the face of the map to a point, cut out by $\delta = 0$. On a smooth projective curve $C$ the same holds with $N^1(C) = \mathbb{R}$ by degree (Example P7.2.9), so $\operatorname{Amp}(C) = \mathbb{R}_{> 0}$ and $\operatorname{Nef}(C) = \operatorname{Eff}(C) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$, the last because a point is an effective divisor of degree $1$. On the other hand a divisor class of degree $0$ is effective only if it is $0$ in $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$: effectivity of a line bundle is not a numerical property, which is why $\operatorname{Eff}(X)$ is defined as the cone spanned by classes of effective divisors.

**Example P7.4.17 (the faces of a ruling).** Let $p_1 \colon X = \mathbb{P}^a \times \mathbb{P}^b \to \mathbb{P}^a$ be the first projection, with $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, $H_1$, $H_2$ as in Proposition P7.2.21, so $\ell_2$ is a line in a fibre $\{x\} \times \mathbb{P}^b$. By Example P7.3.15, $\overline{NE}(X) = \langle [\ell_1], [\ell_2] \rangle$, and by part 2 of Proposition P7.4.11 with $A = \mathcal{O}(1)$,
$$F_{p_1} = \overline{NE}(X) \cap H_1^\perp = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [\ell_2],$$
since $H_1 \cdot (s [\ell_1] + t [\ell_2]) = s$. The curves contracted by $p_1$ are the curves in the fibres, whose classes are multiples of $[\ell_2]$. Similarly $F_{p_2} = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [\ell_1]$, the face of the constant map is $\overline{NE}(X)$, and the face of an isomorphism is $\{0\}$. These are the four faces of the simplicial cone $\overline{NE}(X)$ (Example P7.4.6), and each is the face of a morphism.

### Proof

*Theorem P7.4.9.* Part 1. By part 2 of Proposition P7.3.9, $\delta$ is nef if and only if $\delta \cdot z \geq 0$ for all $z \in \overline{NE}(X)$, so $\operatorname{Nef}(X) = \overline{NE}(X)^\vee$. By part 2 of Proposition P7.4.3, $\operatorname{Nef}(X)^\vee = (\overline{NE}(X)^\vee)^\vee = \overline{NE}(X)$.

Part 2. Both cones are closed, $\overline{NE}(X)$ by definition and $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ by part 1 of Proposition P7.4.3. By Definition P7.2.4, $N_1(X)$ is spanned by the classes of curves, so $NE(X)$ spans $N_1(X)$ and $\overline{NE}(X)$ is full-dimensional; by part 3 of Proposition P7.4.3, its dual $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ is salient. An ample line bundle exists since $X$ is projective, so $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is a nonempty open subset of $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ (parts 1 and 4 of Theorem P7.3.12), $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ is full-dimensional, and its dual $\overline{NE}(X)$ is salient, again by part 3 of Proposition P7.4.3. This is also Corollary P7.3.14.

Part 3. The first statement is part 4 of Theorem P7.3.12. By part 3 of Proposition P7.4.3 with $K = \overline{NE}(X)$, the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(X) = K^\vee$ is the set of classes positive on $\overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$, which is Theorem P7.3.13 again. The same part with $K = \operatorname{Nef}(X)$ and part 1 gives the interior of $\overline{NE}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X)^\vee$.

Part 4. By part 4 of Proposition P7.4.3, $F_\delta$ is a face and it is $\{0\}$ if and only if $\delta$ is in the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$, which is $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ by part 3. A nef class that is not ample is in $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ but not in its interior, so it is on the boundary.

*Proposition P7.4.11.* Part 1. For a curve $C$, $f_* [C]$ is $0$ or a positive integer multiple of the class of the curve $f(C)$, so $f_*$ maps $NE(X)$ into $NE(Y)$, and by continuity of the linear map $f_*$ it maps $\overline{NE}(X)$ into $\overline{NE}(Y)$.

Part 2. For $z \in N_1(X)$, the projection formula of part 2 of Proposition P7.2.7 gives $f^* A \cdot z = A \cdot f_* z$; it is stated for $1$-cycles and passes to classes since both sides are linear in $z$. For $z \in \overline{NE}(X)$, $f_* z \in \overline{NE}(Y)$ by part 1, and $A$ is positive on $\overline{NE}(Y) \setminus \{0\}$ by Theorem P7.3.13. So $f^* A \cdot z = 0$ if and only if $f_* z = 0$. The class $f^* A$ is nef by part 5 of Proposition P7.3.9, so $F_f$ is a face by part 4 of Theorem P7.4.9. The definition of $F_f$ does not involve $A$.

Part 3. If $f(C)$ is a point, $f_* [C] = 0$ by definition, so $[C] \in F_f$. If $f(C)$ is a curve, $f_* [C] = d [f(C)]$ with $d \geq 1$, and $A \cdot f_* [C] = d (A \cdot f(C)) > 0$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14, so $[C] \notin F_f$. The second equivalence is part 2.

Part 4. By part 4 of Theorem P7.4.9 and part 2, $F_f = \{0\}$ if and only if $f^* A$ is ample, and by part 5 of Proposition P7.3.9 this holds if and only if $f$ is finite. If $f$ contracts a curve $C$, then $0 \neq [C] \in F_f$, since an ample line bundle has positive degree on $C$ (part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14), so $f$ is not finite and $f^* A$ is not ample.

Part 5. By part 2 of Proposition P7.2.7, $f_*$ is the transpose of $f^*$ under the perfect pairings, and $(h \circ f)^* = f^* \circ h^*$ on line bundles, so $(h \circ f)_* = h_* \circ f_*$. Hence $f_* z = 0$ implies $(h \circ f)_* z = 0$. In the Stein factorization, $g$ is finite, so $Y'$ is proper over $k$. The line bundle $g^* A$ is ample in the sense of Definition P3.6.22: for a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $Y'$, $g_* \mathcal{F}$ is coherent and $g_* (\mathcal{F} \otimes g^* A^n) \cong g_* \mathcal{F} \otimes A^n$ is generated by global sections for $n \gg 0$, and since $g$ is affine, a sheaf $\mathcal{G}$ on $Y'$ is generated by global sections as soon as $g_* \mathcal{G}$ is, because over an affine open $U \subseteq Y$ the module $\mathcal{G}(g^{-1} U) = (g_* \mathcal{G})(U)$ is the same module over the larger ring. So some power of $g^* A$ is very ample (Hartshorne, Theorem II.7.6, in Har77), and $Y'$, being proper, is projective. By part 2, applied to $f$ with $A$ and to $f'$ with $g^* A$,
$$F_f = \overline{NE}(X) \cap (f^* A)^\perp = \overline{NE}(X) \cap (f'^* g^* A)^\perp = F_{f'}.$$

Part 6. The morphism $\varphi$ given by a basis of $H^0(X, L)$ satisfies $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong L$ by part 2 of Theorem P3.6.21. Apply parts 2 and 3 with $A = \mathcal{O}(1)$, which is ample on $\mathbb{P}^N$.

*Proposition P7.4.12.* By part 1 of Proposition P7.4.11, $f_* NE(Y) \subseteq NE(X)$. Let $C$ be a curve on $X$. By Lemma P7.2.6 there is a curve $C'$ on $Y$ with $f(C') = C$, and $f_* [C'] = d [C]$ with $d = [K(C') : K(C)] \geq 1$, so $[C] = f_*([C'] / d) \in f_* NE(Y)$. So $f_* NE(Y) = NE(X)$. Then $NE(X) \subseteq f_* \overline{NE}(Y) \subseteq \overline{NE}(X)$, by continuity, and the closure of $f_* \overline{NE}(Y)$ is $\overline{NE}(X)$. The last statement is part 3 of Proposition P7.3.9.

*Lemma P7.4.14.* Let $s$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$, which vanishes exactly on $\operatorname{Supp} D$. Since $C \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp} D$, the restriction $s|_C$ is a nonzero section of $L = \mathcal{O}_X(D)|_C$, nonzero at the generic point of the integral curve $C$, so multiplication by $s|_C$ is an injection $\mathcal{O}_C \to L$ whose cokernel $\mathcal{Q}$ has finite support. Then $\chi(L) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_C) = \chi(\mathcal{Q}) = h^0(\mathcal{Q}) \geq 0$. By Theorem P5.3.10, $\chi(L) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_C) = \deg L$, and $\deg L = D \cdot C$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.1.7.

*Proposition P7.4.15.* Part 1. Let $H$ be an ample line bundle. Some power $H^m$ is very ample, so by part 1 of Lemma P7.1.1 it has a nonzero section, whose divisor $D$ is an effective Cartier divisor with $[D] = m [H]$; so $[H] \in \operatorname{Eff}(X)$. A positive combination of classes of ample line bundles is then in $\operatorname{Eff}(X)$, so $\operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Eff}(X)$. Since $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is a nonempty open set, $\operatorname{Eff}(X)$ is full-dimensional. Taking closures and using part 4 of Theorem P7.3.12 gives $\operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$.

Part 2. Let $D$ be an effective Cartier divisor. Since $X$ is integral, $\operatorname{Supp} D$ is a proper closed subset (a local equation of $D$ is a nonzero element of a domain), so some $C \in \Sigma$ is not contained in it, and $D \cdot \gamma = D \cdot C \geq 0$ by Lemma P7.4.14. So $\gamma$, viewed as a functional on $N^1(X)$, is nonnegative on the generators of $\operatorname{Eff}(X)$, hence on $\operatorname{Eff}(X)$ and, by continuity, on its closure.

Part 3. On a smooth surface Weil and Cartier divisors agree (Hartshorne, Proposition II.6.11, in Har77), so an effective Cartier divisor is a sum $\sum_i a_i C_i$ of curves with integers $a_i \geq 0$, and every curve is an effective Cartier divisor. Under the isomorphism of Proposition P7.2.8, which sends $[C]$ to $[\mathcal{O}_S(C)]$, the generators of $NE(S)$ and of $\operatorname{Eff}(S)$ correspond, so $\operatorname{Eff}(S) = NE(S)$ and the closures agree. The duality is part 1 of Theorem P7.4.9, and the inclusion is part 1.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $\pi \colon \tilde X \to X$ be the blow-up of a point of a smooth projective variety of dimension $n \geq 2$, with $e$ a line in the exceptional divisor. Using Proposition P7.2.23, show that $F_\pi = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [e]$ and that $\pi^* \operatorname{Nef}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(\tilde X) \cap [e]^\perp$.
2. (A) Let $D$ be an effective Cartier divisor on a projective variety and $C$ a curve contained in $\operatorname{Supp} D$. Give an example with $D \cdot C < 0$.
3. (B) Let $\delta_1, \delta_2 \in \operatorname{Nef}(X)$. Show that $F_{\delta_1 + \delta_2} = F_{\delta_1} \cap F_{\delta_2}$, and deduce that for morphisms $f_i \colon X \to Y_i$, the face of $(f_1, f_2) \colon X \to Y_1 \times Y_2$ is $F_{f_1} \cap F_{f_2}$.
4. (B) Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a surjective morphism of projective varieties. Show that $f^* \operatorname{Nef}(Y) = \operatorname{Nef}(X) \cap f^* N^1(Y)$ and that every class of $f^* \operatorname{Nef}(Y)$ vanishes on $F_f$.
5. (B) Let $X$ be a projective variety of dimension $n$ and $H$ very ample. Show that the curves $D_1 \cap \dots \cap D_{n-1}$, for general $D_i \in |H|$ with integral intersection, form a set $\Sigma$ as in part 2 of Proposition P7.4.15, and deduce that $(H^{n-1} \cdot \delta) \geq 0$ for every pseudo-effective class $\delta$.
6. (C) Let $X$ be a projective scheme such that $\overline{NE}(X)$ is spanned by finitely many classes of curves. Show that every face of $\overline{NE}(X)$ is cut out by a nef class in $N^1(X)_\mathbb{Q}$. Then show that on $E \times E$, with the round cone of Example P5.12.9, there are boundary rays of $\overline{NE}$ that are cut out by no rational nef class.

### Lean

The project library defines `MinimalModel.nefCone` as the classes of nef $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisors, `MinimalModel.effCone` as the cone spanned by the classes of Cartier divisors with a nonzero section, and `MinimalModel.IntersectionData.coneOfCurves` as $\overline{NE}(X)$, all as subsets of the space of functions on curves. The duality of Theorem P7.4.9 and the face of a morphism are not stated in the library. Mathlib has no intersection numbers on projective schemes, so the statements of this section are not formalized there. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The duality between $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ and $\overline{NE}(X)$ is Kleiman's (Kle66, Chapter IV), and Lazarsfeld, Section 1.4.C (Laz04), states it in the present form. The face of a morphism is the starting point of Mori's program: Mori's cone theorem, Theorem P5.12.13 for the bound on rational curves and Theorem P5.12.14 for its form on surfaces, describes the $K_X$-negative part of $\overline{NE}(X)$, and the contraction theorem constructs, for every $K_X$-negative extremal face $F$, a contraction $f$ with $F_f = F$. That a contraction $f$ is determined up to isomorphism by $F_f$ uses $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y$ and the rigidity of contractions, treated in the volumes on the minimal model program. The pseudo-effective cone and its duality with the cone of movable curves, which refines part 2 of Proposition P7.4.15, is the theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell in Chapter 5.
