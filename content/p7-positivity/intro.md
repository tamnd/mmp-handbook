# Intersection numbers, positivity and vanishing

The minimal model program is run on numerical data. A step of the program contracts the curves on which the canonical class is negative, and the curves to contract are found as extremal rays of a cone of curves, a closed convex cone in a finite-dimensional real vector space. Ampleness, the property that a line bundle embeds some multiple of the variety, is read off from this cone by Kleiman's criterion. The vanishing theorems of Kodaira, Kawamata and Viehweg then turn numerical positivity into vanishing of cohomology, which is what lifts sections and makes the contractions exist. This volume builds that numerical machinery on a projective variety over an algebraically closed field, without a base and without a boundary. Volume 1 takes it up again in the relative and logarithmic setting of the program.

The volume has eleven chapters in three groups. This edition contains Chapters 1 to 7; the other chapters are the plan of the volume and are not yet written.

- **Intersection numbers and ampleness, Chapters 1 to 4.** Chapter 1 defines the intersection number $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{F})$ of line bundles with a coherent sheaf through Snapper's theorem on Euler characteristics, following Kleiman, and proves the cycle formula, the projection formula and asymptotic Riemann-Roch. Chapter 2 passes to numerical equivalence, defines the real vector spaces $N^1(X)$ and $N_1(X)$ and their perfect pairing, and proves the theorem of the base over $\mathbb{C}$. Chapter 3 proves the criteria of Nakai-Moishezon, Kleiman and Seshadri, and gives Mumford's example of a divisor that is positive on every curve and is not ample. Chapter 4 studies the nef cone, the ample cone and the closed cone of curves $\overline{NE}(X)$ as dual convex cones, relates faces of $\overline{NE}(X)$ to morphisms, and computes the cones of products of projective spaces, of the Hirzebruch surfaces and of blow-ups of the plane and of projective space.
- **Bigness and asymptotic invariants, Chapters 5 to 7.** Chapter 5 treats big and pseudo-effective divisors, Kodaira's lemma, and movable curves with the duality theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell. Chapter 6 treats section rings, the Iitaka dimension, the Iitaka fibration and the Kodaira dimension. Chapter 7 treats the volume of a divisor and the stable, augmented and restricted base loci.
- **Vanishing, Chapters 8 to 11.** Chapter 8 proves Kodaira vanishing and Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing through cyclic covers. Chapter 9 proves Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing and Kollár's injectivity and torsion-freeness theorems. Chapter 10 treats multiplier ideals and Nadel vanishing. Chapter 11 treats characteristic $p$: Raynaud's counterexample to Kodaira vanishing, the theorem of Deligne and Illusie, and Frobenius splitting.

The spine is Lazarsfeld's *Positivity in algebraic geometry* (Laz04), Debarre's *Higher-dimensional algebraic geometry*, Chapter 1 (Deb01), and Kleiman's paper *Toward a numerical theory of ampleness* (Kle66), with Kollár and Mori, Chapters 1 and 2 (KM98), for the vanishing chapters. Chapters 1 to 4 follow Lazarsfeld, Chapter 1, and Debarre, Chapter 1, and take the definition of intersection numbers and the proof of Kleiman's theorem from Kleiman's paper. Chapters 5 to 7 follow Lazarsfeld, Chapters 2, 10 and 11, with Nakayama's *Zariski-decomposition and abundance* (Nak04) for the surface case and the $\sigma$-decomposition and the paper of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell (BDPP13) for movable curves. Each section lists in `reads_with` the places of the spine texts that read in parallel.

The volume needs P4 for cohomology, Euler characteristics, Serre's vanishing theorem and the cohomological criterion for ampleness, and P5 Ch. 5 to 8 for the intersection theory of surfaces, blow-ups and the negativity lemma, which give the examples and the surface case of every result here. Chapters 8 to 11 need P6 Ch. 6 for Hodge theory. From P3 the volume uses projective schemes, divisors, ample and very ample line bundles, projective bundles and blow-ups. Throughout, $k$ is an algebraically closed field, of arbitrary characteristic unless the text says otherwise, and a *variety* is an integral separated scheme of finite type over $k$. A *curve* on a scheme $X$ is an integral closed subscheme of dimension $1$. Line bundles and invertible sheaves are the same thing, and the group law on them is written additively or multiplicatively as convenient.
