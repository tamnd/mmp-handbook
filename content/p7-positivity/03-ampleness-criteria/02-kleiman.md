## Nef classes and Kleiman's criterion

### Statement

Throughout, $X$ is a projective scheme over $k$, $N^1(X)$ and $N_1(X)$ are the finite-dimensional spaces of Chapter 2 (Theorem P7.2.12), with their Euclidean topology, and a subvariety is an integral closed subscheme.

**Definition P7.3.8 (real intersection numbers, nef classes and the cones).**

1. Let $\delta_1, \dots, \delta_r \in N^1(X)$ and $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent sheaf with $\dim \operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F} \leq r$. The intersection number $(\delta_1 \cdots \delta_r \cdot \mathcal{F})$ is the value at $(\delta_1, \dots, \delta_r)$ of the unique symmetric $\mathbb{R}$-multilinear form on $N^1(X)$ that takes the value $(L_1 \cdots L_r \cdot \mathcal{F})$ on classes of line bundles. It exists because intersection numbers are symmetric and multilinear (parts 1 and 4 of Theorem P7.1.6), depend only on numerical classes (Proposition P7.2.2), and $N^1(X) = N^1(X)_\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{R}$ (Theorem P7.2.12). It is a polynomial, hence continuous, function of the $\delta_i$. For an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor $D$ write $(D^r \cdot V) = ([D]^r \cdot \mathcal{O}_V)$.
2. A class $\delta \in N^1(X)$ is *nef* if $\delta \cdot [C] \geq 0$ for every curve $C$ on $X$. A line bundle or an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor is nef if its class is. The set of nef classes is the *nef cone* $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$.
3. The *ample cone* $\operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_i a_i [H_i]$ with $a_i > 0$ real and $H_i$ ample line bundles. A class in $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is *ample*, and so is an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor whose class is ample.
4. The *cone of curves* $NE(X) \subseteq N_1(X)$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_j b_j [C_j]$ with $b_j \geq 0$ real and $C_j$ curves, and its closure $\overline{NE}(X)$ is the *closed cone of curves*.

**Proposition P7.3.9 (first properties of nef classes).**

1. Ample line bundles and line bundles generated by global sections are nef, and $\operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Nef}(X)$.
2. $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ is a closed convex cone, and $\delta$ is nef if and only if $\delta \cdot z \geq 0$ for every $z \in \overline{NE}(X)$.
3. Let $f \colon Y \to X$ be a morphism of projective schemes. If $\delta$ is nef, so is $f^* \delta$. If $f$ is surjective and $f^* \delta$ is nef, then $\delta$ is nef.
4. A class is nef if and only if its restriction to every irreducible component of $X$ is nef.
5. Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a morphism of projective schemes and $A$ an ample line bundle on $Y$. Then $f^* A$ is nef, and it is ample if and only if $f$ is finite.

Lean: `MinimalModel.IsNef`, in the project library, is the condition of part 2 of Definition P7.3.8 for an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor and an `IntersectionData`.

**Lemma P7.3.10 (two tools).** Let $H$ be an ample line bundle on $X$.

1. For every norm $\| \cdot \|$ on $N^1(X)$ there is $c > 0$ with $|\delta \cdot [C]| \leq c \, \| \delta \| \, (H \cdot C)$ for every $\delta \in N^1(X)$ and every curve $C$, and then $|\delta \cdot z| \leq c \, \| \delta \| \, (H \cdot z)$ for every $z \in \overline{NE}(X)$.
2. Let $V \subseteq X$ be a closed subscheme of dimension at most $r \geq 1$ and $A_1, \dots, A_{r-1}$ very ample line bundles. There is a $1$-cycle $\gamma = \sum_j b_j C_j$ with integers $b_j \geq 0$ such that $(A_1 \cdots A_{r-1} \cdot \delta \cdot V) = \delta \cdot [\gamma]$ for every $\delta \in N^1(X)$. In particular $(A_1 \cdots A_{r-1} \cdot \delta \cdot V) \geq 0$ if $\delta$ is nef.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.3.11 (Kleiman).** Let $L$ be a nef line bundle on $X$.

1. $(L^k \cdot V) \geq 0$ for every subvariety $V \subseteq X$ of dimension $k$.
2. If $A$ is ample, then $L^a \otimes A^b$ is ample for all integers $a \geq 0$ and $b \geq 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.3.12 (the ample cone).**

1. $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is an open convex cone in $N^1(X)$.
2. A line bundle $L$ is ample if and only if $[L] \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$. So an ample $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor with integral coefficients is an ample Cartier divisor, and the two meanings of ample agree.
3. If $\delta$ is nef and $\alpha \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$, then $\delta + \alpha \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$.
4. $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$, and $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ is the closure of $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$.
5. For every nef class $\delta$ and every subvariety $V$ of dimension $k$, $(\delta^k \cdot V) \geq 0$, and $(\alpha^k \cdot V) > 0$ if $\alpha$ is ample.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.3.13 (Kleiman's criterion).** A class $\delta \in N^1(X)$ is ample if and only if $\delta \cdot z > 0$ for every $z \in \overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$. In particular a line bundle $L$ is ample if and only if $L$ is positive on $\overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$.

Lean: `MinimalModel.KleimanCriterion`, in the project library, states this for an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor on a normal projective variety, with `MinimalModel.IntersectionData.coneOfCurves` for $\overline{NE}(X)$.

**Corollary P7.3.14 (the closed cone of curves is salient).** Let $h \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$.

1. $h \cdot z > 0$ for every $z \in \overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$, and $\overline{NE}(X) \cap (-\overline{NE}(X)) = \{0\}$.
2. The slice $\{ z \in \overline{NE}(X) : h \cdot z = 1 \}$ is compact, and every nonzero $z \in \overline{NE}(X)$ is a positive multiple of a point of it.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Kleiman's theorem is proved by induction on the dimension with the polynomial $P(t) = ((L + tH)^n)$ for a very ample $H$. On proper subvarieties induction gives nonnegativity, so by the Nakai-Moishezon criterion $L + tH$ is ample for rational $t > 0$ exactly when $P(t) > 0$. Splitting $P(t)$ as $((L + tH)^{n-1} \cdot L) + t((L + tH)^{n-1} \cdot H)$, the first term is nonnegative whenever $L + tH$ is ample, because an ample class cut down to a curve pairs nonnegatively with a nef class, and the second term is positive. So $P$ cannot vanish on the positive axis, and letting $t$ tend to $0$ gives $(L^n) \geq 0$.

The ample cone is open because a small perturbation of an ample bundle in any direction of a basis of $N^1(X)$ stays ample, by Serre's theorem. A nef class plus an ample class is ample: for rational classes this is Kleiman's theorem, and real classes are approximated by rational ones, using a bound of all curve degrees by the degree for $H$. The interior of the nef cone is then the ample cone, and Kleiman's criterion follows from compactness of the unit sphere: a class positive on $\overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$ stays nef after subtracting a small ample class.

### Examples

**Example P7.3.15 (products of projective spaces).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^a \times \mathbb{P}^b$, with $H_1$, $H_2$, $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$ as in Proposition P7.2.21. Every curve class is $(H_1 \cdot C)[\ell_1] + (H_2 \cdot C)[\ell_2]$ with nonnegative coefficients, and $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$ are curves, so $NE(X) = \overline{NE}(X) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [\ell_1] + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} [\ell_2]$. A class $x H_1 + y H_2$ is nef if and only if $x, y \geq 0$, and by Theorem P7.3.13 it is ample if and only if $x, y > 0$. So $\mathcal{O}(a', b') = p_1^* \mathcal{O}(a') \otimes p_2^* \mathcal{O}(b')$ is ample exactly when $a', b' > 0$ and nef exactly when $a', b' \geq 0$. The nef classes on the boundary, $H_1$ and $H_2$, are the pullbacks of ample bundles by the two projections, as part 5 of Proposition P7.3.9 predicts. For $a = b = 1$ this is Example P3.10.34.

**Example P7.3.16 (the blow-up of projective space at a point).** Let $\tilde X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^n$ with $H$, $E$, $e$, $\tilde\ell_p$ as in Example P7.2.25. By that example every curve class is a nonnegative combination of $[e]$ and $[\tilde\ell_p]$, so $NE(\tilde X) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[e] + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\tilde\ell_p]$, which is closed. For $\delta = xH - yE$,
$$\delta \cdot e = y, \qquad \delta \cdot \tilde\ell_p = x - y.$$
So $\delta$ is nef if and only if $0 \leq y \leq x$, and ample if and only if $0 < y < x$. The nef cone is spanned by $H$ and $H - E$. Both are nef and not ample: $H$ is the pullback of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ under the birational morphism $\pi$, which contracts $E$, and $H - E$ is the pullback of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ under the projection $q \colon \tilde X \to \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ from $p$, which contracts the lines $\tilde\ell_p$. For instance $2H - E$ is ample. For $n = 2$ this is Example P5.12.7.

**Example P7.3.17 (the Hirzebruch surfaces).** On $\mathbb{F}_n$ with $n \geq 1$, every curve class is $(F \cdot C)[C_-] + (C_+ \cdot C)[F]$ with nonnegative coefficients (Proposition P7.2.24), so $NE(\mathbb{F}_n) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[C_-] + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[F]$, and by the duality of Proposition P7.2.24 the nef cone is spanned by $F$ and $C_+$. The fibre class $F$ is nef with $F^2 = 0$, the pullback of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ from $\mathbb{P}^1$; the class $C_+$ is nef, being generated by global sections (proof of Proposition P7.2.24), and $C_+ \cdot C_- = 0$, so it is not ample. A class $aC_- + bF$ is ample if and only if $a > 0$ and $b > na$, which is Example P5.12.8. For $n = 0$ the same computation gives Example P7.3.15 with $a = b = 1$.

### Proof

*Proposition P7.3.9.* Part 1 is parts 1 and 2 of Proposition P7.1.14 for curves, and $\operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Nef}(X)$ follows by linearity. Part 2: $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ is an intersection of closed half-spaces $\{ \delta : \delta \cdot [C] \geq 0 \}$. If $\delta$ is nef, then $\delta \geq 0$ on $NE(X)$, hence on its closure by continuity, and the converse is clear. Part 3: by part 2 of Proposition P7.2.7, $f^* \delta \cdot [C'] = \delta \cdot f_*[C']$, and $f_*[C']$ is $0$ or a positive multiple of $[f(C')]$. If $f$ is surjective, every curve $C$ on $X$ is $f(C')$ for a curve $C'$ on $Y$ (Lemma P7.2.6), and $f_*[C'] = d [C]$ with $d \geq 1$. Part 4: every curve on $X$ lies in an irreducible component. Part 5: $f^* A$ is nef by parts 1 and 3. If $f$ is finite, $f^* A$ is ample by part 2 of Corollary P4.2.11. If $f$ is not finite, then some fibre is infinite by Corollary P4.6.17, as $f$ is projective, and contains a curve $C$ with $f(C)$ a point; then $f^* A \cdot C = 0$ by Theorem P7.1.15, and $f^* A$ is not ample by part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14. $\square$

*Lemma P7.3.10.* Part 1. By part 2 of Proposition P7.2.5, choose line bundles $L_1, \dots, L_\rho$ whose classes form a basis of $N^1(X)$. By part 2 of Proposition P3.10.27, there is $m$ such that $H^m \otimes L_j$ and $H^m \otimes L_j^{-1}$ are very ample, hence ample, for every $j$. By part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14, $m (H \cdot C) \pm (L_j \cdot C) > 0$, so $|L_j \cdot C| \leq m (H \cdot C)$ for every curve. For $\delta = \sum_j x_j [L_j]$, $|\delta \cdot [C]| \leq m (\sum_j |x_j|) (H \cdot C)$, and $\sum_j |x_j|$ is a norm, bounded by a multiple of $\| \delta \|$. The inequality passes to nonnegative combinations of curves and to limits.

Part 2. Argue by induction on $r$. For $r = 1$, take $\gamma = [\mathcal{O}_V]_1$, the cycle of Definition P7.1.12, which has nonnegative coefficients; then $(\delta \cdot V) = \delta \cdot [\gamma]$ by Theorem P7.1.13 for line bundles, hence for all classes by linearity. For $r \geq 2$, by part 1 of Lemma P7.1.1 choose $\sigma \in H^0(A_1)$ that does not vanish at the finitely many associated points of $\mathcal{O}_V$. By part 6 of Theorem P7.1.6 and Theorem P7.1.13, $(A_1 \cdots A_{r-1} \cdot \delta \cdot V) = (A_2 \cdots A_{r-1} \cdot \delta \cdot (\mathcal{O}_V)_\sigma) = \sum_W m_W (A_2 \cdots A_{r-1} \cdot \delta \cdot W)$, a sum with integers $m_W \geq 0$ over subvarieties $W$ of dimension $r - 1$. Apply induction to each $W$. $\square$

*Theorem P7.3.11.* Part 1. The restriction of $L$ to a subvariety $V$ is nef, and $(L^k \cdot V)$ is computed on $V$ by part 7 of Theorem P7.1.6. So it suffices to show $(L^n) \geq 0$ when $X$ is integral of dimension $n$, assuming by induction on $n$ that $(L^k \cdot W) \geq 0$ for every subvariety $W \subseteq X$ of dimension $k < n$. For $n = 1$ this is the definition. Let $n \geq 2$, fix a very ample line bundle $H$ on $X$, and for real $t$ put
$$P(t) = ((L + tH)^n) = \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} t^i \, (L^{n-i} \cdot H^i),$$
with the real intersection numbers of Definition P7.3.8.

First, $(L^{k-i} \cdot H^i \cdot W) \geq 0$ for every subvariety $W$ of dimension $k < n$ and $0 \leq i \leq k$, with strict inequality for $i = k$. For $i = 0$ this is the induction hypothesis, and for $i = k$ it is part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14. For $0 < i < k$, cutting $W$ by $i$ sections of $H$ as in the proof of part 2 of Lemma P7.3.10 writes $(L^{k-i} \cdot H^i \cdot W)$ as a sum with nonnegative coefficients of numbers $(L^{k-i} \cdot W')$ with $\dim W' = k - i$, which are nonnegative by induction. The same cutting applied to $X$ with one section of $H$ shows that $(L^{n-1-i} \cdot H^{i+1}) \geq 0$ for $0 \leq i \leq n - 1$, with strict inequality for $i = n - 1$.

Second, let $t = p/q > 0$ be rational with $P(t) > 0$. Then $M = L^q \otimes H^p$ is ample. Indeed $(M^k \cdot W) = q^k ((L + tH)^k \cdot W) > 0$ for every subvariety $W \neq X$ of dimension $k \geq 1$ by the first step, and $(M^n) = q^n P(t) > 0$, so Theorem P7.3.2 applies.

Third, write $P(t) = Q(t) + t R(t)$ with
$$Q(t) = ((L + tH)^{n-1} \cdot L), \qquad R(t) = ((L + tH)^{n-1} \cdot H) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \binom{n-1}{i} t^i (L^{n-1-i} \cdot H^{i+1}).$$
For $t > 0$, $R(t) \geq t^{n-1} (H^n) > 0$ by the first step. If $t = p/q > 0$ is rational with $P(t) > 0$, then $M$ is ample, $M^s$ is very ample for some $s \geq 1$ (Theorem P3.6.25), and $Q(t) = (sq)^{-(n-1)} ((M^s)^{n-1} \cdot L) \geq 0$ by part 2 of Lemma P7.3.10, as $L$ is nef.

Now $P$ is a polynomial with leading coefficient $(H^n) > 0$, so it has a largest real root or none. Suppose it has a largest real root $t_0 > 0$. Then $P(t) > 0$ for $t > t_0$, so $Q(t) \geq 0$ for rational $t > t_0$, and by continuity $Q(t_0) \geq 0$. Hence $P(t_0) = Q(t_0) + t_0 R(t_0) > 0$, a contradiction. So $P(t) > 0$ for every $t > 0$, and $(L^n) = P(0) = \lim_{t \to 0^+} P(t) \geq 0$. This proves part 1.

Part 2. The second step, now valid for every rational $t > 0$, shows that $L^q \otimes H^p$ is ample for all integers $p, q \geq 1$, for every very ample $H$. Let $A$ be ample, and choose $s$ with $A^s$ very ample (Theorem P3.6.25). For $a \geq 1$ and $b \geq 1$, $(L^a \otimes A^b)^s = L^{as} \otimes (A^s)^b$ is ample, so $L^a \otimes A^b$ is ample by part 1 of Proposition P3.6.24. For $a = 0$, $A^b$ is ample. $\square$

*Theorem P7.3.12.* Part 1. $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is a convex cone by definition. Let $H$ be ample and $L_1, \dots, L_\rho$ line bundles whose classes form a basis. As in the proof of Lemma P7.3.10 there is $m$ with $H^m \otimes L_j^{\pm 1}$ ample for all $j$, so the $2\rho$ points $[H] \pm \frac{1}{m}[L_j]$ lie in $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$. Their convex hull lies in $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ and contains an open neighbourhood $U$ of $[H]$. An element of $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is $a_1 [H_1] + \beta$ with $a_1 > 0$ and $\beta \in \operatorname{Amp}(X) \cup \{0\}$, and applying this to $H = H_1$ gives the open neighbourhood $a_1 U + \beta$ inside $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$.

Part 2 and part 5 for ample classes. Let $\alpha = \sum_i a_i [H_i]$ with $a_i > 0$ and $H_i$ ample. For a subvariety $V$ of dimension $k$, multilinearity expands $(\alpha^k \cdot V)$ as a sum, with positive coefficients, of numbers $(H_{i_1} \cdots H_{i_k} \cdot V)$, which are positive by part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14. So $(\alpha^k \cdot V) > 0$. If $[L] = \alpha$, then $(L^k \cdot V) > 0$ for all $V$, and $L$ is ample by Theorem P7.3.2. The converse is the definition.

Part 3. Let $h = [H]$ with $H$ ample and $c$ as in part 1 of Lemma P7.3.10. As $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is open, $\alpha - \varepsilon h \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$ for some rational $\varepsilon > 0$, and $\delta + \alpha = (\delta + \varepsilon h) + (\alpha - \varepsilon h)$, so it suffices to show $\delta + \varepsilon h \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$. Choose $r > 0$ such that the ball of radius $r$ around $\frac{\varepsilon}{2} h$ lies in $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$. Rational classes are dense (Definition P7.2.15), so choose a rational class $\delta'$ with $\| \delta - \delta' \| < \min(r, \frac{\varepsilon}{4c})$. For every curve $C$,
$$(\delta' + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{4} h) \cdot [C] \geq \delta \cdot [C] - c \| \delta - \delta' \| (H \cdot C) + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{4} (H \cdot C) \geq 0,$$
so $\nu = \delta' + \frac{\varepsilon}{4} h$ is a rational nef class. By part 3 of Corollary P7.2.16 there is an integer $q \geq 1$ with $q \nu = [L]$ for a line bundle $L$, which is nef, and with $q \varepsilon / 4$ an integer. By part 2 of Theorem P7.3.11, $L \otimes H^{q\varepsilon/4}$ is ample, so $\nu + \frac{\varepsilon}{4} h \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$. Finally
$$\delta + \varepsilon h = (\nu + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{4} h) + \bigl( (\delta - \delta') + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2} h \bigr),$$
and the second summand lies in the ball of radius $r$ around $\frac{\varepsilon}{2} h$. So $\delta + \varepsilon h$ is a sum of two elements of $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$.

Part 4. $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is open and contained in $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ (part 1 of Proposition P7.3.9), so it lies in the interior. If $\delta$ is in the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$, then $\delta - \varepsilon h$ is nef for small $\varepsilon > 0$, and $\delta = (\delta - \varepsilon h) + \varepsilon h \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$ by part 3. Every nef $\delta$ is the limit of $\delta + \varepsilon h \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$, and $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ is closed, so it is the closure of $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$.

Part 5 for nef classes. A nef $\delta$ is the limit of the ample classes $\delta + \varepsilon h$, for which $((\delta + \varepsilon h)^k \cdot V) > 0$, and the intersection number is continuous. $\square$

*Theorem P7.3.13.* Let $\delta$ be ample. It is nef, so $\delta \geq 0$ on $\overline{NE}(X)$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.3.9. Suppose $\delta \cdot z = 0$ for some $z \in \overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$. The pairing is perfect (Theorem P7.2.12), so there is $w \in N^1(X)$ with $w \cdot z = 1$. As $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ is open, $\delta - \varepsilon w$ is ample, hence nef, for small $\varepsilon > 0$, and $(\delta - \varepsilon w) \cdot z = -\varepsilon < 0$, a contradiction.

Conversely, let $\delta$ be positive on $\overline{NE}(X) \setminus \{0\}$, and fix a norm on $N_1(X)$ and an ample class $h$. If $\overline{NE}(X) = \{0\}$, then $X$ has no curves, so $N_1(X) = 0$, $N^1(X) = 0$, and $\delta = 0$ is the class of the ample bundle $\mathcal{O}_X$ of the finite scheme $X$. Otherwise $K = \{ z \in \overline{NE}(X) : \| z \| = 1 \}$ is compact and nonempty, $\delta$ has a minimum $\mu > 0$ on $K$, and $h$ has a maximum $M$ on $K$. Then $\delta - \frac{\mu}{M} h \geq 0$ on $K$, hence on the cone $\overline{NE}(X)$, so it is nef by part 2 of Proposition P7.3.9, and $\delta = (\delta - \frac{\mu}{M} h) + \frac{\mu}{M} h$ is ample by part 3 of Theorem P7.3.12. The statement for a line bundle follows from part 2 of Theorem P7.3.12. $\square$

*Corollary P7.3.14.* Part 1. The first statement is Theorem P7.3.13 applied to $h$. If $z$ and $-z$ both lie in $\overline{NE}(X)$, then $h \cdot z \geq 0$ and $h \cdot (-z) \geq 0$, so $h \cdot z = 0$ and $z = 0$. Part 2. With $K$ as in the proof of Theorem P7.3.13, $h$ has a minimum $\mu > 0$ and a maximum $M$ on $K$, so $\mu \| z \| \leq h \cdot z \leq M \| z \|$ on $\overline{NE}(X)$. The slice is closed and contained in the ball of radius $1/\mu$, hence compact, and $z \neq 0$ is $(h \cdot z)$ times the point $z / (h \cdot z)$ of the slice. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) On $\mathbb{F}_n$, find all classes $aC_- + bF$ that are nef and not ample, and for each one describe a morphism that it defines, using Example P7.3.17.
2. (A) Let $X$ be a projective scheme, $L$ a nef line bundle and $A$ ample. Show that $\chi(X, L^m \otimes A)$ is a polynomial in $m$ of degree at most $\dim X$ whose leading coefficient is nonnegative.
3. (B) Let $f \colon Y \to X$ be a surjective morphism of projective schemes. Show that $f^* \colon N^1(X) \to N^1(Y)$ maps $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$ onto $\operatorname{Nef}(Y) \cap f^* N^1(X)$, and that if $f$ is finite it maps $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ onto $\operatorname{Amp}(Y) \cap f^* N^1(X)$.
4. (B) Let $X$ be a projective variety of dimension $n$, $L$ nef and $H$ ample. Show that $(L^{n-i} \cdot H^i) \geq 0$ for all $i$, and that $L$ is numerically trivial if $(L \cdot H^{n-1}) = 0$. (For the second part, use the Hodge index theorem on a surface cut out by $H$, Theorem P5.5.22.)
5. (B) Show that Theorem P7.3.13 can be stated as the equality of $\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ with the interior of the dual cone of $\overline{NE}(X)$, and deduce that $\overline{NE}(X)$ has nonempty interior in $N_1(X)$ if and only if it spans $N_1(X)$, which it always does.
6. (C) Let $X$ be a complete scheme over $k$, not necessarily projective, that carries a nonzero effective $1$-cycle $\gamma = \sum_j b_j C_j$ with $(L \cdot \gamma) = 0$ for every line bundle $L$. Show that $X$ is not projective. Hironaka's smooth complete threefold that is not projective (Hartshorne, Appendix B, Example 3.4.1) is of this kind; read the construction and find such a cycle.

### Lean

The project library defines `MinimalModel.IsNef I D` for an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $D$ and an `IntersectionData` $I$ as $0 \leq I.\mathrm{dot}\, D\, C$ for every curve $C$, which is part 2 of Definition P7.3.8, and `MinimalModel.IntersectionData.coneOfCurves` as the closure of the cone spanned by the curve classes in `MinimalModel.IntersectionData.N₁`, which is part 4. `MinimalModel.KleimanCriterion` states Theorem P7.3.13 for $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisors on a normal projective variety, as an assumption used by the library rather than a proved theorem. Kleiman's theorem P7.3.11, the openness of the ample cone and the identification with the interior of the nef cone are not in the library. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The theorem and the criterion are due to Kleiman (Kle66), who worked with complete schemes under hypotheses that hold for projective schemes. Without projectivity the criterion can fail: Fujino (2005) gave complete toric varieties, not projective, on which it fails.
