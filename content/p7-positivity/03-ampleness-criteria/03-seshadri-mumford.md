## Seshadri's criterion and Mumford's example

### Statement

Throughout, $X$ is a smooth projective variety of dimension $n \geq 2$. For a closed point $x \in X$ let $\pi_x \colon \tilde X_x \to X$ be the blow-up of $x$, with exceptional divisor $E_x \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, as in Proposition P7.1.23. For a curve $C \subseteq X$ through $x$, its strict transform $\tilde C = \operatorname{Bl}_x C \subseteq \tilde X_x$ (Definition P3.11.11) is a curve, not contained in $E_x$, that $\pi_x$ maps birationally onto $C$ (Theorem P3.11.3).

**Definition P7.3.18 (Seshadri constants).** Let $x \in X$ be a closed point.

1. For a curve $C$ through $x$, the *multiplicity* of $C$ at $x$ is $m_x(C) = E_x \cdot \tilde C$.
2. For a nef line bundle or nef class $L$ on $X$, the *Seshadri constant* of $L$ at $x$ is
$$\varepsilon(L; x) = \sup \{ t \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} : \pi_x^* L - t E_x \text{ is nef} \},$$
and the *global Seshadri constant* is $\varepsilon(L) = \inf_x \varepsilon(L; x)$, over all closed points.

**Proposition P7.3.19 (Seshadri constants through curves).** Let $L$ be nef and $x$ a closed point.

1. $m_x(C)$ is a positive integer for every curve $C$ through $x$. If $X$ is a surface, it is the multiplicity of Definition P5.6.1.
2. $\varepsilon(L; x) = \inf \{ (L \cdot C) / m_x(C) : C \text{ a curve through } x \}$, and $\pi_x^* L - t E_x$ is nef exactly for $0 \leq t \leq \varepsilon(L; x)$.
3. $\varepsilon(L; x)^n \leq (L^n)$. In particular $\varepsilon(L; x)$ is finite.
4. For nef $L$, $L'$ and real $a > 0$, $\varepsilon(aL; x) = a \, \varepsilon(L; x)$ and $\varepsilon(L + L'; x) \geq \varepsilon(L; x) + \varepsilon(L'; x)$.
5. If $L$ is very ample, then $\pi_x^* L - E_x$ is generated by global sections, and $\varepsilon(L; x) \geq 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.3.20 (Seshadri's criterion).** Let $L$ be a line bundle on $X$.

1. If $L$ is ample, then $\varepsilon(L) > 0$: there is $\varepsilon > 0$ with $(L \cdot C) \geq \varepsilon \, m_x(C)$ for every closed point $x$ and every curve $C$ through $x$.
2. Conversely, suppose there is $\varepsilon > 0$ with $(L \cdot C) \geq \varepsilon \, m_x(C)$ for every closed point $x$ and every curve $C$ through $x$. Then $L$ is positive on every curve and $(L^n) \geq \varepsilon^n > 0$. If $n = 2$, then $L$ is ample.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.3.21 (positive on curves, zero square).** Let $S$ be a smooth projective surface and $D$ a divisor with $D \cdot C > 0$ for every curve $C$ and $D^2 = 0$. Then:

1. $D$ is nef and not ample, and its class lies on the boundary of $\operatorname{Nef}(S)$.
2. $D \cdot z > 0$ for every $z \in NE(S) \setminus \{0\}$, while $[D] \in \overline{NE}(S) \setminus NE(S)$ and $D \cdot [D] = 0$, under the identification $N_1(S) = N^1(S)$ of Proposition P7.2.8.
3. $NE(S)$ is not closed, and Theorem P7.3.13 fails with $NE(S)$ in place of $\overline{NE}(S)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The Seshadri constant measures how far $\pi_x^* L$ can be pushed into the negative direction $-E_x$ while staying nef. Curves on the blow-up are either in $E_x$, where $-E_x$ is positive, or strict transforms of curves through $x$, on which $\pi_x^* L - t E_x$ has degree $(L \cdot C) - t \, m_x(C)$. This gives the description through curves. Kleiman's theorem on $\tilde X_x$ gives $((\pi_x^* L - t E_x)^n) = (L^n) - t^n \geq 0$, so the constant is bounded by the volume.

For an ample $L$, a very ample multiple separates $x$ from the other points to first order, so $\pi_x^* (mL) - E_x$ is generated by sections and $\varepsilon(L; x) \geq 1/m$ for every $x$. Conversely, a uniform lower bound makes $L$ positive on curves and gives $(L^n) \geq \varepsilon^n$, which on a surface is exactly what the Nakai-Moishezon criterion asks for. In higher dimension the same argument on every subvariety is needed, and it requires blow-ups of singular varieties, so the converse is cited for $n \geq 3$.

Mumford's example shows that positivity on every curve does not suffice, even on a surface: there is a ruled surface with a divisor that is positive on every curve and has self-intersection $0$. Such a divisor is positive on $NE(S)$ and vanishes on its own class, which lies in the closure. The Seshadri constants of this divisor have infimum $0$.

### Examples

**Example P7.3.22 (projective space).** On $\mathbb{P}^n$, $\varepsilon(\mathcal{O}(d); x) = d$ for every $x$ and every $d \geq 1$. Indeed $\varepsilon(\mathcal{O}(1); x) \geq 1$ by part 5 of Proposition P7.3.19, and a line $\ell$ through $x$ has $\mathcal{O}(1) \cdot \ell = 1$ and $m_x(\ell) = E \cdot \tilde\ell_p = 1$ by Example P7.2.25, so equality holds by part 2. Part 4 gives the value $d$ for $\mathcal{O}(d)$, and $\varepsilon^n = d^n = (\mathcal{O}(d)^n)$, so the bound of part 3 is attained.

**Example P7.3.23 (the quadric surface).** On $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ let $a, b \geq 1$. Then $\varepsilon(\mathcal{O}(a, b); x) = \min(a, b)$ for every $x$. Let $c = \min(a, b)$. The bundle $\mathcal{O}(1, 1)$ is very ample (Example P3.10.34), so $\varepsilon(\mathcal{O}(1, 1); x) \geq 1$, and $\mathcal{O}(a - c, b - c)$ is nef by Example P7.3.15, with Seshadri constant at least $0$. By part 4 of Proposition P7.3.19, $\varepsilon(\mathcal{O}(a, b); x) \geq c$. The two fibres through $x$ are smooth curves, so their multiplicity at $x$ is $1$ (part 1 of Proposition P7.3.19 and Definition P5.6.1), and $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ has degrees $a$ and $b$ on them. So $\varepsilon(\mathcal{O}(a, b); x) \leq c$. Here $\varepsilon^2 = c^2 < 2ab = (\mathcal{O}(a,b)^2)$, so the bound of part 3 is strict.

**Example P7.3.24 (Mumford's example).** Let $k = \mathbb{C}$ and let $C$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g \geq 2$. Mumford showed that there is a locally free sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ of rank $2$ and degree $0$ on $C$ such that for every $d \geq 1$ every invertible subsheaf of $\operatorname{Sym}^d \mathcal{E}$ has negative degree; the sheaf comes from a representation of the fundamental group of $C$ in $SU(2)$ with dense image, and the property is the stability of all the symmetric powers (Hartshorne, *Ample subvarieties*, Chapter I, Example 10.6, in Har70; Lazarsfeld, Example 1.5.2). Let $X = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$, with $\pi \colon X \to C$, fibre class $f$ and $\xi = \mathcal{O}_X(1)$. Then:

1. $\xi^2 = \deg \mathcal{E} = 0$, by part 2 of Theorem P5.9.4.
2. $\xi \cdot C' > 0$ for every curve $C' \subseteq X$. If $C'$ is a fibre, $\xi \cdot C' = 1$. Otherwise $\pi(C') = C$, and $d = C' \cdot f \geq 1$ by Proposition P5.5.5, since $C'$ meets every fibre. By part 1 of Theorem P5.9.4, $\mathcal{O}_X(C') \cong \mathcal{O}_X(d) \otimes \pi^* \mathcal{M}$ for an invertible $\mathcal{M}$ on $C$. Its canonical section is a nonzero element of $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(d) \otimes \pi^* \mathcal{M}) = H^0(C, \operatorname{Sym}^d \mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{M})$, by part 1 of Proposition P5.9.3 and the projection formula. So there is a nonzero map $\mathcal{M}^{-1} \to \operatorname{Sym}^d \mathcal{E}$, which is injective because $C$ is a smooth curve, and its image is an invertible subsheaf of degree $-\deg \mathcal{M}$. Hence $\deg \mathcal{M} > 0$. By part 2 of Theorem P5.9.4, $\pi^* \mathcal{M} \equiv (\deg \mathcal{M}) f$, so $\xi \cdot C' = d \, \xi^2 + \deg \mathcal{M} = \deg \mathcal{M} > 0$.
3. By Proposition P7.3.21, $\xi$ is nef and not ample, $NE(X)$ is not closed, and $[\xi] \in \overline{NE}(X) \setminus NE(X)$. As $\xi^2 = 0$, part 2 of Theorem P7.3.20 shows that no $\varepsilon > 0$ bounds $(\xi \cdot C)/m_x(C)$ from below, so $\varepsilon(\xi) = 0$.

The surface $X$ has Picard number $2$ and $N^1(X) = \mathbb{R} \xi \oplus \mathbb{R} f$, with $\xi^2 = f^2 = 0$ and $\xi \cdot f = 1$. Exercise 5 shows that $\overline{NE}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \xi + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} f$, while $NE(X)$ contains the ray of $f$ and not the ray of $\xi$.

### Proof

*Proposition P7.3.19.* Write $\pi = \pi_x$ and $E = E_x$. Part 1. The curve $\tilde C$ is not contained in $E$, and $\pi(\tilde C) = C$ contains $x$, so $\tilde C$ meets $E$. The local equation of $E$ is a nonzerodivisor on $\mathcal{O}_{\tilde C}$, as $\tilde C$ is integral and not contained in $E$, so by part 6 of Theorem P7.1.6 and part 1 of Proposition P7.1.7, $E \cdot \tilde C$ is the length of the nonempty finite scheme $\tilde C \cap E$, a positive integer. On a surface, $E \cdot \tilde C = m_x(C)$ in the sense of Definition P5.6.1 by part 3 of Proposition P5.6.4.

Part 2. The curves on $\tilde X$ are the curves in $E$ and the strict transforms $\tilde C$ of curves $C$ on $X$; a curve $C'$ not in $E$ is the strict transform of $\pi(C')$, as $\pi$ is an isomorphism off $E$. A curve in $E$ of degree $d$ has class $d[e]$ by part 3 of Proposition P7.2.23, and $(\pi^* L - tE) \cdot e = t$ by part 6 of Proposition P7.1.23, so it is nonnegative for $t \geq 0$. For a curve $C$ not through $x$, $E \cdot \tilde C = 0$ by part 6 of Proposition P7.1.23, and $\pi^* L \cdot \tilde C = L \cdot C \geq 0$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.2.7. For a curve $C$ through $x$, $(\pi^* L - tE) \cdot \tilde C = (L \cdot C) - t \, m_x(C)$. So, for $t \geq 0$, $\pi^* L - tE$ is nef if and only if $t \leq (L \cdot C)/m_x(C)$ for every curve $C$ through $x$, which proves both statements.

Part 3. Let $0 \leq t \leq \varepsilon(L; x)$ be rational, $t = b/q$, and first let $L$ be a line bundle. The class $\pi^* L - tE$ is nef by part 2, so by part 5 of Theorem P7.3.12 and part 5 of Proposition P7.1.23 applied to $L^q$,
$$0 \leq q^n ((\pi^* L - tE)^n) = ((\pi^* L^q - bE)^n) = q^n (L^n) - b^n.$$
So $t^n \leq (L^n)$, and letting $t$ tend to $\varepsilon(L; x)$ gives the claim. For a nef class $L$, the same computation holds with real intersection numbers, by multilinearity and parts 2 to 4 of Proposition P7.1.23.

Part 4 follows from part 2, since $(aL + L') \cdot C / m_x(C) = a (L \cdot C)/m_x(C) + (L' \cdot C)/m_x(C)$ and the infimum of a sum is at least the sum of the infima.

Part 5. Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ be an embedding with $L = \mathcal{O}(1)|_X$. The ideal of $x$ in $\mathbb{P}^N$ is generated by $N$ linear forms vanishing at $x$, so their restrictions $s_1, \dots, s_N \in H^0(X, L)$ generate $\mathfrak{m}_x L$, and they generate $L$ at every point other than $x$. Their pullbacks $\pi^* s_i$ vanish on $E$, so they are sections of $\pi^* L \otimes \mathcal{O}_{\tilde X}(-E) = \pi^* L \otimes \mathfrak{m}_x \mathcal{O}_{\tilde X}$, by part 2 of Theorem P3.11.3. They generate it: off $E$ because $\pi$ is an isomorphism there, and along $E$ because $\mathfrak{m}_x \mathcal{O}_{\tilde X}$ is generated by the pullbacks of generators of $\mathfrak{m}_x$. So $\pi^* L - E$ is generated by global sections, hence nef by part 1 of Proposition P7.3.9, and $\varepsilon(L; x) \geq 1$. $\square$

*Theorem P7.3.20.* Part 1. Let $L$ be ample. It is nef, and $L^m$ is very ample for some $m \geq 1$ (Theorem P3.6.25). By parts 4 and 5 of Proposition P7.3.19, $\varepsilon(L; x) = \varepsilon(L^m; x)/m \geq 1/m$ for every $x$, and part 2 of Proposition P7.3.19 gives $(L \cdot C) \geq \frac{1}{m} m_x(C)$.

Part 2. Every curve $C$ contains a closed point $x$, and $m_x(C) \geq 1$ by part 1 of Proposition P7.3.19, so $(L \cdot C) \geq \varepsilon > 0$. So $L$ is nef, $\varepsilon(L; x) \geq \varepsilon$ for every $x$ by part 2 of Proposition P7.3.19, and $(L^n) \geq \varepsilon^n$ by part 3. If $n = 2$, the subvarieties of positive dimension are $X$ and its curves, and $L$ is ample by Theorem P7.3.2. $\square$

*Proposition P7.3.21.* Part 1. $D$ is nef by definition. It is not ample, since an ample class has positive square by part 5 of Theorem P7.3.12. As $\operatorname{Amp}(S)$ is the interior of $\operatorname{Nef}(S)$ (Theorem P7.3.12), $[D]$ lies on the boundary.

Part 2. A nonzero $z \in NE(S)$ is $\sum_j b_j [C_j]$ with $b_j \geq 0$ and some $b_j > 0$, so $D \cdot z = \sum_j b_j (D \cdot C_j) > 0$. By Lemma P5.12.4, a nef class lies in $\overline{NE}(S)$, and under Proposition P7.2.8 this is the closed cone of curves of Definition P7.3.8. If $[D]$ were in $NE(S)$, then $D \cdot [D] > 0$ by what was just shown, while $D \cdot [D] = D^2 = 0$. So $[D] \in \overline{NE}(S) \setminus NE(S)$.

Part 3. By part 2, $NE(S) \neq \overline{NE}(S)$. The class $[D]$ is positive on $NE(S) \setminus \{0\}$ and is not ample. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $x \in X$. Compute $m_x(C)$ for a plane curve $C$ of degree $d$ with an ordinary $r$-fold point at $x$, and check that $(\mathcal{O}(1) \cdot C) \geq m_x(C)$.
2. (A) Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface and $L$ a nef divisor with $L^2 = 0$. Show that $\varepsilon(L; x) = 0$ for every $x$.
3. (B) On $\mathbb{F}_1$, the plane blown up at a point, compute $\varepsilon(H; y)$ for the pullback $H$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ at a point $y \notin E$ and at a point $y \in E$, and compute $\varepsilon(2H - E; y)$ for $y \notin E$.
4. (B) Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety and $L$ an ample line bundle. Show that $\varepsilon(L; x) \leq (L \cdot C)$ for every curve $C$ through $x$, and that $\varepsilon(L) \leq \min_C (L \cdot C)$.
5. (B) In Example P7.3.24, write the class of a curve as $d \xi + b f$ and use part 2 of the example to show that $d \geq 0$ and $b > 0$. Deduce, with Lemma P5.12.4, that $\overline{NE}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X) = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \xi + \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} f$ and $NE(X) = \{ a \xi + b f : a \geq 0, b > 0 \} \cup \{0\}$.
6. (C) Explain why the construction of Example P7.3.24 needs characteristic $0$: in characteristic $p > 0$ the pullback of $\mathcal{E}$ by the Frobenius of $C$ can acquire an invertible subsheaf of positive degree. Read the discussion after Lazarsfeld, Example 1.5.2.

### Lean

The project library has no blow-up of a point and no Seshadri constant, and its nef predicate `MinimalModel.IsNef` is the only part of this section it defines. The ruled surface of Mumford's example is not in Mathlib, which has no projective bundles. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Seshadri's criterion, with the multiplicity of a curve at a point, is presented in Hartshorne's *Ample subvarieties* (Har70), Chapter I, for complete schemes. The general converse, for every $n$, is Lazarsfeld, Theorem 1.4.13, and its proof applies the argument of part 2 to every subvariety $V$, through the blow-up of $V$ at a smooth point and the inequality $(L^{\dim V} \cdot V) \geq \varepsilon^{\dim V}$. The definition by blow-ups is Demailly's, and Lazarsfeld, Proposition 5.1.5, shows that it agrees with the infimum over curves of Proposition P7.3.19 in general. Mumford's example is in Hartshorne's *Ample subvarieties* (Har70), Chapter I, Example 10.6, and in Lazarsfeld, Example 1.5.2; the numbering of both should be checked against the editions used. The literature state is theorem. The example is the reason the minimal model program works with $\overline{NE}(X)$ and not with $NE(X)$: the cone theorem describes the closed cone, and in Mumford's example the closed cone has a ray, spanned by $\xi$, that contains no curve.
