# The criteria of Nakai-Moishezon, Kleiman and Seshadri

A line bundle is ample when its sections, after a twist, generate every coherent sheaf, and a positive multiple then embeds the variety in projective space. This definition says nothing about curves. The three criteria of this chapter recover ampleness from intersection numbers alone. The Nakai-Moishezon criterion asks for positive top self-intersection on every subvariety. Kleiman's criterion asks for positivity on the closed cone of curves. Seshadri's criterion asks for positivity on curves, with a lower bound proportional to their multiplicities. In each case the answer depends only on the numerical class, so ampleness becomes a property of a point of $N^1(X)$, and the ample classes form an open convex cone whose closure is the nef cone.

Section 3.1 proves the Nakai-Moishezon criterion for a line bundle on a projective scheme, by induction on the dimension through the exact sequences of a divisor, following Lazarsfeld. It deduces that ampleness can be tested after a finite surjective morphism and that it depends only on the numerical class. Section 3.2 defines nef classes and proves Kleiman's theorem that a nef class has nonnegative degree on every subvariety. The consequences are the description of the ample cone as the interior of the nef cone and Kleiman's criterion. Nef divisors that are not ample occur on every blow-up and every fibration, and the section computes the basic examples. Section 3.3 treats Seshadri constants through the blow-up of a point, proves Seshadri's criterion in one direction in general and in the other direction on surfaces, and presents Mumford's example of a divisor on a ruled surface that is positive on every curve and is not ample.

On a surface the three criteria are the results of P5 Ch. 12, and the proofs here reduce to those when the dimension is $2$. The new points in higher dimension are the induction in the Nakai-Moishezon criterion and Kleiman's inequality $(D^k \cdot V) \geq 0$ for nef $D$, which replaces the Hodge index theorem.

The chapter follows Lazarsfeld, Sections 1.2, 1.4 and 1.5 (Laz04), Debarre, Chapter 1 (Deb01), and Kleiman's paper (Kle66).
