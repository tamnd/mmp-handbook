## Kodaira vanishing

### Statement

Throughout Chapters 8 to 10, $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. A *smooth projective scheme* is a projective scheme over $k$ that is smooth over $k$; its connected components are smooth projective varieties. For a smooth projective variety $X$ of dimension $n$, $\omega_X = \bigwedge^n \Omega_X$ is the canonical line bundle and $K_X$ a canonical divisor, so that $\omega_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X(K_X)$ (Corollary P4.7.21).

**Definition P7.8.1 ($\mathbb{Q}$-divisors and rounding).** Let $X$ be a normal variety.

1. A *$\mathbb{Q}$-divisor* on $X$ is a finite sum $D = \sum_j d_j D_j$ with $d_j \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $D_j$ distinct prime divisors. Its *round-down* is $\lfloor D \rfloor = \sum_j \lfloor d_j \rfloor D_j$, its *round-up* is $\lceil D \rceil = -\lfloor -D \rfloor$, and its *fractional part* is $\{D\} = D - \lfloor D \rfloor$, whose coefficients lie in $[0, 1)$.
2. $D$ is *$\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier* if $mD$ is Cartier for some integer $m \geq 1$. Two $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors $D$, $D'$ on a projective variety are *numerically equivalent*, $D \equiv D'$, if $D \cdot C = D' \cdot C$ for every curve $C$, and $D$ is *ample*, *nef* or *big* if $mD$ is for some $m \geq 1$ with $mD$ Cartier, where a Cartier divisor $B$ on a projective variety of dimension $n$ is big if $\operatorname{vol}(B) > 0$, that is, if $h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mB)) \geq c m^n$ for some $c > 0$ and infinitely many $m$.
3. Let $X$ be smooth. A reduced divisor $D = \sum_j D_j$ has *simple normal crossings*, or is *snc*, if every $D_j$ is smooth and every closed point $x \in X$ has local coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$ such that each $D_j$ through $x$ is $\{x_i = 0\}$ near $x$ for some $i$, distinct $j$ giving distinct $i$. A $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor has *snc support* if the reduced divisor $\sum_{d_j \neq 0} D_j$ is snc.

On a smooth variety every $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, since every Weil divisor is Cartier. Ampleness and nefness of a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor depend only on its numerical class, by Corollary P7.3.4 and Definition P7.3.8, and so does bigness of a nef divisor: a nef $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ is big if and only if $(D^n) > 0$ (Laz04, Section 2.2). Rounding does not commute with numerical equivalence: $\lceil D \rceil$ depends on $D$ as a divisor, not on its class.

**Theorem P7.8.2 (Kodaira vanishing).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$ and $L$ an ample line bundle on $X$. Then
$$H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = 0 \quad \text{for } i > 0, \qquad H^i(X, L^{-1}) = 0 \quad \text{for } i < n.$$
The two statements are equivalent by Serre duality.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P7.8.3 (adjoint bundles are generated).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$ and $A$ a very ample line bundle on $X$. Then $\omega_X \otimes A^{m}$ is generated by its global sections for every $m \geq n + 1$, and $H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes A^m) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and $m \geq 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Kodaira proved the theorem over $\mathbb{C}$ by harmonic theory. An ample line bundle carries a hermitian metric of positive curvature, and the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity shows that a harmonic $(n, i)$-form with values in $L$ must vanish for $i > 0$; Hodge theory identifies the cohomology with harmonic forms. P6 carries out that argument: Theorem P6.6.4 is the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity, Corollary P6.6.13 is the vanishing on a compact complex manifold with a positive line bundle, and Corollary P6.7.17 transports it to a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$ by GAGA. This volume cites the theorem from there. Over another algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, $X$ and $L$ are defined over a finitely generated subfield, which embeds into $\mathbb{C}$, and cohomology commutes with the flat extension of the ground field, so the theorem holds over $k$; this is Corollary P6.7.27. The second form of the statement is the Serre dual of the first.

The corollary is the first use of the theorem. Embed $X$ by $A$ into a projective space. Kodaira's theorem kills the cohomology that Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity asks to vanish, so $\omega_X \otimes A^{n+1}$ is $0$-regular, and a $0$-regular sheaf is generated by global sections.

The examples test the hypotheses. Projective space and curves show that the statement is sharp. On the blow-up of the plane at a point, $H + E$ is big and not nef and the vanishing fails; on $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, the class $\mathcal{O}(2, 0)$ is nef and not big and the vanishing fails. Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, in Section 8.3, replaces ample by nef and big, and the two examples show that neither half of this can be dropped.

### Examples

**Example P7.8.4 (projective space).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $L = \mathcal{O}(d)$ with $d \geq 1$. Then $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}(-n-1)$ (Corollary P4.7.21 and the Euler sequence) and $\omega_X \otimes L = \mathcal{O}(d - n - 1)$. By Theorem P4.2.1, $H^i(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(m)) = 0$ for $0 < i < n$ and every $m$, and $H^n(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(m)) = 0$ if and only if $m \geq -n$. Since $d - n - 1 \geq -n$, every $H^i(\omega_X \otimes L)$ with $i > 0$ is zero, as the theorem says. For $d = 0$ the line bundle $L = \mathcal{O}$ is not ample and $H^n(\mathbb{P}^n, \omega_X) = k$. So the theorem fails for the trivial bundle, and $d \geq 1$ is the exact range.

**Example P7.8.5 (curves).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ and $L$ a line bundle of degree $d$. By Serre duality $H^1(C, \omega_C \otimes L) \cong H^0(C, L^{-1})'$, and $H^0(C, L^{-1}) = 0$ when $d > 0$, since a nonzero section of a line bundle of negative degree would have an effective divisor of negative degree. This is Kodaira's theorem on a curve, where ample means $d > 0$. For $d = 0$ the space $H^0(C, L^{-1})$ is zero unless $L \cong \mathcal{O}_C$, so on a curve the vanishing holds for every nontrivial line bundle of degree $0$ and fails only for $L = \mathcal{O}_C$, where $H^1(C, \omega_C) = k$.

**Example P7.8.6 (big and not nef).** Let $\pi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up of a point, with $H$ the pullback of a line and $E$ the exceptional curve, so that $H^2 = 1$, $H \cdot E = 0$, $E^2 = -1$ and $K_X = -3H + E$ (Section P5.6). Let $L = H + E$. It is big, since $H$ is nef with $H^2 > 0$ and $L - H = E$ is effective, and it is not nef, since $L \cdot E = -1$. Put $D = K_X + L = -2H + 2E$. Then $h^0(X, D) = 0$: a section of $\mathcal{O}_X(-2H + 2E)$ is a section of $\mathcal{O}_X(2E - 2H)$, and since $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_X(2E) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}$ (Proposition P4.4.16) these are sections of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-2)$, which are zero. By Serre duality $h^2(X, D) = h^0(X, K_X - D) = h^0(X, -H - E) = 0$. Riemann-Roch on the surface gives
$$\chi(\mathcal{O}_X(D)) = \chi(\mathcal{O}_X) + \tfrac12 D \cdot (D - K_X) = 1 + \tfrac12 (-2H + 2E) \cdot (H + E) = 1 + \tfrac12(-2 - 2) = -1.$$
So $h^1(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = 1$, and the vanishing fails for a big line bundle that is not nef.

**Example P7.8.7 (nef and not big).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, with $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}(-2, -2)$, and let $L = \mathcal{O}(2, 0)$, the pullback of $\mathcal{O}(2)$ by the first projection. It is nef, since it is generated by global sections, and it is not big, since $L^2 = 0$. Then $\omega_X \otimes L = \mathcal{O}(0, -2)$, and the Künneth formula of Example P4.4.17 gives
$$H^1(X, \mathcal{O}(0, -2)) \cong H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}) \otimes H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(-2)) = k.$$
So the vanishing fails for a nef line bundle that is not big. For $L = \mathcal{O}(1, 0)$ the same computation gives $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}(-1, -2)) = 0$ for all $i$, because $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)$ has no cohomology. A nef line bundle that is not big can therefore satisfy the vanishing or not, and the theorem gives no information about it.

### Proof

*Theorem P7.8.2.* This is Kodaira's theorem (Kod53), also proved over $\mathbb{C}$ in Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 1, Section 2 (GH78), Huybrechts, Section 5.2 (Huy05), and Lazarsfeld, Section 4.2 (Laz04). For $k = \mathbb{C}$ the first statement is Corollary P6.7.17, which is Corollary P6.6.13 on the complex manifold $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ transported to $X$ by Theorem P6.7.5. For a general $k$ of characteristic $0$ both statements are Corollary P6.7.27, whose proof is the following reduction to $\mathbb{C}$. Over a general $k$ of characteristic $0$, choose a finitely generated subfield $k_0 \subseteq k$ over which $X$ and $L$ are defined, with models $X_0$ and $L_0$, and an embedding $k_0 \subseteq \mathbb{C}$. Cohomology of coherent sheaves commutes with flat base change of the field, so $\dim_k H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = \dim_{k_0} H^i(X_0, \omega_{X_0} \otimes L_0) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^i(X_{\mathbb{C}}, \omega_{X_\mathbb{C}} \otimes L_\mathbb{C})$, and $L_\mathbb{C}$ is ample since ampleness is preserved by field extension. The equivalence of the two forms is Corollary P4.7.21 with $\mathcal{E} = L^{-1}$: $H^i(X, L^{-1}) \cong H^{n-i}(X, \omega_X \otimes L)'$, and $i < n$ if and only if $n - i > 0$.

*Corollary P7.8.3.* The second statement is Theorem P7.8.2, since $A^m$ is ample for $m \geq 1$. For the first, let $j \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^r$ be the embedding defined by $A$, so that $A = j^* \mathcal{O}(1)$, and put $\mathcal{F} = j_* (\omega_X \otimes A^{n+1})$, a coherent sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^r$. For $p > 0$, $H^p(\mathbb{P}^r, \mathcal{F}(-p)) = H^p(X, \omega_X \otimes A^{n+1-p})$ by the projection formula and since $j$ is a closed embedding. For $1 \leq p \leq n$ this is zero by Theorem P7.8.2, since $n + 1 - p \geq 1$, and for $p > n$ it is zero since $\dim X = n$. So $\mathcal{F}$ is $0$-regular in the sense of Definition P4.2.15, and by Theorem P4.2.16 it is $m'$-regular for $m' \geq 0$ and $\mathcal{F}(m')$ is generated by global sections. Restricting to $X$, $\omega_X \otimes A^{n+1+m'}$ is generated by global sections for every $m' \geq 0$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^a \times \mathbb{P}^b$ and $L = \mathcal{O}(c, d)$. Using Example P4.4.17 in its general Künneth form, find all $(c, d)$ with $H^i(X, \omega_X \otimes L) = 0$ for every $i > 0$, and check that the set contains every ample class.
2. (A) Show that Example P7.8.6 also violates the dual form: compute $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-H - E))$.
3. (B) Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$ and $A$ very ample. Show that $\omega_X \otimes A^{n+1}$ is $0$-regular and also that $\omega_X \otimes A^{n}$ need not be generated: take $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $A = \mathcal{O}(1)$.
4. (B) Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ be a smooth hypersurface of degree $d$, with $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}_X(d - n - 2)$ by adjunction. Use Theorem P7.8.2 to show $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m)) = 0$ for $0 < i < n$ when $m > d - n - 2$ or $m < 0$, and then use the exact sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}(m - d) \to \mathcal{O}(m) \to \mathcal{O}_X(m) \to 0$ and Theorem P4.2.1 to get the vanishing for every $m$.
5. (B) Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety of dimension $n \geq 2$, $L$ ample and $D \in |L|$. Use the exact sequence $0 \to L^{-1} \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_D \to 0$ and Theorem P7.8.2 to show that $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to H^0(D, \mathcal{O}_D)$ is surjective, and deduce that $D$ is connected.
6. (C) Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety and $L$ a line bundle such that $L^m$ is generated by global sections for some $m \geq 1$ and the morphism it defines is generically finite onto its image. Show that $L$ is nef and big. Then show, using Example P7.8.7, that generation of $L^m$ alone does not give the vanishing of $H^1(X, \omega_X \otimes L)$.

### Lean

The project library states Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing in the relative klt form as `MinimalModel.KawamataViehweg`, with no proof, and Kodaira's theorem is a special case of that statement, with zero boundary and the structure map to a point; the comparison is not stated in the library. Mathlib has no sheaf cohomology of coherent sheaves on projective schemes, so neither statement of this section is formalized there. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Kodaira proved the theorem in 1953 (Kod53) by the Bochner technique, as a step toward his embedding theorem; Akizuki and Nakano extended it to $H^q(X, \Omega^p_X \otimes L)$ for $p + q > n$. The literature state is theorem. Algebraic proofs came much later: Deligne and Illusie (DI87) prove it in characteristic $0$ by reduction to characteristic $p$, through the decomposition of the de Rham complex of Chapter 11, and Esnault and Viehweg, Lectures 1 to 5 (EV92), derive it and its generalizations from the degeneration of the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence. Raynaud's example in Chapter 11 shows that the theorem is false in characteristic $p$. Corollary P7.8.3 is the simplest instance of a theme of the minimal model program: vanishing gives generation of adjoint bundles, and Fujita's conjecture asks for the optimal bound $m \geq n + 1$ for an ample, not necessarily very ample, $A$.
