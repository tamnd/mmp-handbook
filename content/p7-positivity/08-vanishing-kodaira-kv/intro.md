# Vanishing I: Kodaira and Kawamata-Viehweg

The minimal model program needs cohomology to vanish. A section of an adjoint bundle $K_X + L$ on a divisor lifts to $X$ when $H^1$ of a smaller adjoint bundle is zero, and the base point free theorem, the cone theorem and the existence of contractions are all proved by lifting sections in this way. Kodaira's theorem gives the vanishing for ample $L$. The program needs more: it works with divisors that are nef and big but not ample, because the pullback of an ample class by a birational morphism is never ample, and it works with $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors, because boundaries and log canonical thresholds have fractional coefficients. The theorem of Kawamata and Viehweg covers both.

Section 8.1 fixes the language of $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors and rounding, states Kodaira's theorem with its citation from P6, and shows by examples on blow-ups and products that neither bigness nor nefness can be dropped. Section 8.2 builds the coverings that turn fractional divisors into integral ones: the trace splitting of a finite flat cover, which makes cohomology on the base a direct summand of cohomology upstairs, the cyclic cover that extracts a $q$-th root of a smooth divisor, and the cover of Bloch and Gieseker that extracts a $q$-th root of any line bundle while keeping a given divisor with simple normal crossings. Section 8.3 proves that snc pairs with coefficients less than $1$ have discrepancies greater than $-1$, deduces from relative Serre vanishing that relative vanishing follows from absolute vanishing, proves Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing for an ample class plus a fractional snc divisor by induction on the number of components, and passes to nef and big classes by Kodaira's lemma and a log resolution. The worked example is a pencil of concurrent lines in the plane, where the log resolution is the blow-up of the common point.

Throughout the chapter $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. In characteristic $p > 0$ Kodaira's theorem fails, and Chapter 11 gives the counterexample. The proofs in Sections 8.2 and 8.3 use characteristic $0$ in three places: Kodaira's theorem itself, the theorems of Bertini and Hironaka, and the division by the degree in the trace splitting.

Kodaira's theorem is the one input that this volume does not prove. Its proof goes through Hodge theory, which belongs to P6: Corollary P6.6.13 proves it on a compact complex manifold with a positive line bundle, Corollary P6.7.17 gives the algebraic form over $\mathbb{C}$ by GAGA, and Corollary P6.7.27 extends it to every field of characteristic $0$. The theorem is cited from there, and the original sources are Kodaira (Kod53), Griffiths-Harris (GH78), Huybrechts (Huy05) and Lazarsfeld, Section 4.2 (Laz04). Chapter 11 sketches the algebraic proof of Deligne and Illusie.

The chapter follows Lazarsfeld, Sections 4.1 to 4.3 (Laz04), Kollár and Mori, Section 2.4 (KM98), and Esnault and Viehweg, Lectures 1 to 5 (EV92).
