# Section rings and Iitaka dimension

The sections of all the multiples of a line bundle form a graded ring, the section ring, and the growth of its graded pieces is measured by one integer, the Iitaka dimension. It is $-\infty$ when no multiple has a section, it is the dimension of the variety exactly when the bundle is big, and in between it is the dimension of the image of the rational maps given by large multiples. For the canonical bundle of a smooth projective variety it is the Kodaira dimension, the first invariant of the birational classification, and the canonical ring is the ring whose finite generation the minimal model program proves.

Section 6.1 defines the section ring, the semigroup and the exponent of a line bundle, proves that the fields generated by ratios of sections stabilize, and proves that $h^0(L^m)$ grows like $m^\kappa$ along the semigroup. It identifies the big line bundles as those with $\kappa = n$ and computes the Iitaka dimension on curves and on blow-ups, with an effective divisor and a nef divisor that have Iitaka dimension $0$ without being torsion. Section 6.2 states the Iitaka fibration, proves the birational invariance of the plurigenera and the canonical ring, and computes the Kodaira dimension of curves, hypersurfaces, products and surfaces. Section 6.3 proves that section rings of semiample line bundles are finitely generated, works out Zariski's example of a big and nef line bundle whose section ring is not finitely generated, and states the finite generation of canonical rings.

The Iitaka dimension is not a numerical invariant, and the examples of the chapter show the two ways in which it fails to be one: a numerically trivial bundle has $\kappa = 0$ or $-\infty$ according to whether it is torsion, and on the plane blown up at nine points of a cubic the anticanonical class has $\kappa = 0$ or $\kappa = 1$ according to the group law on the cubic. The volume of Chapter 7 is the numerical invariant that replaces it for big classes.

The chapter follows Lazarsfeld, Sections 2.1 and 2.3 (Laz04), Iitaka's paper (Iit71), Zariski's paper on high multiples of an effective divisor on a surface (Zar62), and Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan (BCHM10).
