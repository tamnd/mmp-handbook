## Finite generation of section rings

### Statement

Throughout, $X$ is a projective variety over $k$ of dimension $n \geq 1$ and $L$ is a line bundle on $X$. A line bundle is *semiample* if some power $L^m$, $m \geq 1$, is generated by global sections. The stable base locus $\mathbf{B}(L)$ is that of part 4 of Definition P3.10.1.

**Lemma P7.6.20 (ample line bundles).** Let $Y$ be a projective scheme over $k$, $A$ an ample line bundle and $\mathcal{G}$ a coherent sheaf on $Y$. Then $R(Y, A) = \bigoplus_{j \geq 0} H^0(Y, A^j)$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra, and $\bigoplus_{j \geq 0} H^0(Y, \mathcal{G} \otimes A^j)$ is a finitely generated $R(Y, A)$-module.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.6.21 (semiample line bundles).** If $L$ is semiample, then $R(X, L)$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra and $\mathbf{B}(L) = \emptyset$.

Lean: `MinimalModel.IsSemiample`, in the project library, is the condition that an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor is $\mathbb{R}$-linearly equivalent to a nonnegative combination of Cartier divisors that have base point free multiples; the finite generation of the section ring is not stated there.

**Theorem P7.6.22 (Zariski's example).** Let $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be a smooth cubic with a flex $O$, used as origin of the group law (Example P5.1.17), and $R \in C$ a point of infinite order. Choose distinct points $P_1, \dots, P_{12} \in C$ with $P_1 + \dots + P_{12} = R$ in the group law. Let $\pi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up of the twelve points, with exceptional curves $E_i$, $H$ the pullback of a line, $C' \subseteq X$ the strict transform of $C$, and $L = \mathcal{O}_X(4H - \sum_i E_i)$. Then:

1. $C' \sim 3H - \sum_i E_i$, $C'^2 = -3$, $L \cong \mathcal{O}_X(H + C')$, $(L^2) = 4$ and $L \cdot C' = 0$; $L$ is nef and big.
2. $C'$ is contained in the base locus of $|L^m|$ for every $m \geq 1$, and for $m \geq 1$ the general member of $|L^m|$ is $C' + G$ with $G$ not containing $C'$. In particular $\mathbf{B}(L) = C'$, and $L$ is not semiample.
3. $R(X, L)$ is not a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.6.23 (finite generation of canonical rings).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over a field of characteristic $0$. Then the canonical ring $R(X, K_X)$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

Lean: `MinimalModel.FiniteGenerationCanonical`, in the project library, is this statement for smooth projective varieties, recorded as a proposition with the reference to Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan; it is not proved there.

### Proof in words

For an ample bundle the section ring is, up to finitely many degrees, the homogeneous coordinate ring of a projective embedding, and Serre's theorems on projective space give finite generation. A semiample bundle is the pullback of an ample bundle by a morphism with connected fibres, and the projection formula transfers the finite generation. Finite generation does not need semiampleness: a fixed divisor that splits off every multiple does not change the ring.

Zariski's example is the first case where finite generation fails. The curve $C'$ lies in every member of $|mL|$ because $L$ restricts to a line bundle of degree $0$ and infinite order on $C'$. On the other hand, a vanishing of $H^1$ proved by induction on the multiple of $C'$ shows that $C'$ splits off only once: the general member of $|mL|$ contains $C'$ with multiplicity exactly $1$, for every $m$. A finitely generated ring would force the multiplicity along $C'$ to grow linearly with $m$, since every product of $r$ generators vanishes to order at least $r$. The finite generation of canonical rings, the main theorem of the minimal model program in characteristic $0$, is stated with its reference.

### Examples

**Example P7.6.24 (finite generation without semiampleness).** Let $X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2$ and $D = H + 2E$, the divisor of Example P5.8.27, with Zariski decomposition $P = H$, $N = 2E$. By Corollary P5.8.26 every member of $|nD|$ contains $2nE$, and multiplication by $s_E^{2n}$ is an isomorphism $H^0(nH) \cong H^0(nD)$. These isomorphisms are compatible with products, so $R(X, D) \cong R(X, H)$, which is $R(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(1)) = k[x, y, z]$ by Proposition P7.6.12 and part 1 of Theorem P4.2.1. So $R(X, D)$ is finitely generated. But $D \cdot E = 0 + 2E^2 = -2 < 0$, so $D$ is not nef and therefore not semiample, since a semiample line bundle is nef by part 1 of Proposition P7.3.9. Here $\mathbf{B}(D) = E$: every section of $nD$ vanishes on $E$, and the sections $s_E^{2n} \pi^* t$, $t \in H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(n))$, have no common zero off $E$.

**Example P7.6.25 (canonical rings of curves).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g$. If $g = 0$, $R(C, K_C) = k$, since $\omega_C^m$ has negative degree for $m \geq 1$. If $g = 1$, $\omega_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C$ and $R(C, K_C) \cong k[t]$, with $t$ the section $1$ in degree $1$. If $g \geq 2$, $\omega_C$ is ample by part 3 of Corollary P5.1.20, and $R(C, K_C)$ is finitely generated by Lemma P7.6.20. For $g \geq 1$ the Krull dimension of the canonical ring is $\kappa(C) + 1$, as Exercise 6 of Section 6.1 predicts.

**Example P7.6.26 (twisting by a torsion bundle).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve and $\eta$ a line bundle of degree $0$. If $\eta$ is torsion of order $r$, $R(C, \eta) = k[t]$ with $t$ of degree $r$, finitely generated. If $\eta$ has infinite order, $R(C, \eta) = k$. In both cases the ring is finitely generated, but $\mathbf{B}(\eta)$ is empty in the first case and equal to $C$ in the second. Zariski's example shows that on a surface the infinite order of the restriction to a curve with negative square can do more damage than on the curve itself.

### Proof

*Lemma P7.6.20.* By Theorem P3.6.25 there is $r \geq 1$ with $A^r$ very ample, so there is a closed immersion $i \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}^N$ with $i^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong A^r$. Let $S = k[x_0, \dots, x_N]$. First let $\mathcal{F}$ be a coherent sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^N$ and $M = \bigoplus_{q \geq 0} H^0(\mathbb{P}^N, \mathcal{F}(q))$, a graded $S$-module, with $S_q = H^0(\mathcal{O}(q))$ acting by multiplication (part 1 of Theorem P4.2.1). By Theorem P3.6.23 there are $j_0$ and a surjection $\mathcal{O}^a \to \mathcal{F}(j_0)$, that is, $\mathcal{O}(-j_0)^a \to \mathcal{F}$, with coherent kernel $\mathcal{K}$. By part 2 of Theorem P4.2.7 there is $q_0 \geq j_0$ with $H^1(\mathcal{K}(q)) = 0$ for $q \geq q_0$, so $S_{q - j_0}^a \to H^0(\mathcal{F}(q))$ is surjective for $q \geq q_0$. Since $S_{q - j_0} = S_{q - q_0} S_{q_0 - j_0}$, the part of $M$ in degrees at least $q_0$ is generated by $H^0(\mathcal{F}(q_0))$. Each $H^0(\mathcal{F}(q))$ is finite-dimensional by part 1 of Theorem P4.2.7, so $M$ is generated by the finite-dimensional space $\bigoplus_{q \leq q_0} H^0(\mathcal{F}(q))$. Now for $0 \leq \rho < r$ let $\mathcal{G}_\rho = i_*(\mathcal{G} \otimes A^\rho)$, coherent on $\mathbb{P}^N$. By Proposition P4.4.10, $H^0(\mathbb{P}^N, \mathcal{G}_\rho(q)) = H^0(Y, \mathcal{G} \otimes A^{\rho + rq})$, so
$$\bigoplus_{j \geq 0} H^0(Y, \mathcal{G} \otimes A^j) = \bigoplus_{\rho = 0}^{r - 1} \bigoplus_{q \geq 0} H^0(\mathbb{P}^N, \mathcal{G}_\rho(q))$$
is a finitely generated module over $S$, acting through the map $S \to R(Y, A)$ that sends $x_i$ to the section $i^* x_i$ of $A^r$. With $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{O}_Y$, $R(Y, A)$ is generated as an algebra by the images of the $x_i$ and finitely many module generators, so it is finitely generated. For general $\mathcal{G}$, the module is finitely generated over the image of $S$, hence over $R(Y, A)$.

*Proposition P7.6.21.* Let $L^m$ be generated by global sections. By part 4 of Definition P3.10.1, $\mathbf{B}(L) \subseteq \operatorname{Bs}(\Gamma(X, L^m)) = \emptyset$. The sections of $L^m$ define a morphism $\varphi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^N$ with $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong L^m$. Let $X \xrightarrow{f} Y \xrightarrow{g} \mathbb{P}^N$ be its Stein factorization (Theorem P4.6.15), with $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y$ and $g$ finite. The scheme $Y$ is projective, and it is integral since $f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ is a sheaf of domains and $f$ is surjective. The line bundle $A = g^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ is ample by Corollary P7.3.3, and $f^* A \cong L^m$. For $0 \leq \rho < m$ and $j \geq 0$, Proposition P4.4.10 gives $f_*(L^\rho \otimes f^* A^j) \cong f_* L^\rho \otimes A^j$, so
$$\bigoplus_{j \geq 0} H^0(X, L^{\rho + mj}) = \bigoplus_{j \geq 0} H^0(Y, f_* L^\rho \otimes A^j).$$
The morphism $f$ is projective, since $X$ is projective, so $f_* L^\rho$ is coherent by Theorem P4.4.8. For $\rho = 0$ the right side is $R(Y, A)$, so $R(X, L^m) \cong R(Y, A)$ is finitely generated by Lemma P7.6.20, and for each $\rho$ the right side is a finitely generated module over it, by the same lemma. So $R(X, L)$ is a finitely generated module over the finitely generated algebra $R(X, L^m)$, and is a finitely generated algebra.

*Theorem P7.6.22.* The points exist: choose $P_1, \dots, P_{11}$ distinct and put $P_{12} = R - (P_1 + \dots + P_{11})$, which differs from $P_1, \dots, P_{11}$ for all but finitely many choices of $P_{11}$. Since $P \mapsto [P - O]$ is a group isomorphism $C \to \operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$ (part 2 of Proposition P5.1.13), $\sum_i P_i \sim R + 11 O$ as divisors on $C$, and $3O \sim H|_C$ by Example P5.1.17.

Part 1. The curve $C$ is smooth at each $P_i$, so by parts 2 and 3 of Proposition P5.6.4, applied to the twelve blow-ups in turn, $\pi^* C = C' + \sum_i E_i$ and $C' \cdot E_i = 1$. Hence $C' \sim 3H - \sum_i E_i$ and $C'^2 = 9 - 12 = -3$, using $H^2 = 1$, $H \cdot E_i = 0$, $E_i^2 = -1$ and $E_i \cdot E_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$. Then $4H - \sum_i E_i = H + C'$, $(L^2) = 16 - 12 = 4$ and $L \cdot C' = 12 - 12 = 0$. For an integral curve $\Gamma \neq C'$, $H \cdot \Gamma \geq 0$ since $H$ is nef, as the pullback of an ample line bundle (part 3 of Proposition P7.3.9), and $C' \cdot \Gamma \geq 0$ by Lemma P7.4.14. So $L \cdot \Gamma \geq 0$ for every curve, and $L$ is nef. It is big, since multiplication by $s_{C'}^m$ embeds $H^0(mH)$, of dimension $\binom{m + 2}{2}$, in $H^0(L^m)$.

The restriction to $C'$. Identify $C'$ with $C$ by $\pi$. The curve $C'$ meets $E_i$ transversally in the point over $P_i$, so $\mathcal{O}_X(E_i)|_{C'} \cong \mathcal{O}_C(P_i)$, and $\mathcal{O}_X(H)|_{C'} \cong \mathcal{O}_C(H|_C) \cong \mathcal{O}_C(3O)$. So
$$L|_{C'} \cong \mathcal{O}_C(12 O - \textstyle\sum_i P_i) \cong \mathcal{O}_C(O - R), \qquad \mathcal{O}_X(C')|_{C'} \cong \mathcal{O}_C(9 O - \textstyle\sum_i P_i) \cong \mathcal{O}_C(-2O - R).$$
The bundle $\mathcal{O}_C(O - R)$ has degree $0$ and corresponds to $-R$ in $\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$, which has infinite order. For integers $b, a$, $\mathcal{O}_X(bH + aC')|_{C'} \cong \mathcal{O}_C((3b - 2a) O - aR)$, of degree $3(b - a)$.

Vanishing. For integers $b \geq a \geq 0$, $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(bH + aC')) = 0$. For $a = 0$: by Theorem P5.5.13 with $K_X = -3H + \sum_i E_i$ (Example P5.6.8), $\chi(bH) = 1 + \frac12 bH \cdot (bH + 3H - \sum_i E_i) = \frac{(b + 1)(b + 2)}{2}$. By part 3 of Corollary P5.6.5, applied to each blow-up, and Theorem P4.2.1, $h^0(bH) = h^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(b)) = \frac{(b + 1)(b + 2)}{2}$. By Corollary P4.7.21, $h^2(bH) = h^0(K_X - bH)$, and $(K_X - bH) \cdot H = -3 - b < 0$, so $K_X - bH$ is not pseudo-effective by part 1 of Proposition P7.5.13 and has no section. Hence $h^1(bH) = 0$. For $a \geq 1$ and $b \geq a$, the exact sequence
$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(bH + (a - 1) C') \to \mathcal{O}_X(bH + aC') \to \mathcal{O}_{C'}(bH + aC') \to 0$$
and the induction hypothesis for $(b, a - 1)$ reduce the claim to $H^1(C', \mathcal{O}_C((3b - 2a) O - aR)) = 0$. If $b > a$, this bundle has positive degree on a curve of genus $1$, and its $H^1$ is dual to $H^0$ of a bundle of negative degree, which is $0$ (Corollary P4.7.21 and $\omega_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C$ by part 1 of Proposition P5.1.13). If $b = a \geq 1$, the bundle is $\mathcal{O}_C(a(O - R))$, of degree $0$ and nontrivial, since $aR \neq 0$; so $h^0 = 0$, and $h^1 = h^0 - \deg - 1 + g = 0$ by Theorem P5.1.10.

Part 2. Let $m \geq 1$ and $s \in H^0(L^m)$. Then $s|_{C'}$ is a section of $\mathcal{O}_C(m(O - R))$, which is nontrivial of degree $0$ and so has no nonzero section. Hence $s$ vanishes on $C'$, and multiplication by $s_{C'}$ is an isomorphism $H^0(L^m(-C')) \cong H^0(L^m)$. Now $L^m(-C') \cong \mathcal{O}_X(mH + (m - 1) C')$. For $m = 1$ this is $\mathcal{O}_X(H)$, which is generated by global sections. For $m \geq 2$, consider
$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(mH + (m - 2) C') \to \mathcal{O}_X(mH + (m - 1) C') \to \mathcal{O}_{C'}(mH + (m - 1) C') \to 0.$$
The vanishing with $(b, a) = (m, m - 2)$ shows that the restriction map $H^0(X, mH + (m - 1) C') \to H^0(C', \mathcal{O}_C((m + 2) O - (m - 1) R))$ is surjective. The target bundle has degree $3$ on a curve of genus $1$, so it is generated by global sections by part 1 of Corollary P5.1.20. Hence $|mH + (m - 1) C'|$ has no base point on $C'$. Off $C'$, the sections $s_{C'}^{m - 1} \pi^* t$ with $t \in H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(m))$ have no common zero, since $\mathcal{O}(m)$ is generated by global sections. So $|L^m(-C')|$ is base point free, its general member $G$ does not contain $C'$, and $\operatorname{Bs}(\Gamma(L^m)) = C'$ with general member $C' + G$. Taking the intersection over $m$, $\mathbf{B}(L) = C'$, and $L$ is not semiample by Proposition P7.6.21.

Part 3. For a nonzero section $s$ of $L^m$ let $v(s) \geq 0$ be its order of vanishing along $C'$, the valuation at the generic point of $C'$ of a local expression of $s$. Then $v(st) = v(s) + v(t)$ and $v(s + t) \geq \min(v(s), v(t))$, and $v(s) \geq 1$ for $m \geq 1$ by part 2. Suppose $R(X, L)$ is generated by homogeneous elements of degrees at most $d$. A monomial of degree $m$ in the generators is a product of at least $m / d$ elements of positive degree, so it has $v \geq m / d$, and so does every element of $H^0(L^m)$. For $m = 2d$ this gives $v(s) \geq 2$ for all $s \in H^0(L^{2d})$, while by part 2 the general section has $v(s) = 1$. This contradiction proves part 3.

*Theorem P7.6.23.* This is Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan, Corollary 1.1.2 (BCHM10), not proved here. For $X$ of general type it follows from the existence of a canonical model; for arbitrary $\kappa(X) \geq 0$ it is deduced from the general type case for log pairs, applied to the base of the Iitaka fibration of Theorem P7.6.11, by a result of Fujino and Mori. By Theorem P7.6.14 the ring depends only on the birational class of $X$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $R(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(1, 1))$ is generated in degree $1$ and compute a presentation. Do the same for $R(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(1, 0))$.
2. (A) In Example P7.6.24, compute $R(X, H + bE)$ for every integer $b \geq 0$ and $R(X, H - E)$, and decide which of these divisors are semiample.
3. (B) Show that $L$ is semiample if and only if $R(X, L)$ is finitely generated and $\mathbf{B}(L) = \emptyset$, using that a finitely generated graded ring has a Veronese subring generated in degree $1$.
4. (B) In Theorem P7.6.22, show that $K_X \sim -C'$, and use Theorem P5.5.13 and the vanishing in the proof to show that $h^0(L^m) = 2m^2 + 1$ for all $m \geq 1$, which grows like $(L^2) m^2 / 2$.
5. (C) In Theorem P7.6.22, replace $R$ by a point of finite order $r$ and keep the other hypotheses. Show that $L^r|_{C'}$ is trivial, find a section of $L^r$ that does not vanish on $C'$, and conclude that $\mathbf{B}(L) \neq C'$.
6. (B) Let $X_9$ be the surface of Example P7.6.9, with $\sum P_i = R$ of infinite order. Show that $R(X_9, -K_{X_9}) \cong k[t]$, which is finitely generated, although $-K_{X_9}$ is nef and not semiample. Compare with Zariski's example, where the bundle is also big.

### Lean

The project library defines `MinimalModel.IsSemiample` and `MinimalModel.canonicalRing`, and records the finite generation of canonical rings of smooth projective varieties as the proposition `MinimalModel.FiniteGenerationCanonical`, the statement of Corollary 1.1.2 of BCHM10 taken as a hypothesis. Lemma P7.6.20, Proposition P7.6.21 and Zariski's example are not stated there; the library has no section ring of an arbitrary line bundle. Mathlib has finitely generated algebras, `Algebra.FiniteType`, but no section rings of line bundles on schemes. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Zariski constructed the example in 1962 (Zar62), in the paper in which he proved the decomposition of P5 Ch. 8 and used it to study the growth of $h^0(mD)$ for an effective divisor $D$ on a surface. Lazarsfeld, Section 2.3.A (Laz04), presents it as in the proof here. The literature state is theorem. The example needs a point of infinite order on the cubic, so it fails over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, where every point of $C$ is torsion; over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ every nef and big line bundle on a surface is semiample, by results of Artin and Keel. The finite generation of canonical rings, and of the adjoint rings $R(X, K_X + \Delta)$ of klt pairs with big boundary, was proved by Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan (BCHM10), and for varieties of general type independently by Siu by analytic methods.
