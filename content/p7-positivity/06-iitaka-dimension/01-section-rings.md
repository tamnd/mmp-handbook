## The section ring and the growth of sections

### Statement

Throughout, $X$ is a projective variety over $k$ of dimension $n \geq 1$, $L$ is a line bundle on $X$, and $h^0(L^m) = \dim_k H^0(X, L^m)$. Since $X$ is integral, the product of two nonzero sections of powers of $L$ is nonzero, and two sections $s, t$ of the same power $L^m$ with $t \neq 0$ have a ratio $s / t$ in the function field $k(X)$.

**Definition P7.6.1 (section ring, semigroup and maps).** The *section ring* of $L$ is the graded $k$-algebra
$$R(X, L) = \bigoplus_{m \geq 0} H^0(X, L^m),$$
with the multiplication of sections; it is an integral domain. The *semigroup* of $L$ is $N(L) = \{ m \geq 1 : H^0(X, L^m) \neq 0 \}$. If $N(L)$ is not empty, the *exponent* $e(L)$ is the greatest common divisor of its elements. For $m \in N(L)$ choose a basis $s_0, \dots, s_r$ of $H^0(X, L^m)$ with $s_0 \neq 0$. The rational map $\varphi_m = [s_0 : \dots : s_r] \colon X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^r$ is defined on the open set where some $s_i$ does not vanish, and $Y_m \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r$ is the closure of its image, a projective variety. The field $k(\varphi_m) \subseteq k(X)$ is the subfield generated over $k$ by the ratios $s_i / s_0$; it contains all ratios $s / t$ of sections of $L^m$ with $t \neq 0$ and does not depend on the basis. For a Cartier divisor $D$ write $R(X, D) = R(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D))$, and similarly for the other notions.

The map $\varphi_m$ is dominant onto $Y_m$, and $\varphi_m^*$ identifies the function field $k(Y_m)$, generated by the ratios $y_i / y_0$ of the coordinates, with $k(\varphi_m)$. Another basis changes $\varphi_m$ by a linear automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^r$.

**Definition P7.6.2 (Iitaka dimension).** The *Iitaka dimension* of $L$ is
$$\kappa(L) = \kappa(X, L) = \max_{m \in N(L)} \dim Y_m,$$
and $\kappa(L) = -\infty$ if $N(L)$ is empty. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D$, $\kappa(D) = \kappa(aD)$ for any $a \geq 1$ with $aD$ Cartier; by part 1 of Corollary P7.6.6 this does not depend on $a$.

**Lemma P7.6.3 (the semigroup).** If $N(L)$ is not empty, it is closed under addition, it is contained in $e(L) \mathbb{Z}$, and it contains $m \, e(L)$ for every large $m$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P7.6.4 (the stable field).** Let $N(L)$ be nonempty.

1. For $m \in N(L)$, $\dim Y_m$ is the transcendence degree of $k(\varphi_m)$ over $k$.
2. For $m, a \in N(L)$, $k(\varphi_m) \subseteq k(\varphi_{m + a})$.
3. The union $K_\infty = \bigcup_{m \in N(L)} k(\varphi_m)$ is a subfield of $k(X)$, finitely generated over $k$. There is $m_1 \in N(L)$ with $k(\varphi_{m_1}) = K_\infty$, and $k(\varphi_m) = K_\infty$ for every large $m \in N(L)$.
4. $\kappa(L)$ is the transcendence degree of $K_\infty$ over $k$, and $\kappa(L) = \dim Y_m$ for every large $m \in N(L)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P7.6.5 (growth of sections).** Let $\kappa = \kappa(L) \geq 0$. There are constants $a, b > 0$ with
$$h^0(L^m) \leq b \, m^\kappa \quad \text{for all } m \geq 1, \qquad h^0(L^m) \geq a \, m^\kappa \quad \text{for all large } m \in N(L).$$
So $\kappa(L)$ is the only integer $j$ for which $h^0(L^m) / m^j$ is bounded above for all $m$ and bounded below by a positive constant for infinitely many $m$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P7.6.6 (first properties).**

1. $\kappa(L^a) = \kappa(L)$ for every $a \geq 1$, and $\kappa(L) = \kappa(L')$ if $L \cong L'$; for Cartier divisors, $\kappa(D) = \kappa(D')$ if $D \sim D'$.
2. $\kappa(L(F)) \geq \kappa(L)$ for every effective Cartier divisor $F$.
3. $\kappa(L) \in \{ -\infty, 0, 1, \dots, n \}$, and $\kappa(L) = 0$ if and only if $N(L)$ is not empty and $h^0(L^m) \leq 1$ for every $m \geq 1$.
4. $L$ is big, in the sense of Definition P7.5.2, if and only if $\kappa(L) = n$.
5. If $X$ is a smooth curve or surface, $\kappa(L)$ is the Iitaka dimension of Definition P5.11.1.

Lean: `MinimalModel.iitakaDim`, in the project library, is the Iitaka dimension of an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor, defined by the growth condition of the last sentence of Theorem P7.6.5; the equality with Definition P7.6.2 is not stated there.

### Proof in words

A section of $L^m$ and a section of $L^{m'}$ multiply to a section of $L^{m + m'}$, so the multiples with sections form a semigroup, and the ratios of sections of $L^m$ survive into $L^{m + a}$ after multiplication by a fixed section of $L^a$. So the fields $k(\varphi_m)$ increase along the semigroup. They live in the finitely generated field $k(X)$, so they stop growing, at a field $K_\infty$ of transcendence degree $\kappa$.

The lower bound for the growth comes from one map $\varphi_{m_0}$ with image of dimension $\kappa$: the polynomials of degree $j$ in the sections of $L^{m_0}$ that do not vanish on the image give at least as many sections of $L^{j m_0}$ as the Hilbert function of $Y_{m_0}$, which grows like $j^\kappa$. The upper bound uses that every section of $L^m$ is determined by its restriction to a subvariety $W$ of dimension $\kappa$ chosen to map onto the image: the ratio of two sections is a function pulled back from the image, and such a function cannot vanish on $W$ without vanishing everywhere. On $W$ the sections grow at most like $m^{\kappa}$ by Lemma P7.5.1.

### Examples

**Example P7.6.7 (curves).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ and $L$ of degree $d$. If $d < 0$, then $h^0(L^m) = 0$ for $m \geq 1$, and $\kappa(L) = -\infty$. If $d > 0$, then $h^0(L^m) = md + 1 - g$ for $md > 2g - 2$ by Corollary P5.1.11, so $N(L)$ contains every large $m$, $e(L) = 1$ and $\kappa(L) = 1$. If $d = 0$, then $h^0(L^m)$ is $1$ when $L^m \cong \mathcal{O}_X$ and $0$ otherwise, since a nonzero section of a line bundle of degree $0$ has no zeros. So $\kappa(L) = 0$ if $L$ is torsion, of order $r$ say, with $N(L) = r \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}$ and $e(L) = r$, and $\kappa(L) = -\infty$ if $L$ has infinite order in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$. On a curve, then, a line bundle has $\kappa = 0$ exactly when it is torsion. On an elliptic curve with origin $O$, $\mathcal{O}(P - O)$ for a point $P$ of order $r$ is of the first kind, and a point of infinite order, as in Example P5.10.26, gives the second.

**Example P7.6.8 (an effective divisor with $\kappa = 0$).** Let $\pi \colon X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ with exceptional curve $E$. By part 3 of Corollary P5.6.5, $h^0(mE) = 1$ for $m \geq 0$, so $\kappa(E) = 0$ and $N(E) = \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}$. The divisor $E$ is not torsion, and no multiple is numerically trivial: $(mE)^2 = -m^2 \neq 0$ for $m \geq 1$, while a torsion line bundle is numerically trivial. So in dimension $2$, unlike on curves, $\kappa = 0$ does not force torsion, and a divisor with $\kappa = 0$ can have nonzero class in $N^1(X)$. The class $[E]$ is pseudo-effective and not big, on the boundary of $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$ (Example P7.5.15). By part 2 of Corollary P5.6.5, $H^0(m(H - E))$ is the space of plane curves of degree $m$ with multiplicity at least $m$ at $p$, the binary forms of degree $m$ in coordinates with $p = [0 : 0 : 1]$; so $\kappa(H - E) = 1$, and $\varphi_1$ is the projection from $p$, a morphism $X \to \mathbb{P}^1$.

**Example P7.6.9 (a nef divisor with $\kappa = 0$).** Let $X = X_9$ be the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at nine points $P_1, \dots, P_9$ on a smooth cubic $C$ with flex $O$, with $\sum P_i = R$ of infinite order, as in Example P5.10.26. There $-K_X$ is nef and $h^0(-mK_X) = 1$ for all $m \geq 0$, so $\kappa(-K_X) = 0$. The divisor $-K_X = 3H - \sum E_i$ is not torsion: $-K_X \cdot H = 3$, so its class in $N^1(X)$ is not zero, although $(-K_X)^2 = 0$. So a nef divisor can have $\kappa = 0$ without being numerically trivial. If instead $\sum P_i = 0$ in the group law, Example P5.10.24 gives a second cubic $C'$ through the nine points, the strict transforms of $C$ and $C'$ span a pencil in $|-K_X|$, and $\kappa(-K_X) \geq 1$. Now the restriction of $-K_X$ to $\tilde C$ is trivial, and the restriction sequences of Example P5.10.26 give $h^0(-mK_X) \leq h^0(-(m-1)K_X) + 1$, so $h^0(-mK_X) \leq m + 1$ and $\kappa(-K_X) = 1$ by Theorem P7.6.5. The two surfaces have the same intersection form and the same numerical class $-K_X$, so the Iitaka dimension is not a numerical invariant.

**Example P7.6.10 (the lower bound holds only on the semigroup).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times E$ with $E$ an elliptic curve, and $L = \mathcal{O}(1) \boxtimes \eta$ with $\eta$ of degree $0$ and order $2$. By Lemma P5.11.4, $h^0(L^m) = (m + 1) \, h^0(E, \eta^m)$, which is $m + 1$ for $m$ even and $0$ for $m$ odd. So $N(L) = 2 \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}$, $e(L) = 2$ and $\kappa(L) = 1$. The upper bound of Theorem P7.6.5 holds with $b = 2$, but no bound $h^0(L^m) \geq a m$ holds for all large $m$. This is why the theorem restricts the lower bound to $N(L)$.

### Proof

*Lemma P7.6.3.* If $s \in H^0(L^m)$ and $t \in H^0(L^{m'})$ are nonzero, then $st \in H^0(L^{m + m'})$ is nonzero, since $X$ is integral. So $N = N(L)$ is closed under addition, and $N \subseteq e \mathbb{Z}$ with $e = e(L)$ by definition. Some finite subset $n_1, \dots, n_t$ of $N$ has greatest common divisor $e$, and by Bezout $e = \sum_i c_i n_i$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$. Let $P$ be the sum of the terms $c_i n_i$ with $c_i > 0$ and $Q$ minus the sum of the terms with $c_i < 0$, so $e = P - Q$, and $P, Q$ lie in $N \cup \{0\}$. If $Q = 0$, then $e \in N$ and every multiple of $e$ lies in $N$. Otherwise write $q = Q / e \geq 1$. Every integer $M \geq q(q - 1)$ can be written $M = \alpha q + \beta$ with $0 \leq \beta < q$ and $\alpha \geq q - 1 \geq \beta$. Then
$$M e = \alpha Q + \beta (P - Q) = (\alpha - \beta) Q + \beta P,$$
a nonnegative combination of $P$ and $Q$, which lies in $N$ when $M \geq 1$.

*Proposition P7.6.4.* Part 1. The map $\varphi_m \colon X \dashrightarrow Y_m$ is dominant, so $\varphi_m^* \colon k(Y_m) \to k(X)$ is injective with image the field generated by the pullbacks $s_i / s_0$ of $y_i / y_0$, which is $k(\varphi_m)$. The dimension of $Y_m$ is the transcendence degree of its function field by Theorem P3.7.4.

Part 2. Fix a nonzero $t \in H^0(L^a)$. For sections $s, s'$ of $L^m$ with $s' \neq 0$, $s / s' = ts / ts'$, a ratio of two sections of $L^{m + a}$. So the generators of $k(\varphi_m)$ lie in $k(\varphi_{m + a})$.

Part 3. For $m, m' \in N$, part 2 puts $k(\varphi_m)$ and $k(\varphi_{m'})$ in $k(\varphi_{m + m'})$. So the family of subfields is directed, and its union $K = K_\infty$ is a field. It has finite transcendence degree $\kappa' \leq n$, since it lies in $k(X)$. Choose a transcendence basis $t_1, \dots, t_{\kappa'}$ of $K$ over $k$ and extend it by $u_1, \dots, u_{n - \kappa'} \in k(X)$ to a transcendence basis of $k(X)$ over $k$. Then $k(X)$ is finite over $k(t, u)$, of degree $\delta$ say. The elements $u$ are algebraically independent over $K$. Let $c_1, \dots, c_p \in K$ be linearly independent over $k(t)$. If $\sum_i c_i g_i = 0$ with $g_i \in k(t, u)$, clear denominators so that each $g_i$ is a polynomial in $u$ with coefficients in $k(t)$. The coefficient of each monomial $u^\alpha$ gives $\sum_i c_i g_{i, \alpha} = 0$ with $g_{i, \alpha} \in k(t)$, because a polynomial in $u$ with coefficients in $K$ vanishes only if its coefficients do. So all $g_{i, \alpha} = 0$, and the $c_i$ stay linearly independent over $k(t, u)$. Hence $p \leq \delta$, and $[K : k(t)] \leq \delta$. So $K$ is finite over $k(t)$ and finitely generated over $k$. Each of finitely many generators lies in some $k(\varphi_m)$, and by directedness all of them lie in $k(\varphi_{m_1})$ for one $m_1 \in N$. Then $k(\varphi_{m_1}) = K$. For $m \in N$ with $m - m_1 \in N$, part 2 gives $K = k(\varphi_{m_1}) \subseteq k(\varphi_m) \subseteq K$. By Lemma P7.6.3 every large $m \in N$ has this property, since $m - m_1$ is then a large multiple of $e$.

Part 4. Each $k(\varphi_m)$ lies in $K$, with equality for large $m \in N$. By part 1, $\dim Y_m \leq \operatorname{trdeg}_k K$ with equality for large $m \in N$.

*Theorem P7.6.5.* Write $N = N(L)$, $e = e(L)$ and $\kappa = \kappa(L) \geq 0$.

Lower bound. Choose $m_0 \in N$ with $\dim Y_{m_0} = \kappa$ and a basis $s_0, \dots, s_r$ of $H^0(L^{m_0})$. Let $S = k[y_0, \dots, y_r]$. The map $S_j \to H^0(L^{j m_0})$, $P \mapsto P(s_0, \dots, s_r)$, has as kernel the forms of degree $j$ that vanish on $Y_{m_0}$: on the open set where $\varphi_{m_0}$ is defined, $P(s)$ vanishes at a point $x$ exactly when $P$ vanishes at $\varphi_{m_0}(x)$, and a section that vanishes on a dense open set is zero. So $h^0(L^{j m_0})$ is at least the Hilbert function of $Y_{m_0}$ in degree $j$. By Theorem P4.3.4 and Definition P3.13.12, this is a polynomial of degree $\dim Y_{m_0} = \kappa$ with positive leading coefficient for large $j$. So there are $a_0 > 0$ and $j_0$ with $h^0(L^{j m_0}) \geq a_0 j^\kappa$ for $j \geq j_0$. The integer $m_0$ is a multiple of $e$. By Lemma P7.6.3, for each $\rho \in \{0, e, 2e, \dots, m_0 - e\}$ there is $r_\rho \in N$ with $r_\rho \equiv \rho \pmod{m_0}$; put $R = \max_\rho r_\rho$. Let $m \in N$ with $m \geq R + j_0 m_0$. Then $m \equiv \rho \pmod{m_0}$ for some $\rho$ as above, and $m = r_\rho + j m_0$ with $j \geq j_0$. Multiplication by a nonzero section of $L^{r_\rho}$ embeds $H^0(L^{j m_0})$ in $H^0(L^m)$, so $h^0(L^m) \geq a_0 j^\kappa$. For $m \geq 2R$, $j \geq (m - R) / m_0 \geq m / (2 m_0)$, and $h^0(L^m) \geq a_0 (2 m_0)^{-\kappa} m^\kappa$.

Upper bound. For $m \notin N$ there is nothing to prove. Take $m_1$ as in part 3 of Proposition P7.6.4, so $k(\varphi_{m_1}) = K_\infty$, and fix a nonzero $s_0 \in H^0(L^{m_1})$. Let $U$ be the open set where $\varphi_{m_1}$ is defined, $\Gamma \subseteq X \times Y_{m_1}$ the closure of the graph of $\varphi_{m_1}|_U$, and $X_\infty$ the normalization of $\Gamma$ (Theorem P3.7.36). Then $X_\infty$ is a normal projective variety, the first projection gives a birational morphism $\mu \colon X_\infty \to X$, and the second gives a morphism $f_0 \colon X_\infty \to Y_{m_1}$, surjective because it is proper with dense image. Let $Y_\infty \to Y_{m_1}$ be the normalization. Since $X_\infty$ is normal and $f_0$ is dominant, $f_0$ factors through the normalization (Hartshorne, Exercise II.3.8, in Har77), as a surjective morphism $f \colon X_\infty \to Y_\infty$, and $f^* k(Y_\infty) = K_\infty$ inside $k(X_\infty) = k(X)$, where $k(X_\infty)$ is identified with $k(X)$ by $\mu$.

Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $Y_\infty$ and $X_\eta$ the generic fibre of $f$, of dimension $n - \kappa$. The effective Cartier divisor $\operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0)$ does not contain the generic point of $X_\infty$, so it meets $X_\eta$ in a proper closed subset. If $\kappa = n$, put $W = X_\infty$. Otherwise choose a closed point $w$ of $X_\eta$ outside $\operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0)$, and let $W$ be its closure in $X_\infty$. Its function field $\kappa(w)$ is finite over $k(\eta)$, so $W$ is a projective variety of dimension $\kappa$ that maps onto $Y_\infty$, and $W \not\subseteq \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0)$.

Let $m \in N$ and $s \in H^0(L^m)$ nonzero. The function $g = s^{m_1} / s_0^m$ is a ratio of two sections of $L^{m m_1}$, and $m m_1 \in N$, so $g \in K_\infty$ and $g = f^* h$ with $h \in k(Y_\infty)^*$. As Weil divisors on the normal variety $X_\infty$,
$$m_1 \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s) = m \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0) + \operatorname{div}(f^* h).$$
On the normal variety $Y_\infty$, $h$ and $h^{-1}$ are regular outside $Z = \operatorname{Supp} \operatorname{div}(h)$, by Proposition P3.8.4, so $f^* h$ is a unit outside $f^{-1}(Z)$. Hence $\operatorname{Supp} \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s) \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} \operatorname{div}(\mu^* s_0) \cup f^{-1}(Z)$. Neither closed set contains $W$: the first by the choice of $W$, the second because $W$ maps onto $Y_\infty$ and $Z$ is a proper closed subset. Since $W$ is irreducible, $\mu^* s$ does not vanish identically on $W$.

So the linear map $H^0(X, L^m) \to H^0(W, \mu^* L^m|_W)$, $s \mapsto \mu^* s|_W$, is injective for $m \in N$. Lemma P7.5.1 with $Z = W$, $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}_W$, $d = \kappa$, the line bundle $\mu^* L|_W$ and $M = \mathcal{O}_W$ gives $h^0(L^m) \leq b \, m^\kappa$ for all $m \geq 1$.

The last sentence of the theorem follows: for $j > \kappa$ the lower bound makes $h^0(L^m) / m^j$ unbounded along $N$, and for $j < \kappa$ the upper bound makes it tend to $0$, so it is not bounded below by a positive constant for infinitely many $m$ unless $j \leq \kappa$, and not bounded above unless $j \geq \kappa$.

*Corollary P7.6.6.* Part 1. If $N(L)$ is empty, so is $N(L^a)$. Otherwise $N(L^a) = \{ m : am \in N(L) \}$ is not empty, since $N(L)$ contains all large multiples of $e(L)$ by Lemma P7.6.3. By Theorem P7.6.5 for $L$, $h^0(L^{am}) \leq b a^\kappa m^\kappa$ for all $m$ and $h^0(L^{am}) \geq a' a^\kappa m^\kappa$ for all large $m \in N(L^a)$, with $\kappa = \kappa(L)$. By the last sentence of Theorem P7.6.5 for $L^a$, $\kappa(L^a) = \kappa$. An isomorphism $L \cong L'$ induces isomorphisms $H^0(L^m) \cong H^0(L'^m)$ compatible with ratios, so the fields $k(\varphi_m)$ are the same.

Part 2. Let $s_F$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal{O}_X(F)$. Multiplication by $s_F^m$ embeds $H^0(L^m)$ in $H^0(L(F)^m)$, so $N(L) \subseteq N(L(F))$ and $h^0(L(F)^m) \geq h^0(L^m)$. If $\kappa(L) \geq 0$, the lower bound for $L$ and the upper bound for $L(F)$ give $a m^{\kappa(L)} \leq b \, m^{\kappa(L(F))}$ for infinitely many $m$, so $\kappa(L(F)) \geq \kappa(L)$.

Part 3. Each $Y_m$ is the closure of the image of a variety of dimension $n$, so $\dim Y_m \leq n$. The image $Y_m$ is a point if and only if all sections of $L^m$ are proportional, that is, $h^0(L^m) = 1$.

Part 4. If $\kappa(L) = n$, the lower bound of Theorem P7.6.5 gives $h^0(L^m) \geq a m^n$ for infinitely many $m$, so $L$ is big. If $\kappa(L) < n$, the upper bound gives $h^0(L^m) / m^n \leq b \, m^{\kappa - n} \to 0$, or $h^0(L^m) = 0$ for all $m$, so $L$ is not big.

Part 5. By Theorem P7.6.5, $h^0(L^m) \leq b m^\kappa$ for all $m$, and no bound $h^0(L^m) \leq c \, m^j$ with $j < \kappa$ holds, because of the lower bound. So $\kappa$ is the least $j$ of Definition P5.11.1, and both definitions give $-\infty$ when $N(L)$ is empty.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $H$ a hyperplane. Show that $h^0(mH) = \binom{m + n}{n}$, and find constants $a$ and $b$ as in Theorem P7.6.5 for $m \geq 1$. Show that $\kappa(-H) = -\infty$.
2. (A) Let $D = P$ be a point on an elliptic curve. Show that $Y_1$ is a point, that $Y_2 = \mathbb{P}^1$, and that $\varphi_3$ is a closed embedding. So $\dim Y_m < \kappa(D)$ can happen for small $m \in N(D)$.
3. (A) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times E$ and $L = \mathcal{O}(1) \boxtimes \eta$ with $\eta$ of degree $0$ and order $3$. Compute $N(L)$, $e(L)$ and $\kappa(L)$, and the integer $m_1$ of Proposition P7.6.4.
4. (B) Let $L_1$, $L_2$ be line bundles with $\kappa(L_1), \kappa(L_2) \geq 0$. Show that $\kappa(L_1 \otimes L_2) \geq \max(\kappa(L_1), \kappa(L_2))$. Give an example on $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ where $\kappa(L_1) = \kappa(L_2) = 1$ and $\kappa(L_1 \otimes L_2) = 2$.
5. (B) On $\operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2$, compute $\kappa(aH + bE)$ for all integers $a, b$, using Corollary P5.6.5, and show that $\kappa$ takes each of the values $-\infty, 0, 1, 2$. On $\mathbb{P}^1 \times E$, find two line bundles with the same class in $N^1$ and different Iitaka dimensions.
6. (C) Show that the field of degree $0$ fractions $s / t$ of homogeneous elements of $R(X, L)$ of the same degree is $K_\infty$, and that the fraction field of $R(X, L)$ has transcendence degree $\kappa(L) + 1$ over $k$ when $\kappa(L) \geq 0$. Deduce that $\kappa(L) + 1$ is the Krull dimension of $R(X, L)$ when this ring is finitely generated.

### Lean

The project library defines `MinimalModel.iitakaDim` for an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $D$ on a normal variety as $\bot$ when no $\lfloor mD \rfloor$ with $m \geq 1$ has a nonzero section, and otherwise as the largest $\kappa$ such that $h^0(\lfloor mD \rfloor) \geq c \, m^\kappa$ for some $c > 0$ and infinitely many $m$, using `MinimalModel.h0`. For a Cartier divisor this is the characterization in the last sentence of Theorem P7.6.5, so the theorem says that the Lean definition agrees with Definition P7.6.2. The library also defines `MinimalModel.numericalDim`. The section ring of a general line bundle, the semigroup and the stable field are not in the library. Mathlib has no section rings of line bundles on schemes. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Iitaka introduced the $D$-dimension in 1971 (Iit71), for line bundles on compact complex manifolds, as the largest dimension of the images of the maps $\varphi_m$, and proved the growth of $h^0(mD)$ and the fibration of Section 6.2. The proof of the growth here follows Lazarsfeld, Section 2.1 (Laz04), which works with the semigroup and the exponent in the same way; the upper bound through a subvariety that maps onto the image is written so that it uses only Lemma P7.5.1. The literature state is theorem, over any algebraically closed field. Volume 1, Chapter 4, of the program restates these results for Cartier divisors in characteristic $0$; this section is the prerequisite form, for line bundles over any algebraically closed field. On surfaces, Proposition P5.11.3 proved the upper bound, and the lower bound for $\kappa = 2$, directly.
