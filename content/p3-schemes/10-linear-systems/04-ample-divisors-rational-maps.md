## Ample divisors and rational maps

### Statement

Let $k$ be a field and $X$ a scheme of finite type over $k$. A composite of immersions is an immersion, and a base change of an immersion is an immersion. Both facts are used without comment below.

**Definition P3.10.26 (ample divisors).** Let $X$ be integral.

1. A Cartier divisor $D$ is *ample*, *very ample* or *base point free* if $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ is ample, very ample over $k$, or generated by global sections. It is *semiample* if $mD$ is base point free for some $m \geq 1$.
2. Let $X$ be normal. A $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $\Delta$ is *ample* if $m \Delta$ is an ample Cartier divisor for some $m \geq 1$ with $m\Delta$ Cartier.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.10.27 (sums and restrictions).** Let $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{M}$ be invertible on $X$.

1. If $\mathcal{L}$ is very ample and $\mathcal{M}$ is generated by global sections, then $\mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{M}$ is very ample.
2. If $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, then $\mathcal{L}^m \otimes \mathcal{M}$ is very ample for all $m$ large enough.
3. Let $X$ be proper over $k$. If $\mathcal{L}$ is ample and $\mathcal{M}$ is ample or generated by global sections, then $\mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{M}$ is ample.
4. Let $X$ be proper over $k$ and $Z \subseteq X$ a closed subscheme. If $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, then $\mathcal{L}|_Z$ is ample.
5. In part 2 of Definition P3.10.26 the condition does not depend on $m$: if $\Delta$ is ample and $m'\Delta$ is Cartier, then $m'\Delta$ is ample. For $X$ normal and proper, a sum of ample $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors is ample, and so is a positive rational multiple.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.10.28 (rational maps).** Let $X$ be a reduced scheme over $k$ and $Y$ a separated scheme over $k$. A *rational map* $\varphi \colon X \dashrightarrow Y$ is an equivalence class of pairs $(U, \varphi_U)$ of a dense open $U \subseteq X$ and a morphism $\varphi_U \colon U \to Y$ over $k$, where two pairs are equivalent if the morphisms agree on a dense open subset of $U \cap U'$. It is *defined* at $x$ if some representative has $x \in U$. If $X$ and $Y$ are integral, $\varphi$ is *dominant* if $\varphi_U(U)$ is dense, and *birational* if it is dominant and has a dominant inverse $\psi$ with $\psi \circ \varphi = \mathrm{id}_X$ and $\varphi \circ \psi = \mathrm{id}_Y$ as rational maps.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.PartialMap`, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap` and its domain `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap.domain`, and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Birational`.

**Theorem P3.10.29 (domains and linear systems).** Let $X$ be integral, with function field $K$.

1. A rational map $\varphi \colon X \dashrightarrow Y$ has a largest open set of definition $\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)$, and a unique representative $\operatorname{dom}(\varphi) \to Y$.
2. Let $X$ be normal and Noetherian and $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$ a closed subscheme. Then every rational map $X \dashrightarrow Y$ is defined at every point $x$ with $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \leq 1$. So $X \setminus \operatorname{dom}(\varphi)$ has codimension at least $2$.
3. Let $X$ be normal and Noetherian of dimension $1$ and $Y$ projective over $k$. Then every rational map $X \dashrightarrow Y$ is a morphism. A birational map between normal integral projective curves over $k$ is an isomorphism.
4. Let $X$ be normal and Noetherian, $D$ a Weil divisor, and $V \subseteq \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D))$ a linear system with basis $g_0, \dots, g_n \in K$. On $U = X \setminus \operatorname{Bs}(V)$ the divisor $D$ is Cartier and $V$ is base point free, and the morphism $\varphi_{V|_U} \colon U \to \mathbb{P}^n_k$ is given on $X_{g_l} \cap U$ by $x_j / x_l \mapsto g_j / g_l$. The rational map $\varphi_V = [g_0 : \dots : g_n]$ that it defines depends only on the $g_i$ up to a common factor in $K^*$. It does not change if $D$ is replaced by $[D] - F$, with $F$ the fixed part of $V$, and then $\operatorname{Bs}(V)$ has codimension at least $2$.
5. Conversely, let $X$ be normal and Noetherian and $\varphi \colon X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^n_k$ a rational map whose image lies in no hyperplane. Then there is a Weil divisor $D$ and a linear system $V \subseteq \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D))$ without fixed component with $\varphi = \varphi_V$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated. Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap.equivFunctionFieldOver` identifies $S$-rational maps from an integral $S$-scheme $X$ to an $S$-scheme $Y$ locally of finite type with $S$-morphisms $\operatorname{Spec} K \to Y$. This is the starting point of the proof of part 2.

### Proof in words

Very ample bundles give immersions, a bundle with sections gives a morphism, and the Segre map combines the two into an immersion for the tensor product. Ampleness is then a statement about multiples. A rational map is a morphism on a dense open set, and two morphisms that agree on a dense open set of a reduced scheme agree everywhere they are defined. So a rational map has a largest domain. At a point of codimension one of a normal scheme the local ring is a discrete valuation ring, and dividing the coordinates by the one of least order makes them all regular. So a rational map from a normal scheme fails to be defined only in codimension two. The coordinates of a rational map to projective space are rational functions, and their common poles give the divisor of the linear system.

### Examples

**Example P3.10.30 (projection from a point).** Let $n \geq 2$, $p = [0 : \dots : 0 : 1] \in \mathbb{P}^n_k$, and $V = \langle x_0, \dots, x_{n - 1} \rangle \subseteq \Gamma(\mathcal{O}(1))$, the hyperplanes through $p$. Then $\operatorname{Bs}(V) = \{p\}$, and $\varphi_V = [x_0 : \dots : x_{n - 1}]$ is the projection from $p$. It is not defined at $p$. If it were defined on an open $W \ni p$, then for each line $\ell$ through $p$ the morphism $\varphi_V|_{\ell \cap W}$ would agree with the constant map to the point $[\ell] \in \mathbb{P}^{n - 1}$ on the dense open set $(\ell \cap W) \setminus \{p\}$, and so take the value $[\ell]$ at $p$ by Theorem P3.4.11. Two distinct lines give two values.

**Example P3.10.31 (the standard Cremona transformation).** On $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ let $V = \langle yz, xz, xy \rangle \subseteq \Gamma(\mathcal{O}(2))$, the conics through the three points $p_x = [1 : 0 : 0]$, $p_y = [0 : 1 : 0]$ and $p_z = [0 : 0 : 1]$. Then $\operatorname{Bs}(V) = \{p_x, p_y, p_z\}$ and $\varphi_V = [yz : xz : xy] = [1/x : 1/y : 1/z]$. On the open set $xyz \neq 0$ it is the involution $(x, y, z) \mapsto (1/x, 1/y, 1/z)$ of the torus, so $\varphi_V \circ \varphi_V = \mathrm{id}$ as rational maps, and $\varphi_V$ is birational. On the line $V_+(x)$ minus the base points it is $[yz : 0 : 0] = p_x$: it contracts the three coordinate lines to the three points. It is not defined at $p_x$. In the chart $x = 1$, on the line $y = tz$ through $p_x$, it is $[t z^2 : z : t z] = [tz : 1 : t]$, which takes the value $[0 : 1 : t]$ at $p_x$ along this line, and the argument of Example P3.10.30 applies.

**Example P3.10.32 (projecting a conic from one of its points).** Let $C = V_+(xz - y^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ and $p = [1 : 0 : 0] \in C$. The projection from $p$ is $[y : z]$, which is not defined at $p$ as a map from $\mathbb{P}^2$. On $C$, $y \cdot y = x z$, so $[y : z] = [x : y]$ where both are defined, and $[x : y]$ is defined at $p$ with value $[1 : 0]$. So the projection extends to a morphism $C \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$, as part 3 of Theorem P3.10.29 predicts. It is the inverse of $[s : t] \mapsto [s^2 : st : t^2]$, since $[st : t^2] = [s : t]$ and $[s^2 : st] = [s : t]$. The value at $p$ is the tangent line $V_+(z)$ of $C$ at $p$, the limit of the secant lines through $p$.

**Example P3.10.33 (the rulings of the quadric cone).** Let $k$ be perfect and $Q_0 = V_+(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$ as in Example P3.10.7, with $L = V_+(x, z)$, $M = V_+(y, z)$ and $L_a = V_+(z - ax, y - a^2 x)$. The rational map $\varphi = [x : z] \colon Q_0 \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^1_k$ has $\operatorname{div}(z/x) = (L + M) - 2L = M - L$, so the construction of part 5 of Theorem P3.10.29 gives $D = L$ and $V = \langle 1, z/x \rangle \subseteq \Gamma(\mathcal{O}_{Q_0}(L))$. On $L_a \setminus \{v\}$, $\varphi$ is constant with value $[1 : a]$, so as in Example P3.10.30, $\varphi$ is not defined at the vertex. This is the projection of $Q_0$ from its vertex to a conic, followed by the isomorphism of Example P3.10.32. The divisor $L$ is an ample $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor, as $2L \sim H$ is very ample, but $|L|$ defines only a rational map.

**Example P3.10.34 (ample line bundles on the quadric surface).** On $X = \mathbb{P}^1_k \times_k \mathbb{P}^1_k$, $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ is ample if and only if $a, b > 0$. If $a, b \geq 1$ it is very ample by part 3 of Corollary P3.10.21. Conversely, the restriction of $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ to $\{c\} \times \mathbb{P}^1 \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$, for a $k$-point $c$, is $\mathcal{O}(b)$, and to $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \{c\}$ it is $\mathcal{O}(a)$. By part 4 of Proposition P3.10.27 these are ample if $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ is, and $\mathcal{O}(e)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$ is not ample for $e \leq 0$ (Example P3.6.26). The bundle $\mathcal{O}(1, 0)$ is base point free and not ample: it is the pullback of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ by the first projection, Example P3.10.16, and it is trivial on the fibres.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.10.27.* (1) Let $i \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^a_k$ be an immersion with $i^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{L}$. As $X$ is quasi-compact, finitely many global sections generate $\mathcal{M}$, since the open sets $X_t$ cover $X$. They give $j \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^b_k$ with $j^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{M}$. The morphism $(i, j) \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^a \times \mathbb{P}^b$ is the composite of the graph $X \to X \times_k \mathbb{P}^b$, a closed immersion by Proposition P3.4.10, and $i \times \mathrm{id} \colon X \times_k \mathbb{P}^b \to \mathbb{P}^a \times_k \mathbb{P}^b$, a base change of $i$. So it is an immersion. Composing with the Segre embedding gives an immersion $X \to \mathbb{P}^{ab + a + b}_k$ that pulls $\mathcal{O}(1)$ back to $(i, j)^* \mathcal{O}(1, 1) \cong \mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{M}$, by Theorem P3.10.20.

(2) By Theorem P3.6.25, $\mathcal{L}^{m_1}$ is very ample for some $m_1 \geq 1$. By the definition of ampleness, applied to the coherent sheaf $\mathcal{M}$, there is $n_0$ with $\mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{L}^n$ generated by global sections for $n \geq n_0$. For $m \geq m_1 + n_0$, $\mathcal{L}^m \otimes \mathcal{M} = \mathcal{L}^{m_1} \otimes (\mathcal{L}^{m - m_1} \otimes \mathcal{M})$ is very ample by (1).

(3) Let $\mathcal{M}$ be generated by global sections. By Theorem P3.6.25, $\mathcal{L}^m$ is very ample for some $m \geq 1$, and $\mathcal{M}^m$ is generated by global sections by Proposition P3.10.2. So $(\mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{M})^m$ is very ample by (1), so ample by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.24, and $\mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{M}$ is ample by part 1 of that proposition. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be ample. Then $\mathcal{M}^n$ is very ample for some $n$, so generated by global sections, and $\mathcal{L}^n$ is ample. By the first case $(\mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{M})^n$ is ample, so $\mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{M}$ is ample.

(4) By Theorem P3.6.25, $\mathcal{L}^m$ is very ample for some $m$, via an immersion $i$. The restriction of $i$ to $Z$ is an immersion that pulls $\mathcal{O}(1)$ back to $\mathcal{L}^m|_Z$. As $Z$ is proper over $k$, $\mathcal{L}^m|_Z$ is ample by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.24, and $\mathcal{L}|_Z$ is ample by part 1.

(5) If $m\Delta$ and $m'\Delta$ are Cartier, then $\mathcal{O}_X(mm'\Delta) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(m\Delta)^{m'} \cong \mathcal{O}_X(m'\Delta)^m$ by Proposition P3.9.3. By part 1 of Proposition P3.6.24, $m\Delta$ is ample if and only if $mm'\Delta$ is, if and only if $m'\Delta$ is. For ample $\Delta, \Delta'$, choose $m$ with $m\Delta$ and $m\Delta'$ Cartier and ample. Then $m(\Delta + \Delta')$ is ample by (3). For $c = p/q > 0$, $mq \cdot c\Delta = p \cdot m\Delta$ is ample by part 1 of Proposition P3.6.24. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.10.29.* (1) Let $(U_\lambda, \varphi_\lambda)$ be all representatives. For $\lambda, \mu$, the set $U_\lambda \cap U_\mu$ is a dense open subset of the reduced scheme $X$, and $\varphi_\lambda$, $\varphi_\mu$ agree on a dense open subset of it, so they agree on $U_\lambda \cap U_\mu$ by Theorem P3.4.11. So the $\varphi_\lambda$ glue to a morphism on $\bigcup_\lambda U_\lambda$, which is the largest domain, and the representative on it is unique by Theorem P3.4.11 again.

(2) Let $\varphi \colon U \to Y$ represent the map, and $\eta$ the generic point. Choose $i$ with $\varphi(\eta) \in D_+(x_i)$, and put $f_j = \varphi^*(x_j / x_i) \in K$. Let $x \in X$ with $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \leq 1$. By part 4 of Proposition P3.7.32, $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is regular, so it is $K$ or a discrete valuation ring with valuation $v$. Among the $j$ with $f_j \neq 0$, which include $i$, choose $l$ with $v(f_l)$ least. Then $g_j = f_j / f_l \in \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ for all $j$, and $g_l = 1$. Choose an open $W \ni x$ on which all $g_j$ are regular, and let $\psi \colon W \to D_+(x_l) \cong \mathbb{A}^n_k$ be the morphism with $\psi^*(x_j / x_l) = g_j$. Since $f_l \neq 0$, $\varphi(\eta) \in D_+(x_l)$, and on the dense open set $W \cap U \cap \varphi^{-1}(D_+(x_i) \cap D_+(x_l))$ both $\psi$ and $\varphi$ pull $x_j / x_l$ back to $f_j / f_l$. So $\psi = \varphi$ on $W \cap U$ by Theorem P3.4.11, and $\varphi$ extends to $U \cup W$. The closed subscheme $\psi^{-1}(Y) \subseteq W$ contains a dense open subset of $W$, so its ideal sheaf consists of functions that vanish on a dense open subset of the reduced scheme $W$, and it is $0$. So $\psi$ factors through $Y$, and $\varphi$ is defined at $x$. The complement of $\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)$ is closed and contains no point $x$ with $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \leq 1$, so every irreducible component has codimension at least $2$.

(3) A projective $Y$ is a closed subscheme of some $\mathbb{P}^n_k$, and every point of $X$ has $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \leq 1$, so (2) applies. If $\varphi \colon X \to Y$ and $\psi \colon Y \to X$ are inverse birational maps of normal projective curves, both are morphisms, and $\psi \circ \varphi$ agrees with $\mathrm{id}_X$ on a dense open set, so $\psi \circ \varphi = \mathrm{id}_X$ by Theorem P3.4.11, and likewise $\varphi \circ \psi = \mathrm{id}_Y$.

(4) By part 5 of Proposition P3.10.2, $D$ is Cartier at every point of $U$, and $V$ is base point free on $U$ by definition. By Theorem P3.6.21 the morphism of $V$ on $X_{g_l}$ is $x_j / x_l \mapsto g_j / g_l$, the ratio of sections, which is the ratio in $K$. Multiplying all $g_i$ by $h \in K^*$ does not change these ratios. The elements $g_i$ are also a basis of $V$ viewed in $\Gamma(\mathcal{O}_X([D] - F))$ by part 3 of Theorem P3.10.3, applied on the open set where $D$ is Cartier and extended by Theorem P3.8.29, so the rational map is the same. If $V$ has no fixed component, then no prime divisor $P$ lies in $\operatorname{Bs}(V)$: otherwise every member $E$ would have $\operatorname{mult}_P E > 0$, and the minimum would be positive. So $\operatorname{Bs}(V)$ has codimension at least $2$.

(5) As in (2) choose $i$ with $\varphi(\eta) \in D_+(x_i)$, and let $f_j = \varphi^*(x_j / x_i) \in K$, with $f_i = 1$. If $\sum a_j f_j = 0$ with $a \neq 0$, then the pullback of $\sum a_j x_j$ vanishes on a dense open set, so the image of that set lies in a hyperplane, and so does the image of $\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)$ by continuity. So the $f_j$ are linearly independent over $k$, and in particular nonzero. Let $D = -\min_j \operatorname{div}(f_j)$, the Weil divisor whose coefficient at $P$ is $-\min_j \operatorname{ord}_P(f_j)$, which is zero for all but finitely many $P$. Then $\operatorname{div}(f_j) + D \geq 0$, so $f_j \in \Gamma(\mathcal{O}_X(D))$ by Theorem P3.8.29, and for every $P$ some $\operatorname{div}(f_j) + D$ has coefficient $0$ at $P$. So $V = \langle f_0, \dots, f_n \rangle$ has no fixed component. By (4), $\varphi_V = [f_0 : \dots : f_n]$, which agrees with $\varphi$ on $U \cap \varphi^{-1}(D_+(x_i)) \cap X \setminus \operatorname{Bs}(V)$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\mathcal{O}(a, b)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ is generated by global sections for $a, b \geq 0$.
2. (A) Show that the Cremona transformation of Example P3.10.31 maps the line $V_+(x - y)$ to a line, and the general line to a conic through the three points.
3. (A) Let $D$ be an ample Cartier divisor and $E$ any Cartier divisor on a projective $X$. Show that $mD + E$ is very ample for $m$ large, and that $mD - E$ is very ample for $m$ large.
4. (B) Let $X$ be proper over $k$, $D$ semiample and $E$ ample. Show that $D + \varepsilon E$ is an ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor for every rational $\varepsilon > 0$.
5. (B) Show that every rational map $\mathbb{A}^2_k \setminus \{0\} \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^1_k$ given by $[f : g]$ with $f, g \in k[x, y]$ coprime is defined everywhere except at the common zeros of $f$ and $g$, and that $[x : y]$ does not extend to $\mathbb{A}^2_k$.
6. (B) Show that the rational map $[x : y] \colon C = V_+(y^2 z - x^3) \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^1_k$ from the cuspidal cubic is not defined at the cusp $[0 : 0 : 1]$, but that its composite with the normalization $\mathbb{P}^1 \to C$ extends. Explain why part 3 of Theorem P3.10.29 needs normality.
7. (C) Let $X$ be a normal integral projective variety over $k$ and $\varphi \colon X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^n_k$ given by a linear system $V$ without fixed component. Show that the closure $\Gamma$ of the graph of $\varphi|_{\operatorname{dom} \varphi}$ in $X \times \mathbb{P}^n$ is projective, that its first projection $\Gamma \to X$ is birational, and that it is an isomorphism over $\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)$.

### Lean

Mathlib defines partial maps and rational maps of schemes, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.PartialMap` and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap`, their domain of definition, and birational schemes, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Birational`. Ample divisors and the extension in codimension one are not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Rational maps and their extension in codimension one go back to the Italian school. Part 3 of Theorem P3.10.29 is the statement that a normal projective curve is determined by its function field, Hartshorne, Section I.6, for curves over an algebraically closed field. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program a variety is changed only by birational maps, and these are defined outside codimension two on a normal variety. This is why Weil divisors, and so canonical divisors, can be transported along small birational maps such as flips. The Cremona transformation is the model of a birational map that contracts curves and blows up points, and Chapter 11 resolves it by blowing up the three base points. The graph closure of Exercise 7 is the first step of the resolution of indeterminacy.
