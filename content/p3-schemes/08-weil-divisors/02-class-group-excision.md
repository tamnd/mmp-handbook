## Weil divisors, the class group and excision

### Statement

As in Section 8.1, $X$ is an integral Noetherian scheme with function field $K$.

**Definition P3.8.10 (Weil divisors and the class group).** The group $\operatorname{Div}(X)$ of *Weil divisors* is the free abelian group on the prime divisors of $X$. A Weil divisor $D = \sum_i n_i D_i$, with distinct $D_i$, is *effective*, written $D \geq 0$, if all $n_i \geq 0$, and its *support* is $\operatorname{Supp} D = \bigcup_{n_i \neq 0} D_i$. For $f \in K^*$ the *principal divisor* of $f$ is
$$
\operatorname{div}(f) = \sum_D \operatorname{ord}_D(f) \, D ,
$$
a finite sum by Lemma P3.8.3. Two divisors $D, D'$ are *linearly equivalent*, $D \sim D'$, if $D - D'$ is principal. The *class group* is $\operatorname{Cl}(X) = \operatorname{Div}(X)/\{\operatorname{div}(f) : f \in K^*\}$.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle`, `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.IsWeilDivisor` and `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.weilDivisors` define Weil divisors as locally finite functions on the points supported at points of coheight one (definitions). Principal divisors and the class group of a scheme are not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.8.11 (the divisor sequence).** Let $X$ be normal.

1. The map $\operatorname{div} \colon K^* \to \operatorname{Div}(X)$ is a homomorphism, and the sequence
$$
1 \to \mathcal{O}_X(X)^* \to K^* \xrightarrow{\operatorname{div}} \operatorname{Div}(X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(X) \to 0
$$
is exact. In particular $\operatorname{div}(f) \geq 0$ if and only if $f \in \mathcal{O}_X(X)$.
2. If $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$, then $D \mapsto \mathfrak{p}_D$ identifies $\operatorname{Div}(X)$ with $\operatorname{Div}(A)$ and $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ with $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ of Definition P1.8.9.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.8.12 (factorial rings).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian domain. Then $A$ is factorial if and only if $A$ is normal and $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Spec} A) = 0$. In particular $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{A}^n_k) = 0$ for a field $k$, and $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Spec} A) = 0$ for a principal ideal domain $A$.

Lean: Mathlib `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_forall_isPrincipal_of_height_eq_one` is the algebraic criterion (proved in Mathlib). The statement for schemes is not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.8.13 (excision).** Let $X$ be normal, $Z \subsetneq X$ a closed subset and $U = X \setminus Z$. Let $Z_1, \dots, Z_r$ be the irreducible components of $Z$ of codimension $1$ in $X$.

1. $U$ is normal, integral and Noetherian, and $D \mapsto D \cap U$ defines a surjective homomorphism $\operatorname{Div}(X) \to \operatorname{Div}(U)$ with kernel $\bigoplus_i \mathbb{Z} Z_i$.
2. There is an exact sequence
$$
\bigoplus_{i=1}^r \mathbb{Z} Z_i \longrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(X) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(U) \longrightarrow 0 .
$$
3. If $Z$ has codimension at least $2$, that is $r = 0$, then $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(U)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.8.14 (products with the affine line).** Let $X$ be normal and $\pi \colon X \times_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{A}^1_\mathbb{Z} \to X$ the projection. Then $X \times \mathbb{A}^1$ is a normal integral Noetherian scheme, $D \mapsto \pi^{-1}(D)$ sends prime divisors to prime divisors, and it induces an isomorphism $\pi^* \colon \operatorname{Cl}(X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(X \times \mathbb{A}^1)$. By induction $\operatorname{Cl}(X \times \mathbb{A}^n) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The divisor of a function records its zeros and poles, and on a normal scheme a function without zeros and poles is a unit, by Proposition P3.8.4. On an affine scheme this is exactly the algebra of P1 Ch. 8, so a ring is factorial if and only if its class group vanishes. Removing a closed set removes the prime divisors inside it and nothing else, so the class group of the complement is the quotient by the classes of the removed divisors. For $X \times \mathbb{A}^1$ every prime divisor is either a pullback from $X$ or meets the generic fibre $\mathbb{A}^1_K$, and on the generic fibre every divisor is principal because $K[t]$ is a principal ideal domain.

### Examples

**Example P3.8.15 (Dedekind schemes).** Let $A$ be a Dedekind domain. The prime divisors of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ are the closed points, $\operatorname{Div}(\operatorname{Spec} A)$ is the group of fractional ideals, and $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Spec} A)$ is the ideal class group (Example P1.8.16). For $A = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ it is $\mathbb{Z}/2$, generated by the point $\mathfrak{p} = (2, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$ (Example P1.7.27). By Theorem P3.8.14, $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}][t_1, \dots, t_n]) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ as well.

**Example P3.8.16 (removing a closed set from the plane).** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2_k$. By Proposition P3.8.12, $\operatorname{Cl}(X) = 0$. Removing the origin, which has codimension $2$, gives $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{A}^2_k \setminus \{0\}) = 0$ by part 3 of Theorem P3.8.13, although $\mathbb{A}^2_k \setminus \{0\}$ is not affine. More generally every open subset of $\mathbb{A}^n_k$ has class group $0$, since excision gives a surjection from $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{A}^n_k)$.

**Example P3.8.17 (the quadric cone, first part).** Let $X = V(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3_k$ and $L = V(x, z)$, as in Example P3.8.8. Then $V(x) = L$, and $X \setminus L = \operatorname{Spec} A_x$ with $A_x = k[x, x^{-1}, z]$, a factorial ring, as $y = z^2/x$. By excision $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is generated by $[L]$, and $2[L] = [\operatorname{div}(x)] = 0$. Section 8.3 shows that $[L] \neq 0$, so $\operatorname{Cl}(X) = \mathbb{Z}/2$.

**Example P3.8.18 (the line with a doubled origin).** Let $X$ be the line with a doubled origin over a field $k$, glued from two copies of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ along $\mathbb{A}^1_k \setminus \{0\}$, with origins $0$ and $0'$. It is integral, Noetherian and normal, and it is not separated. Its function field is $k(t)$, and for $f \in k(t)^*$ both $\operatorname{ord}_0(f)$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{0'}(f)$ equal the order of $f$ at $t = 0$, because the two local rings are the same subring $k[t]_{(t)}$ of $k(t)$. So the map $\operatorname{Div}(X) \to \mathbb{Z}$, $D \mapsto n_0(D) - n_{0'}(D)$, vanishes on principal divisors. Exercise 4 shows that it induces $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $[0] - [0']$. So a non-separated curve can have class group $\mathbb{Z}$ without being proper.

**Example P3.8.19 (the generic fibre).** Let $A$ be a Noetherian normal domain with fraction field $K$. The ring $A[t]$ has two kinds of height one primes. Those with $P \cap A \neq 0$ are the $\mathfrak{p}A[t]$ with $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p} = 1$, the pullbacks of the prime divisors of $\operatorname{Spec} A$. Those with $P \cap A = 0$ correspond to the nonzero primes of $K[t] = (A \setminus 0)^{-1} A[t]$, the irreducible polynomials over $K$. For $A = \mathbb{Z}$ the prime $(2t - 1)$ is of the second kind: its closure in $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[t]$ is a horizontal curve, the closure of the point $t = 1/2$ of $\mathbb{A}^1_\mathbb{Q}$.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.8.11.* (1) Each $\operatorname{ord}_D$ is a homomorphism (Proposition P3.8.2), so $\operatorname{div}$ is one. By part 2 of Proposition P3.8.4 with $U = X$, $\operatorname{div}(f) = 0$ if and only if $f \in \mathcal{O}_X(X)^*$, so the kernel is $\mathcal{O}_X(X)^*$, embedded in $K^*$ by Proposition P3.2.24. The exactness at $\operatorname{Div}(X)$ and at $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is the definition of $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$. The last statement is part 1 of Proposition P3.8.4.

(2) By part 3 of Proposition P3.8.2, the prime divisors of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ correspond to the primes of height $1$, and the orders are the valuations $v_\mathfrak{p}$. So the two definitions of $\operatorname{Div}$, $\operatorname{div}$ and $\operatorname{Cl}$ agree. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.8.12.* A factorial domain is normal by Proposition P1.4.19. So in both directions $A$ is a Noetherian normal domain, and by part 2 of Proposition P3.8.11, $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Spec} A) = \operatorname{Cl}(A)$. Proposition P1.8.10 says that $\operatorname{Cl}(A) = 0$ if and only if $A$ is factorial. The polynomial ring $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ and a principal ideal domain are factorial. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.8.13.* (1) $U$ is a nonempty open subscheme of $X$, as $Z \neq X$, so it is integral, Noetherian and normal, with the same function field and the same local rings. Let $D$ be a prime divisor of $X$ with $D \cap U \neq \emptyset$. Then $D \cap U$ is irreducible and closed in $U$, of codimension $1$ in $U$ by part 2 of Proposition P3.7.2, so it is a prime divisor of $U$, with the same generic point. Conversely, for a prime divisor $E$ of $U$, its closure $\overline{E}$ in $X$ is irreducible with $\overline{E} \cap U = E$, of codimension $1$ in $X$ by the same part. So $D \mapsto D \cap U$ is a bijection from the prime divisors of $X$ that meet $U$ to the prime divisors of $U$. By Lemma P3.8.3, the prime divisors of $X$ that do not meet $U$ are the $Z_i$. Define $\operatorname{Div}(X) \to \operatorname{Div}(U)$ by $D \mapsto D \cap U$ on prime divisors meeting $U$ and $Z_i \mapsto 0$. It is surjective, with kernel $\bigoplus_i \mathbb{Z} Z_i$.

(2) The local ring of $X$ along $D$ and the local ring of $U$ along $D \cap U$ are the same stalk, so $\operatorname{ord}_{D \cap U} = \operatorname{ord}_D$ on $K^*$, and the map of (1) sends $\operatorname{div}_X(f)$ to $\operatorname{div}_U(f)$. So it induces a surjection $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(U)$, and the composite $\bigoplus \mathbb{Z} Z_i \to \operatorname{Cl}(U)$ is zero. Let $D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)$ with $[D \cap U] = 0$, that is $D \cap U = \operatorname{div}_U(f)$ for some $f \in K^*$. Then $D - \operatorname{div}_X(f)$ lies in the kernel of the map of (1), so it is a combination of the $Z_i$, and $[D]$ lies in the image of $\bigoplus \mathbb{Z} Z_i$.

(3) If $r = 0$, part 2 gives the isomorphism. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.8.14.* Write $Y = X \times \mathbb{A}^1$. For an affine open $\operatorname{Spec} A$ of $X$, $\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec} A) = \operatorname{Spec} A[t]$, and $A[t]$ is a Noetherian normal domain with fraction field $K(t)$ by Theorem P1.8.12. As $X$ is covered by finitely many such opens, and any two of the opens $\operatorname{Spec} A[t]$ meet, $Y$ is a normal integral Noetherian scheme with function field $K(t)$. Its generic fibre $Y_\xi = \pi^{-1}(\xi)$ is $\operatorname{Spec} K[t]$, by Proposition P3.3.28, and on each $\operatorname{Spec} A[t]$ it is the set of primes $P$ with $P \cap A = 0$.

*Two kinds of prime divisors.* Let $E$ be a prime divisor of $Y$ and $U = \operatorname{Spec} A$ an affine open of $X$ such that $E$ meets $\pi^{-1}(U)$, so that $E \cap \pi^{-1}(U) = V(P)$ for a prime $P \subseteq A[t]$ of height one (part 3 of Proposition P3.8.2). If $P \cap A \neq 0$, then $P = \mathfrak{p}A[t]$ with $\mathfrak{p} = P \cap A$ of height one, by the proof of Theorem P1.8.12. Then $E \cap \pi^{-1}(U) = \pi^{-1}(V(\mathfrak{p}))$, and $E = \pi^{-1}(D)$ for the prime divisor $D$ of $X$ with $D \cap U = V(\mathfrak{p})$: both are irreducible closed sets with the generic point $P$, as $\pi^{-1}(D)$ is covered by the irreducible sets $\pi^{-1}(D \cap U')$, any two of which meet. Conversely, for every prime divisor $D$ of $X$, $\pi^{-1}(D)$ is a prime divisor of this kind, with generic point $\mathfrak{p}A[t]$. If $P \cap A = 0$, the generic point of $E$ lies in the generic fibre. Call these $E$ *horizontal*. They correspond to the nonzero primes of $K[t]$, and the local ring of $Y$ along $E$ is $A[t]_P = K[t]_{PK[t]}$.

*Orders.* For a prime divisor $D$ of $X$ with $D \cap U = V(\mathfrak{p})$ and a uniformizer $\varpi$ of $A_\mathfrak{p}$, the maximal ideal of $A[t]_{\mathfrak{p}A[t]}$ is generated by $\varpi$, so $\operatorname{ord}_{\pi^{-1}(D)}(f) = \operatorname{ord}_D(f)$ for $f \in K^*$. Hence $\pi^*$, defined on $\operatorname{Div}(X)$ by $D \mapsto \pi^{-1}(D)$, is an injective homomorphism onto the subgroup of divisors with no horizontal component, and $\pi^* \operatorname{div}_X(f) = \operatorname{div}_Y(f)$ for $f \in K^*$, since $f$ has order $0$ along every horizontal divisor, being a unit of $K[t]$. So $\pi^*$ induces a homomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(Y)$.

*Injective.* Let $\pi^* D = \operatorname{div}_Y(g)$ with $g \in K(t)^*$. Then $g$ has order $0$ along every horizontal prime divisor, that is, at every nonzero prime of $K[t]$. As $K[t]$ is factorial, $g \in K^*$. Then $\pi^*(D - \operatorname{div}_X(g)) = 0$, and $D = \operatorname{div}_X(g)$ by injectivity on $\operatorname{Div}$.

*Surjective.* Let $E \in \operatorname{Div}(Y)$, and let $E_1, \dots, E_s$ be its horizontal components, with coefficients $m_j$, corresponding to irreducible polynomials $q_j \in K[t]$. Put $g = \prod_j q_j^{m_j} \in K(t)^*$. Then $\operatorname{div}_Y(g)$ and $E$ have the same horizontal part, since the order of $g$ along $E_j$ is its order at $q_j$ in $K[t]$. So $E - \operatorname{div}_Y(g)$ has no horizontal component, and it equals $\pi^* D$ for some $D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)$. So $[E] = \pi^*[D]$. The statement for $\mathbb{A}^n$ follows by induction, as $X \times \mathbb{A}^n = (X \times \mathbb{A}^{n-1}) \times \mathbb{A}^1$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(U) = 0$ for every nonempty open $U \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n_k$, and describe $\mathcal{O}(U)^*$ for $U = \mathbb{A}^n_k \setminus V(f)$ in terms of the irreducible factors of $f$.
2. (A) Let $A = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (2, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$. Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Spec} A \setminus \{\mathfrak{p}\}) = 0$, and find a closed point $\mathfrak{q} \neq \mathfrak{p}$ of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ that is not principal.
3. (A) Let $X$ be the affine plane with a doubled origin. Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(X) = 0$.
4. (B) Complete Example P3.8.18: show that $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ for the line with a doubled origin, and that $\mathcal{O}_X(X) = k[t]$.
5. (B) Let $X$ be normal. Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(X \times \mathbb{G}_m) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X)$, where $\mathbb{G}_m = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]$, by applying excision to $X \times \{0\} \subseteq X \times \mathbb{A}^1$.
6. (B) Let $X = V(xw - yz) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^4_k$, and let $P = V(x, y)$ and $Q = V(x, z)$. Show that $\operatorname{div}(x) = P + Q$ and that $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is generated by $[P]$. (Example P1.8.15 shows that $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.)
7. (C) Let $X$ be normal and $x \in X$. Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X, x})$, $D \mapsto$ the divisor of $\operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ given by the prime divisors through $x$, is surjective. Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X, x}) = 0$ if and only if every prime divisor through $x$ is defined by one equation on some open neighbourhood of $x$.

### Lean

Mathlib defines algebraic cycles on a scheme as functions on the points with locally finite support, in `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/AlgebraicCycle/Basic.lean`, so that a prime divisor is recorded by its generic point. `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.IsWeilDivisor` asks that the support consists of points of coheight one, and `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.weilDivisors` is the subgroup of Weil divisors. Principal divisors, linear equivalence, the class group of a scheme, excision and Theorem P3.8.14 are not in Lean as stated. For a Dedekind domain the class group is Mathlib `ClassGroup`. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Weil divisors are named after Weil's work on the foundations of algebraic geometry, in which a divisor is a formal sum of subvarieties of codimension one. The class group of a Noetherian normal domain is the divisor class group of Krull domains, and the comparison with unique factorization is due to the theory of Krull domains, as in Samuel's lectures on unique factorization domains. Theorem P3.8.13 and Theorem P3.8.14 are Hartshorne, Propositions II.6.5 and II.6.6. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program the class group of a germ is the obstruction to $\mathbb{Q}$-factoriality: a normal variety is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial at $x$ exactly when $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X, x})$ is torsion (Corollary P1.8.21), and the runs of the program in Volume 4 keep $\mathbb{Q}$-factoriality at every step.
