## Class groups of projective space, quadrics and cones

### Statement

Let $k$ be a field, $S = k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$, and $\mathbb{P}^n = \mathbb{P}^n_k = \operatorname{Proj} S$ with $n \geq 1$. Its function field is $K = k(x_1/x_0, \dots, x_n/x_0)$, and every $f \in K^*$ is a quotient $F/G$ of homogeneous polynomials of the same degree. For a homogeneous irreducible polynomial $F$ of positive degree write $D_F = V_+(F)$.

**Theorem P3.8.20 (the class group of projective space).**

1. For every homogeneous irreducible $F \in S$ of positive degree, $D_F$ is a prime divisor. Every prime divisor of $\mathbb{P}^n$ is of this form, and $F$ is unique up to a factor in $k^*$. Put $\deg D_F = \deg F$, and extend $\deg$ linearly to $\operatorname{Div}(\mathbb{P}^n)$.
2. Let $F, G$ be nonzero homogeneous polynomials of the same degree with factorizations $F = c \prod_i P_i^{a_i}$ and $G = c' \prod_i P_i^{b_i}$, where the $P_i$ are pairwise non-associate homogeneous irreducible polynomials, $a_i, b_i \geq 0$ and $c, c' \in k^*$. Then $\operatorname{div}(F/G) = \sum_i (a_i - b_i) D_{P_i}$.
3. The map $\deg \colon \operatorname{Div}(\mathbb{P}^n) \to \mathbb{Z}$ vanishes on principal divisors and induces an isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. The class of a hyperplane $H$ is a generator, and $D \sim (\deg D) H$ for every divisor $D$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P3.8.21 (complements of hypersurfaces).** Let $F = \prod_{i=1}^s F_i^{a_i}$ be homogeneous of positive degree with distinct irreducible factors $F_i$, $a_i \geq 1$, and $U = \mathbb{P}^n \setminus V_+(F)$. Then $\operatorname{Cl}(U) \cong \mathbb{Z}/d$ with $d = \gcd(\deg F_1, \dots, \deg F_s)$. In particular $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^n \setminus D_F) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\deg F$ for $F$ irreducible.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.8.22 (cones and quadrics).** Let $k$ be a perfect field and $n \geq 1$.

1. For the affine cones $X_n = V(x y - z^n) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3_k$, $\operatorname{Cl}(X_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$, generated by the class of the line $L = V(x, z)$, and $\operatorname{Cl}(X_n \setminus \{0\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$.
2. For the projective quadric cone $Q_0 = V_+(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$ with coordinates $x, y, z, w$, $\operatorname{Cl}(Q_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the class of the line $L = V_+(x, z)$, and a plane section $H = V_+(w) \cap Q_0$ satisfies $H \sim 2L$.
3. For the smooth quadric $Q = V_+(xw - yz) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$, $\operatorname{Cl}(Q) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, generated by the classes of two lines $L_1 = V_+(x, y)$ and $L_2 = V_+(x, z)$ from the two rulings, and a plane section satisfies $H \sim L_1 + L_2$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

On $\mathbb{P}^n$ a prime divisor is a hypersurface, a rational function is a ratio of two forms of the same degree, and its divisor is the difference of the two hypersurfaces with multiplicities, so its degree is zero. Conversely a divisor of degree zero is the divisor of the ratio of the corresponding forms. For a cone or a quadric, removing a suitable plane section leaves an affine plane, whose class group vanishes, so excision shows that the components of the removed section generate. The relations come from explicit functions, and the independence of the generators comes from a projection to a curve or from the degree.

### Examples

**Example P3.8.23 (plane curves).** For a plane curve $C = D_F \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ of degree $d$ and a line $\ell$, $C \sim d \ell$. The conic $C = V_+(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ by $(s : t) \mapsto (s^2 : st : t^2)$, and its complement $\mathbb{P}^2_k \setminus C$ has class group $\mathbb{Z}/2$ by Corollary P3.8.21. So $\mathbb{P}^2_k \setminus C$ is not isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^2_k$, although both are complements of a smooth rational curve in $\mathbb{P}^2_k$, the second one of a line.

**Example P3.8.24 (a principal divisor on the plane).** On $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ let $f = (x_0 x_2 - x_1^2)/x_0^2$. Then $\operatorname{div}(f) = C - 2 \ell_0$, where $C$ is the conic and $\ell_0 = V_+(x_0)$. On the chart $x_0 \neq 0$ with coordinates $u = x_1/x_0$, $v = x_2/x_0$ the function is $v - u^2$, with a simple zero along the parabola. On the chart $x_2 \neq 0$, with $a = x_0/x_2$ and $b = x_1/x_2$, it is $(a - b^2)/a^2$, with a pole of order $2$ along $a = 0$. The degree of $\operatorname{div}(f)$ is $2 - 2 = 0$.

**Example P3.8.25 (the quadric cone as a weighted projective plane).** The cone $Q_0$ is the weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ of Example P3.6.7, with $x = s^2$, $y = t^2$, $z = st$ and $w = u$ of degree $2$. Its vertex $(0 : 0 : 0 : 1)$ is the singular point. By part 2 of Proposition P3.8.22, $\operatorname{Cl}(Q_0) = \mathbb{Z}$. Near the vertex $Q_0$ is the affine cone $X_2 = Q_0 \cap D_+(w)$, where $L$ is not locally principal at the vertex by Example P1.8.22, while $2L = \operatorname{div}(x/w)$ on $X_2$. So $L$ is not Cartier at the vertex, while $2L$ is. Chapter 9 makes this precise: $Q_0$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial of index $2$.

**Example P3.8.26 (the smooth quadric and the cone over it).** The surface $Q = V_+(xw - yz)$ is the image of the Segre embedding of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, and its lines $L_1 = V_+(x, y)$ and $L_2 = V_+(x, z)$ belong to the two rulings. The plane section $V_+(x) \cap Q$ is $L_1 \cup L_2$, and the plane section $H = V_+(x - w) \cap Q = V_+(x - w, x^2 - yz)$ is a smooth conic. The function $x/(x - w)$ has $\operatorname{div}(x/(x - w)) = L_1 + L_2 - H$, so $H \sim L_1 + L_2$. The affine cone $V(xw - yz) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^4_k$ has class group $\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}^2/\mathbb{Z}(1, 1)$ (Example P1.8.15, and Exercise 7). It is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial at the vertex: no nonzero multiple of the plane over $L_1$ is locally principal there (Example P1.8.23).

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem P3.8.20.* Write $U_i = D_+(x_i) = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$, a polynomial ring in $n$ variables (Theorem P3.6.3). For homogeneous $G$ of degree $e$ write $G^{(i)} = G/x_i^e$, the dehomogenization, a polynomial on $U_i$ with $V_+(G) \cap U_i = V(G^{(i)})$.

(1) Let $F$ be homogeneous irreducible of degree $e \geq 1$. Choose $i$ with $x_i \nmid F$. Such an $i$ exists, as an irreducible $F$ divisible by $x_0$ and $x_1$ would be associate to both. Then $F^{(i)}$ is irreducible in $k[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$, since a factorization of it homogenizes to a factorization of $F$, and it is not a unit, since $x_i \nmid F$ forces $\deg F^{(i)} = e > 0$. So $V(F^{(i)})$ is the prime divisor $V((F^{(i)}))$ of $U_i$, as height one primes of a factorial ring are principal. Every irreducible component of $D_F$ has dimension $n - 1$ by part 3 of Proposition P3.7.5. The set $D_F \cap V_+(x_i)$ is $V_+(F(x_i = 0))$ inside $V_+(x_i) \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}_k$, where $F(x_i = 0) \neq 0$, so it has dimension $n - 2$ (empty for $n = 1$) by the same part. So no component of $D_F$ lies in $V_+(x_i)$, every component meets $U_i$, and $D_F$ is the closure of the irreducible set $V(F^{(i)})$. It is irreducible of codimension $1$ by Theorem P3.7.4.

Conversely let $D$ be a prime divisor. As $\bigcap_i V_+(x_i) = \emptyset$, $D$ meets some $U_i$, and $D \cap U_i = V(g)$ for an irreducible polynomial $g$ in the variables $x_j/x_i$, by Propositions P3.8.2 and P3.8.12. Let $F$ be the homogenization of $g$, $F = x_i^{\deg g} g(x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i)$. Then $F$ is homogeneous and irreducible, $x_i \nmid F$, and $F^{(i)} = g$. By the first paragraph $D_F$ is a prime divisor with $D_F \cap U_i = V(g) = D \cap U_i$, so $D_F = D$, as both are closures of this set. If $D_F = D_G$ with $F, G$ irreducible, choose $U_i$ meeting $D_F$. Then $x_i$ divides neither $F$ nor $G$, as $D_{x_i} \neq D_F$, and $F^{(i)}$, $G^{(i)}$ generate the same prime ideal, so $F^{(i)} = c\, G^{(i)}$ with $c \in k^*$, and homogenizing gives $F = c\, G$.

(2) Let $D = D_P$ be a prime divisor, with $P$ irreducible, and choose $i$ with $D \cap U_i \neq \emptyset$, so $x_i \nmid P$. On $U_i$ the function $F/G$ is $F^{(i)}/G^{(i)}$, and $F^{(i)} = c \prod_j (P_j^{(i)})^{a_j}$ and $G^{(i)} = c' \prod_j (P_j^{(i)})^{b_j}$ are factorizations in the factorial ring of $U_i$, where $P_j^{(i)}$ is a unit if $P_j$ is associate to $x_i$ and is irreducible otherwise. The order along $D$ is the exponent of the prime element $P^{(i)}$ in this fraction, as in Example P3.8.7, which works for every factorial ring. If $P$ is associate to $P_j$, this exponent is $a_j - b_j$. Otherwise $P^{(i)}$ is not associate to any $P_j^{(i)}$, since homogenization would give $P$ associate to $P_j$, and the exponent is $0$. This is the formula.

(3) Every $f \in K^*$ is $F/G$ with $F$, $G$ homogeneous of the same degree $e$. By part 2, $\deg \operatorname{div}(f) = \sum_i (a_i - b_i) \deg P_i = \deg F - \deg G = 0$. So $\deg$ vanishes on principal divisors. It is surjective, as $\deg H = 1$. Let $D = \sum_j n_j D_{F_j}$ have degree $0$. Then $f = \prod_j F_j^{n_j}$ is a quotient of two forms of the same degree, so $f \in K^*$, and $\operatorname{div}(f) = \sum_j n_j D_{F_j} = D$ by part 2. So the kernel of $\deg$ is the group of principal divisors, and $D - (\deg D)H$ has degree $0$, so it is principal. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary P3.8.21.* By Theorem P3.8.13, $\operatorname{Cl}(U)$ is the quotient of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^n) = \mathbb{Z}$ by the subgroup generated by the classes $[D_{F_i}] = \deg F_i$. This subgroup is $d\mathbb{Z}$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.8.22.* (1) $X_n$ is normal by Example P3.7.37, and $\operatorname{Cl}(X_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$, generated by $[L]$, is Example P1.8.14, with Proposition P3.8.11 identifying the two class groups. The vertex has codimension $2$, so excision gives the second statement.

(2) The scheme $Q_0$ is integral, as $xy - z^2$ is irreducible. By the Jacobian criterion its only singular point is the vertex $v = (0 : 0 : 0 : 1)$, of codimension $2$, so on each affine chart it is a hypersurface that is regular in codimension one, and it is normal by Corollary P1.11.14. The open set $Q_0 \cap D_+(x)$ is $\operatorname{Spec} k[a, b, c]/(b - c^2) \cong \mathbb{A}^2_k$ with $a = w/x$, $b = y/x$, $c = z/x$, so its class group is $0$. Its complement is $V_+(x, z^2) = L$ as a set. By excision $\operatorname{Cl}(Q_0)$ is generated by $[L]$. On $Q_0 \cap D_+(w)$, which is the affine cone $X_2$ with coordinates $x/w$, $y/w$, $z/w$, the function $x/w$ has divisor $2L$ by Example P3.8.8. Along $H = V_+(w) \cap Q_0$, a conic and a prime divisor, $x/w$ has a pole of order $1$, since $w/x$ is a coordinate on $Q_0 \cap D_+(x) \cong \mathbb{A}^2$ that vanishes simply along $H$. So $\operatorname{div}(x/w) = 2L - H$, and $H \sim 2L$.

It remains to show that $[L]$ has infinite order. The vertex has codimension $2$, so $\operatorname{Cl}(Q_0) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(Q_0 \setminus \{v\})$. The projection $p \colon Q_0 \setminus \{v\} \to C = V_+(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$, $(x : y : z : w) \mapsto (x : y : z)$, is a morphism, and over $C \cap D_+(x)$ and $C \cap D_+(y)$, which cover $C$, it is a product with $\mathbb{A}^1$, with fibre coordinates $w/x$ and $w/y$. By Exercise 5, the analogue of Theorem P3.8.14 for such a bundle, $p^* \colon \operatorname{Cl}(C) \to \operatorname{Cl}(Q_0 \setminus \{v\})$ is an isomorphism. As $C \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$, $\operatorname{Cl}(C) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by Theorem P3.8.20, generated by the point $V_+(x, z) \cap C$, and its pullback is $L \setminus \{v\}$. So $[L]$ generates $\operatorname{Cl}(Q_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

(3) $Q$ is a smooth surface by the Jacobian criterion (Proposition P3.7.25), so it is normal. The open set $Q \cap D_+(x) = \operatorname{Spec} k[y/x, z/x, w/x]/(w/x - (y/x)(z/x)) \cong \mathbb{A}^2_k$ has class group $0$, and its complement $V_+(x, yz) \cap Q = L_1 \cup L_2$. By excision $\operatorname{Cl}(Q)$ is generated by $[L_1]$ and $[L_2]$. Let $H = V_+(x - w) \cap Q$, an irreducible conic, and $f = x/(x - w)$. On $Q \cap D_+(x) \cong \mathbb{A}^2_k$ with coordinates $b = y/x$, $c = z/x$, one has $w/x = bc$ and $f = 1/(1 - bc)$, a function with a simple pole along the irreducible curve $V(1 - bc) = H \cap D_+(x)$ and no zero. On $Q \cap D_+(w) \cong \mathbb{A}^2_k$ with coordinates $y/w$, $z/w$, one has $x/w = (y/w)(z/w)$ and $f = (x/w)/(x/w - 1)$, where $x/w - 1$ is a unit at the generic points of $L_1$ and $L_2$. So $f$ vanishes to order $1$ along $L_1 = V(y/w)$ and $L_2 = V(z/w)$ on this chart. As $L_1 \cup L_2$ is the complement of $D_+(x)$ in $Q$, $\operatorname{div}(f) = L_1 + L_2 - H$, and $H \sim L_1 + L_2$. The isomorphism $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to Q$ of the Segre embedding sends the classes of $\{0\} \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \{0\}$ to $[L_1]$ and $[L_2]$, up to the order. By Exercise 4, $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ through the bidegree, with these two classes as a basis. So $[L_1]$ and $[L_2]$ are a basis of $\operatorname{Cl}(Q) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^2_k \setminus V_+(F))$ for $F = x_0 x_1 x_2$, for $F = x_0 (x_0 x_2 - x_1^2)$, and for $F$ a smooth cubic.
2. (A) On $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ compute $\operatorname{div}(f)$ for $f = x_1^2 x_2 / x_0^3$ and for $f = (x_1^2 - x_0 x_2)/(x_1 x_2)$, and check that the degree is $0$.
3. (A) On $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with coordinates $(s_0 : s_1)$ and $(t_0 : t_1)$, show that the diagonal is $V(s_0 t_1 - s_1 t_0)$ and that the graph of $s \mapsto s^d$ is $V(s_1^d t_0 - s_0^d t_1)$. Find their bidegrees in the sense of Exercise 4.
4. (B) Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n_k \times_k \mathbb{P}^m_k$. Show that every prime divisor of $X$ is $V(F)$ for an irreducible bihomogeneous polynomial $F$ in $x_0, \dots, x_n$ and $y_0, \dots, y_m$, unique up to $k^*$, and that the bidegree induces an isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$.
5. (B) Let $p \colon Y \to X$ be a morphism of normal integral Noetherian schemes, and let $X$ have an open cover by sets $U$ with isomorphisms $p^{-1}(U) \cong U \times \mathbb{A}^1$ over $U$. Adapt the proof of Theorem P3.8.14 to show that $D \mapsto p^{-1}(D)$ induces an isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(Y)$.
6. (B) Show that the plane section $H = V_+(x - w) \cap Q_0$ of the quadric cone does not pass through the vertex, and find $f \in K(Q_0)^*$ with $\operatorname{div}(f) = H - 2L$. Deduce that every divisor of $Q_0$ is linearly equivalent to one whose support avoids the vertex.
7. (C) Let $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$ be a normal projective variety whose affine cone $C(V) = \operatorname{Spec} S/I(V)$ is normal, and $H$ a hyperplane section of $V$. Show that $C(V) \setminus \{0\} \to V$ is a $\mathbb{G}_m$-bundle in the sense of Exercise 5 with $\mathbb{A}^1$ replaced by $\mathbb{G}_m$, and that $\operatorname{Cl}(C(V)) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(V)/\mathbb{Z}[H]$. Deduce the class groups of the cones over a conic, over the smooth quadric surface, and over the twisted cubic curve: $\mathbb{Z}/2$, $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/3$.

### Lean

Mathlib constructs $\mathbb{P}^n$ through `AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpectrum` and has the degree of a cycle on a quasi-compact scheme as `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.degree`, the sum of the coefficients. The degree of a divisor on $\mathbb{P}^n$ as the degree of its equation, and the class groups of projective space, of quadrics and of cones, are not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is the scheme form of the statement that two hypersurfaces of the same degree are linearly equivalent, and it is Hartshorne, Proposition II.6.4. The quadric cone is Hartshorne, Example II.6.5.2, and the smooth quadric is Example II.6.6.1. The comparison of the class group of a projective variety with that of its affine cone, Exercise 7, is Hartshorne, Exercise II.6.3. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program these examples are the first test cases: $\mathbb{P}^n$ has Picard number one and is the end of every run on a rational variety of Picard number one, the smooth quadric surface has two extremal rays, one for each ruling, and the quadric cone $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$ is the simplest variety with a non-Cartier Weil divisor.
