# Flat families

A family of schemes over a base $T$ is a morphism $X \to T$, and its members are the fibres $X_t$. Without a condition on the morphism the fibres need not look alike: the blow-up of the plane at a point has one fibre a line and all others points. Flatness is the condition that makes the fibres vary continuously. A flat family of closed subschemes of projective space has the same Hilbert polynomial in every fibre, and over a discrete valuation ring a family over the punctured base has exactly one flat extension, its flat limit. These two facts are the base of every moduli space in the minimal model program.

Section 13.1 defines flat sheaves and flat morphisms, proves that flatness is local, stable under composition and base change, and over a Dedekind scheme the same as having no associated point in a closed fibre. It constructs the flat closure of a family over the generic point of a discrete valuation ring and proves that the fibres of a flat morphism of varieties have the expected dimension. Section 13.2 defines the Hilbert polynomial of a closed subscheme of projective space over a field, proves that over a discrete valuation ring a family is flat exactly when the generic and special fibres have the same Hilbert polynomial, and deduces that the Hilbert polynomial is constant in a flat family over a connected base. Section 13.3 proves a representability criterion for functors that are Zariski sheaves with an open cover by representable subfunctors, and uses it to construct the Grassmannian and its Plücker embedding. Section 13.4 defines the Hilbert functor, proves that the functor of hypersurfaces of degree $d$ is represented by a projective space, and states the representability of the Hilbert functor, a black box of P8 Ch. 1.

In the minimal model program flat families appear in three places. The Hilbert scheme and the Hom scheme of P8 Ch. 1 are the parameter spaces of rational curves in bend and break (Volume 2), and their dimension estimates come from flat families of curves. The flat limit over a discrete valuation ring is the algebraic form of a degeneration, used in the semistable reduction and in the moduli of stable pairs (Volume 13). The Grassmannian is the first example of a scheme defined by a functor, and the model for every representability argument later.

The chapter follows Vakil, Chapter 24, and Hartshorne, Section III.9, with the Stacks Project for the algebra. It uses P1 Ch. 1, 2, 4, 5 and 13, and Chapters 2 to 7 of this volume.
