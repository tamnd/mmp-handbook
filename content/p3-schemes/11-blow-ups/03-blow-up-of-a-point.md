## The blow-up of a point of the plane

### Statement

Let $k$ be a field, $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ with coordinates $x, y, z$, $p = [0 : 0 : 1]$, and $\pi \colon \tilde X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2_k \to \mathbb{P}^2_k$ the blow-up of the reduced point $p$, with exceptional divisor $E$. On $D_+(z) = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y]$ write $x$ and $y$ for $x/z$ and $y/z$. For a line $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ write $H = \pi^* L$ when $p \notin L$.

**Theorem P3.11.17 (the blow-up of a point of the plane).**

1. $\tilde X$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ to $W = V_+(x v - y u) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k \times \mathbb{P}^1_k$, with coordinates $[u : v]$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$ and $\pi$ the first projection. Under this isomorphism $E = \{p\} \times \mathbb{P}^1_k$.
2. $\tilde X$ is a smooth projective integral surface over $k$, $\pi$ is birational, and $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$.
3. The second projection $q \colon \tilde X \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$ has fibre over $[a : b] \in \mathbb{P}^1(k)$ the strict transform of the line $V_+(b x - a y)$ through $p$, and each such fibre is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k$.
4. $\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(\tilde X) = \mathbb{Z} H \oplus \mathbb{Z} E$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.11.18 (multiplicity at a point).** Let $f \in k[x, y]$ be nonzero, $f = \sum_{j \geq 0} f_j$ with $f_j$ homogeneous of degree $j$. The *multiplicity* of $f$ at the origin is the least $m$ with $f_m \neq 0$, and $f_m$ is the *initial form*. For a curve $C = V_+(F) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$, $F$ homogeneous of degree $d$, the multiplicity of $C$ at $p$ is that of $f = F(x, y, 1)$. The lines $V(\ell)$ for the linear factors $\ell$ of $f_m$ are the *tangent lines* of $C$ at $p$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.11.19 (pullback of plane curves).** Let $C = V_+(F)$ be a curve of degree $d$ with multiplicity $m$ at $p$, viewed as the effective Cartier divisor of $F$ (Example P3.9.5). Let $\tilde C$ be the Cartier divisor $\pi^* C - m E$.

1. $\tilde C$ is effective, $E \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp} \tilde C$, and its Weil divisor is $\sum_i a_i \tilde C_i$, where $C = \sum_i a_i C_i$ as Weil divisors and $\tilde C_i$ is the strict transform of the prime divisor $C_i$.
2. $\pi^* C = \tilde C + m E$ and $\tilde C \sim d H - m E$.
3. The pullback of $\tilde C$ to $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$ is the divisor of the binary form $f_m(u, v)$, of degree $m$. Its support is the set of tangent directions of $C$ at $p$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Near $p$ the blow-up of the plane is the blow-up of the affine plane at the origin, computed in Section 11.1, and away from $p$ nothing changes, so the incidence variety of points and lines through $p$ is the whole blow-up. Its charts are planes, so it is smooth, and removing $E$ gives back the plane minus a point, whose class group is $\mathbb{Z}$. So the class group of the blow-up is generated by a line and $E$. A curve through $p$ of multiplicity $m$ has an equation that vanishes to order $m$ along $E$, and dividing by $m$ powers of the equation of $E$ leaves the strict transform. Its restriction to $E$ is the initial form, so the strict transform meets $E$ at the tangent directions.

### Examples

**Example P3.11.20 (lines and conics).** A line $L$ through $p$ has multiplicity $1$, so $\pi^* L = \tilde L + E$ and $\tilde L \sim H - E$. Two such lines meet only at $p$, and their strict transforms are distinct fibres of $q$, so they are disjoint. A line $L'$ not through $p$ has $\pi^* L' = H$, which is disjoint from $E$. A smooth conic $Q$ through $p$, such as $V_+(xz - y^2)$, has $f = x - y^2$ of multiplicity $1$ with initial form $x$, so $\tilde Q \sim 2H - E$ and $\tilde Q$ meets $E$ at the point $[u : v] = [0 : 1]$, the direction of the tangent line $V(x)$.

**Example P3.11.21 (the nodal cubic).** Let $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$ and $C = V_+(y^2 z - x^2(x + z))$, so $f = y^2 - x^2 - x^3$, of multiplicity $2$ with $f_2 = y^2 - x^2 = (y - x)(y + x)$. By Theorem P3.11.19, $\pi^* C = \tilde C + 2E$, $\tilde C \sim 3H - 2E$, and $\tilde C \cdot E$ is the divisor of $v^2 - u^2$ on $E$, the two points $[1 : 1]$ and $[1 : -1]$. Example P3.11.13 shows that $\tilde C$ is smooth near $E$, so $\tilde C$ is smooth, as $C \setminus \{p\}$ is smooth. It is the normalization of $C$, a curve isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ by Exercise 4.

**Example P3.11.22 (the cuspidal cubic).** Let $C = V_+(y^2 z - x^3)$, so $f = y^2 - x^3$ has multiplicity $2$ and $f_2 = y^2$. Then $\tilde C \sim 3H - 2E$, and $\tilde C \cdot E$ is the divisor of $v^2$, the point $[1 : 0]$ with multiplicity $2$. As in Example P3.11.14, $\tilde C$ is smooth and tangent to $E$.

**Example P3.11.23 (the tacnode).** Let $C$ be the closure of $V(y^2 - x^4)$, the union of the two conics $y = x^2$ and $y = -x^2$ (for $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$), which are tangent at the origin. Then $f_2 = y^2$, and on the chart $y = x v$ the strict transform is $V(v^2 - x^2)$, two lines crossing at $(x, v) = (0, 0)$: a node. After one blow-up the two branches still meet, at the point $[1 : 0]$ of $E$, and a second blow-up at that point separates them. The number of blow-ups needed to separate two branches measures how tangent they are.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem P3.11.17.* (1) Over $U = D_+(z) \cong \mathbb{A}^2_k$, $W \cap (U \times \mathbb{P}^1) = V_+(x v - y u) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, which is $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^2_k$ by Example P3.11.5. Over $U' = \mathbb{P}^2_k \setminus \{p\}$ the morphism $[x : y] \colon U' \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$ is defined (Example P3.10.30), and $W \cap (U' \times \mathbb{P}^1)$ is its graph. Indeed, on $D_+(x) \times \mathbb{P}^1$ the equation becomes $v = (y/x) u$, which defines the graph of $[1 : y/x]$, and on $D_+(y)$ it becomes $u = (x/y) v$. So $W \to \mathbb{P}^2$ is an isomorphism over $U'$, and its restriction over $U$ is the blow-up of $p$ in $U$.

The ideal $\mathcal{I}_p \mathcal{O}_W$ is invertible: over $U'$ it is the unit ideal, and over $U$ it is invertible by part 2 of Theorem P3.11.3. By Theorem P3.11.9 there is a unique morphism $g \colon W \to \tilde X$ over $\mathbb{P}^2_k$. Over $U'$ both $W$ and $\tilde X$ map isomorphically to $U'$ (part 3 of Theorem P3.11.3), so $g$ is an isomorphism there. Over $U$ both are $\operatorname{Bl}_p U$, by part 1 of Theorem P3.11.3 for $\tilde X$, and $g$ restricted over $U$ is a morphism $\operatorname{Bl}_p U \to \operatorname{Bl}_p U$ over $U$, so it is the identity by the uniqueness in Theorem P3.11.9. So $g$ is an isomorphism. Under it, $E = \pi^{-1}(p)$ is cut out by $x = y = 0$ in $W$, which is $\{p\} \times \mathbb{P}^1$, as both equations of $W$ vanish there.

(2) The charts of $W$ over $U$ are planes by Theorem P3.11.4, and $W$ over $U'$ is isomorphic to $U'$, so $W$ is covered by open sets that are smooth over $k$ of dimension $2$. It is integral and $\pi$ is birational by part 4 of Theorem P3.11.3. It is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^2 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, which is projective by part 2 of Corollary P3.10.21, so it is projective.

(3) The fibre of $q$ over a $k$-point $[a : b]$ is $V_+(b x - a y) \times \{[a : b]\} \subseteq W$, as the equation $x v - y u$ becomes $b x - a y$ up to a unit. It maps isomorphically to the line $L = V_+(b x - a y) \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$. It contains $\pi^{-1}(L \setminus \{p\})$ as a dense open subset, so it is the closure of this set, which is $\tilde L$ by Proposition P3.11.12. Both are integral closed subschemes, so they agree.

(4) $\tilde X$ is regular, as it is smooth over $k$ (Theorem P3.7.24), so $\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(\tilde X)$ by Corollary P3.9.11. Let $V = \tilde X \setminus E \cong \mathbb{P}^2_k \setminus \{p\}$. By Theorem P3.8.13 there is an exact sequence $\mathbb{Z} E \to \operatorname{Cl}(\tilde X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(V) \to 0$, and $\operatorname{Cl}(V) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^2_k) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by part 3 of Theorem P3.8.13 and Theorem P3.8.20, generated by the restriction of a line $L'$ with $p \notin L'$. The divisor $H = \pi^* L'$ is the prime divisor $\pi^{-1}(L')$, as $\pi$ is an isomorphism near $L'$, and it restricts to $L' \cap V$. So $H$ and $E$ generate $\operatorname{Cl}(\tilde X)$. Let $a H + b E = \operatorname{div}(g)$. Restricting to $V$ gives $a [L'] = 0$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(V) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, so $a = 0$. Then $g$ has no zeros or poles on $V$, so $g \in \mathcal{O}(V)^*$ by Proposition P3.8.4. By Theorem P3.7.33, $\mathcal{O}(V) = \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{P}^2_k) = k$ (Theorem P3.2.11), so $g \in k^*$ and $b = 0$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.11.19.* Write $\operatorname{ord}_E$ for the order of vanishing along the prime divisor $E$ (Proposition P3.8.2). On the chart $W_1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, t]$ of $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^2_k$ with $y = x t$, $t = v/u$, the divisor $E$ is $V(x)$ and
$$
\pi^* f = f(x, x t) = \sum_{j \geq m} x^j f_j(1, t) = x^m \tilde f , \qquad \tilde f = f_m(1, t) + x \, g(x, t) .
$$
The polynomial $f_m(1, t) = \sum_i c_i t^i$, for $f_m = \sum_i c_i x^{m - i} y^i$, is nonzero, as $f_m \neq 0$. So $x \nmid \tilde f$ in the factorial ring $k[x, t]$, and $\operatorname{ord}_E(\pi^* f) = m$. The same holds on the chart $W_2 = \operatorname{Spec} k[y, s]$ with $x = y s$, $s = u/v$, where $\pi^* f = y^m \tilde f_2$ with $\tilde f_2 = f_m(s, 1) + y \, g_2(y, s)$.

(1) On $W_1$ the Cartier divisor $\pi^* C - mE$ has the local equation $\pi^* f / x^m = \tilde f$, which is regular, and similarly on $W_2$. Away from $E$, $\pi^* C - m E = \pi^* C$ is effective, by part 2 of Proposition P3.9.27. So $\tilde C$ is effective, and $\operatorname{ord}_E(\tilde C) = 0$, so $E \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp} \tilde C$. The Weil divisor of $\tilde C$ has no component $E$, and on $\tilde X \setminus E \cong \mathbb{P}^2 \setminus \{p\}$ it equals that of $C$. So it is $\sum_i a_i D_i$, where $D_i$ is the closure of $\pi^{-1}(C_i \setminus \{p\})$, which is $\tilde C_i$ by Proposition P3.11.12.

(2) The first statement is the definition of $\tilde C$. For the second, $C - d L' = \operatorname{div}(F / \ell'^d)$ for a linear form $\ell'$ defining $L'$, so $\pi^* C - d H = \operatorname{div}(\pi^*(F / \ell'^d))$ by part 3 of Proposition P3.9.27. So $\pi^* C \sim d H$ and $\tilde C \sim d H - m E$.

(3) The generic point of $E$ is not in $\operatorname{Supp} \tilde C$, so the pullback to $E$ is defined (Definition P3.9.26). On $E \cap W_1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$ it has the local equation $\tilde f |_{x = 0} = f_m(1, t)$, and on $E \cap W_2 = \operatorname{Spec} k[s]$ the local equation $f_m(s, 1)$. With $t = v/u$ and $s = u/v$ these are the dehomogenizations of the binary form $f_m(u, v)$ of degree $m$, so the pullback is the divisor $D_{f_m}$ of Theorem P3.8.20 on $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$, of degree $m$. A $k$-point $[a : b]$ lies in its support if and only if $f_m(a, b) = 0$, that is, if and only if $b x - a y$ divides $f_m$, so that $V(b x - a y)$ is a tangent line. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $\tilde C$ and its pullback to $E$ for the curve $C = V_+(x y z - x^3 - y^3)$, the folium.
2. (A) Show that the strict transforms of two distinct lines through $p$ are disjoint, and that the strict transform of a line not through $p$ is disjoint from $E$.
3. (A) Let $C$ be a curve of degree $d$ with multiplicity $m$ at $p$. Show that $m \leq d$, and that $m = d$ if and only if $F$ does not involve $z$, so that over an algebraic closure of $k$ the curve $C$ is a union of lines through $p$.
4. (B) Show that the strict transform of the nodal cubic in Example P3.11.21 is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, using the parametrization $t \mapsto (t^2 - 1, t(t^2 - 1))$ and the universal property.
5. (B) Show that $q^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\tilde X}(H - E)$, and that $\pi^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\tilde X}(H)$.
6. (B) Show that $\Gamma(\tilde X, \mathcal{O}(E)) = k$, so that $E$ is the only effective divisor in its class, while $\Gamma(\tilde X, \mathcal{O}(H - E))$ has dimension $2$.
7. (C) Let $p_1, p_2$ be two distinct $k$-points of $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ and $Y$ the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ along the reduced subscheme $\{p_1, p_2\}$. Show that $\operatorname{Cl}(Y) = \mathbb{Z} H \oplus \mathbb{Z} E_1 \oplus \mathbb{Z} E_2$, and that the strict transform $\tilde L$ of the line through $p_1$ and $p_2$ satisfies $\tilde L \sim H - E_1 - E_2$ and $\Gamma(Y, \mathcal{O}(\tilde L)) = k$.

### Lean

Blow-ups, multiplicities of plane curves and the class group of a blow-up are not in Lean as stated. Mathlib has Weil divisors, `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle.weilDivisors`, which would carry part 4 of Theorem P3.11.17. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The blow-up of a point of a surface is Hartshorne, Section V.3, and for plane curves Section I.4. The literature state is theorem. In the language of intersection numbers, developed in P8, the exceptional curve satisfies $E^2 = -1$ and $H \cdot E = 0$, and the canonical divisor of $\tilde X$ is $\pi^* K_{\mathbb{P}^2} + E$, which Chapter 12 proves with differentials. A smooth rational curve with self-intersection $-1$ on a smooth surface is a $(-1)$-curve, and Castelnuovo's theorem says that every $(-1)$-curve on a smooth projective surface can be contracted to a smooth point, so that it arises in this way. Contracting $(-1)$-curves is the minimal model program for smooth surfaces, and the class of $E$ spans the extremal ray contracted by $\pi$.
