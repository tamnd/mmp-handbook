## The Rees algebra and the blow-up

### Statement

**Definition P3.11.1 (Rees algebra and blow-up).** Let $A$ be a ring and $I \subseteq A$ an ideal.

1. The *Rees algebra* of $I$ is the graded ring $R(I) = \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} I^d t^d \subseteq A[t]$, with $I^0 = A$ and $R(I)_d = I^d t^d$.
2. For $f \in I$ the *affine blow-up algebra* $A[I/f]$ is the subring of $A_f$ generated by $A$ and the fractions $a/f$ with $a \in I$.

Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme and $Z \subseteq X$ a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$, a coherent ideal sheaf by Proposition P3.5.23.

3. The *Rees algebra* of $\mathcal{I}$ is the quasi-coherent graded $\mathcal{O}_X$-algebra $\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}) = \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} \mathcal{I}^d$, with $\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})(V) = R(\mathcal{I}(V))$ on affine open sets $V$.
4. The *blow-up* of $X$ along $Z$ is $\pi \colon \operatorname{Bl}_Z X = \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_X \mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}) \to X$, the relative Proj of Definition P3.6.31. The scheme $Z$ is the *centre*.
5. The *exceptional divisor* is the closed subscheme $E = \pi^{-1}(Z) = Z \times_X \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$, with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_Z X}$, the image of $\pi^* \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_Z X}$.

Lean: Mathlib `reesAlgebra` defines $R(I)$ as a subalgebra of the polynomial ring, and `reesAlgebra.fg` proves that it is finitely generated when $I$ is (proved in Mathlib). The blow-up of a scheme is not in Lean as stated. Ideal sheaves are `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData`, and the ideal $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}$ of a preimage is `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.comap`.

**Proposition P3.11.2 (the charts of the blow-up).** Let $A$ be a ring, $I = (f_0, \dots, f_r)$ and $B = \operatorname{Proj} R(I)$.

1. $R(I)$ is generated over $A$ by the elements $f_i t$ of degree $1$, and $B = D_+(f_0 t) \cup \dots \cup D_+(f_r t)$.
2. For every $i$, $R(I)_{(f_i t)} \cong A[I/f_i] = A[f_0/f_i, \dots, f_r/f_i] \subseteq A_{f_i}$, so $D_+(f_i t) \cong \operatorname{Spec} A[I/f_i]$.
3. The surjection $A[u_0, \dots, u_r] \to R(I)$, $u_i \mapsto f_i t$, makes $B$ a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^r_A$ contained in $V_+(f_i u_j - f_j u_i : 0 \leq i < j \leq r)$.
4. On $D_+(f_i t)$ the ideal $I \mathcal{O}_B$ is generated by $f_i$, and $f_i$ is a nonzerodivisor in $A[I/f_i]$. So $I \mathcal{O}_B$ is an invertible ideal sheaf.
5. For $f \in I$ the morphism $\pi^{-1}(D(f)) \to D(f)$ is an isomorphism.
6. The exceptional divisor is $E = \operatorname{Proj} \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} I^d / I^{d+1}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.11.3 (first properties of the blow-up).** Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme and $Z \subseteq X$ a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$.

1. $\operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ exists, and $\pi$ is proper. For every open $U \subseteq X$, $\pi^{-1}(U) = \operatorname{Bl}_{Z \cap U} U$.
2. $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_Z X}$ is an invertible ideal sheaf. So $E$ is an effective Cartier divisor, a closed subscheme with invertible ideal sheaf (Proposition P3.9.2).
3. $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $X \setminus Z$.
4. If $X$ is integral and $Z \neq X$, then $\operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ is integral and $\pi$ is birational.
5. If $\mathcal{I}$ is invertible, then $\pi$ is an isomorphism. If $Z = X$, then $\operatorname{Bl}_Z X = \emptyset$.
6. For $n \geq 1$, $\operatorname{Bl}_Z X \cong \operatorname{Bl}_{Z_n} X$ over $X$, where $Z_n$ is the closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}^n$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.11.4 (blowing up a coordinate subspace).** Let $B$ be a ring, $A = B[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, $1 \leq r \leq n$, $I = (x_1, \dots, x_r)$ and $Z = V(I) \cong \mathbb{A}^{n-r}_B$.

1. For $1 \leq i \leq r$, $A[I/x_i] = B[x_i, x_j/x_i \ (j \leq r, j \neq i), x_{r+1}, \dots, x_n]$ is a polynomial ring over $B$ in these $n$ elements.
2. $\operatorname{Bl}_Z \mathbb{A}^n_B = V_+(x_i u_j - x_j u_i : 1 \leq i < j \leq r) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{r-1}_A$, with homogeneous coordinates $u_1, \dots, u_r$.
3. $E = \mathbb{P}^{r-1}_Z = Z \times \mathbb{P}^{r-1}$, and $E \to Z$ is the projection.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The Rees algebra records all powers of the ideal at once. Its Proj covers the scheme by charts, one for each generator $f_i$, and on the chart of $f_i$ the other generators become multiples of $f_i$. So the pulled back ideal is principal and generated by a nonzerodivisor: the centre has become a divisor. Away from the centre some generator is a unit, the Rees algebra is a polynomial ring in one variable, and the blow-up changes nothing. For a coordinate subspace of affine space each chart is again an affine space, and the blow-up is the incidence variety of points and lines through the centre.

### Examples

**Example P3.11.5 (the plane at the origin).** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y]$ and $Z$ the origin. By Theorem P3.11.4 with $B = k$, $n = r = 2$,
$$
\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^2_k = V_+(x v - y u) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2_k \times \mathbb{P}^1_k ,
$$
with coordinates $[u : v]$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$. The chart $u \neq 0$ is $\operatorname{Spec} k[x, y/x]$, with $(x, t) \mapsto (x, xt)$, the chart of Example P3.9.32. The chart $v \neq 0$ is $\operatorname{Spec} k[y, x/y]$. Over a point $(a, b) \neq (0, 0)$ the fibre is the one point $[u : v] = [a : b]$, the line through the origin and $(a, b)$. Over the origin the fibre is $E = \{0\} \times \mathbb{P}^1_k$, the lines through the origin. On the chart $u \neq 0$, $E = V(x)$, and on the chart $v \neq 0$, $E = V(y)$. This agrees with Example P3.6.39: for the ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$, $\operatorname{Sym} \mathfrak{m}$ and $R(\mathfrak{m})$ have the same Proj.

**Example P3.11.6 (a divisor and the unit ideal).** Let $f \in A$ be a nonzerodivisor and $I = (f)$. Then $A[I/f] = A$ and $B = D_+(f t) = \operatorname{Spec} A$, as part 5 of Theorem P3.11.3 says. For $I = A$ the element $1 \in I$ gives $A[I/1] = A$, and the blow-up is $\operatorname{Spec} A$. For $I = 0$, $R(I) = A$ is concentrated in degree $0$, so $R(I)_+ = 0$ and $\operatorname{Proj} R(I) = \emptyset$. For $A = k[x]$ and $I = (x^2)$ the centre is a double point on a line, and blowing it up does nothing. A non-reduced centre does not always disappear, as the next example shows.

**Example P3.11.7 (a non-reduced point of the plane).** Let $A = k[x, y]$ and $I = (x, y^2)$. The chart of $y^2$ is $A[x/y^2]$, and with $w = x/y^2$ the map $k[y, w] \to A[I/y^2]$ is an isomorphism, as in part 1 of Theorem P3.11.4, since $A_{y} = k[y, y^{-1}][x] \cong k[y, y^{-1}][w]$. The chart of $x$ is $A[y^2/x]$, and with $v = y^2/x$ there is a surjection $k[x, y, v]/(x v - y^2) \to A[I/x]$. The source is a domain of dimension $2$, since $xv - y^2$ is irreducible, and the target is a subring of $A_x$ of dimension $2$, as it contains $A$ and lies in $A_x$. A surjection between domains of the same finite dimension, of finite type over $k$, is an isomorphism by Theorem P3.7.4. So the chart is the quadric cone $xv = y^2$, which is singular at the origin (Example P3.7.18). Blowing up a smooth surface along a non-reduced point can produce a singular surface.

**Example P3.11.8 (two crossing lines).** Let $A = k[x, y]/(xy)$, $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $I = (x, y)$. In $A_x$ the element $y = xy/x$ is $0$, so $A_x = k[x, x^{-1}]$ and $A[I/x] = k[x]$, generated by the image of $A$ and $y/x = 0$. In the same way $A[I/y] = k[y]$. The overlap $D_+(xt) \cap D_+(yt)$ is the chart of $y/x$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A[I/x]$, and $y/x = 0$ there, so it is empty. So $\operatorname{Bl}_0 X = \mathbb{A}^1_k \sqcup \mathbb{A}^1_k$, the two lines pulled apart, and $E$ is two reduced points. The blow-up of a reducible scheme need not be connected, and part 4 of Theorem P3.11.3 needs $X$ integral. Here $\operatorname{Bl}_0 X$ is the normalization of $X$.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.11.2.* (1) $I^d$ is generated by the monomials of degree $d$ in the $f_i$, so $I^d t^d$ is generated by the monomials of degree $d$ in the $f_i t$. Part 1 of Proposition P3.6.4 gives the cover.

(2) Localization is exact, so $R(I)_{f_i t} \subseteq A[t]_{f_i t} = A_{f_i}[t, t^{-1}]$, the last equality since $t = f_i t / f_i$ and $f_i$ is a unit in $A_{f_i}$. The degree $0$ part of $R(I)_{f_i t}$ consists of the elements $a t^d / (f_i t)^d$ with $a \in I^d$, which are the elements $a / f_i^d$ of $A_{f_i}$. These form the subring generated by $A$ and the $f_j / f_i$, since every $a \in I^d$ is a sum of products $c \, f_{j_1} \cdots f_{j_d}$ with $c \in A$.

(3) The map is surjective by (1). By parts 4 and 6 of Proposition P3.6.4, $B$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^r_A$, cut out by the kernel. The kernel contains $f_i u_j - f_j u_i$, which maps to $f_i f_j t - f_j f_i t = 0$.

(4) In $A[I/f_i]$, $f_j = (f_j / f_i) f_i$, so $I A[I/f_i] = f_i A[I/f_i]$. Since $f_i$ is a unit in $A_{f_i}$, it is a nonzerodivisor in every subring of $A_{f_i}$. So the ideal sheaf $I \mathcal{O}_B$ is generated on $D_+(f_i t)$ by a nonzerodivisor, and $\mathcal{O} \to I \mathcal{O}$, $1 \mapsto f_i$, is an isomorphism there.

(5) The open set $D(f) = \operatorname{Spec} A_f$ has $\pi^{-1}(D(f)) = \operatorname{Proj} R(I_f)$ by the construction of the relative Proj (part 1 of Proposition P3.6.32), and $I_f = A_f$. Then $R(A_f) = A_f[t]$, whose Proj is $D_+(t) = \operatorname{Spec} A_f$.

(6) The closed subscheme $E$ is cut out on $D_+(f_i t)$ by $I A[I/f_i]$, which is the degree $0$ part of $(I R(I))_{f_i t}$. So $E = V_+(I R(I))$, which is $\operatorname{Proj} R(I)/I R(I)$ by part 4 of Proposition P3.6.4. In degree $d$, $(I R(I))_d = I^{d+1} t^d$, so $R(I)/I R(I) = \bigoplus_d I^d / I^{d+1}$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.11.3.* (1) $\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})$ is quasi-coherent, with $\mathcal{R}_0 = \mathcal{O}_X$ and $\mathcal{R}_1 = \mathcal{I}$ coherent, and it is generated by $\mathcal{R}_1$. By Proposition P3.6.32 the relative Proj exists, and $\pi$ is of finite type and proper. The relative Proj over $U$ is the preimage of $U$ by construction.

(2) and (3) are local on $X$, so they follow from parts 4 and 5 of Proposition P3.11.2, since $X \setminus Z$ is covered by the $D(f)$ with $f \in \mathcal{I}(V)$, $V$ affine.

(4) Let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be a nonempty affine open set, so $A$ is a domain with fraction field $K$, and $I = \mathcal{I}(V)$. If $I = 0$, then $\pi^{-1}(V) = \emptyset$. If $I \neq 0$, the charts of the nonzero generators $f_i$ cover $\pi^{-1}(V)$, as $D_+(0) = \emptyset$, and $A[I/f_i] \subseteq A_{f_i} \subseteq K$ is a domain with fraction field $K$. Two such charts meet in $D_+(f_i f_j t^2)$, which is nonempty, as it is the spectrum of the localization $A[I/f_i]_{f_j/f_i}$ of a domain at a nonzero element. So $\pi^{-1}(V)$ is integral with function field $K$. As $X$ is irreducible, $\mathcal{I}(V) \neq 0$ for every nonempty $V$, since otherwise $Z \supseteq V$ and $Z = X$. Two nonempty preimages $\pi^{-1}(V)$, $\pi^{-1}(V')$ contain the nonempty open set $\pi^{-1}(V \cap V' \setminus Z)$, which is isomorphic to $V \cap V' \setminus Z$ by (3). So $\operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ is irreducible, and it is reduced, so it is integral. The isomorphism of (3) over the dense open set $X \setminus Z$ makes $\pi$ birational.

(5) Locally $\mathcal{I} = (f)$ with $f$ a nonzerodivisor, and Example P3.11.6 applies. If $Z = X$, then $\mathcal{I} = 0$, and Example P3.11.6 applies again.

(6) $\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}^n)$ is the Veronese algebra $\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})^{(n)} = \bigoplus_d \mathcal{I}^{nd}$, so part 5 of Proposition P3.6.4 gives the isomorphism on every affine open set, compatible with restriction. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.11.4.* (1) Let $C = B[y_1, \dots, y_n]$. The ring map $A_{x_i} \to C_{y_i}$, $x_i \mapsto y_i$, $x_j \mapsto y_i y_j$ for $j \leq r$, $j \neq i$, and $x_j \mapsto y_j$ for $j > r$, is an isomorphism. Its inverse is $y_i \mapsto x_i$, $y_j \mapsto x_j / x_i$ for $j \leq r$, $j \neq i$, and $y_j \mapsto x_j$ for $j > r$, and both composites fix the generators. It sends $A[I/x_i]$, the subring generated by $A$ and the $x_j / x_i$, onto the subring generated by the $y_j$, which is $C$. So $A[I/x_i] \cong C$, a polynomial ring over $B$ in $x_i$, the $x_j/x_i$ for $j \leq r$, $j \neq i$, and the $x_j$ for $j > r$.

(2) Let $W = V_+(x_i u_j - x_j u_i)$. By part 3 of Proposition P3.11.2, $\operatorname{Bl}_Z \mathbb{A}^n_B \subseteq W$ as closed subschemes of $\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_A$, with $u_i \mapsto x_i t$. The open sets $D_+(u_i)$ cover $\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_A$. On $D_+(u_i)$ put $v_j = u_j / u_i$. The coordinate ring of $W \cap D_+(u_i)$ is $A[v_j]/J$, with $J$ generated by $x_j - x_i v_j$ and $x_j v_l - x_l v_j$, and the second kind lies in the ideal of the first kind, as $x_j v_l - x_l v_j \equiv x_i v_j v_l - x_i v_l v_j = 0$. So $A[v_j]/J = B[x_i, v_j \ (j \leq r, j \neq i), x_{r+1}, \dots, x_n]$, a polynomial ring. The closed immersion $\operatorname{Bl}_Z \mathbb{A}^n_B \cap D_+(u_i) \to W \cap D_+(u_i)$ corresponds to the surjection $A[v_j]/J \to A[I/x_i]$, $v_j \mapsto x_j / x_i$, which is the isomorphism of (1). So the two closed subschemes agree on every $D_+(u_i)$.

(3) $E$ is cut out in $W$ by $x_1, \dots, x_r$. All the equations of $W$ lie in $(x_1, \dots, x_r)$, so $E = V_+(x_1, \dots, x_r) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{r-1}_A$, which is $\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_{A/I}$, and $A/I = B[x_{r+1}, \dots, x_n]$ is the coordinate ring of $Z$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^1_k \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$ is an isomorphism, and describe the exceptional divisor.
2. (A) Let $A = k[x, y, z]$ and $I = (x, y)$. Write the two charts of the blow-up of $\mathbb{A}^3_k$ along the $z$-axis, and show that the fibre over every point of the axis is $\mathbb{P}^1$.
3. (A) Let $A = k[x, y]$ and $I = (x, y)^2$. Write the three charts of $\operatorname{Proj} R(I)$ for the generators $x^2, xy, y^2$, and identify them with charts of $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^2_k$, as part 6 of Theorem P3.11.3 predicts.
4. (B) Let $f_0, \dots, f_r$ generate $I$. Show that the line bundle $\mathcal{O}_B(1)$ on $B = \operatorname{Proj} R(I)$ is isomorphic to $I \mathcal{O}_B$: on $D_+(f_i t)$ send the generator $f_i t$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ to $f_i$, and check that the gluings agree.
5. (B) Let $A = k[x, y]$ and $I = (x^2, y^3)$. Compute the charts of the blow-up and decide which are smooth.
6. (B) Let $X = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y]/(y^2)$, the double line, and $Z$ the origin. Show that the chart of $y$ is empty, that the blow-up is $\operatorname{Spec} k[x, w]/(w^2)$ with $w = y/x$, and that it is not the blow-up of the reduced line.
7. (C) Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $f_0, \dots, f_r$ a regular sequence. Show that the kernel of $A[u_0, \dots, u_r] \to R(I)$ is generated by the $f_i u_j - f_j u_i$. Start with $r = 1$, where $f_0 u_1 - f_1 u_0$ generates the kernel because $f_0$ is a nonzerodivisor modulo $f_1$.

### Lean

Mathlib has the Rees algebra of an ideal, `reesAlgebra`, with `reesAlgebra.fg` and `adjoin_monomial_eq_reesAlgebra`, which says that $R(I)$ is generated by $I t$, the ring part of part 1 of Proposition P3.11.2. The relative Proj and the blow-up of a scheme are not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Blowing up a point of the plane goes back to the resolution of singularities of plane curves by the Italian school and by Noether, and the construction as Proj of the Rees algebra is Grothendieck's. Hartshorne treats the blow-up in Section II.7 and, for varieties, in Section I.4. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program a blow-up is the basic birational morphism: Hironaka's resolution of singularities is a sequence of blow-ups along smooth centres, and the log resolutions used to define discrepancies are built from them. The divisorial contractions of the program are, in the simplest cases, inverse to blow-ups.
