## The universal property and strict transforms

### Statement

Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme, $Z \subseteq X$ a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$, and $\pi \colon \operatorname{Bl}_Z X \to X$ the blow-up. For a morphism $f \colon T \to X$, $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_T$ is the image of $f^* \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{O}_T$, the ideal sheaf of $f^{-1}(Z) = Z \times_X T$.

**Theorem P3.11.9 (the universal property of the blow-up).** Let $f \colon T \to X$ be a morphism such that $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_T$ is an invertible ideal sheaf. Then there is exactly one morphism $g \colon T \to \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ with $\pi \circ g = f$. It satisfies $g^{-1}(E) = f^{-1}(Z)$ as closed subschemes. The morphism $\pi$ itself has this property, so $\operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ is the final object among the $X$-schemes on which $\mathcal{I}$ becomes invertible.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P3.11.10 (functoriality).** Let $h \colon Y \to X$ be a morphism of Noetherian schemes and $Z_Y = h^{-1}(Z)$, with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_Y$.

1. There is exactly one morphism $\tilde h \colon \operatorname{Bl}_{Z_Y} Y \to \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ with $\pi \circ \tilde h = h \circ \pi_Y$.
2. If $h$ is a closed immersion, then $\tilde h$ is a closed immersion.
3. If $h$ is an open immersion, then $\tilde h$ is an open immersion onto $\pi^{-1}(h(Y))$.
4. Let $\mathcal{J}$ be an invertible ideal sheaf on $X$, and $Z'$ the closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{J}$. Then $\operatorname{Bl}_{Z'} X \cong \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ over $X$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.11.11 (strict and total transform).** Let $Y \subseteq X$ be a closed subscheme. The *total transform* of $Y$ is $\pi^{-1}(Y) = Y \times_X \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$. The *strict transform* of $Y$ is the closed subscheme $\tilde Y = \operatorname{Bl}_{Z \cap Y} Y \subseteq \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$, where $Z \cap Y = Z \times_X Y$ and the inclusion is the closed immersion of part 2 of Corollary P3.11.10.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.11.12 (the strict transform as a closure).** Let $Y \subseteq X$ be an integral closed subscheme with $Y \not\subseteq Z$. Then $\tilde Y$ is integral, it is the closure of $\pi^{-1}(Y \setminus Z)$ with its reduced structure, and $\pi|_{\tilde Y} \colon \tilde Y \to Y$ is proper and birational. As sets, $\pi^{-1}(Y) = \tilde Y \cup \pi^{-1}(Y \cap Z)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Locally the ideal is generated by finitely many functions $f_0, \dots, f_r$. If their pullbacks generate an invertible ideal on $T$, then near every point of $T$ one of them, say $f_i$, generates it, and the ratios $f_j / f_i$ are regular functions there. These ratios are exactly the coordinates of the chart of $f_i$ in the blow-up, so they give the morphism, and they are forced, since $f_i$ is a nonzerodivisor. Every construction with blow-ups follows from this property: maps between blow-ups, the blow-up of a subscheme inside the blow-up of the ambient scheme, and the independence of the result from invertible factors of the ideal. The strict transform of a subvariety is its own blow-up, and it is the closure of the part away from the centre.

### Examples

**Example P3.11.13 (the node).** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2_k$ with $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, $Z$ the origin and $Y = V(y^2 - x^2(x + 1))$. On the chart $u \neq 0$ of Example P3.11.5, with $y = x v$, the total transform is $V(x^2 v^2 - x^2(x + 1)) = V(x^2) \cup V(v^2 - x - 1)$: twice the exceptional line and one more curve. The strict transform is $\operatorname{Bl}_0 Y$. With $A = k[x, y]/(y^2 - x^2(x + 1))$, the ring $A[I/x]$ is generated by $x$ and $v = y/x$, and $v^2 = x + 1$. So $A[I/x] = k[v]$, the normalization of Example P3.7.38, and the strict transform is $V(v^2 - x - 1)$ on this chart, a smooth curve. It meets $E = V(x)$ in the two points $v = \pm 1$, the two tangent directions $y = \pm x$ of the node. On the chart $v \neq 0$, with $x = y w$, the strict transform is $V(1 - w^2(y w + 1))$. Its points on $E = V(y)$ are $w = \pm 1$, the same two points, and the direction $w = 0$, that is $[0 : 1]$, is not on it. So $\tilde Y$ lies in the chart $u \neq 0$, and $\tilde Y = \operatorname{Spec} k[v] \cong \mathbb{A}^1_k$: one blow-up resolves the node.

**Example P3.11.14 (the cusp).** Let $Y = V(y^2 - x^3)$. With $y = x v$ the total transform is $V(x^2(v^2 - x))$, and the strict transform is $V(v^2 - x) \cong \mathbb{A}^1_k$, with coordinate $v$. It meets $E = V(x)$ only at $v = 0$, the one tangent direction $y = 0$, and the restriction of $x$ to $\tilde Y$ is $v^2$. So $\tilde Y$ is tangent to $E$: one blow-up makes the cusp smooth, but the strict transform and the exceptional line meet with multiplicity $2$. On the chart $v \neq 0$, with $x = y w$, the curve is $1 = y w^3$, which does not meet $E$.

**Example P3.11.15 (the quadric cone).** Let $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$, $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $I = (x, y, z)$, the vertex. In $A[I/x]$ put $z' = z/x$. Then $y/x = xy/x^2 = z'^2$, so $A[I/x]$ is generated by $x$ and $z'$. Its fraction field is that of $A$, of transcendence degree $2$, so $x$ and $z'$ are algebraically independent and $A[I/x] = k[x, z']$. In the same way $A[I/y] = k[y, z'']$ with $z'' = z/y$. The chart of $z$ lies in the other two: $(xt)(yt) = (zt)^2$ in $R(I)$, so $xt$ is a unit where $zt$ is. On the overlap of the charts of $x$ and $y$, $z'' = 1/z'$ and $y = x z'^2$. So $\operatorname{Bl}_0 X$ is smooth, glued from two planes. The exceptional divisor is $V(x) = \operatorname{Spec} k[z']$ on the first chart and $V(y) = \operatorname{Spec} k[z'']$ on the second, glued by $z'' = 1/z'$: it is $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. By part 6 of Proposition P3.11.2, it is $\operatorname{Proj} k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$, the conic. The blow-up of the vertex is the minimal resolution of the $A_1$ singularity, and Section 11.4 identifies it with an open subset of the Hirzebruch surface $F_2$.

**Example P3.11.16 (the hypothesis of the universal property).** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2_k$, $Z$ the origin and $f \colon \mathbb{A}^1_k \to X$, $s \mapsto (s, s^2)$. Then $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O} = (s, s^2) = (s)$ is invertible, and $g$ is $s \mapsto ((s, s^2), [1 : s])$ on the chart $u \neq 0$, which sends $0$ to the point $[1 : 0]$ of $E$, the tangent direction of the parabola. For the inclusion $f \colon \operatorname{Spec} k \to X$ of the origin, $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O} = 0$, which is not invertible. Every point of $E$ gives a morphism $\operatorname{Spec} k \to \operatorname{Bl}_0 X$ over $f$, so without the hypothesis there is no uniqueness.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem P3.11.9.* First let $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$, with $I = \mathcal{I}(X) = (f_0, \dots, f_r)$, and let $\mathcal{L} = I \mathcal{O}_T$.

Step 1, the cover. Let $T_i \subseteq T$ be the set of points $p$ where $f_i$ generates $\mathcal{L}_p$. Near a point of $T$, $\mathcal{L}$ is free on a generator $e$, and $\mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{L}$, $1 \mapsto e$, is an isomorphism, so $e$ is a nonzerodivisor on every smaller open set. Write $f_j = c_j e$ there. Then $T_i$ is the open set where $c_i$ is a unit. At a point $p$, $e = \sum b_j f_j = (\sum b_j c_j) e$ for some $b_j \in \mathcal{O}_{T, p}$, so $\sum b_j c_j = 1$ and some $c_j$ is a unit in the local ring. So the $T_i$ cover $T$. On $T_i$, $f_i$ generates $\mathcal{L}$, so it is a nonzerodivisor, and $f_j = a_j f_i$ for unique $a_j \in \Gamma(T_i, \mathcal{O})$.

Step 2, the morphism on $T_i$. The ring map $A[u_j : j \neq i] \to \Gamma(T_i, \mathcal{O})$, $u_j \mapsto a_j$, extends $f^\#$. Let $P$ be in the kernel of $A[u_j] \to A_{f_i}$, $u_j \mapsto f_j / f_i$, of degree at most $d$ in the $u_j$. Then $f_i^d P(f_j / f_i)$ is an element of $A$ that is $0$ in $A_{f_i}$, so $f_i^{m + d} P(f_j / f_i) = 0$ in $A$ for some $m$. Its image in $\Gamma(T_i, \mathcal{O})$ is $f_i^{m + d} P(a_j)$, as $f_j = a_j f_i$. As $f_i$ is a nonzerodivisor on $T_i$, $P(a_j) = 0$. So the map factors through $A[I/f_i]$, and Theorem P3.1.22 gives $g_i \colon T_i \to \operatorname{Spec} A[I/f_i] = D_+(f_i t)$ over $X$.

Step 3, uniqueness on charts. Let $g \colon T \to \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ be any morphism over $X$. On $D_+(f_l t)$, $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}$ is generated by $f_l$ (Proposition P3.11.2), so on $g^{-1}(D_+(f_l t))$, $\mathcal{L}$ is generated by $f_l$, and $g^{-1}(D_+(f_l t)) \subseteq T_l$. Conversely let $p \in T_i$ and $g(p) \in D_+(f_l t)$. Near $p$, $f_i = g^\#(f_i / f_l) f_l$ and $f_l = a_l f_i$, so $f_i = g^\#(f_i / f_l) a_l f_i$, and $g^\#(f_i / f_l) a_l = 1$, as $f_i$ is a nonzerodivisor near $p$. So $f_i / f_l$ is a unit at $g(p)$, and $g(p) \in D_+(f_i t)$. So $g^{-1}(D_+(f_i t)) = T_i$. On $T_i$, $g^\#(f_j / f_i)$ satisfies $f_i \, g^\#(f_j / f_i) = f_j = f_i a_j$, so $g^\#(f_j / f_i) = a_j$, and $g|_{T_i} = g_i$ by Theorem P3.1.22.

Step 4, gluing. On $T_i \cap T_j$ the morphisms $g_i$ and $g_j$ both land in $D_+(f_i t) \cap D_+(f_j t)$, as $a_j$ is a unit there, and both are determined by $f_l / f_i \mapsto a_l$, since $f_l / f_j = (f_l / f_i)(f_j / f_i)^{-1}$. So they agree, and the $g_i$ glue to $g$ by Proposition P3.2.4. Step 3 shows that $g$ is unique.

For general $X$ cover $X$ by affine open sets $V$. By part 1 of Theorem P3.11.3, $\pi^{-1}(V) = \operatorname{Bl}_{Z \cap V} V$, so the affine case gives unique morphisms $f^{-1}(V) \to \pi^{-1}(V)$. On $f^{-1}(V \cap V')$ the two morphisms agree, by the uniqueness over the affine open subsets of $V \cap V'$. So they glue, and the glued morphism is unique.

The ideal sheaf of $g^{-1}(E)$ is $(\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}}) \mathcal{O}_T = \mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_T$, which is the ideal sheaf of $f^{-1}(Z)$. The blow-up has the property by part 2 of Theorem P3.11.3. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary P3.11.10.* (1) The ideal $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_{Z_Y} Y} = (\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_Y) \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_{Z_Y} Y}$ is invertible by part 2 of Theorem P3.11.3, so Theorem P3.11.9 applies to $h \circ \pi_Y$.

(2) The question is local on $X$, so let $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$, $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A/\mathfrak{a}$ and $I = \mathcal{I}(X)$. Then $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_Y$ corresponds to $\bar I = I (A/\mathfrak{a})$, and $I^d \to \bar I^d$ is surjective. So $R(I) \to R(\bar I)$ is a surjection of graded $A$-algebras, and it induces a closed immersion $\operatorname{Proj} R(\bar I) \to \operatorname{Proj} R(I)$ by part 4 of Proposition P3.6.4. This closed immersion lies over $h$, so it is $\tilde h$ by the uniqueness in (1).

(3) For $h$ the inclusion of an open set $U$, $\pi^{-1}(U) = \operatorname{Bl}_{Z \cap U} U$ by part 1 of Theorem P3.11.3, and the inclusion lies over $h$, so it is $\tilde h$ by the uniqueness in (1). An open immersion is an isomorphism onto an open set, so the general case follows.

(4) Let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open set on which $\mathcal{J} = (g)$ with $g$ a nonzerodivisor, and let $I = \mathcal{I}(V)$. Multiplication by $g^d$ is an isomorphism $I^d \to g^d I^d = (I g)^d$, as $g$ is a nonzerodivisor. So $a t^d \mapsto g^d a t^d$ is an isomorphism of graded $A$-algebras $R(I) \to R(I g)$, and it gives an isomorphism of the Proj over $V$. If $g$ is replaced by $u g$ with $u \in A^*$, the isomorphism changes by the graded automorphism $a t^d \mapsto u^d a t^d$ of $R(I)$. This automorphism induces the identity on $\operatorname{Proj} R(I)$: on the chart of $f t$ it sends $a t^d / (f t)^d$ to $u^d a t^d / (u f t)^d$, the same element. So the isomorphisms over the $V$ agree on overlaps and glue. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.11.12.* As $Z \cap Y \neq Y$, $\tilde Y = \operatorname{Bl}_{Z \cap Y} Y$ is integral and $\pi_Y \colon \tilde Y \to Y$ is proper and birational, by Theorem P3.11.3. By the same theorem $\pi_Y$ is an isomorphism over $Y \setminus Z$, and so is $\pi$ over $X \setminus Z$, so the closed immersion $\tilde Y \to \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ maps $\pi_Y^{-1}(Y \setminus Z)$ onto $\pi^{-1}(Y \setminus Z)$. The set $\pi_Y^{-1}(Y \setminus Z)$ is a nonempty open subset of the irreducible space $\tilde Y$, so it is dense, and its closure in $\operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ is the closed set $\tilde Y$. As $\tilde Y$ is reduced, it is the reduced induced structure on this closure by Proposition P3.3.12. The total transform is $\pi^{-1}(Y \setminus Z) \cup \pi^{-1}(Y \cap Z)$, and $\tilde Y = \pi^{-1}(Y \setminus Z) \cup (\tilde Y \cap E)$ with $\tilde Y \cap E \subseteq \pi^{-1}(Y \cap Z)$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) In Example P3.11.13, compute the strict transform of the line $V(y - x)$ and of the line $V(y)$, and check that they meet $E$ in the points $[1 : 1]$ and $[1 : 0]$.
2. (A) Let $Y = V(y^2 - x^5) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2_k$. Show that the strict transform after one blow-up of the origin is again a cusp, and that two blow-ups make it smooth.
3. (A) Let $f \colon \mathbb{A}^1_k \to \mathbb{A}^2_k$, $s \mapsto (s^2, s^3)$. Compute the lift $g$ to $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^2_k$ and check that it is an isomorphism onto the strict transform of the cusp.
4. (B) Let $X = \mathbb{A}^3_k$, $Z$ the origin and $Y$ the cone $V(xy - z^2)$. Show that the strict transform of $Y$ in $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^3_k$ is the blow-up of Example P3.11.15, and that its intersection with $E \cong \mathbb{P}^2_k$ is the conic $V_+(xy - z^2)$.
5. (B) Show that the blow-up of $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ along the ideal $(x^2, xy) = (x)(x, y)$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ to $\operatorname{Bl}_0 \mathbb{A}^2_k$, using part 4 of Corollary P3.11.10, and also by computing the charts.
6. (B) Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme and $Z_1, Z_2$ closed subschemes. Show that the blow-up of $\operatorname{Bl}_{Z_1} X$ along the preimage of $Z_2$ and the blow-up of $\operatorname{Bl}_{Z_2} X$ along the preimage of $Z_1$ are both isomorphic over $X$ to the blow-up along the ideal $\mathcal{I}_1 \mathcal{I}_2$.
7. (C) Let $A \to A'$ be a flat ring map and $I \subseteq A$ an ideal. Show that $I^d \otimes_A A' \cong (I A')^d$, that $R(I) \otimes_A A' \cong R(I A')$, and that $\operatorname{Bl}_{V(IA')} \operatorname{Spec} A' \cong \operatorname{Bl}_{V(I)} \operatorname{Spec} A \times_A \operatorname{Spec} A'$. Find an example with $A \to A'$ not flat where the two sides differ.

### Lean

The ideal sheaf of a preimage is `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.comap`, defined from the fibre product with the closed subscheme, and the closed subscheme of an ideal sheaf is `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.subscheme`. The blow-up, its universal property and strict transforms are not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The universal property is Hartshorne, Proposition II.7.14, and the functoriality with the closed immersion of strict transforms is Hartshorne, Corollary II.7.15. The literature state is theorem. The universal property makes the blow-up the smallest modification that turns a closed subscheme into a Cartier divisor, and this is how the program uses it: a log resolution is a proper birational morphism on which the exceptional locus and the strict transform of a boundary form a divisor with simple normal crossings, and one builds it by blowing up until all the relevant ideals are invertible. Hartshorne, Theorem II.7.17, shows conversely that every projective birational morphism of quasi-projective varieties is a blow-up of some ideal sheaf.
