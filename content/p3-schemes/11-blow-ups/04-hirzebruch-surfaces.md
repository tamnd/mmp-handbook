## Hirzebruch surfaces

### Statement

Let $k$ be a field and $n \geq 0$. Let $F_n = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(n))$ over $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, with $\pi \colon F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$, as in Example P3.6.37. Over $U_0 = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$ and $U_1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[s]$ it has the charts $V_0 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times U_0$ with coordinates $([a : b], t)$ and $V_1 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times U_1$ with coordinates $([a' : b'], s)$, glued by
$$
s = t^{-1}, \qquad [a' : b'] = [a : t^n b] .
$$

**Definition P3.11.24 (the curves on $F_n$).** The *negative section* is $C_- = V(b) \cup V(b')$, the *positive section* is $C_+ = V(a) \cup V(a')$, and the *fibre* over $c \in \mathbb{P}^1$ is $F_c = \pi^{-1}(c)$. Write $F_0 = V(t)$ and $F_\infty = V(s)$. On $V_0 \setminus C_+$ and $V_0 \setminus C_-$ put $r = b/a$ and $w = a/b$, and on $V_1$ put $r' = b'/a'$ and $w' = a'/b'$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.11.25 (structure of the Hirzebruch surfaces).**

1. $F_n$ is a smooth proper integral surface over $k$. It contains $V_0 \setminus C_+ = \operatorname{Spec} k[r, t] \cong \mathbb{A}^2_k$ as a dense open subset, so it is rational.
2. $C_-$ and $C_+$ are disjoint, and $\pi$ restricts to isomorphisms $C_\pm \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$. Every fibre over a $k$-point is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k$.
3. $\operatorname{div}(b/a) = C_- - C_+ + n F_\infty$. So $C_+ \sim C_- + n F$, where $F$ is the class of a fibre over a $k$-point.
4. $\operatorname{Pic}(F_n) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(F_n) = \mathbb{Z} F \oplus \mathbb{Z} C_-$.
5. Let $n \geq 1$. Then $\Gamma(F_n \setminus C_-, \mathcal{O}) = k$. So $\Gamma(F_n, \mathcal{O}(m C_-)) = k$ for every $m \geq 0$, and $m C_-$ is the only effective divisor in its class.
6. $\Gamma(F_n \setminus C_+, \mathcal{O}) = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} r^i \, k[t]_{\leq n i}$, where $k[t]_{\leq e}$ is the space of polynomials of degree at most $e$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.11.26 (the blow-up of the plane is $F_1$).** Let $\tilde X = \operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2_k$, $p = [0 : 0 : 1]$, with $q \colon \tilde X \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$ as in Theorem P3.11.17. There is an isomorphism $\tilde X \cong F_1$ over $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. It sends $E$ to $C_-$, the strict transform of a line through $p$ to a fibre, and $\pi^* V_+(z)$ to $C_+$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The Hirzebruch surface is glued from two copies of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{A}^1$, and the twist $t^n$ in the gluing decides everything. Near the negative section the fibre coordinate is multiplied by $t^{-n}$, so a function regular off this section would be a polynomial whose degree is bounded by a negative number, and only constants survive: the negative section cannot move. Near the positive section the fibre coordinate is multiplied by $t^n$, and there are many functions. The class group is generated by a fibre and a section, since the complement of a fibre and a section is a plane. The blow-up of the plane at a point has the same two charts and the same gluing with $n = 1$, so it is $F_1$.

### Examples

**Example P3.11.27 (the quadric surface).** For $n = 0$ the gluing is $[a' : b'] = [a : b]$, so $F_0 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$. Then $C_-$ and $C_+$ are two fibres of the second projection, and $C_+ \sim C_-$ by part 3 of Theorem P3.11.25, as both are pullbacks of points of $\mathbb{P}^1$. Here $\Gamma(F_0 \setminus C_-, \mathcal{O}) = k[w]$, and part 5 fails: $C_-$ moves in the pencil of fibres. The class group is $\mathbb{Z}^2$, as in Example P3.9.12.

**Example P3.11.28 (the plane as a contraction of $F_1$).** By Theorem P3.11.26 the blow-up $\pi \colon \tilde X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ is a morphism $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^2_k$ that contracts $C_-$ to a point and is an isomorphism elsewhere. The two bases of the class group match: $H = C_+$, $E = C_-$, and the fibre class is $H - E$. The relation $C_+ \sim C_- + F$ of Theorem P3.11.25 is $H \sim E + (H - E)$. So $F_1$ has two contractions: the $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ and the blow-down $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$.

**Example P3.11.29 (the cone and $F_2$).** The blow-up $Y$ of the vertex of the cone $xy = z^2$ in Example P3.11.15 is glued from $\operatorname{Spec} k[x, z']$ and $\operatorname{Spec} k[y, z'']$ along $z'' = 1/z'$, $y = z'^2 x$. The open set $F_2 \setminus C_+$ is glued from $V_0 \setminus C_+ = \operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$ and $V_1 \setminus C_+ = \operatorname{Spec} k[r', s]$ along $s = 1/t$, $r' = t^2 r$, since $r' = b'/a' = t^2 b / a$. So $(x, z') \mapsto (r, t)$ and $(y, z'') \mapsto (r', s)$ define an isomorphism $Y \cong F_2 \setminus C_+$, which sends the exceptional curve $V(x)$ to $C_- = V(r)$. By part 6 of Theorem P3.11.25 with $n = 2$, the functions $1, r, r t, r t^2$ are regular on $F_2 \setminus C_+$, and they correspond to $1, x, z, y$ on $Y$, since $z = x z'$ and $y = x z'^2$. This is the map $Y \to \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$.

**Example P3.11.30 (moving the positive section).** For a polynomial $c(t)$ of degree at most $n$, the curve $\sigma_c = V(a - c(t) b)$ on $V_0$ is a section of $\pi$ over $U_0$. On $V_1$, $a'/b' = t^{-n} a/b$, so $\sigma_c$ is $V(a' - s^n c(1/s) b')$, and $s^n c(1/s)$ is a polynomial in $s$. So $\sigma_c$ extends to a section of $\pi$. It is disjoint from $C_-$, and it meets $C_+$ at the zeros of the binary form $u_1^n c(u_0/u_1)$ of degree $n$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$, with $t = u_0/u_1$. Let $\varphi = (a - c(t) b)/a = 1 - c(t) r$. On $\operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$ it is regular with zero locus $\sigma_c$, and on $\operatorname{Spec} k[w, t]$ it is $(w - c(t))/w$. On $V_1$, with $\tilde c(s) = s^n c(1/s)$, it is $1 - \tilde c(s) r'$ and $(w' - \tilde c(s))/w'$. So $\operatorname{div}(\varphi) = \sigma_c - C_+$, and $\sigma_c \sim C_+$. So the positive section moves in a family of dimension $n + 1$, while the negative section does not move at all, by part 5 of Theorem P3.11.25.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem P3.11.25.* (1) The open sets $V_0 \setminus C_\pm$ and $V_1 \setminus C_\pm$ are planes: for instance $V_0 \setminus C_+ = \{a \neq 0\} = \operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$. So $F_n$ is covered by four open sets smooth over $k$ of dimension $2$. The map $\pi$ is proper by Proposition P3.6.32, and $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is proper over $k$, so $F_n$ is proper over $k$. The four planes are integral, and any two of them meet in a nonempty open set, since each contains the open set $\{a b t \neq 0\}$. So $F_n$ is irreducible and reduced, hence integral, and the plane $\operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$ is dense.

(2) $C_-$ is $\{[1 : 0]\} \times U_0$ on $V_0$ and $\{[1 : 0]\} \times U_1$ on $V_1$, and the gluing respects these, so $\pi|_{C_-}$ is glued from the isomorphisms $\{[1 : 0]\} \times U_i \to U_i$. The same holds for $C_+$ with $[0 : 1]$. They are disjoint, as $[1 : 0] \neq [0 : 1]$. The fibre over a $k$-point of $U_i$ is $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \{\text{point}\}$.

(3) Compute on the four planes. On $\operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$, $b/a = r$, with divisor $V(r) = C_- \cap V_0$. On $\operatorname{Spec} k[w, t]$, $b/a = 1/w$, with divisor $-V(w) = -C_+ \cap V_0$. On $\operatorname{Spec} k[r', s]$, $b/a = t^{-n} b'/a' = s^n r'$, with divisor $n V(s) + V(r')$. On $\operatorname{Spec} k[w', s]$, $b/a = s^n / w'$, with divisor $n V(s) - V(w')$. These glue to $C_- - C_+ + n F_\infty$. Fibres over $k$-points are linearly equivalent: for $c, c' \in \mathbb{P}^1(k)$, $c - c' = \operatorname{div}(h)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$ for a ratio $h$ of linear forms (Theorem P3.8.20), and $F_c - F_{c'} = \operatorname{div}(\pi^* h)$ by part 3 of Proposition P3.9.27, as $\pi^* c = F_c$: the local equation of $c$ pulls back to a local equation of the reduced fibre, by the product structure of $V_0$ and $V_1$.

(4) $F_n$ is smooth, so regular and locally factorial, and $\operatorname{Pic}(F_n) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(F_n)$ by Corollary P3.9.11. The complement of the plane $\operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$ is $C_+ \cup F_\infty$. As $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{A}^2_k) = 0$ (Proposition P3.8.12), Theorem P3.8.13 shows that $C_+$ and $F_\infty$ generate $\operatorname{Cl}(F_n)$, and by (3) so do $C_-$ and $F = F_\infty$. Let $\alpha F_\infty + \beta C_- = \operatorname{div}(g)$. Restrict to $V_0 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{A}^1$. There $C_- \cap V_0$ is the preimage of the point $[1 : 0] \in \mathbb{P}^1$, and by Theorem P3.8.14 the pullback $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^1) \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{A}^1)$ is an isomorphism. The class of a point generates $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, so $\beta = 0$. Then $g$ has no zeros or poles on $V_0$, so it is a unit of $\Gamma(V_0, \mathcal{O}) = \Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1_{k[t]}, \mathcal{O}) = k[t]$ (Theorem P3.2.11 and Proposition P3.8.4). So $g \in k^*$ and $\alpha = 0$.

(5) $F_n \setminus C_-$ is the union of $\operatorname{Spec} k[w, t]$ and $\operatorname{Spec} k[w', s]$, glued along $t s = 1$ with $w' = a'/b' = t^{-n} w$, that is $w = t^n w'$. A regular function is an element $\sum c_{i j} w^i t^j$ of $k[w, t]$ that lies in $k[w', s]$ inside $k[w, t, t^{-1}]$. As $w^i t^j = w'^i s^{-n i - j}$, and the monomials $w'^i s^e$ are linearly independent, this requires $n i + j \leq 0$ for all $(i, j)$ with $c_{i j} \neq 0$. For $n \geq 1$ and $i, j \geq 0$ only $i = j = 0$ is left, so the function is constant. A section of $\mathcal{O}(m C_-)$ is a rational function $g$ with $\operatorname{div}(g) + m C_- \geq 0$ (Theorem P3.8.29), so $g$ has no poles on $F_n \setminus C_-$ and is regular there by Proposition P3.8.4. So $g \in k$. An effective divisor $D \sim m C_-$ is $m C_- + \operatorname{div}(g)$ for such a $g$, so $D = m C_-$.

(6) $F_n \setminus C_+$ is the union of $\operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$ and $\operatorname{Spec} k[r', s]$ with $r' = t^n r$, so $r = s^n r'$, and $r^i t^j = r'^i s^{n i - j}$. As in (5), $\sum c_{i j} r^i t^j$ is regular on $F_n \setminus C_+$ if and only if $j \leq n i$ whenever $c_{i j} \neq 0$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.11.26.* By Theorem P3.11.17, $\tilde X = V_+(x v - y u) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, with $q$ the second projection. Over $U_0 = \{u \neq 0\}$, with $t = v/u$, the equation is $y = t x$, and $q^{-1}(U_0) = V_+(y - t x) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_{k[t]}$. The map $k[t][x, y, z]/(y - t x) \to k[t][a, b]$, $x \mapsto b$, $y \mapsto t b$, $z \mapsto a$, is an isomorphism of graded rings, so by parts 4 and 6 of Proposition P3.6.4 it gives an isomorphism $V_0 = \mathbb{P}^1_{U_0} \to q^{-1}(U_0)$, $([a : b], t) \mapsto ([b : t b : a], [1 : t])$. Over $U_1 = \{v \neq 0\}$, with $s = u/v$, the equation is $x = s y$, and in the same way $([a' : b'], s) \mapsto ([s b' : b' : a'], [s : 1])$ is an isomorphism $V_1 \to q^{-1}(U_1)$. On the overlap, with $s = t^{-1}$, the point $([a : t b], s)$ goes to $[s t b : t b : a] = [b : t b : a]$, the image of $([a : b], t)$. So the two isomorphisms are compatible with the gluing $[a' : b'] = [a : t b]$ of $F_1$, and they glue to $F_1 \cong \tilde X$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$. Under it, $E = V(x, y)$ corresponds to $b = 0$, that is $C_-$, and $\pi^* V_+(z) = V(z)$ corresponds to $a = 0$, that is $C_+$. The fibres of $q$ are the strict transforms of the lines through $p$ by Theorem P3.11.17. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $F_n \setminus (C_- \cup C_+)$ is the total space of a line bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ minus its zero section, and that it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k \times \mathbb{G}_m$ over $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ only for $n = 0$, by computing its global units.
2. (A) Use part 6 of Theorem P3.11.25 to show that $\Gamma(F_n, \mathcal{O}(C_+))$ has dimension $n + 2$, with basis $1$ and $r t^j$ for $0 \leq j \leq n$. The function $r^i$ has a pole of order $i$ along $C_+$.
3. (A) Check that the sections $\sigma_c$ of Example P3.11.30 for $n = 1$ correspond under Theorem P3.11.26 to the strict transforms of the lines not through $p$.
4. (B) For $n = 1$, show that the morphism given by the base point free linear system $|C_+|$ is the blow-down $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^2_k$.
5. (B) For $n = 2$, show that $|C_+|$ defines a morphism $F_2 \to \mathbb{P}^3_k$, $[1 : r : r t : r t^2]$ on the plane $\operatorname{Spec} k[r, t]$, whose image is the projective quadric cone $V_+(X_1 X_3 - X_2^2)$, and that it contracts $C_-$ to the vertex and is an isomorphism elsewhere.
6. (B) Show that $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}(e) \oplus \mathcal{O}(e + n)) \cong F_n$ over $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ for every $e \in \mathbb{Z}$, using part 2 of Proposition P3.6.35.
7. (C) Show that $F_1$ is not isomorphic to $F_0 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$. On $F_1$ the curve $C_-$ satisfies $\Gamma(\mathcal{O}(C_-)) = k$. On $F_0$ show that every nonzero effective divisor $D$ has $\dim_k \Gamma(\mathcal{O}(D)) \geq 2$, using $\operatorname{Cl}(F_0) = \mathbb{Z}^2$ and the bihomogeneous equations of curves.

### Notes and history

The surfaces $F_n$ are due to Hirzebruch, who showed in 1951 that $F_n$ and $F_m$ are isomorphic only if $n = m$, while they are diffeomorphic when $n \equiv m \bmod 2$. Hartshorne, Section V.2, treats them as ruled surfaces over $\mathbb{P}^1$ with invariant $e = n$. The literature state is theorem. The minimal rational surfaces are $\mathbb{P}^2$ and the $F_n$ with $n \neq 1$, and over an algebraically closed field every smooth projective rational surface is obtained from one of them by a sequence of blow-ups of points. In the language of the minimal model program, $F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1$ is a Mori fibre space, $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ is a divisorial contraction, and for $n = 2$ the contraction of $C_-$ in Exercise 5 is the minimal resolution of the quadric cone. Blowing up a point of a fibre of $F_n$ and contracting the strict transform of that fibre gives $F_{n + 1}$ or $F_{n - 1}$, an elementary transformation, the first example of a Sarkisov link.
