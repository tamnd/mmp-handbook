# Schemes, divisors and line bundles

This volume builds the language in which the minimal model program is stated. A variety in the program is an integral separated scheme of finite type over a field. Its divisors are Weil divisors, its line bundles are invertible sheaves, and its canonical divisor $K_X$ comes from the sheaf of differentials. The maps of the program are projective morphisms $f \colon X \to Z$ over a base, and the contractions and flips of Volumes 7 and 8 are constructed as $\operatorname{Proj}$ of graded algebras. Each of these words is defined in this volume, with its first properties proved in full.

The volume has thirteen chapters in four groups.

- **Schemes and morphisms, Chapters 1 to 4.** Chapter 1 makes the prime spectrum of P1 Ch. 2 into a locally ringed space, the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec} A$, and proves that rings and affine schemes are the same thing with the arrows reversed. Chapter 2 glues affine schemes into schemes, constructs projective space and the affine line with a doubled origin, defines reduced, integral and Noetherian schemes, and identifies a scheme with its functor of points by the Yoneda lemma of P2 Ch. 1. Chapter 3 treats morphisms: immersions, closed subschemes, fibre products, fibres and base change, and the finiteness conditions. Chapter 4 defines separated and proper morphisms, proves the valuative criteria in the form the program uses, and proves that projective space is proper.
- **Sheaves on schemes, Chapters 5 and 6.** Chapter 5 defines the quasi-coherent sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ of a module, proves that quasi-coherent sheaves on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ are the same as $A$-modules, and shows that quasi-coherent and coherent sheaves form abelian categories. Chapter 6 constructs $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a graded ring, the twisting sheaves $\mathcal{O}(n)$, projective morphisms, ample and very ample sheaves, and the projective bundle $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$.
- **Geometry of varieties, Chapters 7 to 10.** Chapter 7 treats dimension, regular and smooth schemes, normal schemes and the normalization. Chapter 8 defines Weil divisors and the class group on a normal scheme and computes the examples of the program, among them the quadric cone, where a Weil divisor is not Cartier. Chapter 9 defines Cartier divisors, line bundles, the Picard group, $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisors and $\mathbb{Q}$-factoriality. Chapter 10 treats linear systems and the morphisms they define.
- **Tools, Chapters 11 to 13.** Chapter 11 constructs the blow-up and the Hirzebruch surfaces. Chapter 12 defines the sheaf of differentials and the canonical sheaf, and proves the adjunction formula. Chapter 13 treats flat families and representable functors, the base of the Hilbert schemes of P8 Ch. 1.

The spine is Vakil's *The rising sea* (Vak25), Hartshorne's *Algebraic geometry*, Chapter II (Har77), and Görtz-Wedhorn, *Algebraic geometry I* (GW20). The order of the chapters follows Vakil more than Hartshorne: the functor of points comes early, and quasi-coherent sheaves come before $\operatorname{Proj}$. Each section lists in `reads_with` the places of the spine texts that read in parallel.

The volume needs P1 for commutative algebra and P2 Ch. 1, 5 and 6 for categories, sheaves and sheaves of modules. Rings are commutative with $1$. From this volume on, $k$ is a field, and the text says when it must be algebraically closed. A statement is written over a base scheme $S$ or $Z$ when the program needs the relative version.
