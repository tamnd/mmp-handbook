# Schemes

A scheme is a locally ringed space that is locally an affine scheme. A manifold is built from open balls in the same way. The open pieces are the affine schemes of Chapter 1, and the gluing maps are isomorphisms of locally ringed spaces. A scheme can then fail to be affine in two ways: it can have too few global functions, as projective space does, or its global functions can fail to separate its points, as on the affine line with a doubled origin.

Section 2.1 defines schemes, open subschemes and morphisms, and proves the gluing theorem for schemes and for morphisms. It constructs the affine line with a doubled origin and the projective line. Section 2.2 constructs projective space $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ over any ring by gluing $n + 1$ affine spaces, computes its global functions, and computes its points with values in a field and in a local ring. Chapter 6 constructs $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ again as $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a polynomial ring. Section 2.3 treats the first properties of schemes: quasi-compact, reduced, irreducible, integral, locally Noetherian and Noetherian schemes, generic points and the function field. The tool is the affine communication lemma, which checks a property on one affine cover and gets it on every affine open set. The section ends with the definition of a variety that the handbook uses from now on. Section 2.4 identifies a scheme with its functor of points $T \mapsto \operatorname{Hom}(T, X)$ by the Yoneda lemma of P2 Ch. 1, and proves that the restriction of this functor to affine schemes, the functor $R \mapsto X(R)$ on rings, already determines $X$.

The functor of points is the language of the moduli problems of the program. The Hilbert scheme of P8 Ch. 1 and the Chow variety are defined as schemes that represent functors, and Chapter 13 starts that theory. The doubled origin is the basic example of a non-separated scheme, and Chapter 4 defines separatedness to exclude it.

The chapter follows Vakil, Chapters 4, 5 and 6, and Hartshorne, Sections II.2 and II.3, with Görtz-Wedhorn, Chapters 3 and 4. It uses Chapter 1, P2 Ch. 1 for the Yoneda lemma and P2 Ch. 5 for the gluing of sheaves.
