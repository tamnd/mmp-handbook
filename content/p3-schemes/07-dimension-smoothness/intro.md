# Dimension, smoothness and normalization

The algebra of P1 measures a ring by its Krull dimension, decides regularity by the cotangent space of a local ring, and decides normality by integral closure. This chapter carries these notions to schemes. All three are local, so they are defined point by point through the local rings $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$, and the chapter proves the facts that make them geometric: the dimension of a variety is the transcendence degree of its function field, the smooth points of a variety form an open set, the singular points of a normal scheme have codimension at least $2$, and every variety has a finite birational map from a normal one.

Section 7.1 defines the dimension and the codimension of a closed subset, proves that $\dim Z + \operatorname{codim}(Z, X) = \dim X$ on a variety, computes the dimension of hypersurfaces and of intersections in $\mathbb{P}^n$, and proves the dimension theorem for the fibres of a dominant morphism. Section 7.2 defines the Zariski tangent space, identifies it with the morphisms from $\operatorname{Spec} k[\varepsilon]$, computes it with the Jacobian matrix, and defines regular schemes. Section 7.3 defines smooth morphisms to a field by the Jacobian criterion, proves that the smooth locus is open, that smooth means regular over a perfect field, and treats the regular curve $y^2 = x^p - t$ that is not smooth. Section 7.4 defines normal schemes, proves Serre's criterion and the Hartogs property for schemes, studies finite morphisms, and constructs the normalization by its universal property.

In the minimal model program every variety is normal, because Weil divisors and the canonical divisor are defined only in codimension one and the Hartogs property extends everything else. The terminal, canonical and log terminal singularities of Volume 4 are singularities of normal varieties, and the cones $xy = z^n$ of Section 7.4 are the first canonical singularities. Smoothness is the hypothesis of the adjunction formula of Chapter 12, and the dimension theory of fibres is used for every contraction and every Mori fibre space.

The chapter follows Vakil, Chapters 9, 11 and 12, Hartshorne, Sections I.1, I.5, II.3 and II.6, and Görtz-Wedhorn, Chapters 5, 6 and 12. It uses Chapters 1 to 6 and the commutative algebra of P1 Ch. 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 11.
