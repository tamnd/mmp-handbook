## Graded rings and Proj

### Statement

A *graded ring* is a ring $S$ with a decomposition $S = \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} S_d$ into additive subgroups such that $S_d S_e \subseteq S_{d + e}$. The elements of $S_d$ are *homogeneous of degree $d$*. Then $S_0$ is a subring and every $S_d$ is an $S_0$-module. The *irrelevant ideal* is $S_+ = \bigoplus_{d > 0} S_d$. An ideal is *homogeneous* if it is generated by homogeneous elements, or equivalently if it contains the homogeneous components of each of its elements. The same words apply to $\mathbb{Z}$-graded rings $T = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} T_n$, such as the localization $S_f$ at a homogeneous element $f$, where $\deg(a/f^k) = \deg a - k \deg f$. For homogeneous $f$ of degree $d > 0$, write $S_{(f)} = (S_f)_0$ for the ring of elements $a/f^k$ with $a \in S_{kd}$.

**Definition P3.6.1 (the homogeneous spectrum).** Let $S$ be a graded ring. The set $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is the set of homogeneous prime ideals $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq S$ with $S_+ \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$. For a homogeneous ideal $I$ put $V_+(I) = \{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Proj} S : I \subseteq \mathfrak{p}\}$, and for a homogeneous element $f$ put $D_+(f) = \{\mathfrak{p} : f \notin \mathfrak{p}\}$.

Lean: Mathlib `ProjectiveSpectrum` is the set of relevant homogeneous primes of a graded ring, with `ProjectiveSpectrum.zeroLocus`, `ProjectiveSpectrum.basicOpen` and `ProjectiveSpectrum.vanishingIdeal`.

**Proposition P3.6.2 (homogeneous primes and the topology).** Let $S$ be a graded ring.

1. A homogeneous ideal $\mathfrak{p} \neq S$ is prime if and only if for all homogeneous $a, b$ with $ab \in \mathfrak{p}$, $a \in \mathfrak{p}$ or $b \in \mathfrak{p}$.
2. For a prime $\mathfrak{q}$ of $S$, the ideal $\mathfrak{q}^*$ generated by the homogeneous elements of $\mathfrak{q}$ is a homogeneous prime. For a homogeneous ideal $I$, the radical $\sqrt{I}$ is the intersection of the homogeneous primes that contain $I$, and it is homogeneous.
3. The sets $V_+(I)$ are the closed sets of a topology on $\operatorname{Proj} S$. The sets $D_+(f)$, for $f$ homogeneous of positive degree, form a basis of it, and $D_+(f) \cap D_+(g) = D_+(fg)$.
4. $V_+(I) = \emptyset$ if and only if $S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I}$.

Lean: Mathlib `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_iff` is part 1, `Ideal.IsPrime.homogeneousCore` and `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.radical_eq` give part 2, and `ProjectiveSpectrum.zariskiTopology` with `ProjectiveSpectrum.isTopologicalBasis_basic_opens` for part 3.

**Theorem P3.6.3 (the affine pieces).** Let $f \in S_d$ with $d > 0$.

1. The map $\psi_f \colon D_+(f) \to \operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$, $\mathfrak{p} \mapsto \mathfrak{p} S_f \cap S_{(f)}$, is a homeomorphism.
2. For $g \in S_e$ with $e > 0$, the element $h = g^d / f^e$ lies in $S_{(f)}$, $\psi_f(D_+(fg)) = D(h)$, and the natural map $(S_{(f)})_h \to S_{(fg)}$ is an isomorphism.
3. There is a scheme structure on $\operatorname{Proj} S$, unique up to unique isomorphism, such that every $D_+(f)$ is an open subscheme and every $\psi_f$ is an isomorphism of schemes $D_+(f) \cong \operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$, compatible with part 2. The maps $S_0 \to S_{(f)}$ glue to a morphism $\operatorname{Proj} S \to \operatorname{Spec} S_0$.
4. The stalk at $\mathfrak{p}$ is $S_{(\mathfrak{p})}$, the degree $0$ part of the localization of $S$ at the homogeneous elements outside $\mathfrak{p}$.

**Proposition P3.6.4 (first properties).** Let $S$ be a graded ring and $X = \operatorname{Proj} S$.

1. If $f_1, \dots, f_r$ are homogeneous of positive degree and generate $S_+$ as an ideal, then $X = D_+(f_1) \cup \dots \cup D_+(f_r)$. This holds if $f_1, \dots, f_r$ generate $S$ as an $S_0$-algebra.
2. The morphism $X \to \operatorname{Spec} S_0$ is separated.
3. If $S$ is generated as an $S_0$-algebra by finitely many homogeneous elements of positive degree, then $X \to \operatorname{Spec} S_0$ is of finite type. If these elements lie in $S_1$, then $X$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^n_{S_0}$ for some $n$, and $X \to \operatorname{Spec} S_0$ is projective, hence proper.
4. For a homogeneous ideal $I$, the surjection $S \to S/I$ induces a closed immersion $\operatorname{Proj} S/I \to \operatorname{Proj} S$ with image $V_+(I)$.
5. For $d \geq 1$, let $S^{(d)} = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} S_{nd}$, graded by $S^{(d)}_n = S_{nd}$. Then $\operatorname{Proj} S^{(d)} \cong \operatorname{Proj} S$.
6. For a ring $A$ and $S = A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ with $\deg x_i = 1$, $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is the projective space $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ of Definition P3.2.10.

Lean: Mathlib proves part 2 in the instance `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.isSeparated`, and, for $S$ of finite type over $S_0$, that `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.toSpecZero` is quasi-compact, locally of finite type and proper, by instances in `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/ProjectiveSpectrum/Proper.lean`. Parts 4, 5 and 6 are not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A homogeneous prime that does not contain $f$ is the same as a homogeneous prime of $S_f$, and $f$ is a unit of positive degree there. Such a prime is determined by its degree $0$ part: a homogeneous element $a$ of degree $n$ lies in it exactly when $a^d / f^n$ does. So $D_+(f)$ is the spectrum of $S_{(f)}$. On the overlap, inverting $g$ in $S_f$ is the same as inverting the degree $0$ element $g^d / f^e$, so the affine pieces fit together as basic open sets, and they glue. Every chart is the spectrum of a ring that the graded ring determines, so the properties of Proj are properties of these rings and of the maps between them.

### Examples

**Example P3.6.5 (projective space and its hypersurfaces).** By part 6 of Proposition P3.6.4, $\mathbb{P}^n_A = \operatorname{Proj} A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$. For a homogeneous polynomial $F$ of degree $e$, part 4 gives the closed subscheme $V_+(F) = \operatorname{Proj} A[x_0, \dots, x_n]/(F)$. On the chart $D_+(x_i)$ it is $\operatorname{Spec}$ of $A[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]/(F/x_i^e)$, the dehomogenization of $F$. This is the hypersurface of Example P3.2.16, and the cubic $y^2 z = x^3 + x^2 z$ of Example P3.2.17 is $\operatorname{Proj} k[x, y, z]/(y^2 z - x^3 - x^2 z)$.

**Example P3.6.6 (small cases).** If $S = A[x]$ with $\deg x = 1$, then $S_{(x)} = A$, and $\operatorname{Proj} S = D_+(x) = \operatorname{Spec} A$. This is $\mathbb{P}^0_A$. If $S = k[x, y]/(xy)$, then $S_{(x)} = k[y/x]/(y/x) = k$ and $S_{(y)} = k$, and $D_+(x) \cap D_+(y) = D_+(xy) = \emptyset$, so $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is two points, the two points $[1 : 0]$ and $[0 : 1]$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. If $S_+$ consists of nilpotent elements, for example $S = k[x]/(x^2)$, then every prime contains $S_+$ and $\operatorname{Proj} S = \emptyset$, as part 4 of Proposition P3.6.2 says.

**Example P3.6.7 (a weighted projective plane).** Let $k$ be a field and $S = k[x, y, z]$ with $\deg x = \deg y = 1$ and $\deg z = 2$. The Veronese ring $S^{(2)}$ is spanned by the monomials of even degree, and it is generated by $u = x^2$, $v = xy$, $w = y^2$ and $z$, all of degree $1$ in $S^{(2)}$, with the one relation $uw = v^2$. So, by parts 5, 6 and 4 of Proposition P3.6.4,
$$\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2) = \operatorname{Proj} S \cong \operatorname{Proj} k[u, v, w, z]/(uw - v^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k,$$
the projective cone over a conic. The chart $D_+(z)$ is $\operatorname{Spec} S_{(z)}$, and $S_{(z)} = k[x^2/z, xy/z, y^2/z] \cong k[u, v, w]/(uw - v^2)$. This is the quadric cone, the quotient of $\mathbb{A}^2$ by $\pm 1$, with its vertex at $[0 : 0 : 1]$. The charts $D_+(x)$ and $D_+(y)$ are affine planes: $S_{(x)} = k[y/x, z/x^2]$. So a weighted projective space can be singular, and the quadric cone of Chapter 8 lives inside it.

**Example P3.6.8 (the graded ring is not determined by Proj).** Let $S = k[x_0, x_1]$. Then $\operatorname{Proj} S^{(2)} \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$ by part 5, while $S^{(2)} = k[x_0^2, x_0 x_1, x_1^2] \cong k[u, v, w]/(uw - v^2)$ is not a polynomial ring. So $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is also the conic $V_+(uw - v^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$: this is the Veronese map of Example P3.3.8. In the same way, changing finitely many graded pieces of $S$ in positive degree does not change $\operatorname{Proj} S$ (Exercise 3).

**Example P3.6.9 (a homogeneous prime that contains the irrelevant ideal).** In $S = k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ the homogeneous primes containing $S_+$ are $S_+$ itself, the origin of the affine cone $\operatorname{Spec} S$. The points of $\operatorname{Proj} S$ are the homogeneous primes other than $S_+$, and a closed point $[a_0 : \dots : a_n]$ of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$, for $k$ algebraically closed, is the prime generated by the $a_i x_j - a_j x_i$: the line through the origin of $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_k$ in the direction $(a_0, \dots, a_n)$. So $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is the set of lines through the vertex of the cone $\operatorname{Spec} S$, and $V_+(S_+) = \emptyset$.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.6.2.* (1) One direction is clear. For the other, let $a, b \notin \mathfrak{p}$. Subtracting from $a$ and $b$ their homogeneous components that lie in $\mathfrak{p}$ does not change whether $ab \in \mathfrak{p}$, since $\mathfrak{p}$ is an ideal. So assume that no homogeneous component of $a$ or $b$ lies in $\mathfrak{p}$, and that $a \neq 0 \neq b$. Let $a_i$ and $b_j$ be the components of lowest degree. The component of $ab$ of degree $i + j$ is $a_i b_j$, which is not in $\mathfrak{p}$ by hypothesis. As $\mathfrak{p}$ is homogeneous, $ab \notin \mathfrak{p}$.

(2) The ideal $\mathfrak{q}^*$ is homogeneous, and $\mathfrak{q}^* \neq S$ since $1 \notin \mathfrak{q}$. If $a, b$ are homogeneous with $ab \in \mathfrak{q}^*$, then $ab \in \mathfrak{q}$, so $a$ or $b$ is in $\mathfrak{q}$, and so in $\mathfrak{q}^*$, being homogeneous. By part 1, $\mathfrak{q}^*$ is prime. Now let $I$ be homogeneous. For every prime $\mathfrak{q} \supseteq I$, the homogeneous prime $\mathfrak{q}^*$ contains $I$, since $I$ is generated by homogeneous elements of $\mathfrak{q}$, and $\mathfrak{q}^* \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$. So the intersection of the homogeneous primes that contain $I$ lies between $\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q} \supseteq I} \mathfrak{q}^*$ and $\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q} \supseteq I} \mathfrak{q} = \sqrt{I}$ (Theorem P1.1.9), and both are equal to $\sqrt{I}$. An intersection of homogeneous ideals is homogeneous.

(3) $V_+(S) = \emptyset$, $V_+(0) = \operatorname{Proj} S$, $V_+(I) \cup V_+(J) = V_+(IJ)$ by primality, and $\bigcap V_+(I_\lambda) = V_+(\sum I_\lambda)$. So the $V_+(I)$ are the closed sets of a topology. The complement of $V_+(I)$ is the union of the $D_+(a)$ over the homogeneous $a \in I$. If $\mathfrak{p} \in D_+(a)$ with $\deg a = 0$, choose a homogeneous $b \in S_+ \setminus \mathfrak{p}$, which exists since $S_+$ is generated by homogeneous elements and $S_+ \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$. Then $ab \in I$ has positive degree and $\mathfrak{p} \in D_+(ab) \subseteq D_+(a)$. So the $D_+(f)$ with $\deg f > 0$ form a basis. The equality $D_+(f) \cap D_+(g) = D_+(fg)$ holds since $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime.

(4) If $S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I}$, every prime that contains $I$ contains $S_+$, so $V_+(I) = \emptyset$. Conversely, if $V_+(I) = \emptyset$, every homogeneous prime that contains $I$ contains $S_+$, so $S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I}$ by part 2. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.6.3.* (1) Prime ideals of $S$ that do not contain $f$ correspond to prime ideals of $S_f$, by $\mathfrak{p} \mapsto \mathfrak{p} S_f$ and contraction (Theorem P1.2.6). Under this bijection homogeneous primes correspond to homogeneous primes: $\mathfrak{p} S_f$ is generated by the homogeneous elements $a/1$, $a \in \mathfrak{p}$ homogeneous, and the contraction of a homogeneous ideal is homogeneous, as the components of $a/1$ are the $a_i/1$. So it suffices to show that $P \mapsto P \cap S_{(f)}$ is a bijection from the homogeneous primes of the $\mathbb{Z}$-graded ring $T = S_f$ to $\operatorname{Spec} T_0$.

Let $P$ be a homogeneous prime of $T$ and $a \in T_n$. Since $f$ is a unit of $T$ and $P$ is prime, $a \in P$ if and only if $a^d \in P$, if and only if $a^d / f^n \in P \cap T_0$. So $P$ is determined by $P \cap T_0$, and the map is injective. For surjectivity let $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq T_0$ be prime, and let $P$ be the set of $a \in T$ such that every homogeneous component $a_n$ satisfies $a_n^d / f^n \in \mathfrak{q}$. Let $a, b \in T_n$ lie in $P$. Every term $\binom{2d}{i} a^i b^{2d - i} / f^{2n}$ of $(a + b)^{2d} / f^{2n}$ has $i \geq d$ or $2d - i \geq d$, so it is $(a^d / f^n)$ or $(b^d / f^n)$ times an element of $T_0$, and lies in $\mathfrak{q}$. So $((a + b)^d / f^n)^2 \in \mathfrak{q}$, and $(a + b)^d / f^n \in \mathfrak{q}$ since $\mathfrak{q}$ is prime. For $c \in T_m$ and $a \in P \cap T_n$, $(ca)^d / f^{m + n} = (c^d / f^m)(a^d / f^n) \in \mathfrak{q}$. So $P$ is a homogeneous ideal. For homogeneous $a \in T_m$ and $b \in T_n$ with $ab \in P$, the product $(a^d / f^m)(b^d / f^n)$ lies in $\mathfrak{q}$, so one factor does, and $P$ is prime by part 1 of Proposition P3.6.2. It is not $T$ because $1 \notin \mathfrak{q}$. For $a \in T_0$, $a \in P$ if and only if $a^d \in \mathfrak{q}$, if and only if $a \in \mathfrak{q}$. So $P \cap T_0 = \mathfrak{q}$, and $\psi_f$ is a bijection.

For homogeneous $a \in S$ of degree $e$, $\psi_f(D_+(f) \cap D_+(a)) = D(a^d / f^e)$, because $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$ if and only if $a^d \notin \mathfrak{p}$, if and only if $a^d / f^e \notin \psi_f(\mathfrak{p})$. Every element of $S_{(f)}$ has the form $b/f^k$ with $b$ homogeneous, and $D(b/f^k) = D(b^d/f^{kd})$ as $b^d/f^{kd} = (b/f^k)^d$. So $\psi_f$ maps the open sets $D_+(f) \cap D_+(a)$, which form a basis of $D_+(f)$ by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.2, onto the basic open sets $D(c)$ of $\operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$, and each $D(c)$ is such an image. A bijection that maps a basis onto a basis is a homeomorphism.

(2) The element $h = g^d / f^e$ has degree $ed - de = 0$, and the previous paragraph gives $\psi_f(D_+(fg)) = D(h)$. For the rings, $S_{fg} = (S_f)_g = (S_f)_{g^d} = T_h$, since $f$ is a unit of $T = S_f$ and $g^d = h f^e$. Localization at the degree $0$ element $h$ is computed degree by degree: $T = \bigoplus_n T_n$ as $T_0$-modules, and $T_h = \bigoplus_n (T_n)_h$, where $(T_n)_h$ is the localization of the $T_0$-module $T_n$. So $S_{(fg)} = (T_h)_0 = (T_0)_h = (S_{(f)})_h$.

(3) For $f$ homogeneous of positive degree let $X_f = \operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$. By part 2, $X_f$ and $X_g$ contain the open sets $D(g^d/f^e)$ and $D(f^e/g^d)$, both identified with $\operatorname{Spec} S_{(fg)}$, which gives an isomorphism $\varphi_{fg}$ between them. These isomorphisms come from the identity of $S_{(fg)}$, so for three elements $f, g, l$ they agree on the triple overlap, which is $\operatorname{Spec} S_{(fgl)}$: the cocycle condition holds. Theorem P3.2.3 glues the $X_f$ to a scheme. Its underlying set is $\operatorname{Proj} S$, since the bijections $\psi_f$ are compatible with the identifications by part 2 and the $D_+(f)$ cover $\operatorname{Proj} S$. Its topology is the topology of Proposition P3.6.2, since both restrict to the same topology on every $D_+(f)$ by part 1. Uniqueness holds because a scheme structure is determined by its restrictions to an open cover and the gluing maps (Proposition P3.2.4). The ring maps $S_0 \to S_{(f)}$ commute with the maps $S_{(f)} \to S_{(fg)}$, so the morphisms $X_f \to \operatorname{Spec} S_0$ glue.

(4) Choose $f$ of degree $d > 0$ with $\mathfrak{p} \in D_+(f)$ and let $\mathfrak{q} = \psi_f(\mathfrak{p})$. The stalk is $(S_{(f)})_\mathfrak{q}$ by Theorem P3.1.11. The ring map $S_{(f)} \to S_{(\mathfrak{p})}$, $a/f^k \mapsto a/f^k$, sends every $b/f^l \notin \mathfrak{q}$ to a unit, with inverse $f^l/b$, since then $b \notin \mathfrak{p}$. So it extends to a ring map $(S_{(f)})_\mathfrak{q} \to S_{(\mathfrak{p})}$, $(a/f^k)/(b/f^l) \mapsto a f^l / (b f^k)$. It is surjective: an element $a/s$ with $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$ homogeneous of degree $m$ and $a \in S_m$ is the image of $(a s^{d - 1}/f^m)/(s^d/f^m)$. It is injective: if $a f^l / (b f^k) = 0$ in $S_{(\mathfrak{p})}$, then $t a f^l = 0$ in $S$ for a homogeneous $t \notin \mathfrak{p}$. So $ta = 0$ in $S_f$, and $(t^d / f^{\deg t}) \cdot (a / f^k) = 0$ in $S_{(f)}$. The element $t^d / f^{\deg t}$ is not in $\mathfrak{q}$, so $a/f^k = 0$ in $(S_{(f)})_\mathfrak{q}$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.6.4.* (1) Let $\mathfrak{p} \in X$ contain every $f_i$. Then $\mathfrak{p} \supseteq S_+$, which is impossible. If the $f_i$ generate $S$ as an $S_0$-algebra, every homogeneous element of positive degree is an $S_0$-combination of monomials in the $f_i$ of positive degree, and each such monomial is a multiple of some $f_i$. So the $f_i$ generate $S_+$.

(2) Let $f, g$ be homogeneous of degrees $d, e > 0$. The intersection $D_+(f) \cap D_+(g) = D_+(fg)$ is affine. The map $S_{(f)} \otimes_{S_0} S_{(g)} \to S_{(fg)}$ is surjective, since for $b \in S_{m(d + e)}$,
$$\frac{b}{(fg)^m} = \frac{b \, g^{m(d - 1)}}{f^{m(e + 1)}} \cdot \frac{f^{me}}{g^{md}},$$
where the first factor lies in $S_{(f)}$ and the second in $S_{(g)}$, by a count of degrees. By Proposition P3.4.3 the morphism is separated.

(3) Let $f_1, \dots, f_r$ be homogeneous generators of positive degree. By part 1 the $D_+(f_i)$ cover $X$, so it suffices to show that every $S_{(f)}$, $f = f_i$ of degree $d$, is a finitely generated $S_0$-algebra. The map $S^{(d)} \to S_{(f)}$, $a \mapsto a/f^n$ for $a \in S_{nd}$, is a surjective ring map. The ring $S^{(d)}$ is spanned over $S_0$ by the monomials $f^\alpha = f_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots f_r^{\alpha_r}$ whose degree is divisible by $d$. Write $\alpha_j = d q_j + \rho_j$ with $0 \leq \rho_j < d$. Then $f^\alpha = \prod_j (f_j^d)^{q_j} \cdot f^\rho$, and $f_j^d$ and $f^\rho$ have degrees divisible by $d$. So $S^{(d)}$ is generated by the $f_j^d$ and the finitely many $f^\rho$ with $\rho_j < d$ and degree divisible by $d$. Hence $S_{(f)}$ is finitely generated. If the generators $f_0, \dots, f_n$ lie in $S_1$, the map $S_0[x_0, \dots, x_n] \to S$, $x_i \mapsto f_i$, is a surjection of graded rings, and parts 4 and 6 make $X$ a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^n_{S_0}$. It is projective in the sense of Definition P3.4.29, and proper by Corollary P3.4.30. The proofs of parts 4 and 6 below do not use part 3.

(4) For $f$ homogeneous of positive degree with image $\bar{f}$, the map $S_{(f)} \to (S/I)_{(\bar{f})}$ is surjective with kernel $I_{(f)} = I S_f \cap S_{(f)}$, by exactness of localization (Theorem P1.2.5) in each degree. So $\operatorname{Spec} (S/I)_{(\bar f)} \to D_+(f)$ is a closed immersion. The homogeneous primes of $S/I$ are the $\mathfrak{p}/I$ with $\mathfrak{p} \supseteq I$, and $D_+(\bar{f})$ maps onto $V_+(I) \cap D_+(f)$. These closed immersions are compatible with the gluing, so they glue to a morphism $\operatorname{Proj} S/I \to X$ whose restriction over each $D_+(f)$ is a closed immersion, and part 1 of Proposition P3.3.11 gives the claim.

(5) The two rings have the same charts: for $f \in S_e$ with $e > 0$, $f^d \in S^{(d)}_e$, and $S_{(f)} = S_{(f^d)} = (S^{(d)})_{(f^d)}$, since $a/f^k = a f^{k(d - 1)} / (f^d)^k$. The open sets $D_+(f^d)$, $f$ homogeneous of positive degree in $S$, cover $\operatorname{Proj} S^{(d)}$: a homogeneous $g \in S^{(d)}_+$ is homogeneous of positive degree in $S$, and $D_+(g) = D_+(g^d)$ in $\operatorname{Proj} S^{(d)}$. The gluing data are the identities of the rings $S_{(fg)}$, the same on both sides. By the construction in part 3, both schemes are glued from the same affine schemes along the same isomorphisms, so they are isomorphic.

(6) The $x_i$ generate $S$, so $\operatorname{Proj} S = \bigcup_i D_+(x_i)$ by part 1. The charts $D_+(x_i) = \operatorname{Spec} S_{(x_i)}$ and their overlaps $\operatorname{Spec} S_{(x_i x_j)}$ are the charts and overlaps of Definition P3.2.10, glued by the same identities. By part 3, a scheme glued from a subfamily of charts that covers $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is $\operatorname{Proj} S$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Proj} k[x, y, z]/(xz - y^2)$, with all degrees $1$, is covered by $D_+(x)$ and $D_+(z)$, that both are affine lines, and that the glued scheme is $\mathbb{P}^1_k$.
2. (A) Let $S = A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$. Show that $V_+(I) = V_+(J)$ for the ideals $I = (x_0^2, x_0 x_1)$ and $J = (x_0)$ of $A[x_0, x_1]$, and compute the closed subschemes $\operatorname{Proj} S/I$ and $\operatorname{Proj} S/J$ on the two charts.
3. (A) Let $S$ be a graded ring and $m \geq 1$. Let $S' = S_0 \oplus \bigoplus_{d \geq m} S_d$. Show that $\operatorname{Proj} S' \cong \operatorname{Proj} S$.
4. (B) Let $S = A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ with weights $\deg x_i = a_i \geq 1$, and $\mathbb{P}(a_0, \dots, a_n) = \operatorname{Proj} S$. Show that $\mathbb{P}(1, 2) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ and, more generally, that $\mathbb{P}(a_0, \dots, a_n) \cong \mathbb{P}(c a_0, \dots, c a_n)$ for $c \geq 1$. Describe the chart $D_+(x_n)$ of $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$ as the spectrum of an invariant ring.
5. (B) Let $\varphi \colon S \to T$ be a map of graded rings, with $\varphi(S_d) \subseteq T_d$. Show that $\mathfrak{q} \mapsto \varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$ defines a morphism from the open set $U = \operatorname{Proj} T \setminus V_+(\varphi(S_+) T)$ to $\operatorname{Proj} S$. Compute $U$ and the morphism for the projection $k[x_0, x_1] \to k[x_0, x_1, x_2]$, and describe it as the projection from the point $[0 : 0 : 1]$.
6. (B) Let $S$ be generated over $S_0 = A$ by homogeneous elements $f_0, \dots, f_n$ of degrees $a_0, \dots, a_n \geq 1$, with $n \geq 1$, and let $L$ be the least common multiple of the $a_i$. Show that $S^{(d)}$ is generated by $S_d$ for $d = nL$. Deduce from parts 3 and 5 of Proposition P3.6.4 that $\operatorname{Proj} S \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is projective, hence proper.
7. (C) Let $S$ be a graded ring with $S_0$ a field $k$ and $S$ generated by $S_1$ with $\dim_k S_1 = n + 1$. Show that $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$. Show that the affine cone $\operatorname{Spec} S$ minus the point $S_+$ maps to $\operatorname{Proj} S$, with fibres $\mathbb{G}_m$ over closed points when $k$ is algebraically closed.

### Lean

Mathlib constructs $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a graded ring in `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/ProjectiveSpectrum/Scheme.lean` as a locally ringed space whose structure sheaf is built from homogeneous fractions, and proves in `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.basicOpenIsoSpec` that $D_+(f)$ is $\operatorname{Spec}$ of the degree $0$ part of $S_f$. The file `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/ProjectiveSpectrum/Proper.lean` proves that $\operatorname{Proj} S \to \operatorname{Spec} S_0$ is separated, and proper when $S$ is of finite type over $S_0$, by the valuative criterion. The identification with the projective space of Definition P3.2.10, closed subschemes from homogeneous ideals and the Veronese isomorphism are not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The homogeneous spectrum is due to Grothendieck, EGA II, Section 2, after Serre's treatment of projective varieties in FAC. Hartshorne, Proposition II.2.5, constructs the structure sheaf directly from homogeneous fractions and proves that $D_+(f) \cong \operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$; this section constructs the scheme by gluing the affine pieces instead, which gives the same scheme by part 3 of Theorem P3.6.3. The Veronese isomorphism is Hartshorne, Exercise II.5.13. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program, Proj enters through the canonical ring: a variety of general type with finitely generated canonical ring $R(X, K_X) = \bigoplus_m H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m K_X))$ has the canonical model $\operatorname{Proj} R(X, K_X)$, and flips are constructed as Proj of finitely generated relative rings. Weighted projective spaces and the Veronese trick of part 5 appear whenever these rings are generated in several degrees.
