## Relative Proj and projective bundles

### Statement

Let $Y$ be a scheme. A *quasi-coherent graded $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra* is a quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra $\mathcal{S}$ (Definition P3.5.34) with a decomposition $\mathcal{S} = \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} \mathcal{S}_d$ into quasi-coherent submodules such that $\mathcal{S}_d \mathcal{S}_e \subseteq \mathcal{S}_{d + e}$. For every affine open $V \subseteq Y$, $\mathcal{S}(V)$ is then a graded ring, and $\mathcal{S}(D(g)) = \mathcal{S}(V)_g$ for $g \in \mathcal{O}_Y(V)$ (Corollary P3.5.13).

**Definition P3.6.31 (relative Proj).** The *relative Proj* of $\mathcal{S}$ is a $Y$-scheme $\pi \colon \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Y \mathcal{S} \to Y$ with isomorphisms $\pi^{-1}(V) \cong \operatorname{Proj} \mathcal{S}(V)$ over $V$, for all affine open sets $V \subseteq Y$, compatible with the inclusions of part 1 of Proposition P3.6.32.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.6.32 (construction of the relative Proj).** Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a quasi-coherent graded $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra.

1. For affine open sets $W \subseteq V$ there is a natural isomorphism over $W$ between $\operatorname{Proj} \mathcal{S}(W)$ and the preimage of $W$ in $\operatorname{Proj} \mathcal{S}(V)$. So $\operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Y \mathcal{S}$ exists, and it is unique up to unique isomorphism.
2. The morphism $\pi$ is separated.
3. Assume that $\mathcal{S}_0 = \mathcal{O}_Y$, that $\mathcal{S}_1$ is of finite type, and that $\mathcal{S}$ is generated by $\mathcal{S}_1$ as an $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra. Then $\pi$ is of finite type and proper, and the sheaves $\mathcal{O}(1)$ on the $\operatorname{Proj} \mathcal{S}(V)$ glue to an invertible sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}} \mathcal{S}}(1)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.6.33 (projective bundle).** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a quasi-coherent sheaf on $Y$. The *projective bundle* of $\mathcal{E}$ is $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}) = \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Y \operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{E}$, with $\pi \colon \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}) \to Y$ and the sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)$. Here $\operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{E}$ is the quasi-coherent algebra of Proposition P3.5.36, graded by $\operatorname{Sym}^d \mathcal{E}$, and the glued sheaf $\mathcal{O}(1)$ exists for every quasi-coherent $\mathcal{E}$ by the proof of part 3 of Proposition P3.6.32. This is Grothendieck's convention: $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ parametrizes invertible quotients of $\mathcal{E}$, by Theorem P3.6.34, and $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_Y^{n+1}) = \mathbb{P}^n_Y$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.6.34 (the universal property of the projective bundle).** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be quasi-coherent on $Y$ and $\pi \colon \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}) \to Y$.

1. There is a natural surjection $\pi^* \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)$.
2. For every $Y$-scheme $g \colon T \to Y$, the map that sends $\varphi \colon T \to \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ over $Y$ to the pullback $g^* \mathcal{E} = \varphi^* \pi^* \mathcal{E} \to \varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ of the surjection of part 1 is a bijection from $\operatorname{Hom}_Y(T, \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}))$ to the set of surjections $g^* \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{M}$ onto invertible sheaves $\mathcal{M}$, up to isomorphism of $\mathcal{M}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.6.35 (properties of projective bundles).** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be quasi-coherent on $Y$.

1. A section of $\pi$, a morphism $\sigma \colon Y \to \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ with $\pi \sigma = \mathrm{id}$, is the same as a surjection $\mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{L}$ onto an invertible sheaf, up to isomorphism of $\mathcal{L}$.
2. For an invertible sheaf $\mathcal{L}$ there is an isomorphism $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L}) \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ over $Y$ under which $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L})}(1)$ corresponds to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1) \otimes \pi^* \mathcal{L}$.
3. If $\mathcal{E}$ is locally free of rank $r \geq 1$, then $\pi^{-1}(U) \cong \mathbb{P}^{r - 1}_U$ over every open $U$ on which $\mathcal{E}$ is free, and $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(n) \cong \operatorname{Sym}^n \mathcal{E}$ for $n \geq 0$. In particular $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})} = \mathcal{O}_Y$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Proj of a graded ring commutes with inverting a function of degree $0$: the preimage of $D(g)$ in $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is $\operatorname{Proj} S_g$. So the Proj of the graded rings $\mathcal{S}(V)$ glue, exactly as the spectra of the rings $\mathcal{A}(V)$ glue to the relative spectrum. For $\mathcal{S} = \operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{E}$, a morphism to the chart $D_+(m)$ of $\operatorname{Proj} \operatorname{Sym} M$ is a linear form on $M$ that takes the value $1$ on $m$, and the proof of Theorem P3.6.21, with $M$ in place of $A^{n+1}$, gives the universal property. Every other property of the projective bundle follows from the universal property.

### Examples

**Example P3.6.36 (products with projective space).** $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_Y^{n+1}) = \mathbb{P}^n_Y = \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}} \times Y$: over an affine $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$, $\operatorname{Sym}(A^{n+1}) = A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$, and Theorem P3.6.34 is Theorem P3.6.21 for $Y$-schemes. In particular $\mathbb{P}^1_k \times_k \mathbb{P}^1_k = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}^2)$ over $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. For an invertible $\mathcal{L}$, $\operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{L} = \bigoplus_d \mathcal{L}^d$ is locally $A[t]$, and $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}) = Y$ by Example P3.6.6. The unique invertible quotient of $\mathcal{L}$ is $\mathcal{L}$ itself, as part 1 of Proposition P3.6.35 predicts.

**Example P3.6.37 (Hirzebruch surfaces).** Let $k$ be a field, $n \geq 0$, and $F_n = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(n))$ over $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. Over $U_0 = D_+(x_0) = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, $t = x_1/x_0$, the sheaf $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(n)$ is free on $e = (1, 0)$ and $f_0 = (0, x_0^n)$; over $U_1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[s]$, $s = t^{-1}$, it is free on $e$ and $f_1 = (0, x_1^n)$, and $f_1 = t^n f_0$ on the overlap. So $\pi^{-1}(U_i) = \mathbb{P}^1 \times U_i$ with fibre coordinates $[e : f_i]$, glued by
$$([a : b], t) \mapsto ([a : t^n b], t^{-1}).$$
For $n = 0$ this is $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$. The quotients $\mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{O}$ and $\mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{O}(n)$ give two sections of $\pi$ by Proposition P3.6.35: the curve $b = 0$ and the curve $a = 0$, which are disjoint. By part 2, $F_n \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}(-n) \oplus \mathcal{O})$, so the sign of $n$ only matters up to this isomorphism. Chapter 11 shows that $F_1$ is the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ at a point. Together with $\mathbb{P}^2_k$, the surfaces $F_n$ with $n \neq 1$ are the minimal rational surfaces, and every $F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$ is one of the first examples of a Mori fibre space.

**Example P3.6.38 (a sheaf that is not locally free).** Let $A = k[x, y]$, $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$, and $\mathcal{E} = (A/\mathfrak{m})^\sim$, the skyscraper sheaf at the origin of $\mathbb{A}^2_k$. Then $\operatorname{Sym}_A(A/\mathfrak{m}) = A[t]/(xt, yt)$ with $\deg t = 1$. On $D_+(t)$, inverting $t$ kills $x$ and $y$, so $S_{(t)} = k$, and $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ is one reduced point over the origin. Its fibres are empty over the points where $\mathcal{E}$ vanishes. For a quasi-coherent sheaf of finite type the fibre of $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ over $y$ is $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}(y))$, a projective space of dimension $\dim \mathcal{E}(y) - 1$ (Exercise 4), and it jumps with the fibre dimension of Definition P3.5.24.

**Example P3.6.39 (the projective bundle of an ideal).** Let $A = k[x, y]$ and $\mathcal{E} = \widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}$. The ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ has the presentation $A \to A^2 \to \mathfrak{m} \to 0$, $1 \mapsto (y, -x)$, $(a, b) \mapsto ax + by$. As $\operatorname{Sym}$ is right exact on presentations, $\operatorname{Sym} \mathfrak{m} = A[u, v]/(yu - xv)$, with $u, v$ the images of $x, y$. So $\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}) = V_+(yu - xv) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{A}^2}$. Over a point $(a, b) \neq (0, 0)$ the fibre is the one point $[u : v] = [a : b]$, and over the origin it is the whole line $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. This is the blow-up of $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ at the origin, which Chapter 11 constructs as Proj of the Rees algebra; for the ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, generated by a regular sequence, the two graded algebras agree.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.6.32.* (1) Let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$, $S = \mathcal{S}(V)$ and $g \in A$. The preimage of $D(g)$ in $\operatorname{Proj} S$ meets $D_+(f)$, $f \in S_d$ with $d > 0$, in the open set $D(g) \subseteq \operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$, whose ring is $(S_{(f)})_g = (S_g)_{(f)}$, since $g$ has degree $0$ and localization at $g$ is computed degree by degree, as in part 2 of Theorem P3.6.3. These identifications are compatible with the gluing, so the preimage of $D(g)$ is $\operatorname{Proj} S_g = \operatorname{Proj} \mathcal{S}(D(g))$. For general affine $W \subseteq V$, cover $W$ by open sets that are basic in both $V$ and $W$ (Lemma P3.2.22 and its proof); the identifications over these basic open sets agree on overlaps, as all are induced by the restriction maps of $\mathcal{S}$, so they glue to an isomorphism over $W$. These isomorphisms are compatible for $W' \subseteq W \subseteq V$, so the schemes $\operatorname{Proj} \mathcal{S}(V)$ glue (Theorem P3.2.3), as in the proof of Theorem P3.5.35. Uniqueness holds because the identifications are part of the data.

(2) Separatedness is local on the target (Exercise 3 of Section 4.1), and over an affine $V$ it is part 2 of Proposition P3.6.4.

(3) Over an affine $V$ on which $\mathcal{S}_1$ is generated by finitely many sections, $\mathcal{S}(V)$ is generated by finitely many elements of degree $1$, so $\pi^{-1}(V)$ is of finite type over $V$ and proper, by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.4. Such $V$ cover $Y$, and properness is local on the target. The sheaf $\mathcal{S}(1)$ is a quasi-coherent graded $\mathcal{S}$-module, and the sheaves $\mathcal{S}(V)(1)^\sim$ on the $\pi^{-1}(V)$ glue by the module version of part 1, which has the same proof. They are invertible by part 4 of Proposition P3.6.11. For $\mathcal{S} = \operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{E}$ with $\mathcal{E}$ arbitrary, $\mathcal{S}(V)$ is generated by $\mathcal{S}(V)_1 = \mathcal{E}(V)$, possibly by infinitely many elements, and the same argument gives an invertible $\mathcal{O}(1)$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.6.34.* *The affine case.* Let $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A$, $\mathcal{E} = \widetilde{M}$, $S = \operatorname{Sym}_A M$ and $X = \operatorname{Proj} S$. The ring $S$ is generated by $S_1 = M$, so $X = \bigcup_{m \in M} D_+(m)$ by part 1 of Proposition P3.6.4, applied to the ideal $S_+$ generated by $M$; the proof there does not use finiteness. First, a lemma: for $m \in M$, the map $S \to S_{(m)}$ that sends $a \in S_k$ to $a/m^k$ is a surjective ring map with kernel $(m - 1)S$. It is additive and multiplicative, it kills $m - 1$, and it is surjective by definition of $S_{(m)}$. If $a = \sum_k a_k$ maps to $0$, then $m^{N} \sum_k a_k m^{K - k} = 0$ in $S$ for some $N$ and $K \geq \deg a$, and modulo $m - 1$ this element is congruent to $a$. So $a \in (m - 1)S$. By the universal properties of $\operatorname{Sym}$ and of quotients, ring maps $S_{(m)} \to R$ to an $A$-algebra $R$ are the $A$-linear maps $h \colon M \to R$ with $h(m) = 1$.

By Theorem P3.5.2 and the adjunction of Theorem P2.6.19, a morphism $g^* \widetilde{M} \to \mathcal{M}$ is an $A$-linear map $\lambda \colon M \to \Gamma(T, \mathcal{M})$, and it is surjective if and only if the sections $\lambda(m)$ generate $\mathcal{M}$. The surjection of part 1 is the map $M = S_1 \to \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X(1))$, whose images generate $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ since the $D_+(m)$ cover $X$. Now repeat the proof of Theorem P3.6.21, with the following changes. The open sets $X_{s_i}$ become the $T_m = T_{\lambda(m)}$, $m \in M$. The functions $g_{ij}$ become the $A$-linear maps $h_m \colon M \to \mathcal{O}(T_m)$, $\lambda(m') = h_m(m') \lambda(m)$, with $h_m(m) = 1$, which define $\varphi_m \colon T_m \to D_+(m)$ by the lemma. On $T_m \cap T_{m'}$, $h_m(m')$ is a unit, so $\varphi_m$ lands in $D_+(m m')$. The ring $S_{(mm')}$ is generated by $S_{(m)}$ and $m/m'$, and $\varphi_m$ and $\varphi_{m'}$ give the same values $\lambda(m'')/\lambda(m)$ on the generators $m''/m$ and $\lambda(m)/\lambda(m')$ on $m/m'$. So they agree and glue. The isomorphism $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{M}$ and the uniqueness are proved as there.

*The general case.* For an affine open $V \subseteq Y$, a $Y$-morphism $T \to \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ restricts to a $V$-morphism $g^{-1}(V) \to \pi^{-1}(V) = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}|_V)$, and conversely a family of such morphisms that agree on overlaps glues (Proposition P3.2.4). In the same way a surjection $g^* \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{M}$ is a family of surjections over the $g^{-1}(V)$ that agree on overlaps up to the chosen isomorphisms. The bijections of the affine case are natural, so they are compatible with restriction to smaller affine open sets, and they glue. The surjections of part 1 over the affine $V$ glue for the same reason. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.6.35.* (1) Apply Theorem P3.6.34 to $T = Y$ and $g = \mathrm{id}$.

(2) For a $Y$-scheme $g \colon T \to Y$, tensoring with $g^* \mathcal{L}^{-1}$ is a bijection from surjections $g^*(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L}) \to \mathcal{M}$ onto invertible sheaves to surjections $g^* \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{M} \otimes g^* \mathcal{L}^{-1}$, natural in $T$. By Theorem P3.6.34, $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L})$ and $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ represent isomorphic functors on $Y$-schemes, so they are isomorphic over $Y$ by the Yoneda lemma (Theorem P3.2.33). Under this isomorphism the universal quotient $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L})}(1)$ corresponds to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1) \otimes \pi^* \mathcal{L}$.

(3) Over an open $U$ with $\mathcal{E}|_U \cong \mathcal{O}_U^r$, $\pi^{-1}(U) = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_U^r) = \mathbb{P}^{r - 1}_U$ by Example P3.6.36. For every affine $V$ the map $\operatorname{Sym}^n \mathcal{E}(V) \to \Gamma(\pi^{-1}(V), \mathcal{O}(n))$ of Section 6.2 is natural in $V$, so these maps glue to a morphism $\operatorname{Sym}^n \mathcal{E} \to \pi_* \mathcal{O}(n)$. On an affine $V$ on which $\mathcal{E}$ is free of rank $r \geq 2$, it is an isomorphism by Theorem P3.6.12 for $\mathbb{P}^{r - 1}_V$. For $r = 1$, $\pi^{-1}(V) = \operatorname{Proj} A[x] = V$ and $\mathcal{O}(n)$ is free on $x^n$, so both sides are $A x^n$. Such $V$ cover $Y$, so the morphism is an isomorphism. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}) \times_Y Y' \cong \mathbb{P}(h^* \mathcal{E})$ for every morphism $h \colon Y' \to Y$, using Theorem P3.6.34.
2. (A) In the charts of Example P3.6.37, write down the two sections of $F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1$ given by the quotients $\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(n) \to \mathcal{O}$ and $\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(n) \to \mathcal{O}(n)$, and check that they are disjoint. For $n \geq 1$, find a third section that meets the curve $a = 0$ in exactly $n$ points.
3. (A) Let $\mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{F}$ be a surjection of quasi-coherent sheaves. Show that it induces a closed immersion $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}) \to \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ over $Y$ under which $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)$ restricts to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})}(1)$.
4. (B) Let $\mathcal{E}$ be quasi-coherent of finite type on $Y$ and $y \in Y$. Show that the fibre $\pi^{-1}(y)$ is $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}(y)) \cong \mathbb{P}^{e - 1}_{\kappa(y)}$ with $e = \dim_{\kappa(y)} \mathcal{E}(y)$, which is empty if $e = 0$.
5. (B) Let $\mathcal{E}$ be locally free of rank $r$. Show that the kernel of the surjection $\pi^* \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)$ is locally free of rank $r - 1$. For $Y = \operatorname{Spec} k$ and $\mathcal{E} = k^{n+1}$, this gives an exact sequence $0 \to \mathcal{K} \to \mathcal{O}^{n+1} \to \mathcal{O}(1) \to 0$ on $\mathbb{P}^n_k$; Chapter 12 identifies $\mathcal{K} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-1)$ with the sheaf of differentials.
6. (B) Let $\mathcal{E}$ be locally free of rank $r \geq 1$ on $Y$. Show that $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)$ is very ample relative to $Y$ in the sense that, over every affine open $V$ on which $\mathcal{E}$ is free, its sections give a closed immersion into $\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_V$. Show that if $Y$ is projective over a field $k$ with very ample $\mathcal{L}$, then $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1) \otimes \pi^* \mathcal{L}^a$ is ample for $a \gg 0$, granting that $\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L}^a$ is generated by global sections for $a \gg 0$ (Theorem P3.6.23).
7. (C) Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a quasi-coherent graded $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra and $\mathcal{L}$ invertible. Show that $\operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Y \mathcal{S} \cong \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Y \mathcal{S}'$ for $\mathcal{S}' = \bigoplus_d \mathcal{S}_d \otimes \mathcal{L}^d$, and that $\operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Y \mathcal{S} \cong \operatorname{\mathbf{Proj}}_Y \mathcal{S}^{(d)}$ for $d \geq 1$. Deduce part 2 of Proposition P3.6.35 without the universal property.

### Lean

Mathlib has neither the relative Proj of a sheaf of graded algebras nor the projective bundle of a quasi-coherent sheaf; they are not in Lean as stated. Mathlib's `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` is Proj of a single graded ring, which is the case $Y = \operatorname{Spec} S_0$. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The relative Proj and the projective bundle are EGA II, Sections 3 and 4.1, and Hartshorne, Section II.7, where Proposition II.7.12 is Theorem P3.6.34 and Lemma II.7.9 is Exercise 7. Conventions differ. In Grothendieck's convention, used here and by Hartshorne, $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ parametrizes invertible quotients of $\mathcal{E}$, that is, lines in the dual of the fibres. Many texts on the minimal model program and on vector bundles use the convention of lines in the fibres, which is $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}^\vee)$ here, and the reader must check which one a source uses. In terms of the total space of Proposition P3.5.36, $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ is the space of lines through the zero section of $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}^\vee)$. Grothendieck calls a morphism projective if it is a closed immersion into some $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ with $\mathcal{E}$ quasi-coherent of finite type; Definition P3.4.29 implies this condition, and the two agree when $Y$ has an ample invertible sheaf, for example when $Y$ is affine or quasi-projective over a field. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program, projective bundles over curves, among them the Hirzebruch surfaces, are the first Mori fibre spaces, and the relative Proj of a finitely generated sheaf of graded algebras constructs flips and relative canonical models.
