## Morphisms to projective space, ample and very ample sheaves

### Statement

Throughout, $A$ is a ring, $\mathbb{P}^n_A = \operatorname{Proj} A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$, and $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is its twisting sheaf, generated by the global sections $x_0, \dots, x_n$ (Exercise 1 of Section 6.2). For a morphism $\varphi \colon X \to Y$, an $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module $\mathcal{G}$ and $t \in \Gamma(Y, \mathcal{G})$, the pullback $\varphi^* t \in \Gamma(X, \varphi^* \mathcal{G})$ is the image of $t$ under the unit $\mathcal{G} \to \varphi_* \varphi^* \mathcal{G}$ of Theorem P2.6.19.

**Definition P3.6.20 (globally generated).** An $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$ is *generated by global sections* if there are global sections $(t_\lambda)$ whose germs generate $\mathcal{F}_x$ for every $x \in X$, that is, if $\bigoplus_\lambda \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{F}$, $e_\lambda \mapsto t_\lambda$, is surjective. For an invertible sheaf $\mathcal{L}$ and sections $s_0, \dots, s_n$, this means $X = X_{s_0} \cup \dots \cup X_{s_n}$, with $X_s$ as in Section 6.2.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.6.21 (morphisms to projective space).** Let $X$ be a scheme over $A$.

1. If $\varphi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^n_A$ is a morphism over $A$, then $\mathcal{L} = \varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ is invertible, the sections $s_i = \varphi^* x_i$ generate $\mathcal{L}$, and $\varphi^{-1}(D_+(x_i)) = X_{s_i}$.
2. Conversely, let $\mathcal{L}$ be invertible and $s_0, \dots, s_n \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L})$ generate $\mathcal{L}$. There is a unique morphism $\varphi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^n_A$ over $A$ with an isomorphism $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{L}$ that maps $\varphi^* x_i$ to $s_i$ for all $i$. On $X_{s_i}$ it is given by $x_j / x_i \mapsto s_j / s_i$, where $s_j / s_i \in \mathcal{O}(X_{s_i})$ is the function with $s_j = (s_j / s_i) s_i$.
3. These constructions are inverse bijections between $\operatorname{Hom}_A(X, \mathbb{P}^n_A)$ and the set of pairs $(\mathcal{L}, (s_0, \dots, s_n))$ of an invertible sheaf with $n + 1$ generating sections, up to isomorphisms of invertible sheaves that respect the sections.
4. The morphism of part 2 is a closed immersion if and only if every $X_{s_i}$ is affine and the ring map $A[y_0, \dots, y_n] \to \mathcal{O}(X_{s_i})$, $y_j \mapsto s_j/s_i$, is surjective for every $i$.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.fromOfGlobalSections` constructs a morphism $X \to \operatorname{Proj} S$ from a ring map $S \to \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ under which $S_+$ generates the unit ideal. The version with an invertible sheaf is not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.6.22 (very ample and ample).** Let $X$ be a scheme over $A$ and $\mathcal{L}$ an invertible sheaf on $X$.

1. $\mathcal{L}$ is *very ample over $A$* if there is an immersion $i \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^n_A$ over $A$, for some $n$, with $i^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{L}$.
2. If $X$ is Noetherian, $\mathcal{L}$ is *ample* if for every coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ there is $n_0$ such that $\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^n$ is generated by global sections for all $n \geq n_0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.6.23 (Serre).** Let $S$ be a graded ring generated over $S_0 = A$ by finitely many elements of $S_1$, with $A$ Noetherian, and let $X = \operatorname{Proj} S$. For every coherent $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ there is $n_0$ such that $\mathcal{F}(n)$ is generated by finitely many global sections for all $n \geq n_0$. In particular $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is ample.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.6.24 (first properties of ampleness).** Let $X$ be Noetherian and $\mathcal{L}$ invertible.

1. For $m \geq 1$, $\mathcal{L}$ is ample if and only if $\mathcal{L}^m$ is ample.
2. If $X$ is affine, every invertible sheaf on $X$ is ample.
3. If $X$ is proper over a Noetherian ring $A$ and $\mathcal{L}$ is very ample over $A$, then every immersion $i \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^n_A$ over $A$ is a closed immersion, and $\mathcal{L}$ is ample.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.6.25 (ample and very ample).** Let $X$ be of finite type over a Noetherian ring $A$ and $\mathcal{L}$ invertible on $X$. If $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, then $\mathcal{L}^m$ is very ample over $A$ for some $m \geq 1$. If $X$ is proper over $A$, then $\mathcal{L}$ is ample if and only if some power $\mathcal{L}^m$, $m \geq 1$, is very ample, and then $X$ is projective over $A$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A morphism to projective space pulls back the coordinate sections $x_i$, which have no common zero, to sections of a line bundle with no common zero. Conversely, generating sections $s_i$ give the ratios $s_j/s_i$ as functions on the set where $s_i$ does not vanish, and these ratios are the coordinates of a morphism to the chart $D_+(x_i)$. Serre's theorem says that after twisting enough, a coherent sheaf on Proj has enough global sections: a local generator over $D_+(x_i)$ becomes a global section after multiplication by a power of $x_i$, by Lemma P3.6.13. Ampleness is this property for an abstract line bundle. If $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, its powers have sections whose nonvanishing loci are affine and cover $X$, and the generators of these affine rings, multiplied by a power of the section, become further global sections. All of them together define an immersion.

### Examples

**Example P3.6.26 (the Veronese embedding).** For $d \geq 1$ the monomials of degree $d$ in $x_0, \dots, x_n$ are global sections of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(d)$ by Theorem P3.6.12, and they generate it, since $x_i^d$ is among them. Let $N = \binom{n + d}{d} - 1$. The morphism $\nu_d \colon \mathbb{P}^n_A \to \mathbb{P}^N_A$ they define is a closed immersion by part 4 of Theorem P3.6.21: $X_{x_i^d} = D_+(x_i)$ is affine, and its ring $A[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$ is generated by the ratios $x_j x_i^{d-1}/x_i^d = x_j / x_i$. So $\mathcal{O}(d)$ is very ample for $d \geq 1$. For $n \geq 1$ and $d \leq 0$, $\mathcal{O}(d)$ is not ample: for $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}(-1)$, the sheaf $\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{O}(dk) = \mathcal{O}(dk - 1)$ has no nonzero global sections for $k \geq 0$, by Theorem P3.6.12, and it is not zero. For $n = 1$, $d = 2$ this is the conic of Example P3.3.8.

**Example P3.6.27 (projection from a point).** On $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ the sections $x_0, x_1$ of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ have the common zero $p = [0 : 0 : 1]$, so they do not generate $\mathcal{O}(1)$. On $X = \mathbb{P}^2_k \setminus \{p\}$ they do, and Theorem P3.6.21 gives the projection $X \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$, $[x_0 : x_1 : x_2] \mapsto [x_0 : x_1]$. Its fibres are the lines through $p$ with $p$ removed. It does not extend to a morphism $\mathbb{P}^2_k \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$, as Exercise 6 shows. The point $p$ is a *base point* of the linear system spanned by $x_0$ and $x_1$, the notion of Chapter 10, and Chapter 11 removes it by blowing up $p$.

**Example P3.6.28 (affine schemes).** Let $X = \operatorname{Spec} B$ with $B = A[b_1, \dots, b_n]$ of finite type over $A$. The sections $1, b_1, \dots, b_n$ of $\mathcal{O}_X$ generate it, and $X_1 = X$ is affine with ring generated by the $b_j / 1$. By part 4 of Theorem P3.6.21 they define a closed immersion of $X$ into the chart $D_+(y_0) = \mathbb{A}^n_A$, followed by the open immersion into $\mathbb{P}^n_A$. So $\mathcal{O}_X$ is very ample over $A$. It is also ample, by part 2 of Proposition P3.6.24. The immersion is closed if and only if $X$ is proper over $A$: if $X$ is proper by part 3, and conversely by Corollary P3.4.30. For $X = \mathbb{A}^1_k$ it is the inclusion of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ in $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, which is not closed.

**Example P3.6.29 (ample but not very ample).** Let $E$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $1$ over an algebraically closed field $k$ and $p \in E$ a closed point. By the Riemann-Roch theorem, which this volume does not prove, $\dim_k \Gamma(E, \mathcal{O}_E(np)) = n$ for $n \geq 1$, and $\mathcal{O}_E(3p)$ is very ample: its three sections embed $E$ as a plane cubic. So $\mathcal{O}_E(p)$ is ample by Proposition P3.6.24. It is not very ample, and it is not even generated by global sections: every global section of $\mathcal{O}_E(p)$ is a constant multiple of the section $1$, which vanishes at $p$. By part 1 of Theorem P3.6.21, an invertible sheaf of the form $i^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ is generated by global sections, so no morphism $i$ to a projective space has $i^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_E(p)$. So ampleness does not give an embedding by the line bundle itself, only by a power.

**Example P3.6.30 (the weighted projective plane in $\mathbb{P}^3$).** For $S = k[x, y, z]$ with degrees $1, 1, 2$ and $X = \operatorname{Proj} S$, the sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(2)$ is invertible (Example P3.6.17), and its sections $x^2, xy, y^2, z$ generate it. They define the closed immersion of $X$ onto the quadric cone $V_+(uw - v^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$ of Example P3.6.7. So $\mathcal{O}_X(2)$ is very ample, while $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is not even invertible. In the language of Chapter 9, the Weil divisor class behind $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier of index $2$.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem P3.6.21.* (1) Pullback is right exact and maps $\mathcal{O}$ to $\mathcal{O}$ (Theorem P2.6.19), so the surjection $\mathcal{O}^{n + 1} \to \mathcal{O}(1)$ given by the $x_i$ pulls back to a surjection $\mathcal{O}_X^{n + 1} \to \mathcal{L}$ given by the $s_i$, and $\mathcal{L}$ is invertible by part 2 of Proposition P2.6.27. At $x \in X$ the fibre is $\mathcal{L}(x) = \mathcal{O}(1)(\varphi(x)) \otimes_{\kappa(\varphi(x))} \kappa(x)$, and $s_i(x)$ is the image of $x_i(\varphi(x))$. So $s_i(x) \neq 0$ if and only if $x_i(\varphi(x)) \neq 0$, that is, $\varphi(x) \in D_+(x_i)$, by the proof of Theorem P3.6.14.

(2) The sets $X_i = X_{s_i}$ are open and cover $X$, since the $s_i$ generate $\mathcal{L}$. On $X_i$ the section $s_i$ generates $\mathcal{L}$: it does so at every point of $X_i$, as $\mathcal{L}_x$ is free of rank one and $s_i \notin \mathfrak{m}_x \mathcal{L}_x$ (Nakayama's lemma, Theorem P1.1.25). So there are unique $g_{ij} = s_j / s_i \in \mathcal{O}(X_i)$ with $s_j = g_{ij} s_i$, with $g_{ii} = 1$, and $g_{ij}$ is a unit on $X_i \cap X_j$, with inverse $g_{ji}$. Let $\varphi_i \colon X_i \to D_+(x_i) = \operatorname{Spec} A[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$ be the morphism over $A$ with $x_j/x_i \mapsto g_{ij}$ (Theorem P3.1.22). On $X_i \cap X_j$ the function $\varphi_i^*(x_j/x_i) = g_{ij}$ is a unit, so $\varphi_i$ maps $X_i \cap X_j$ into $D(x_j/x_i) = D_+(x_i x_j)$, and there $\varphi_i^*(x_k/x_j) = \varphi_i^*((x_k/x_i)/(x_j/x_i)) = g_{ik}/g_{ij} = g_{jk} = \varphi_j^*(x_k/x_j)$. So $\varphi_i$ and $\varphi_j$ agree on $X_i \cap X_j$, as morphisms into the affine scheme $D_+(x_i x_j)$, and they glue to $\varphi$ by Proposition P3.2.4.

On $X_i$, $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ is free with generator $\varphi^* x_i$, and $\mathcal{L}$ is free with generator $s_i$. The isomorphisms $\varphi^* x_i \mapsto s_i$ agree on $X_i \cap X_j$, since $\varphi^* x_j = \varphi^*(x_j/x_i) \varphi^* x_i \mapsto g_{ij} s_i = s_j$. So they glue to $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{L}$ with $\varphi^* x_j \mapsto s_j$. For uniqueness, let $\psi$ be another such morphism. By part 1, $\psi^{-1}(D_+(x_i)) = X_i$, and on $X_i$ the isomorphism gives $\psi^*(x_j/x_i) \psi^* x_i = \psi^* x_j \mapsto s_j = g_{ij} s_i$, so $\psi^*(x_j/x_i) = g_{ij}$. By Theorem P3.1.22, $\psi|_{X_i} = \varphi_i$.

(3) Part 2 applied to the pair of part 1 gives back $\varphi$, by uniqueness. Part 1 applied to the morphism of part 2 gives a pair isomorphic to $(\mathcal{L}, (s_i))$, by the isomorphism of part 2. Isomorphic pairs give the same morphism, since the construction only uses the functions $s_j / s_i$.

(4) By part 1, $\varphi^{-1}(D_+(x_i)) = X_{s_i}$. By part 1 of Proposition P3.3.11, $\varphi$ is a closed immersion if and only if every $X_{s_i} \to D_+(x_i)$ is one. By part 2 of the same proposition and Theorem P3.3.10, a morphism into an affine scheme is a closed immersion if and only if its source is affine and the ring map is surjective. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.6.23.* Let $x_0, \dots, x_r \in S_1$ generate $S$, so $X = \bigcup D_+(x_i)$, $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is invertible, and $X_{x_i} = D_+(x_i)$ (proof of Theorem P3.6.14). Each $S_{(x_i)}$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra (Proposition P3.6.4), so it is Noetherian (Theorem P1.3.10), and $\mathcal{F}|_{D_+(x_i)} = \widetilde{M_i}$ with $M_i$ a finitely generated $S_{(x_i)}$-module (Proposition P3.5.22). Choose generators $t_{i1}, \dots, t_{ik_i}$ of $M_i$. By part 2 of Lemma P3.6.13 there is $n_0$ such that every $x_i^{n_0} t_{ij}$ extends to a global section $u_{ij}$ of $\mathcal{F}(n_0)$. For $n \geq n_0$, $x_i^{n - n_0} u_{ij}$ is a global section of $\mathcal{F}(n)$ that restricts to $x_i^n t_{ij}$ on $D_+(x_i)$. On $D_+(x_i)$ multiplication by $x_i^n$ is an isomorphism $\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}(n)$, so the $x_i^n t_{ij}$ generate $\mathcal{F}(n)$ there. So the finitely many sections $x_i^{n - n_0} u_{ij}$ generate $\mathcal{F}(n)$ on all of $X$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.6.24.* (1) If $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, so is $\mathcal{L}^m$, by taking $n$ divisible by $m$. Conversely let $\mathcal{L}^m$ be ample and $\mathcal{F}$ coherent. For $0 \leq k < m$ the sheaf $\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^k$ is coherent, so there is $j_k$ with $\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^{k + mj}$ generated by global sections for $j \geq j_k$. Every $n \geq m \max_k j_k$ is $k + mj$ with $j \geq j_k$.

(2) On an affine scheme every quasi-coherent sheaf is $\widetilde{M}$ and is generated by the elements of $M$, its global sections (Theorem P3.5.12). Apply this to $\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^n$.

(3) Let $i = j \circ i'$ with $i' \colon X \to U$ a closed immersion and $j \colon U \to \mathbb{P}^n_A$ an open immersion. Since $X$ is proper over $A$ and $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ is separated over $A$ (Example P3.4.6), $i(X)$ is closed by Proposition P3.4.20. Let $V$ be the complement of $i(X)$. Then $U \cup V = \mathbb{P}^n_A$, $i^{-1}(U) \to U$ is the closed immersion $i'$ and $i^{-1}(V) = \emptyset \to V$ is a closed immersion. By part 1 of Proposition P3.3.11, $i$ is a closed immersion. Now let $\mathcal{L} = i^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ and $\mathcal{F}$ coherent on $X$. A closed immersion is finite, by part 2 of Proposition P3.3.11, so $i_* \mathcal{F}$ is coherent by part 3 of Theorem P3.5.33. By Theorem P3.6.23 and the projection formula (Theorem P2.6.30), $i_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^n) \cong (i_* \mathcal{F})(n)$ is generated by global sections for $n \gg 0$. The global sections of $i_* \mathcal{G}$ are those of $\mathcal{G}$, and the stalk of $i_* \mathcal{G}$ at $i(x)$ is $\mathcal{G}_x$, as $i$ is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset. So $\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^n$ is generated by global sections for $n \gg 0$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.6.25.* The scheme $X$ is Noetherian, since it is quasi-compact and covered by spectra of finitely generated $A$-algebras.

*Step 1: affine nonvanishing loci.* Let $x \in X$. Choose an affine open $U \ni x$ on which $\mathcal{L}$ is trivial, with generator $e$, and let $Y = X \setminus U$ with its reduced induced structure (Proposition P3.3.12). Its ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}_Y$ is coherent (Proposition P3.5.23). Since $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, $\mathcal{I}_Y \otimes \mathcal{L}^n$ is generated by global sections for some $n \geq 1$. Its stalk at $x$ is $\mathcal{L}^n_x$, as $x \notin Y$, so some global section $s$ of $\mathcal{I}_Y \otimes \mathcal{L}^n \subseteq \mathcal{L}^n$ has $s_x \notin \mathfrak{m}_x \mathcal{L}^n_x$, and $x \in X_s$. At a point $y \in Y$, $s_y \in (\mathcal{I}_Y)_y \mathcal{L}^n_y \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_y \mathcal{L}^n_y$, so $X_s \subseteq U$. Writing $s|_U = f e^n$, $X_s = U_f$ is affine.

*Step 2: the embedding.* As $X$ is quasi-compact, finitely many such sections $s_1, \dots, s_k$, with $s_i \in \Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_i})$, have affine loci $X_{s_i}$ that cover $X$. Replacing $s_i$ by a power, which does not change $X_{s_i}$, assume that all $n_i$ equal one $N$. Each $X_{s_i} = \operatorname{Spec} B_i$ with $B_i$ generated as an $A$-algebra by finitely many $b_{i1}, \dots, b_{il}$. By part 2 of Lemma P3.6.13 there is $m$ such that every $s_i^m b_{ij}$, a section of $\mathcal{L}^{Nm}$ over $X_{s_i}$, extends to a global section $c_{ij}$ of $\mathcal{L}^{Nm}$. The sections $s_i^m$ and $c_{ij}$ of $\mathcal{L}^{Nm}$ generate it, since the $X_{s_i^m} = X_{s_i}$ cover $X$. Let $\varphi \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^M_A$ be the morphism they define by Theorem P3.6.21, and let $V_i$ be the chart where the coordinate $y_i$ of $s_i^m$ does not vanish. Then $\varphi^{-1}(V_i) = X_{s_i}$, and the ring map to $B_i$ sends the coordinate ratio of $c_{ij}$ and $s_i^m$ to $c_{ij}/s_i^m = b_{ij}$. It is surjective, so $X_{s_i} \to V_i$ is a closed immersion. With $V = V_1 \cup \dots \cup V_k$, the morphism $X \to V$ is a closed immersion by part 1 of Proposition P3.3.11, and $\varphi$ is an immersion with $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{L}^{Nm}$.

*The proper case.* If $X$ is proper and $\mathcal{L}^m$ is very ample, then $\mathcal{L}^m$ is ample by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.24, and $\mathcal{L}$ is ample by part 1. The immersion is closed by part 3, so $X$ is projective over $A$ in the sense of Definition P3.4.29. $\square$

### Exercises

4. (B) Show that the Segre map $\mathbb{P}^1_k \times_k \mathbb{P}^1_k \to \mathbb{P}^3_k$, $([a_0 : a_1], [b_0 : b_1]) \mapsto [a_0 b_0 : a_0 b_1 : a_1 b_0 : a_1 b_1]$, is a closed immersion onto the quadric $V_+(z_0 z_3 - z_1 z_2)$, using part 4 of Theorem P3.6.21 for the invertible sheaf $p_1^* \mathcal{O}(1) \otimes p_2^* \mathcal{O}(1)$.
5. (B) Let $X$ be Noetherian and $\mathcal{L}$ ample. Show that for every coherent $\mathcal{F}$ there are integers $n \geq 0$ and $N$ and a surjection $(\mathcal{L}^{-n})^{\oplus N} \to \mathcal{F}$. Deduce that every coherent sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^n_A$, $A$ Noetherian, is a quotient of a finite sum of sheaves $\mathcal{O}(-l)$.
6. (B) Show that no morphism $\varphi \colon \mathbb{P}^2_k \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$ restricts to the projection of Example P3.6.27 on $\mathbb{P}^2_k \setminus \{p\}$. (Restrict to the lines $V_+(x_0)$ and $V_+(x_1)$, which pass through $p$, and compute the value of $\varphi$ at $p$ in two ways.)
7. (C) Let $k$ be a field. Assuming that every invertible sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ is isomorphic to some $\mathcal{O}(d)$, which Chapter 9 proves, show that every automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ over $k$ is given by an invertible $(n + 1) \times (n + 1)$ matrix, and that $\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb{P}^n_k) \cong \mathrm{PGL}_{n + 1}(k)$.

### Lean

Mathlib has `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.fromOfGlobalSections`, which builds a morphism into $\operatorname{Proj} S$ from a ring map $S \to \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ under which the irrelevant ideal generates the unit ideal, glued from the charts $D_+(f)$ as in the proof of Theorem P3.6.21. Invertible sheaves with generating sections, very ample and ample sheaves, and Serre's theorem are not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Theorem P3.6.21 is Hartshorne, Theorem II.7.1, and EGA II, Section 4.2. Serre's theorem is FAC, Section 66, and Hartshorne, Theorem II.5.17. Ample sheaves were defined by Serre for projective varieties and by Grothendieck in general, EGA II, Section 4.5, where ampleness is defined by the affine loci $X_s$ as in Step 1 of the proof of Theorem P3.6.25; Hartshorne, Theorem II.7.6, proves the equivalence with the definition used here. Hartshorne defines very ample with immersions, as here, and Definition P3.4.29 uses closed immersions; for $X$ proper over $A$ the two agree by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.24. The literature state is theorem. Ampleness is the central positivity notion of the minimal model program: the cone theorem describes the curves on which $K_X$ is negative, Kleiman's criterion characterizes ample divisors by their intersections with curves, and the base point free theorem produces the morphisms of Theorem P3.6.21 from nef divisors.
