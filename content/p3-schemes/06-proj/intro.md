# Proj, O(n) and P(E)

Chapter 2 made projective space by gluing affine charts. This chapter makes the same construction for every graded ring: the scheme $\operatorname{Proj} S$ has one affine chart $\operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$ for every homogeneous element $f$ of positive degree, and the charts glue because inverting $fg$ is the same as inverting a fraction of degree $0$ on the chart of $f$. Projective varieties, weighted projective spaces and the projective cones over them are all of this kind. A graded module gives a quasi-coherent sheaf on $\operatorname{Proj} S$ in the same way as a module gives one on $\operatorname{Spec} A$, and the shifts of $S$ give the sheaves $\mathcal{O}(n)$. These sheaves are the main source of line bundles in the handbook.

Section 6.1 constructs $\operatorname{Proj} S$, proves that it is separated, and proves that it is projective over $S_0$ when $S$ is finitely generated in degree $1$. It treats the Veronese subrings and the first weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2)$, which is the quadric cone. Section 6.2 constructs the sheaves $\widetilde{M}$ and $\mathcal{O}(n)$, computes $\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^n_A, \mathcal{O}(m))$, and proves that every quasi-coherent sheaf on $\operatorname{Proj} S$ comes from a graded module, so that every closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ is cut out by a homogeneous ideal. Section 6.3 proves that a morphism to $\mathbb{P}^n$ is the same as a line bundle with $n + 1$ generating sections, defines ample and very ample sheaves, and proves Serre's theorem that every coherent sheaf becomes globally generated after a twist by $\mathcal{O}(n)$. Section 6.4 constructs the relative Proj of a sheaf of graded algebras and the projective bundle $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$, proves the universal property of $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$, and computes the Hirzebruch surfaces.

In the minimal model program the canonical model of a variety of general type is the Proj of its canonical ring, and the flip of a small contraction $f \colon X \to Z$ is the relative Proj over $Z$ of the sheaf of graded algebras $\bigoplus_m f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mK_X)$. Ampleness is the positivity notion that the cone theorem and the contraction theorem are stated with, and the projective bundles over curves, among them the Hirzebruch surfaces, are the first Mori fibre spaces. Chapter 10 returns to morphisms to projective space in the language of linear systems, and Chapter 11 constructs blow-ups as a relative Proj.

The chapter follows Vakil, Chapters 4, 15, 16 and 17, Hartshorne, Sections II.2, II.5 and II.7, and Görtz-Wedhorn, Chapter 13. It uses Chapters 1 to 5 and the graded rings of P1 Ch. 13.
