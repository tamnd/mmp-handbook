## The sheaf of a module

### Statement

Throughout the section $A$ is a ring and $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$. For an $A$-module $M$, Definition P3.1.4 gives the $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\widetilde{M}$, with $\widetilde{M}(D(f)) = M_f$ by Corollary P3.1.5 and $\widetilde{M}_\mathfrak{p} = M_\mathfrak{p}$ by Theorem P3.1.11. For a basic open set $D$, write $S_D$ for the multiplicative set of the $g \in A$ with $D \subseteq D(g)$, so that $\widetilde{M}(D) = S_D^{-1} M$ (Definition P3.1.2). Write $\mathsf{Mod}_A$ for the category of $A$-modules.

**Proposition P3.5.1 (the functor $M \mapsto \widetilde{M}$).**

1. An $A$-linear map $u \colon M \to N$ induces a morphism $\widetilde{u} \colon \widetilde{M} \to \widetilde{N}$ of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules, equal to $u_f \colon M_f \to N_f$ on $D(f)$, and this makes $M \mapsto \widetilde{M}$ an additive functor $\mathsf{Mod}_A \to \mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$. On the stalk at $\mathfrak{p}$, $\widetilde{u}$ is $u_\mathfrak{p}$.
2. The functor is exact: if $M' \to M \to M''$ is exact, then $\widetilde{M'} \to \widetilde{M} \to \widetilde{M''}$ is exact.
3. It commutes with arbitrary direct sums: for a family $(M_i)_{i \in I}$ the natural map $\bigoplus_i \widetilde{M_i} \to (\bigoplus_i M_i)^\sim$ is an isomorphism.
4. $\widetilde{M} = 0$ if and only if $M = 0$, and a sequence of $A$-modules is exact if and only if the sequence of their sheaves is exact.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.functor` is the functor, with the instance that it is additive and `AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.isoTop` for the global sections. The functor is a left adjoint, so Mathlib knows that it preserves colimits, in particular direct sums and cokernels. Left exactness is not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.5.2 (the adjunction).** Let $M$ be an $A$-module and $\mathcal{F}$ an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. Then $\psi \mapsto \psi_X$ is a bijection
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\widetilde{M}, \mathcal{F}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(M, \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})),$$
natural in $M$ and $\mathcal{F}$. So $M \mapsto \widetilde{M}$ is left adjoint to $\mathcal{F} \mapsto \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$. In particular:

1. The functor is fully faithful: $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\widetilde{M}, \widetilde{N}) = \operatorname{Hom}_A(M, N)$.
2. For $A$-modules $M$ and $N$ there is a natural isomorphism $(M \otimes_A N)^\sim \cong \widetilde{M} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \widetilde{N}$.
3. For every $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$ there is a natural morphism $\theta_\mathcal{F} \colon \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})^\sim \to \mathcal{F}$, the counit of the adjunction, which is the identity on global sections.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.adjunction` (the functor is left adjoint to `AlgebraicGeometry.moduleSpecΓFunctor`), `AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.fullyFaithfulFunctor` and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.fromTildeΓ` for $\theta_\mathcal{F}$, all in Mathlib. Part 2 is not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P3.5.3 (restriction, pushforward and pullback).**

1. For $f \in A$, under the isomorphism $D(f) \cong \operatorname{Spec} A_f$ of Proposition P3.1.13, $\widetilde{M}|_{D(f)} \cong (M_f)^\sim$.
2. Let $\varphi \colon A \to B$ be a ring map and $g = \operatorname{Spec} \varphi \colon Y = \operatorname{Spec} B \to X$. For a $B$-module $N$, $g_* \widetilde{N} \cong (N_A)^\sim$, where $N_A$ is $N$ viewed as an $A$-module. For an $A$-module $M$, $g^* \widetilde{M} \cong (M \otimes_A B)^\sim$.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.isIso_fromTildeΓ_pushforward` gives part 2 for the pushforward, in the form that $g_* \widetilde{N}$ is again the sheaf of its global sections. Part 1 and the pullback are not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A morphism of sheaves on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is determined by what it does on basic open sets, and on $D(f)$ the sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ is the localization $M_f$. So every statement about $\widetilde{M}$ is a statement about the localizations $M_f$ and $M_\mathfrak{p}$, and localization is exact and commutes with direct sums and with tensor products. This gives the functor and its exactness.

The adjunction says more: a morphism $\widetilde{M} \to \mathcal{F}$ is the same as a module map $M \to \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$. The reason is that $M_f$ is generated by $M$ and by the inverses of $f$, and $f$ acts invertibly on $\mathcal{F}(D(f))$ because $f$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_X(D(f))$. So a map on global sections extends to $D(f)$ in exactly one way, by the universal property of localization. Taking $\mathcal{F} = \widetilde{N}$ shows that no morphisms are lost or added. The sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ is the free-est sheaf with global sections $M$.

### Examples

**Example P3.5.4 (a skyscraper sheaf).** Let $k$ be a field, $A = k[x]$ and $M = A/(x) = k$. For $f \in A$, $M_f = k$ if $f(0) \neq 0$ and $M_f = 0$ if $f(0) = 0$, since then $f$ acts as $0$ on $M$ and is inverted. So $\widetilde{M}(U) = k$ if $0 \in U$ and $0$ otherwise: $\widetilde{M}$ is the skyscraper sheaf $k(0)$ at the origin, in the sense of Example P2.6.8. Its stalks are $k$ at the origin and $0$ at every other point, including the generic point. The exact sequence $0 \to A \xrightarrow{x} A \to M \to 0$ gives the exact sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X \xrightarrow{x} \mathcal{O}_X \to k(0) \to 0$.

**Example P3.5.5 (the function field as a sheaf).** Let $A = k[x]$ and $M = K = k(x)$. Every nonzero $f$ is a unit of $K$, so $K_f = K$ for $f \neq 0$, and every restriction map between nonempty open sets is the identity. So $\widetilde{K}$ is the constant sheaf with value $K$ on the irreducible space $\mathbb{A}^1_k$. Its stalk at every point is $K$. It is not of finite type. Suppose that $\mathcal{O}_U^n \to \widetilde{K}|_U$ is surjective on a nonempty open set $U$, and choose $D(f) \subseteq U$ nonempty. By part 1 of Proposition P3.5.3 and part 1 of Theorem P3.5.2, the restriction to $D(f)$ is $\widetilde{u}$ for an $A_f$-linear map $u \colon A_f^n \to K$, and $u$ is surjective by part 4 of Proposition P3.5.1. But a finitely generated $A_f$-submodule of $K$ has all its elements of the form $a/d$ for one fixed $d \in A$, and $1/p$ is not of this form for an irreducible $p$ that does not divide $d f$. The stalk at the generic point is $K$, which is finitely generated over $\mathcal{O}_{X, \eta} = K$, so the failure is not visible on stalks.

**Example P3.5.6 (tensor products of ideals).** Let $A = k[x, y]$ and $I = (x, y)$. The multiplication map $I \otimes_A I \to I^2$ is surjective, and the element $x \otimes y - y \otimes x$ lies in its kernel. This element is not $0$: the map $I \otimes I \to (I/I^2) \otimes_k (I/I^2)$, a vector space of dimension $4$ with basis $\bar x \otimes \bar x, \bar x \otimes \bar y, \bar y \otimes \bar x, \bar y \otimes \bar y$, sends it to $\bar x \otimes \bar y - \bar y \otimes \bar x \neq 0$. By Theorem P3.5.2, $\widetilde{I} \otimes \widetilde{I} \to \widetilde{I^2}$ is not injective. The kernel is supported at the origin: on $D(x)$ and on $D(y)$ the ideal $I$ becomes the unit ideal and the map is an isomorphism. So the tensor product of two ideal sheaves can have torsion where the ideals are not locally principal.

**Example P3.5.7 (products are not preserved).** Let $A = k[x]$ and $M = \prod_{n \geq 1} A$. The product sheaf $\prod_n \mathcal{O}_X$ has sections $\prod_n A_x$ over $D(x)$, and $(1/x, 1/x^2, 1/x^3, \dots)$ is such a section. It is not in the image of $M_x$, since an element $(a_n)/x^m$ of $M_x$ has denominators bounded by $x^m$. So $\widetilde{M} \to \prod_n \mathcal{O}_X$ is not an isomorphism. The functor $M \mapsto \widetilde{M}$ commutes with direct sums but not with infinite products, in the same way as localization.

**Example P3.5.8 (a discrete valuation ring).** Let $R$ be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$, and $X = \operatorname{Spec} R = \{s, \eta\}$, with $s$ closed and $\eta$ open (Example P3.1.17). The open sets are $\emptyset$, $\{\eta\} = D(t)$ and $X$. So an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module is a triple of an $R$-module $F(X)$, a $K$-vector space $F(\eta)$ and an $R$-linear restriction map $F(X) \to F(\eta)$. The sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ is the triple $(M, M \otimes_R K, m \mapsto m \otimes 1)$. The triple $(0, K, 0)$ is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module that is not of this form, since its global sections are $0$ and its value on $\{\eta\}$ is not. It is the extension by zero $j_! \mathcal{O}_{\{\eta\}}$ for the inclusion $j$ of the open point, the analogue on $\operatorname{Spec} R$ of Example P2.6.33.

**Example P3.5.9 (the counit).** For the sheaf $\mathcal{F} = (0, K, 0)$ of Example P3.5.8, $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0$, so $\theta_\mathcal{F} \colon 0 \to \mathcal{F}$ is not an isomorphism. For $\mathcal{F} = \widetilde{M}$ the counit is the identity of $\widetilde{M}$, since $\Gamma(X, \widetilde{M}) = M$. Section 5.2 proves that $\theta_\mathcal{F}$ is an isomorphism exactly when $\mathcal{F}$ is quasi-coherent.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.5.1.* (1) For basic open sets $D' \subseteq D$ the square formed by $S_D^{-1} u$, $S_{D'}^{-1} u$ and the restriction maps commutes, since both paths send $m/s$ to $u(m)/s$. So the maps $S_D^{-1} u$ form a morphism of sheaves on the basis of basic open sets, linear over $\mathcal{O}$. By part 2 of Theorem P2.5.34 it extends to a unique morphism $\widetilde{u} \colon \widetilde{M} \to \widetilde{N}$ of sheaves of abelian groups. It is $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear: for an open set $U$, $a \in \mathcal{O}_X(U)$ and $s \in \widetilde{M}(U)$, the sections $\widetilde{u}(as)$ and $a\,\widetilde{u}(s)$ agree on every basic open set in $U$, so they are equal. Uniqueness in Theorem P2.5.34 gives $\widetilde{vu} = \widetilde{v}\,\widetilde{u}$, $\widetilde{\mathrm{id}} = \mathrm{id}$ and $\widetilde{u + u'} = \widetilde{u} + \widetilde{u'}$. The stalk at $\mathfrak{p}$ is the colimit over the $D(f) \ni \mathfrak{p}$, and under the isomorphism of Theorem P3.1.11 the colimit of the maps $u_f$ is $u_\mathfrak{p}$.

(2) For every prime $\mathfrak{p}$ the sequence $M'_\mathfrak{p} \to M_\mathfrak{p} \to M''_\mathfrak{p}$ is exact by Theorem P1.2.5. By part 1 this is the sequence of stalks at $\mathfrak{p}$, so the sequence of sheaves is exact by part 3 of Theorem P2.6.3.

(3) The inclusions $M_j \to \bigoplus_i M_i$ give morphisms $\widetilde{M_j} \to (\bigoplus_i M_i)^\sim$, and the universal property of the direct sum in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$ gives the natural map. The direct sum of sheaves is the sheafification of the direct sum of presheaves, so its stalk at $\mathfrak{p}$ is $\bigoplus_i (\widetilde{M_i})_\mathfrak{p} = \bigoplus_i (M_i)_\mathfrak{p}$, since stalks are colimits and colimits commute with direct sums. The stalk of the map at $\mathfrak{p}$ is the natural map $\bigoplus_i (M_i)_\mathfrak{p} \to (\bigoplus_i M_i)_\mathfrak{p}$, an isomorphism because $S^{-1}(-) = S^{-1}A \otimes_A (-)$ commutes with direct sums (Theorem P1.2.5 and part 4 of Proposition P1.1.34). A morphism of sheaves that is an isomorphism on all stalks is an isomorphism, by part 3 of Theorem P2.6.3.

(4) The stalks of $\widetilde{M}$ are the $M_\mathfrak{p}$, so $\widetilde{M} = 0$ if and only if all $M_\mathfrak{p}$ vanish, that is, $M = 0$ by Theorem P1.2.8. If $M' \to M \to M''$ is exact, the sheaf sequence is exact by part 2. Conversely, if the sheaf sequence is exact, then its stalk sequences $M'_\mathfrak{p} \to M_\mathfrak{p} \to M''_\mathfrak{p}$ are exact by part 3 of Theorem P2.6.3. Let $H$ be the homology of $M' \to M \to M''$ at $M$, the kernel modulo the image, and let $w$ be the composite. Localization is exact by Theorem P1.2.5, so $H_\mathfrak{p}$ is the homology of the localized sequence and $w_\mathfrak{p}$ is its composite. So $H_\mathfrak{p} = 0$ and $w_\mathfrak{p} = 0$ for every $\mathfrak{p}$. Then $H = 0$ by Theorem P1.2.8, and $w = 0$ because its image $\operatorname{im} w$ has $(\operatorname{im} w)_\mathfrak{p} = \operatorname{im}(w_\mathfrak{p}) = 0$ for every $\mathfrak{p}$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.5.2.* Let $\varphi \colon M \to \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ be $A$-linear. For a basic open set $D$, every $g \in S_D$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_X(D) = S_D^{-1} A$, so it acts bijectively on the $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$-module $\mathcal{F}(D)$. The composite of $\varphi$ with restriction is an $A$-linear map $M \to \mathcal{F}(D)$ into a module on which $S_D$ acts bijectively. By the universal property of the localization of modules, which has the same proof as Proposition P1.2.4, it factors in exactly one way through an $A$-linear map $\varphi_D \colon S_D^{-1} M \to \mathcal{F}(D)$, namely $\varphi_D(m/g) = g^{-1} \varphi(m)|_D$. This map is $S_D^{-1}A$-linear. For $D' \subseteq D$ the two maps $S_D^{-1} M \to \mathcal{F}(D')$, restriction after $\varphi_D$ and $\varphi_{D'}$ after restriction, are $A$-linear and agree on the image of $M$, so they agree by the uniqueness. So the $\varphi_D$ form a morphism of sheaves on the basis, and by part 2 of Theorem P2.5.34 they extend to a unique morphism $\psi^\varphi \colon \widetilde{M} \to \mathcal{F}$. It is $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear by the argument in part 1 of Proposition P3.5.1, and $(\psi^\varphi)_X = \varphi_{D(1)} = \varphi$.

Conversely, let $\psi \colon \widetilde{M} \to \mathcal{F}$ be $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear and $\varphi = \psi_X$. For every basic open set $D$, the map $\psi_D$ is $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$-linear and its composite with $M \to S_D^{-1} M$ is $\varphi$ followed by restriction. So $\psi_D = \varphi_D$ by the uniqueness above, and $\psi = \psi^\varphi$ by part 2 of Theorem P2.5.34. So $\psi \mapsto \psi_X$ and $\varphi \mapsto \psi^\varphi$ are inverse bijections. Naturality in $\mathcal{F}$ is the statement that $(\alpha \psi)_X = \alpha_X \psi_X$ for $\alpha \colon \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$. Naturality in $M$ is the statement that $(\psi \widetilde{u})_X = \psi_X u$ for $u \colon M' \to M$, which holds because $\widetilde{u}_X = u$.

(1) Take $\mathcal{F} = \widetilde{N}$, with $\Gamma(X, \widetilde{N}) = N$ by Corollary P3.1.5. The bijection sends $\widetilde{u}$ to $\widetilde{u}_X = u$. So every morphism $\widetilde{M} \to \widetilde{N}$ is $\widetilde{u}$ for exactly one $u$.

(2) The global sections $m \in M = \Gamma(X, \widetilde{M})$ and $n \in N$ give the global section $m \otimes n$ of $\widetilde{M} \otimes \widetilde{N}$ (Definition P2.6.11), and $(m, n) \mapsto m \otimes n$ is $A$-bilinear. It induces an $A$-linear map $M \otimes_A N \to \Gamma(X, \widetilde{M} \otimes \widetilde{N})$, and the adjunction turns it into a morphism $\beta \colon (M \otimes_A N)^\sim \to \widetilde{M} \otimes \widetilde{N}$, natural in $M$ and $N$. On the stalk at $\mathfrak{p}$, using part 2 of Theorem P2.6.13 for the target, $\beta$ is the map $(M \otimes_A N)_\mathfrak{p} \to M_\mathfrak{p} \otimes_{A_\mathfrak{p}} N_\mathfrak{p}$, $(m \otimes n)/s \mapsto (m/s) \otimes (n/1)$. This map is an isomorphism, with inverse $(m/s) \otimes (n/t) \mapsto (m \otimes n)/(st)$; both are well defined by the universal property of the tensor product. So $\beta$ is an isomorphism on stalks, hence an isomorphism.

(3) Take $M = \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ and $\varphi = \mathrm{id}$. Then $\theta_\mathcal{F} = \psi^{\mathrm{id}}$ and $(\theta_\mathcal{F})_X = \mathrm{id}$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.5.3.* (1) Identify $D(f)$ with $\operatorname{Spec} A_f$. A basic open set of $\operatorname{Spec} A_f$ is $D(g/f^n) = D(g/1)$, and it corresponds to $D(fg) \subseteq X$. On it, $(M_f)^\sim$ has sections $(M_f)_{g} = M_{fg}$, which are the sections of $\widetilde{M}$ over $D(fg)$, compatibly with restriction. So the two sheaves agree on a basis of $D(f)$, as modules over $\mathcal{O}_X|_{D(f)} = \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec} A_f}$, and they are isomorphic by part 1 of Theorem P2.5.34.

(2) This is Example P2.6.20, whose proof uses the adjunction of Theorem P3.5.2. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $A = \mathbb{Z}$ and $M = \mathbb{Z}/6$. Describe $\widetilde{M}(U)$ for every open set $U$ of $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$, and the stalks of $\widetilde{M}$.
2. (A) Let $M = A^{(I)}$ be a free module. Show that $\widetilde{M} \cong \mathcal{O}_X^{(I)}$, and that the bijection of Theorem P3.5.2 becomes the bijection of Proposition P2.6.4.
3. (A) Let $I \subseteq A$ be an ideal. Show that $\widetilde{I} \to \mathcal{O}_X$ is injective with image the ideal sheaf whose sections over $D(f)$ are $I A_f$, and that $\widetilde{A/I} = \mathcal{O}_X / \widetilde{I}$.
4. (B) Show that the support $\{\mathfrak{p} : M_\mathfrak{p} \neq 0\}$ of $\widetilde{M}$ is closed when $M$ is finitely generated, and give a module over $k[x]$ whose support is not closed. (Use $\bigoplus_{n} k[x]/(x - a_n)$ for distinct $a_n \in k$, with $k$ infinite.)
5. (B) Let $M$ and $N$ be $A$-modules with $M$ finitely presented. Show that $\mathcal{H}om(\widetilde{M}, \widetilde{N}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(M, N)^\sim$. (Construct the map with Theorem P3.5.2, and compare stalks with part 4 of Proposition P2.6.27 and the fact that $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, N)_\mathfrak{p} = \operatorname{Hom}_{A_\mathfrak{p}}(M_\mathfrak{p}, N_\mathfrak{p})$ for $M$ finitely presented.)
6. (B) Let $A = k[x]$ and $M = k[x, x^{-1}]$. Show that $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, A) = 0$, but that $\mathcal{H}om(\widetilde{M}, \mathcal{O}_X)$ has sections $A_x$ over $D(x)$. Conclude that the map of Exercise 5 is not an isomorphism for this $M$, which is not finitely generated.
7. (C) Let $M$ be an $A$-module and $U = \operatorname{Spec} A \setminus V(I)$ for an ideal $I = (f_1, \dots, f_r)$. Show that $\Gamma(U, \widetilde{M})$ is the kernel of $\prod_i M_{f_i} \to \prod_{i, j} M_{f_i f_j}$. For $A = k[x, y]$, $M = A$ and $I = (x, y)$, recover $\Gamma(U, \mathcal{O}) = k[x, y]$ (Example P3.1.10), and compute $\Gamma(U, \widetilde{M})$ for $M = A/(x)$.

### Lean

Mathlib defines `AlgebraicGeometry.tilde` for a module over a commutative ring as a sheaf of modules on `Spec R`, with sections defined by locally fractional elements, like the structure sheaf. The functor `AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.functor` is additive, fully faithful (`AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.fullyFaithfulFunctor`) and left adjoint to global sections (`AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.adjunction`). The maps `AlgebraicGeometry.tilde.toOpen` carry an instance of `IsLocalizedModule.Away f` on $D(f)$, which is the statement $\widetilde{M}(D(f)) = M_f$. The counit is `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.fromTildeΓ`. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ is in EGA I, Chapter I, Section 1.3, where Grothendieck proves the adjunction in the same form. Hartshorne, Proposition II.5.1, lists the exactness and the compatibility with direct sums, tensor products and the maps of affine schemes. The literature state is theorem. Example P3.5.6 is the first sign of the torsion that appears in the tensor products of divisorial sheaves on singular varieties, where the program replaces $\mathcal{O}_X(D) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(D')$ by its reflexive hull.
