# Quasi-coherent and coherent sheaves

A module over a ring $A$ is the algebraic form of a family of abelian groups over $\operatorname{Spec} A$: the localization $M_\mathfrak{p}$ is the group over the point $\mathfrak{p}$, and $M \otimes \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is the fibre. Chapter 1 made a module $M$ into a sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ on $\operatorname{Spec} A$. This chapter proves that this construction loses nothing, and it uses it to define the sheaves that the rest of the handbook works with. A quasi-coherent sheaf on a scheme is a sheaf of modules that looks like $\widetilde{M}$ on every affine open set. Line bundles, vector bundles, ideal sheaves, the sheaves $\mathcal{O}(n)$ of Chapter 6, the sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ of Chapter 8 and the canonical sheaf of Chapter 12 are all of this kind.

Section 5.1 proves that $M \mapsto \widetilde{M}$ is an exact functor that commutes with direct sums and tensor products, and that it is left adjoint to the functor of global sections. In particular it is fully faithful. Section 5.2 proves that a sheaf of modules on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is quasi-coherent if and only if it is $\widetilde{M}$ for a module $M$, so that $A$-modules and quasi-coherent sheaves on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ form equivalent categories. Finitely generated and finitely presented modules correspond to sheaves of finite type and of finite presentation. Section 5.3 proves that the quasi-coherent sheaves on a scheme form an abelian category, defines coherent sheaves on a locally Noetherian scheme, identifies closed subschemes with quasi-coherent ideal sheaves, and proves the sheaf form of Nakayama's lemma: the fibre dimension of a sheaf of finite type is upper semicontinuous, and on a reduced scheme a sheaf of finite type with constant fibre dimension is locally free. Section 5.4 proves that pushforward along a quasi-compact and quasi-separated morphism preserves quasi-coherence, constructs the relative spectrum of a quasi-coherent algebra, and constructs the total space of a vector bundle.

In the minimal model program the sheaves $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mK_X)$ of a contraction are quasi-coherent by Section 5.4, and the canonical ring that defines the canonical model is the algebra of their global sections. The relative spectrum constructs cyclic covers, among them the index one cover of a singularity in Volume 4, and the upper semicontinuity of Section 5.3 is the reason why the locus where a sheaf fails to be locally free is closed.

The chapter follows Vakil, Chapters 13 and 14, Hartshorne, Section II.5, and Görtz-Wedhorn, Chapter 7. It uses Chapters 1 to 4, the sheaves of modules of P2 Ch. 6 and the localization of P1 Ch. 2.
