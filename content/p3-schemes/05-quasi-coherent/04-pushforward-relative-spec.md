## Pushforward, pullback and the relative spectrum

### Statement

Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a morphism of schemes. By Theorem P2.6.19 it gives the adjoint functors $f^*$ and $f_*$ between $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_Y)$ and $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$. This section proves that both preserve quasi-coherence, the pushforward under a finiteness condition on $f$, and uses the pushforward to describe affine morphisms by sheaves of algebras.

**Proposition P3.5.32 (pullback).** Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a quasi-coherent sheaf on $Y$.

1. $f^* \mathcal{G}$ is quasi-coherent. If $\mathcal{G}$ is of finite type, of finite presentation or locally free of rank $r$, then so is $f^* \mathcal{G}$.
2. If $U \subseteq X$ and $V \subseteq Y$ are affine open sets with $f(U) \subseteq V$, then $(f^* \mathcal{G})(U) = \mathcal{G}(V) \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y(V)} \mathcal{O}_X(U)$.
3. For a closed subscheme $Z \subseteq Y$ with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$, the fibre product $Z \times_Y X$ is the closed subscheme of $X$ whose ideal sheaf is the image $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{O}_X$ of $f^* \mathcal{I} \to f^* \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_X$. It is the *scheme-theoretic preimage* $f^{-1}(Z)$.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.pullback` is the functor $f^*$, left adjoint to `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.pushforward` by `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.pullbackPushforwardAdjunction`. The preservation of quasi-coherence and part 2 are not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.5.33 (pushforward).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a quasi-coherent sheaf on $X$.

1. If $f$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, then $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is quasi-coherent. If also $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A$ is affine, then $f_* \mathcal{F} \cong \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})^\sim$, with $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ viewed as an $A$-module.
2. If $f$ is affine, then $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is quasi-coherent, and for every affine open $V \subseteq Y$, $f_* \mathcal{F}|_V \cong (\mathcal{F}(f^{-1}V))^\sim$.
3. If $Y$ is locally Noetherian, $f$ is finite and $\mathcal{F}$ is coherent, then $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is coherent.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.isIso_fromTildeΓ_pushforward` is part 2 for a morphism $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$. Parts 1 and 3 are not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.5.34 (quasi-coherent algebra).** A *quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra* is a sheaf $\mathcal{A}$ of commutative rings on $Y$ with a morphism of sheaves of rings $\mathcal{O}_Y \to \mathcal{A}$ that makes $\mathcal{A}$ a quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module. A morphism of such algebras is a morphism of sheaves of rings compatible with the maps from $\mathcal{O}_Y$. For an affine morphism $f \colon X \to Y$, the sheaf $f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ is a quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra by part 2 of Theorem P3.5.33.

**Theorem P3.5.35 (the relative spectrum).** Let $Y$ be a scheme and $\mathcal{A}$ a quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra.

1. There is a $Y$-scheme $\pi \colon \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A} \to Y$ with the following universal property: for every $Y$-scheme $g \colon T \to Y$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_Y(T, \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_Y\text{-alg}}(\mathcal{A}, g_* \mathcal{O}_T),$$
naturally in $T$. It is unique up to unique isomorphism.
2. The morphism $\pi$ is affine, $\pi^{-1}(V) \cong \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{A}(V)$ over $V$ for every affine open $V \subseteq Y$, and $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A}} \cong \mathcal{A}$.
3. The functors $\mathcal{A} \mapsto \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A}$ and $(f \colon X \to Y) \mapsto f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ are inverse anti-equivalences between the category of quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebras and the category of affine morphisms to $Y$.

**Proposition P3.5.36 (symmetric algebras and total spaces).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a quasi-coherent sheaf on $Y$.

1. The sheafification $\operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{F} = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \operatorname{Sym}^n \mathcal{F}$ of the presheaf $U \mapsto \operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_Y(U)} \mathcal{F}(U)$ is a quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra, with $(\operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{F})(U) = \operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_Y(U)} \mathcal{F}(U)$ for affine $U$. For every quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra $\mathcal{B}$, restriction to $\operatorname{Sym}^1 \mathcal{F} = \mathcal{F}$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_Y\text{-alg}}(\operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{F}, \mathcal{B}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_Y}(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{B})$.
2. Let $\mathcal{E}$ be locally free of rank $r$ and define its *total space* $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}) = \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee)$. Over an open set $U$ on which $\mathcal{E}|_U \cong \mathcal{O}_U^r$, $\pi^{-1}(U) \cong \mathbb{A}^r_U = \mathbb{A}^r_{\mathbb{Z}} \times U$. For every open $U \subseteq Y$, the sections of $\pi$ over $U$, the morphisms $s \colon U \to \mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})$ with $\pi s = \mathrm{id}_U$, are in natural bijection with $\mathcal{E}(U)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

Pullback is local, and on affine pieces it is extension of scalars $M \mapsto M \otimes_A B$, which keeps a presentation a presentation. Pushforward is harder, because the preimage of an affine set need not be affine. If it is covered by finitely many affine sets whose intersections are again covered by finitely many affine sets, the sheaf axiom writes $f_* \mathcal{F}$ as the kernel of a map between finite sums of pushforwards along morphisms of affine schemes. Those are quasi-coherent by the formula $g_* \widetilde{N} = \widetilde{N_A}$, and kernels of quasi-coherent sheaves are quasi-coherent.

The relative spectrum glues the affine schemes $\operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{A}(V)$. They fit together because $\mathcal{A}(D(g)) = \mathcal{A}(V)_g$, which is the quasi-coherence of $\mathcal{A}$, and $\operatorname{Spec}$ of a localization is a basic open set. The universal property holds on each affine piece by the anti-equivalence of Chapter 1, and both sides of it glue. The total space of a vector bundle is the relative spectrum of the symmetric algebra of the dual. A section of it is an algebra map from $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee)$ to functions, that is, a linear form on $\mathcal{E}^\vee$, that is, a section of $\mathcal{E}$.

### Examples

**Example P3.5.37 (pushforward needs quasi-compactness).** Let $Y = \mathbb{A}^1_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, $X = \bigsqcup_{n \geq 1} Y$ and $f \colon X \to Y$ the identity on each copy, a morphism that is not quasi-compact. Then $(f_* \mathcal{O}_X)(V) = \prod_n \mathcal{O}_Y(V)$ for every open $V$. On $D(t)$ the sections are $\prod_n k[t, t^{-1}]$, and by Example P3.3.6 this is not the localization at $t$ of the global sections $\prod_n k[t]$. So $f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ is not quasi-coherent, by part 2 of Corollary P3.5.13. It is the product sheaf of Example P3.5.7.

**Example P3.5.38 (pushforward along open immersions).** Let $j \colon U = \mathbb{A}^2_k \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{A}^2_k$ be the inclusion. It is quasi-compact and separated, so $j_* \mathcal{O}_U$ is quasi-coherent, and by part 1 of Theorem P3.5.33 it is the sheaf of $\Gamma(U, \mathcal{O}_U) = k[x, y]$ (Example P3.1.10). So $j_* \mathcal{O}_U = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^2}$: a function on the punctured plane extends across the origin. For $j \colon \mathbb{A}^1_k \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$ the same theorem gives $j_* \mathcal{O} = (k[x, x^{-1}])^\sim$, quasi-coherent but not of finite type, so not coherent.

**Example P3.5.39 (the normalization of the cusp).** Let $C = \operatorname{Spec} k[t^2, t^3]$, the cusp, and $\nu \colon \mathbb{A}^1_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[t] \to C$ its normalization (Example P3.3.15). The morphism is finite, since $k[t] = k[t^2, t^3] \cdot 1 + k[t^2, t^3] \cdot t$, so $\nu_* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^1} = (k[t])^\sim$ is a coherent $\mathcal{O}_C$-algebra and $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_C \nu_* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^1} = \mathbb{A}^1_k$. The cokernel of $\mathcal{O}_C \to \nu_* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^1}$ is the sheaf of $k[t]/k[t^2, t^3] \cong k \cdot t$, a skyscraper sheaf of length $1$ at the cusp. Its length is the drop in arithmetic genus that the normalization of a cusp produces, and it is the $\delta$-invariant of the singularity.

**Example P3.5.40 (total space of a line bundle).** Let $\mathcal{L}$ be a line bundle on $Y$ with trivializations $\varphi_i \colon \mathcal{L}|_{U_i} \to \mathcal{O}_{U_i}$ and transition functions $g_{ij} = \varphi_i \varphi_j^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}^\times(U_{ij})$ (Proposition P2.6.28). The dual $\mathcal{L}^\vee|_{U_i}$ is free on the form $\varphi_i$, so $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{L}^\vee)|_{U_i} = \mathcal{O}_{U_i}[t_i]$ with $t_i = \varphi_i$, and $\pi^{-1}(U_i) = \mathbb{A}^1_{U_i}$. On $U_{ij}$, $\varphi_i = g_{ij} \varphi_j$, so the coordinates are glued by $t_i = g_{ij} t_j$. A section of $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{L})$ over $U$ is a family of functions $s_i = t_i \circ s$ on $U \cap U_i$ with $s_i = g_{ij} s_j$, which is a section of $\mathcal{L}$ written in the trivializations. On $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ with the line bundles $\mathcal{O}(n)$ of Example P2.6.9, this gluing gives the total spaces of Chapter 6, and the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ is the blow-up of the plane at the origin of Chapter 11.

**Example P3.5.41 (a double cover).** Let $\mathcal{L}$ be a line bundle on $Y$ and $s \in \Gamma(Y, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})$. The section $s$ is a map $(\mathcal{L}^\vee)^{\otimes 2} \to \mathcal{O}_Y$, and it defines a multiplication on $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{O}_Y \oplus \mathcal{L}^\vee$, with $(a, u)(b, v) = (ab + s(u \otimes v), av + bu)$. This algebra is quasi-coherent, and $X = \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A} \to Y$ is finite of degree $2$. On an open set $U = \operatorname{Spec} B$ where $\mathcal{L}$ is trivial, with $s$ given by $b \in B$, it is $\operatorname{Spec} B[y]/(y^2 - b)$. If $2$ is invertible on $Y$, the morphism is ramified exactly over $V(s)$. For $Y = \mathbb{P}^1$, $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{O}(g + 1)$ and $s$ a section with distinct zeros, it is a hyperelliptic curve of genus $g$. Cyclic covers of higher degree are built in the same way from $\bigoplus_{i < m} (\mathcal{L}^\vee)^{\otimes i}$.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.5.32.* (2) Let $j_U \colon U \to X$ and $j_V \colon V \to Y$ be the inclusions and $g \colon U \to V$ the restriction of $f$. For an open immersion, the pullback is the restriction, since by Theorem P2.6.19 and the adjunction of Example P2.6.25 both are left adjoint to the pushforward. So $(f^* \mathcal{G})|_U = j_U^* f^* \mathcal{G} \cong (f j_U)^* \mathcal{G} = (j_V g)^* \mathcal{G} \cong g^*(\mathcal{G}|_V)$ by part 5 of Theorem P2.6.19. With $\mathcal{G}|_V \cong \mathcal{G}(V)^\sim$ (Corollary P3.5.13), Proposition P3.5.3 gives $g^*(\mathcal{G}|_V) \cong (\mathcal{G}(V) \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y(V)} \mathcal{O}_X(U))^\sim$, whose sections over $U$ are as stated.

(1) By part 2, $(f^* \mathcal{G})|_U$ is the sheaf of a module for every affine $U$ that maps into an affine $V$, and such $U$ cover $X$. So $f^* \mathcal{G}$ is quasi-coherent by Corollary P3.5.13. The other properties are part 2 of Proposition P2.6.27.

(3) The question is local on $Y$ and $X$, so let $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A$, $X = \operatorname{Spec} B$ and $Z = \operatorname{Spec} A/I$. By part 2 of Proposition P3.3.20, $Z \times_Y X = \operatorname{Spec} B/IB$. By part 2 and the right exactness of $f^*$, $f^* \widetilde{I} = (I \otimes_A B)^\sim$, and the image of $I \otimes_A B \to B$ is $IB$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.5.33.* (2) Let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open set of $Y$. Then $f^{-1}(V) = \operatorname{Spec} B$ is affine and $\mathcal{F}|_{f^{-1}V} \cong \widetilde{N}$ with $N = \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}V)$, by Corollary P3.5.13. Pushforward commutes with restriction to open sets of the target, so $(f_* \mathcal{F})|_V$ is the pushforward of $\widetilde{N}$ along $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$, which is $(N_A)^\sim$ by Proposition P3.5.3. So $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is quasi-coherent by Corollary P3.5.13.

(1) Quasi-coherence is local on $Y$, so let $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A$. Then $X$ is quasi-compact; cover it by finitely many affine open sets $U_1, \dots, U_r$. Each $U_i \cap U_j$ is quasi-compact. Indeed, $U_i \times_Y U_j$ is an open set of $X \times_Y X$ (part 1 of Proposition P3.3.20), and it is affine, being a fibre product of affine schemes, and its preimage under the diagonal is $U_i \cap U_j$, since both projections composed with $\Delta$ are the identity. The diagonal is quasi-compact, so $U_i \cap U_j$ is quasi-compact. Cover each $U_i \cap U_j$ by finitely many affine open sets $U_{ijk}$. By the sheaf axiom, for every open $V \subseteq Y$ the sequence
$$0 \to \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}V) \to \prod_i \mathcal{F}(U_i \cap f^{-1}V) \to \prod_{i, j, k} \mathcal{F}(U_{ijk} \cap f^{-1}V)$$
is exact, where the second map sends $(s_i)$ to $(s_i - s_j)$ restricted to $U_{ijk}$. In terms of sheaves on $Y$, with $f_i \colon U_i \to Y$ and $f_{ijk} \colon U_{ijk} \to Y$ the restrictions of $f$, this is an exact sequence
$$0 \to f_* \mathcal{F} \to \bigoplus_i f_{i*}(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i}) \to \bigoplus_{i, j, k} f_{ijk*}(\mathcal{F}|_{U_{ijk}}),$$
where the products are finite and so are direct sums. The morphisms $f_i$ and $f_{ijk}$ are morphisms of affine schemes, so the terms on the right are quasi-coherent by part 2, and their finite sums are quasi-coherent. The kernel $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is quasi-coherent by Theorem P3.5.20. On the affine scheme $Y$ it is the sheaf of its global sections $\Gamma(Y, f_* \mathcal{F}) = \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$, by Theorem P3.5.12.

(3) Let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open set of $Y$, with $f^{-1}(V) = \operatorname{Spec} B$ and $B$ a finitely generated $A$-module. The ring $A$ is Noetherian (Proposition P3.2.23), so $B$ is Noetherian, being a quotient of a polynomial ring $A[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ (Theorem P1.3.10), and $N = \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}V)$ is a finitely generated $B$-module by Proposition P3.5.22. If $b_1, \dots, b_m$ generate $B$ over $A$ and $n_1, \dots, n_l$ generate $N$ over $B$, the products $b_i n_j$ generate $N$ over $A$. So $(f_* \mathcal{F})|_V = (N_A)^\sim$ is coherent. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.5.35.* (1) Uniqueness up to unique isomorphism follows from the universal property and the Yoneda lemma (Theorem P2.1.23). For existence, the affine case comes first.

*The affine case.* Let $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $B = \mathcal{A}(Y)$, an $A$-algebra. By Theorem P3.5.12, $\mathcal{A} \cong \widetilde{B}$ as modules, and the multiplication of $\mathcal{A}$ is the sheaf of the multiplication of $B$, because both are morphisms $\widetilde{B} \otimes \widetilde{B} \cong (B \otimes_A B)^\sim \to \widetilde{B}$ (part 2 of Theorem P3.5.2) with the same map on global sections. Put $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A} = \operatorname{Spec} B$ with the morphism given by $A \to B$. For a $Y$-scheme $g \colon T \to Y$, a morphism $T \to \operatorname{Spec} B$ is a ring map $B \to \Gamma(T, \mathcal{O}_T)$ (Theorem P3.1.22), and it is a morphism over $Y$ if and only if this ring map is an $A$-algebra map. On the other side, a morphism of $\mathcal{O}_Y$-modules $\widetilde{B} \to g_* \mathcal{O}_T$ is an $A$-linear map $B \to \Gamma(T, \mathcal{O}_T)$, by Theorem P3.5.2. It is a morphism of algebras if and only if its map on global sections is, because its map on $D(a)$ is the localization of the map on global sections, and a localization of a ring map is a ring map. So both sides are the set of $A$-algebra maps $B \to \Gamma(T, \mathcal{O}_T)$, naturally in $T$.

*Gluing.* For every affine open $V \subseteq Y$ let $\pi_V \colon X_V = \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{A}(V) \to V$ be the affine construction for $\mathcal{A}|_V$. Let $W \subseteq V$ be affine open sets. Then $\pi_V^{-1}(W)$, with the morphism to $W$, has the universal property of $X_W$: a $W$-scheme $T$ is a $V$-scheme whose structure morphism lands in $W$, a $V$-morphism $T \to X_V$ from such a $T$ lands in $\pi_V^{-1}(W)$, and the algebra maps from $\mathcal{A}|_V$ to the pushforward of $\mathcal{O}_T$ to $V$ are the algebra maps from $\mathcal{A}|_W$ to the pushforward of $\mathcal{O}_T$ to $W$. For the last point, let $g_W \colon T \to W$ be the structure morphism and $i \colon W \to V$ the inclusion. The pushforward to $V$ is $i_* g_{W*} \mathcal{O}_T$, and the adjunction for the open immersion $i$ (Example P2.6.25) identifies maps $\mathcal{A}|_V \to i_* g_{W*} \mathcal{O}_T$ with maps $\mathcal{A}|_W \to g_{W*} \mathcal{O}_T$. So there is a unique isomorphism $\rho_{WV} \colon X_W \cong \pi_V^{-1}(W)$ over $W$. By uniqueness these isomorphisms are compatible: for $W' \subseteq W \subseteq V$, $\rho_{W'V} = \rho_{WV} \rho_{W'W}$ on $X_{W'}$. For two affine open sets $V, V'$, cover $V \cap V'$ by affine open sets $W$, and identify $\pi_V^{-1}(W)$ with $\pi_{V'}^{-1}(W)$ through $X_W$. By uniqueness these identifications agree on the overlaps and satisfy the cocycle condition, so the $X_V$ glue (Theorem P3.2.3) to a scheme $X$ with a morphism $\pi \colon X \to Y$, and $\pi^{-1}(V) = X_V$ for every affine $V$.

*Universal property.* A morphism $T \to X$ over $Y$ is the same as a family of morphisms $g^{-1}(V) \to X_V$ over $V$, for $V$ affine, that agree on overlaps, by gluing of morphisms (Proposition P3.2.4). A morphism of algebras $\mathcal{A} \to g_* \mathcal{O}_T$ is the same as a family of morphisms $\mathcal{A}|_V \to (g_* \mathcal{O}_T)|_V$ that agree on overlaps, by the sheaf property of morphisms (part 2 of Theorem P2.5.34 applied on each $V$). The bijections of the affine case are natural, so they are compatible with restriction and glue to the bijection of part 1.

(2) The morphism $\pi$ is affine since $\pi^{-1}(V) = \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{A}(V)$ for affine $V$, and affineness is local on the target (Proposition P3.3.30). The identity of $X$ corresponds by part 1 to a morphism of algebras $\mathcal{A} \to \pi_* \mathcal{O}_X$. On an affine $V$ it is the map $\mathcal{A}(V) \to \Gamma(\operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{A}(V), \mathcal{O}) = \mathcal{A}(V)$, the identity. Both sheaves are quasi-coherent, $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_X$ by part 2 of Theorem P3.5.33, so the morphism is an isomorphism on every affine $V$ by Theorem P3.5.12, hence an isomorphism.

(3) By part 2, $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A}} \cong \mathcal{A}$. Conversely let $f \colon X \to Y$ be affine and $\mathcal{A} = f_* \mathcal{O}_X$. The identity of $\mathcal{A}$ corresponds by part 1 to a morphism $X \to \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A}$ over $Y$. Over an affine $V$ it is the morphism $f^{-1}(V) \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}_X(f^{-1}V)$ given by the identity of $\mathcal{O}_X(f^{-1} V)$, an isomorphism because $f^{-1}(V)$ is affine. So it is an isomorphism. Both constructions are functorial by the universal property, so they are inverse anti-equivalences. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.5.36.* (1) Let $P$ be the presheaf $U \mapsto \operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_Y(U)} \mathcal{F}(U)$, a presheaf of $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebras, and let $U = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be affine with $M = \mathcal{F}(U)$. The sheaf $(\operatorname{Sym}_A M)^\sim$ is a quasi-coherent algebra on $U$, as in the affine case of the previous proof, and $M = \operatorname{Sym}^1_A M$ gives $\mathcal{F}|_U \to (\operatorname{Sym}_A M)^\sim$. For an open $W \subseteq U$, the universal property of the symmetric algebra turns the $\mathcal{O}_Y(W)$-linear map $\mathcal{F}(W) \to (\operatorname{Sym}_A M)^\sim(W)$ into an algebra map $P(W) \to (\operatorname{Sym}_A M)^\sim(W)$, naturally in $W$. On the stalk at $\mathfrak{p}$ it is the map $\operatorname{Sym}_{A_\mathfrak{p}} M_\mathfrak{p} \to (\operatorname{Sym}_A M)_\mathfrak{p}$, since stalks can be computed on basic open sets and $\operatorname{Sym}_{A_f}(M_f) = (\operatorname{Sym}_A M)_f$. This is an isomorphism, because the symmetric algebra commutes with base change, $\operatorname{Sym}_{A_\mathfrak{p}}(M \otimes_A A_\mathfrak{p}) = (\operatorname{Sym}_A M) \otimes_A A_\mathfrak{p}$. So the sheafification of $P$ is $(\operatorname{Sym}_A M)^\sim$ on $U$, which gives quasi-coherence and the sections over $U$. The universal property holds for the presheaf $P$ and an algebra $\mathcal{B}$, open set by open set, and it passes to the sheafification by part 1 of Theorem P2.6.3.

(2) If $\mathcal{E}|_U \cong \mathcal{O}_U^r$, then $\mathcal{E}^\vee|_U \cong \mathcal{O}_U^r$ with the dual basis $t_1, \dots, t_r$, and $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee)|_U = \mathcal{O}_U[t_1, \dots, t_r]$. So $\pi^{-1}(U) = \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_U \mathcal{O}_U[t_1, \dots, t_r]$, which is $\mathbb{A}^r_U$ by the universal property: both represent the functor that sends a $U$-scheme $T$ to $\Gamma(T, \mathcal{O}_T)^r$.

Let $j \colon U \to Y$ be the inclusion of an open set. By part 1 of Theorem P3.5.35, a section $s$ of $\pi$ over $U$, a morphism $U \to \mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})$ over $Y$, is a morphism of algebras $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee) \to j_* \mathcal{O}_U$. By the adjunction of the open immersion $j$ (Example P2.6.25), it is a morphism of algebras $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee)|_U \to \mathcal{O}_U$. By part 1, it is an $\mathcal{O}_U$-linear map $\mathcal{E}^\vee|_U \to \mathcal{O}_U$, that is, a section of $\mathcal{E}^{\vee\vee}$ over $U$ (part 1 of Theorem P2.6.13). For $\mathcal{E}$ locally free of finite rank, the natural map $\mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{E}^{\vee\vee}$ is an isomorphism, since it is one for $\mathcal{O}^r$. So sections of $\pi$ over $U$ correspond to $\mathcal{E}(U)$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $i \colon Z \to X$ be a closed immersion. Show that $i_*$ is an exact, fully faithful functor from $\mathsf{QCoh}(Z)$ to $\mathsf{QCoh}(X)$, and that its image consists of the quasi-coherent sheaves $\mathcal{F}$ with $\mathcal{I}_Z \mathcal{F} = 0$.
2. (A) Let $x \in X$ be a closed point. Show that the skyscraper sheaf $\kappa(x)$ at $x$ is quasi-coherent, and that it is coherent if $X$ is locally Noetherian.
3. (A) Show that $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{O}_Y = Y$, that $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y (\mathcal{O}_Y / \mathcal{I})$ is the closed subscheme of $\mathcal{I}$, and that $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y (\mathcal{A} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y} \mathcal{B}) = \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A} \times_Y \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{B}$.
4. (B) Let $Y' \to Y$ be a morphism and $\mathcal{A}$ a quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra. Show that $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A} \times_Y Y' \cong \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_{Y'} (g^* \mathcal{A})$ for $g \colon Y' \to Y$. Deduce that $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})$ commutes with base change.
5. (B) (Projection formula for affine morphisms.) Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be affine, $\mathcal{F}$ quasi-coherent on $X$ and $\mathcal{E}$ locally free of finite rank on $Y$. Show that $f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes f^* \mathcal{E}) \cong f_* \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{E}$, and compare with Proposition P2.6.30.
6. (B) Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be affine. Show that $f_*$ is an equivalence between $\mathsf{QCoh}(X)$ and the category of quasi-coherent $f_* \mathcal{O}_X$-modules on $Y$. For the double cover of Example P3.5.41, describe $f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ as an $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module.
7. (C) Let $\mathcal{L}$ be a line bundle on $Y$ and let $\mathcal{A} = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}$, with $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes -1} = \mathcal{L}^\vee$. Show that $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}}_Y \mathcal{A}$ is the complement of the zero section in $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{L}^\vee)$, and that it is a principal $\mathbb{G}_m$-bundle over $Y$: locally on $Y$ it is $U \times \mathbb{G}_m$ with $\mathbb{G}_m = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]$.

### Lean

Mathlib has the functors `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.pullback` and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.pushforward` with their adjunction, and proves in `AlgebraicGeometry.isIso_fromTildeΓ_pushforward` that the pushforward along $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ of a sheaf of the form $\widetilde{N}$ is again of this form. The source file states as a goal that quasi-coherence of pushforwards along affine morphisms should follow. The relative spectrum, the symmetric algebra of a sheaf and the total space of a vector bundle are not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The relative spectrum is EGA II, Section 1, and Hartshorne, Exercise II.5.17. Hartshorne, Proposition II.5.8, proves the quasi-coherence of $f_* \mathcal{F}$ for $X$ Noetherian or $f$ quasi-compact and separated; the quasi-separated form is EGA I, Proposition 9.2.1. The convention $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}) = \operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}} \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee)$ makes the sections of $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})$ the sections of $\mathcal{E}$; Grothendieck's $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})$ is $\operatorname{\mathbf{Spec}} \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E})$, whose sections are those of $\mathcal{E}^\vee$, and Chapter 6 states which convention it uses for $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program the relative spectrum constructs the cyclic covers and the index one cover of Volume 4 from a sheaf of algebras $\bigoplus_{i < m} \mathcal{O}_X(-iD)$, and the pushforward theorem shows that the direct images $f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ along a projective contraction are quasi-coherent.
