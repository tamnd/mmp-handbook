## Cartier and Weil divisors

### Statement

In this section $X$ is a normal integral Noetherian scheme with function field $K$, so that Chapter 8 applies. A Weil divisor $D$ is *locally principal at* $x$ if $D|_U = \operatorname{div}(f)|_U$ for an open $U \ni x$ and some $f \in K^*$ (Definition P3.8.27), and *locally principal* if it is so at every point.

**Proposition P3.9.9 (the Weil divisor of a Cartier divisor).** For a Cartier divisor $D = \{(U_i, f_i)\}$ put
$$
[D] = \sum_P \operatorname{ord}_P(f_i) \, P ,
$$
the sum over the prime divisors $P$ of $X$, where for each $P$ the index $i$ is any index with $P \cap U_i \neq \emptyset$.

1. $[D]$ is a well defined Weil divisor, and $D \mapsto [D]$ is an injective homomorphism $\operatorname{CaDiv}(X) \to \operatorname{Div}(X)$ with $[\operatorname{div}(g)] = \operatorname{div}(g)$ for $g \in K^*$.
2. $D$ is effective if and only if $[D] \geq 0$.
3. The image is the group of locally principal Weil divisors.
4. $\mathcal{O}_X(D) = \mathcal{O}_X([D])$ as subsheaves of $\mathcal{K}$, with the sheaf of Proposition P3.9.3 on the left and that of Definition P3.8.27 on the right.
5. $D \mapsto [D]$ induces an injective homomorphism $\operatorname{CaCl}(X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(X)$, and with Theorem P3.9.4 an injective homomorphism $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \operatorname{Cl}(X)$ whose image is the group of classes of locally principal divisors.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.9.10 (locally factorial schemes).** Let $Y$ be an integral Noetherian scheme such that every local ring $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$ is a unique factorization domain. Then $Y$ is normal, every Weil divisor on $Y$ is locally principal, and
$$
\operatorname{CaDiv}(Y) \cong \operatorname{Div}(Y), \qquad \operatorname{Pic}(Y) \cong \operatorname{CaCl}(Y) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(Y) .
$$
More precisely, let $D$ be a Weil divisor on a normal integral Noetherian scheme $X$ and $x \in X$ a point such that $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is factorial. Then $D$ is locally principal at $x$.

Such schemes are *locally factorial*.

Lean: not in Lean as stated. Mathlib proves that the Picard group of a unique factorization domain is trivial, as an instance on `CommRing.Pic` for domains with a gcd, which is the affine local input (proved in Mathlib).

**Corollary P3.9.11 (regular schemes and projective space).** Let $k$ be a field.

1. If $X$ is an integral Noetherian regular scheme, for instance a smooth variety over a perfect field, then $X$ is locally factorial and $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X)$.
2. If $X$ is locally factorial, then so is $X \times_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{A}^1$, and the projection induces $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \operatorname{Pic}(X \times \mathbb{A}^1)$.
3. $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{A}^n_k) = 0$, and $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_k) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $\mathcal{O}(1)$. Every invertible sheaf on $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}(d)$ for exactly one $d \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated. Mathlib has `IsRegularLocalRing`, while the theorem of Auslander and Buchsbaum is not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

On a normal scheme a local equation $f_i$ has an order along each prime divisor, and these orders do not depend on the chart because two local equations differ by a unit, which has order zero. A function with order zero along every prime divisor is a unit, so a Cartier divisor is determined by its Weil divisor, and a Weil divisor comes from a Cartier divisor exactly when it has a local equation everywhere. At a point with factorial local ring each prime divisor through the point is cut out by one prime element, and the product of these elements with the right exponents is a local equation. So on a locally factorial scheme, in particular on a regular scheme, the two notions agree, and the class group is the Picard group.

### Examples

**Example P3.9.12 (the smooth quadric surface).** Let $Q = V_+(xw - yz) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$. On $D_+(x)$ the equation gives $w/x = (y/x)(z/x)$, so $Q \cap D_+(x) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[y/x, z/x] = \mathbb{A}^2_k$. The same holds on $D_+(y)$, $D_+(z)$ and $D_+(w)$, by symmetry, and these four charts cover $Q$. Every local ring of $Q$ is a localization of a polynomial ring in two variables, so it is factorial, and Theorem P3.9.10 with part 3 of Proposition P3.8.22 gives $\operatorname{Pic}(Q) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(Q) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, generated by $\mathcal{O}_Q(L_1)$ and $\mathcal{O}_Q(L_2)$ for two lines from the two rulings. The plane section $H$ has $\mathcal{O}_Q(H) \cong \mathcal{O}_Q(1)$, as in Example P3.9.5, and $H \sim L_1 + L_2$. So $\mathcal{O}_Q(1)$ is the class $(1, 1)$.

**Example P3.9.13 (the line on the cone $xy = z^n$).** Let $k$ be a perfect field, $n \geq 2$, $X_n = \operatorname{Spec} A$ with $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^n)$, $L = V(x, z)$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (x, z)$. The scheme $X_n$ is normal (Example P3.7.37). For $1 \leq m \leq n$ the divisor $mL$ is locally principal at the vertex $0$ if and only if $m = n$. In particular $L$ has order $n$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X_n, 0})$, by Proposition P3.9.19.

The local ring of $X_n$ along $L$ is $A_\mathfrak{p}$. There $y$ is a unit and $x = z^n/y$, so $z$ is a uniformizer and $\operatorname{ord}_L(x) = n$, $\operatorname{ord}_L(z) = 1$. The divisor $L$ is the only prime divisor in $V(x)$, as $x = 0$ forces $z = 0$, so $\operatorname{div}(x) = nL$, and $nL$ is principal. Now let $1 \leq m < n$, and put $I = (x, z^m)$. The ring $A/I = k[y, z]/(z^m)$ has only the associated prime $(z)$, which is the image of $\mathfrak{p}$, so $I$ is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary. In $A_\mathfrak{p}$ the element $x = z^n/y$ lies in $z^m A_\mathfrak{p}$, so $I A_\mathfrak{p} = z^m A_\mathfrak{p}$. Hence $I = A \cap z^m A_\mathfrak{p} = \{ f \in A : \operatorname{ord}_L(f) \geq m \}$, the divisorial ideal of $-mL$, and $\mathcal{O}_{X_n}(-mL) = \widetilde{I}$. If $mL$ were locally principal at $0$, then $\widetilde{I}$ would be invertible near $0$ by part 6 of Proposition P3.8.28, and $I_\mathfrak{m}$ would be principal, with $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y, z)$. Give $A$ the grading with $\deg x = \deg y = n$ and $\deg z = 2$, so that $\mathfrak{m}$ is the ideal of elements of positive degree. Then $I$ is homogeneous and generated by $x$ and $z^m$. Neither generator is a multiple of the other: $A$ has the $k$-basis $x^a z^c$, $y^b z^c$ with $b \geq 1$, and neither $x \in z^m A$ nor $z^m \in xA$ holds in this basis. So the graded form of Nakayama's lemma gives $\dim_k I/\mathfrak{m} I = 2$. As $I/\mathfrak{m} I = I_\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m} I_\mathfrak{m}$, Nakayama's lemma shows that $I_\mathfrak{m}$ needs two generators. This contradiction shows that $mL$ is not locally principal at $0$.

**Example P3.9.14 (Picard and class groups of the cones).** In the situation of Example P3.9.13, $\operatorname{Cl}(X_n) = \mathbb{Z}/n$, generated by $[L]$ (part 1 of Proposition P3.8.22). By Example P3.9.13 a divisor $aL$ is locally principal if and only if $n \mid a$, and then it is principal. So $\operatorname{Pic}(X_n) = 0$, while $\operatorname{Cl}(X_n) = \mathbb{Z}/n$. Away from the vertex the cone is locally factorial: $X_n \setminus \{0\} = D(x) \cup D(y)$, as $x = y = 0$ forces $z = 0$, and $A_x = k[x, x^{-1}, z]$ and $A_y = k[y, y^{-1}, z]$ are factorial. So $\operatorname{Pic}(X_n \setminus \{0\}) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X_n \setminus \{0\}) = \mathbb{Z}/n$ by Theorem P3.9.10 and Proposition P3.8.22. Removing the vertex creates line bundles that do not extend.

**Example P3.9.15 (the projective quadric cone).** Let $k$ be perfect and $Q_0 = V_+(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$, with vertex $v = (0 : 0 : 0 : 1)$, line $L = V_+(x, z)$ and plane section $H = V_+(w) \cap Q_0$. Then $\operatorname{Pic}(Q_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $\mathcal{O}_{Q_0}(H) \cong \mathcal{O}_{Q_0}(1)$, and the map $\operatorname{Pic}(Q_0) \to \operatorname{Cl}(Q_0) = \mathbb{Z}[L]$ is multiplication by $2$. Indeed, the charts $Q_0 \cap D_+(x) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[z/x, w/x]$ and $Q_0 \cap D_+(y) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[z/y, w/y]$ are affine planes, and $Q_0 \cap D_+(w)$ is the cone $X_2$, in which $L$ is $V(x, z)$. These three charts cover $Q_0$, since $x = y = 0$ forces $z = 0$. So every divisor is locally principal away from $v$, and by Example P3.9.13 the divisor $aL$ is locally principal at $v$ if and only if $a$ is even. By part 2 of Proposition P3.8.22, $\operatorname{Cl}(Q_0) = \mathbb{Z}[L]$ and $H \sim 2L$, so by Proposition P3.9.9 the image of $\operatorname{Pic}(Q_0)$ is $2\mathbb{Z}[L]$, generated by $[H]$.

**Example P3.9.16 (Dedekind schemes and curves).** If $X$ is normal of dimension $1$, its local rings at closed points are discrete valuation rings (part 5 of Proposition P3.7.32), which are factorial. So $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X)$, and a line bundle is the same as a divisor $\sum n_x x$ of closed points up to linear equivalence. For $X = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ this gives $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ (Example P3.8.15), and for the line with a doubled origin it gives $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (Example P3.8.18), generated by the divisor $D$ of Example P3.9.8, with $[D] = [0]$.

**Example P3.9.17 (a scheme that is not normal).** Let $C = \operatorname{Spec} B$ with $B = k[x, y]/(y^2 - x^3)$, the cusp. Cartier divisors and Theorem P3.9.4 make sense on $C$, while Proposition P3.9.9 does not apply. The function $x$ gives the effective Cartier divisor $\operatorname{div}(x)$, whose subscheme $\operatorname{Spec} k[y]/(y^2)$ is a point of length $2$ at the cusp. The reduced cusp point, with ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$, is not an effective Cartier divisor: $\mathfrak{m}^2 = (x^2, xy, y^2)$, so the images of $x$ and $y$ form a basis of $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$, and $\mathfrak{m}$ is not principal near the cusp by Nakayama's lemma.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P3.9.9.* (1) *Well defined.* Let $P$ meet $U_i$ and $U_j$. The sets $P \cap U_i$ and $P \cap U_j$ are nonempty open subsets of the irreducible space $P$, so they meet, and $\eta_P \in U_i \cap U_j$. As $f_i/f_j$ is a unit on $U_i \cap U_j$, it is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_{X, P}$, and $\operatorname{ord}_P(f_i) = \operatorname{ord}_P(f_j)$ by part 1 of Proposition P3.8.2. Equivalent data give the same coefficients for the same reason. *Finite.* As $X$ is Noetherian, finitely many $U_i$ cover $X$, and by Lemma P3.8.3 each $f_i$ has nonzero order along finitely many prime divisors. *Homomorphism.* Each $\operatorname{ord}_P$ is a homomorphism, and the local equations of $D + E$ are the products $f_i g_j$. For $\operatorname{div}(g) = \{(X, g)\}$, $[\operatorname{div}(g)] = \sum_P \operatorname{ord}_P(g) P = \operatorname{div}(g)$. *Injective.* Let $[D] = 0$. Then $\operatorname{ord}_P(f_i) = 0$ for every prime divisor $P$ that meets $U_i$, so $f_i \in \mathcal{O}_X(U_i)^*$ by part 2 of Proposition P3.8.4. So $\{(U_i, f_i)\}$ is equivalent to $\{(X, 1)\}$, and $D = 0$.

(2) If $f_i \in \mathcal{O}_X(U_i)$, then $\operatorname{ord}_P(f_i) \geq 0$ by part 4 of Proposition P3.8.2. Conversely, if $[D] \geq 0$, then $\operatorname{ord}_P(f_i) \geq 0$ for every $P$ meeting $U_i$, so $f_i \in \mathcal{O}_X(U_i)$ by part 1 of Proposition P3.8.4.

(3) By construction $[D]|_{U_i} = \operatorname{div}(f_i)|_{U_i}$, so $[D]$ is locally principal. Conversely, let $D'$ be a locally principal Weil divisor, and choose an open cover $(U_i)$ and $f_i \in K^*$ with $D'|_{U_i} = \operatorname{div}(f_i)|_{U_i}$. On $U_i \cap U_j$, $\operatorname{div}(f_i/f_j)$ restricts to $0$, so $f_i/f_j \in \mathcal{O}_X(U_i \cap U_j)^*$ by part 2 of Proposition P3.8.4. So $D = \{(U_i, f_i)\}$ is a Cartier divisor, with $[D] = D'$.

(4) Both sheaves are subsheaves of $\mathcal{K}$, so it suffices to compare them on each $U_i$, and on open sets $V \subseteq U_i$. Let $g \in K^*$. By definition, $g \in \mathcal{O}_X([D])(V)$ if and only if $\operatorname{div}(g) + [D] \geq 0$ on $V$. On $V$, $[D] = \operatorname{div}(f_i)$, so this says $\operatorname{div}(g f_i) \geq 0$ on $V$, that is, $g f_i \in \mathcal{O}_X(V)$ by part 1 of Proposition P3.8.4. This is the condition $g \in f_i^{-1} \mathcal{O}_X(V) = \mathcal{O}_X(D)(V)$.

(5) By (1), $D \mapsto [D]$ maps principal Cartier divisors onto principal Weil divisors. If $[D] = \operatorname{div}(g)$, then $[D - \operatorname{div}(g)] = 0$, and $D = \operatorname{div}(g)$ by injectivity. So the induced map on classes is injective, and its image is described by (3). The composite with the inverse of $\operatorname{CaCl}(X) \cong \operatorname{Pic}(X)$ sends $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ to $[D]$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.9.10.* A factorial domain is normal (Proposition P1.4.19), so $Y$ is normal. Let $X$ be normal, $D$ a Weil divisor and $x \in X$ with $\mathcal{O} = \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ factorial. Choose an affine open $U = \operatorname{Spec} A \ni x$, with $x$ the prime $\mathfrak{q}$, so that $\mathcal{O} = A_\mathfrak{q}$. By part 3 of Proposition P3.8.2, the prime divisors $P$ through $x$ correspond to the primes $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$ of $A$ of height $1$, and so to the primes $\mathfrak{p} \mathcal{O}$ of height $1$ of $\mathcal{O}$, with $\mathcal{O}_{X, P} = A_\mathfrak{p} = \mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}}$.

Let $P_1, \dots, P_r$ be the prime divisors through $x$ with nonzero coefficients $n_1, \dots, n_r$ in $D$. In the factorial ring $\mathcal{O}$ each prime of height $1$ is principal (Proposition P1.8.10), so the prime of $P_i$ is $\pi_i \mathcal{O}$ for a prime element $\pi_i$. Put $f = \prod_i \pi_i^{n_i} \in K^*$. For a prime divisor $P$ through $x$ with prime $\pi \mathcal{O}$, the valuation $\operatorname{ord}_P$ of $\mathcal{O}_{\pi \mathcal{O}}$ has $\operatorname{ord}_P(\pi) = 1$, and $\operatorname{ord}_P(\pi_i) = 0$ when $\pi_i \mathcal{O} \neq \pi \mathcal{O}$, since then $\pi_i$ is a unit in $\mathcal{O}_{\pi \mathcal{O}}$. So $\operatorname{ord}_{P_i}(f) = n_i$, and $\operatorname{ord}_P(f) = 0$ for the other prime divisors through $x$. The Weil divisor $E = D - \operatorname{div}(f)$ therefore has no component through $x$. Its support is a finite union of prime divisors, a closed set that does not contain $x$, and on its complement $V$ the divisor $D|_V$ equals $\operatorname{div}(f)|_V$. So $D$ is locally principal at $x$.

For $Y$ every Weil divisor is locally principal, and Proposition P3.9.9 gives $\operatorname{CaDiv}(Y) \cong \operatorname{Div}(Y)$ and $\operatorname{CaCl}(Y) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(Y)$. Theorem P3.9.4 gives $\operatorname{Pic}(Y) \cong \operatorname{CaCl}(Y)$. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary P3.9.11.* (1) Every local ring is a regular local ring, hence factorial by the theorem of Auslander and Buchsbaum (Theorem P1.9.25). Apply Theorem P3.9.10. A smooth variety over a perfect field is regular by part 3 of Theorem P3.7.24.

(2) Write $\pi \colon Y = X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \to X$. The scheme $Y$ is normal, integral and Noetherian (proof of Theorem P3.8.14). Let $y \in Y$ lie over $x \in X$, and let $U = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open containing $x$, with $x$ the prime $\mathfrak{q}$. Then $y$ is a prime $P$ of $A[t]$ with $P \cap A = \mathfrak{q}$, and $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y} = A[t]_P$ is a localization of $A_\mathfrak{q}[t]$. The ring $A_\mathfrak{q}[t]$ is factorial by Gauss's lemma, and a localization of a factorial domain is factorial. So $Y$ is locally factorial, and Theorem P3.9.10 and Theorem P3.8.14 give $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(Y) \cong \operatorname{Pic}(Y)$. The composite sends $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ to $\mathcal{O}_Y(\pi^{-1} D)$, which is $\pi^* \mathcal{O}_X(D)$ by Proposition P3.9.27 below.

(3) The local rings of $\mathbb{A}^n_k$ are localizations of the factorial ring $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, and $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ is covered by copies of $\mathbb{A}^n_k$. So both are locally factorial. Then $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{A}^n_k) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{A}^n_k) = 0$ by Proposition P3.8.12. For $\mathbb{P}^n_k$, $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^n_k) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is generated by the class of a hyperplane $H$ (Theorem P3.8.20), and $\mathcal{O}(H) \cong \mathcal{O}(1)$ (Example P3.9.5). So $d \mapsto \mathcal{O}(d) \cong \mathcal{O}(dH)$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \to \operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_k)$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_k \times_k \mathbb{A}^m_k) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and that $\operatorname{Pic}(U) = 0$ for every nonempty open $U \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n_k$.
2. (A) Let $F$ be a form on $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ with irreducible factors of degrees $d_1, \dots, d_s$. Show that $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_k \setminus V_+(F)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(d_1, \dots, d_s)$.
3. (B) Show that $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_k \times_k \mathbb{P}^m_k) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, generated by the pullbacks of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ from the two factors. You may use Exercise 4 of Section 8.3 and Proposition P3.9.27.
4. (B) Let $X$ be normal. Show that the locally principal Weil divisors form a subgroup of $\operatorname{Div}(X)$ that contains the principal divisors, and that $D$ is locally principal at $x$ if and only if $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ is invertible on a neighbourhood of $x$.
5. (B) Let $X$ be normal and $x \in X$. Show that every Weil divisor is locally principal at $x$ if and only if $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is factorial. (Use Exercise 7 of Section 8.2 and Proposition P1.8.10.)
6. (B) Let $X$ be locally factorial and $Z \subseteq X$ closed of codimension at least $2$. Show that restriction $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \operatorname{Pic}(X \setminus Z)$ is an isomorphism. Show with Example P3.9.14 that this fails for $X_2$ and $Z = \{0\}$.
7. (C) Let $X$ be the affine plane with a doubled origin. Show that $\operatorname{Pic}(X) = 0$. Let $Y$ be the plane with a doubled line, glued from two copies of $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ along the complement of the line $x = 0$. Show that $\operatorname{Pic}(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

### Lean

The algebraic input of Theorem P3.9.10 is in Mathlib: `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_forall_isPrincipal_of_height_eq_one` characterizes factorial domains by principal primes of height one, and an instance shows that the Picard group `CommRing.Pic R` of a domain with a gcd is trivial, with the Stacks tag 0BCH recorded in the source. `IsRegularLocalRing` is defined, and the theorem of Auslander and Buchsbaum is not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Theorem P3.9.10 is Hartshorne, Proposition II.6.11 and Corollary II.6.16, where the scheme is also assumed separated, and part 3 of Corollary P3.9.11 is Hartshorne, Corollary II.6.17. The literature state is theorem. The regularity of a scheme gives factoriality only through the theorem of Auslander and Buchsbaum, which is the deepest input of this section. In the minimal model program the smooth varieties have $\operatorname{Pic} = \operatorname{Cl}$, while the singular varieties that the program creates, such as the cones of Example P3.9.14, have Weil divisors that are not Cartier. The quotient $\operatorname{Cl}(X)/\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ measures this, and Section 9.3 studies when it is torsion.
