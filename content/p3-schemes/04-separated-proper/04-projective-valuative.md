## Projective space is proper, and the valuative criteria

### Statement

For a ring $A$ and a homogeneous polynomial $F \in A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ of degree $d$, write $V(F) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_A$ for the closed set whose intersection with each chart $U_i$ is $V(F/x_i^d)$, as in Example P3.2.16. For a set $S$ of homogeneous polynomials, $V(S) = \bigcap_{F \in S} V(F)$.

**Theorem P3.4.28 (projective space is proper).** For every ring $A$ the morphism $\mathbb{P}^n_A \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is closed. For every scheme $Y$ the morphism $\mathbb{P}^n_Y = \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}} \times_{\mathbb{Z}} Y \to Y$ is proper.

Lean: Mathlib proves that `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.toSpecZero` is proper for a graded ring finitely generated over its degree zero part, by an instance in `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/ProjectiveSpectrum/Proper.lean` (proved in Mathlib). The handbook's $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ is glued from charts, and its identification with $\operatorname{Proj} A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ is in Chapter 6; so the statement as given is not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.4.29 (projective morphism).** A morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ is *projective* if it factors as a closed immersion $X \to \mathbb{P}^n_Y$ followed by the projection $\mathbb{P}^n_Y \to Y$, for some $n$. A scheme over a field $k$ is *projective over $k$* if its structure morphism is projective.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P3.4.30 (projective implies proper).** A projective morphism is proper. If $X$ is projective over a field $k$ and $Y$ is separated over $k$, every morphism $X \to Y$ over $k$ has closed image.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P3.4.31 (valuative diagram).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a morphism. A *valuative diagram* for $f$ is a valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$ and morphisms $a \colon \operatorname{Spec} K \to X$ and $b \colon \operatorname{Spec} R \to Y$ with $f a = b \circ \iota$, where $\iota \colon \operatorname{Spec} K \to \operatorname{Spec} R$ comes from $R \subseteq K$. A *lift* is a morphism $h \colon \operatorname{Spec} R \to X$ with $f h = b$ and $h \iota = a$.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.ValuativeCommSq` (definition in Mathlib).

**Theorem P3.4.32 (valuative criteria).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a morphism.

1. If $f$ is separated, every valuative diagram for $f$ has at most one lift.
2. If $f$ is universally closed, every valuative diagram for $f$ has at least one lift.
3. Conversely, if $f$ is quasi-separated and every valuative diagram has at most one lift, then $f$ is separated; and if $f$ is quasi-compact and every valuative diagram has at least one lift, then $f$ is universally closed. So $f$ is proper if and only if it is quasi-separated and of finite type and every valuative diagram has exactly one lift.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.IsSeparated.eq_valuativeCriterion`, `AlgebraicGeometry.UniversallyClosed.eq_valuativeCriterion` and `AlgebraicGeometry.IsProper.eq_valuativeCriterion` (proved in Mathlib).

### Proof in words

A point of $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ lies over $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A$ and in $V(S)$ exactly when the polynomials of $S$, reduced to the field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, have a common zero in $\mathbb{P}^n_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}$. Over a field, having no common zero means that the ideal generated by $S$ contains all monomials of some degree $N$. In each degree this is the surjectivity of a matrix with entries in $A$, which is the nonvanishing of a minor modulo $\mathfrak{p}$, an open condition on $\mathfrak{p}$. So the set of primes with no point is open, and the image of $V(S)$ is closed. This is the main theorem of elimination theory.

The valuative criteria replace curves through a point by the spectrum of a valuation ring: $\operatorname{Spec} K$ is a punctured disc, and $\operatorname{Spec} R$ is the disc. Separatedness says that a map of the punctured disc has at most one limit, and universal closedness says that it has at least one. The first direction uses the closed equalizer of Section 4.2. The second takes the closure of the image point and uses that a valuation ring is maximal for domination. The converse directions are harder; they are proved in the Stacks project and in Mathlib, and only the first directions are proved here.

### Examples

**Example P3.4.33 (limits in projective space).** Let $R$ be a valuation ring with fraction field $K$ and valuation $v$, and let $[a_0 : \dots : a_n]$ be a $K$-point of $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}$, as in Proposition P3.2.13. Choose $i$ with $v(a_i)$ minimal and put $b_j = a_j/a_i$. Then all $b_j \in R$ and $b_i = 1$, so $(b_0, \dots, b_n) \in \mathrm{Um}(R)$ defines an $R$-point that restricts to the given $K$-point. For $R = k[t]_{(t)}$ and the $K$-point $[1 : t^{-1}]$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, the limit is $[t : 1] \mapsto [0 : 1]$ at $t = 0$. This is the valuative proof of the existence part for $\mathbb{P}^n$, and Exercise 4 asks for the uniqueness part.

**Example P3.4.34 (the affine line has no limit at infinity).** Let $R = k[t]_{(t)}$, $K = k(t)$, and let $a \colon \operatorname{Spec} K \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$ be given by $x \mapsto t^{-1}$. A lift $\operatorname{Spec} R \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$ is a $k$-algebra map $k[x] \to R$ with $x \mapsto t^{-1}$, and $t^{-1} \notin R$. So existence fails, and $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ is not universally closed over $k$, as Example P3.4.23 showed directly.

**Example P3.4.35 (the doubled origin has two limits).** For the affine line with a doubled origin and $a \colon \operatorname{Spec} k(t) \to X$ given by $x \mapsto t$, the two charts give two lifts $\operatorname{Spec} k[t]_{(t)} \to X$, with limits the two origins. So uniqueness fails, and $X$ is not separated, as in Example P3.4.4. The projective line with a doubled point of Example P3.4.26 has existence and not uniqueness.

**Example P3.4.36 (the closure of the hyperbola).** In $\mathbb{P}^1_k \times_k \mathbb{A}^1_k = \mathbb{P}^1_{k[y]}$, with homogeneous coordinates $u, v$, the closed set $V(u y - v)$ meets the chart $v \neq 0$ in the hyperbola $x y = 1$, with $x = u/v$. Over $y = 0$ it contains the point $[1 : 0]$, the point at infinity. The projection of $V(uy - v)$ to $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ is all of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$, which is closed, as Theorem P3.4.28 requires. The hyperbola $V(xy - 1) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2_k$ had image $D(y)$; the missing point is the limit at infinity.

**Example P3.4.37 (images of projective varieties).** The image of the Veronese morphism $\mathbb{P}^1_k \to \mathbb{P}^2_k$ of Example P3.3.8 is closed by Corollary P3.4.30, and it is the conic $V(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2)$. In the same way the image of any morphism from a projective variety to a separated variety is a closed subset, which is the algebraic form of the fact that a continuous image of a compact space in a Hausdorff space is closed.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem P3.4.28.* Step 1, the closed sets. Let $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_A$ be closed. Then $C = V(S)$, where $S$ is the set of homogeneous $F$ with $C \subseteq V(F)$. Indeed, let $q \notin C$, with $q \in U_i$. Since $C \cap U_i$ is closed in the affine scheme $U_i = \operatorname{Spec} A[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$, there is $g$ in this ring that vanishes on $C \cap U_i$ with $g(q) \neq 0$. Write $g = G/x_i^m$ with $G$ homogeneous of degree $m$, and let $F = x_i G$, of degree $m + 1$. On $U_j$, $F/x_j^{m+1} = (x_i/x_j) \cdot (G/x_j^m)$, and on $U_i \cap U_j$ this equals $(x_i/x_j)^{m+1} g$. A point of $C \cap U_j$ either lies in $U_i$, where $g$ vanishes, or lies in $U_j \setminus U_i = V(x_i/x_j)$, where the factor $x_i/x_j$ vanishes. So $C \subseteq V(F)$, and $q \notin V(F)$ since $F/x_i^{m+1} = g$.

Step 2, the fibre over a prime. Let $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $\kappa = \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, and write $\bar F \in \kappa[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ for the image of $F$. By Proposition P3.3.28 and Example P3.3.22, the points of $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ over $\mathfrak{p}$ form the fibre $\mathbb{P}^n_\kappa$, and on each chart a prime $\mathfrak{Q}$ of $\kappa[x_0/x_i, \dots]$ corresponds to its preimage $\mathfrak{q}$ in $A[x_0/x_i, \dots]$. Since $F/x_i^d \in \mathfrak{q}$ exactly when $\bar F/x_i^d \in \mathfrak{Q}$, the points of $V(S)$ over $\mathfrak{p}$ are the points of $V(\bar S) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_\kappa$.

Step 3, empty sets over a field. Let $\kappa$ be a field and $T$ a set of homogeneous polynomials over $\kappa$. Then $V(T) = \emptyset$ if and only if $(x_0, \dots, x_n)^N \subseteq (T)$ for some $N$. If $V(T) \cap U_i = \emptyset$, the elements $F/x_i^{\deg F}$ generate the unit ideal of $\kappa[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$, since every proper ideal lies in a prime. Clearing denominators in $1 = \sum_k h_k F_k/x_i^{d_k}$ gives $x_i^{N_i} \in (T)$. With $N = (n+1) \max_i N_i$, every monomial of degree $N$ is divisible by some $x_i^{N_i}$, so $(x_0, \dots, x_n)^N \subseteq (T)$. Conversely, if $x_i^N \in (T)$, then dividing by $x_i^N$ shows that the elements $F/x_i^{\deg F}$ generate the unit ideal on $U_i$, so $V(T) \cap U_i = \emptyset$.

Step 4, the open condition. Let $C = V(S)$. By steps 2 and 3, $\mathfrak{p}$ is not in the image of $C$ if and only if $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[x]_N \subseteq (\bar S)$ for some $N$, where $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[x]_N$ is the space of forms of degree $N$. The degree $N$ part of $(\bar S)$ is spanned by the products $\mu \bar F$ with $F \in S$ of degree at most $N$ and $\mu$ a monomial of degree $N - \deg F$. Since $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[x]_N$ has finite dimension $r = \binom{N + n}{n}$, the condition holds if and only if there are finitely many such products $\mu_1 F_1, \dots, \mu_r F_r$ whose images span $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[x]_N$. Write $\mu_l F_l$ in the monomial basis of $A[x]_N$, and let $\delta \in A$ be the determinant of the resulting $r \times r$ matrix. The images span if and only if $\delta \notin \mathfrak{p}$. So the complement of the image of $C$ is the union of the open sets $D(\delta)$, over all $N$ and all choices of $\mu_l F_l$. The image of $C$ is closed.

Step 5, properness. $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}$ is separated by Example P3.4.6 and of finite type, with $n + 1$ charts whose rings are finitely generated. Let $T \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ be a morphism. The base change $\mathbb{P}^n_T \to T$ restricts over each affine open $\operatorname{Spec} A \subseteq T$ to $\mathbb{P}^n_A \to \operatorname{Spec} A$, by part 1 of Proposition P3.3.20 and Example P3.3.22, which is closed by step 4. A map is closed if it is closed over the sets of an open cover of the target, so $\mathbb{P}^n_T \to T$ is closed. So $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}} \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ is proper, and its base change $\mathbb{P}^n_Y \to Y$ is proper by Proposition P3.4.19. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary P3.4.30.* A closed immersion is proper, and $\mathbb{P}^n_Y \to Y$ is proper by Theorem P3.4.28, so the composite is proper by Proposition P3.4.19. The second claim is Proposition P3.4.20. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.4.32.* Part 1. Let $h_1, h_2$ be two lifts. By part 1 of Proposition P3.4.10, applied over $Y$, there is a closed subscheme $E \subseteq \operatorname{Spec} R$ through which a morphism $T \to \operatorname{Spec} R$ factors exactly when its composites with $h_1$ and $h_2$ agree. By Theorem P3.3.10, $E = \operatorname{Spec} R/I$. Since $h_1 \iota = a = h_2 \iota$, the morphism $\iota$ factors through $E$, so $R \to K$ factors through $R/I$, and $I \subseteq \ker(R \to K) = 0$. So $E = \operatorname{Spec} R$ and $h_1 = h_2$.

Part 2. Let $X_R = X \times_Y \operatorname{Spec} R$ with projections $\pi \colon X_R \to \operatorname{Spec} R$ and $p \colon X_R \to X$. The pair $(a, \iota)$ defines $a' \colon \operatorname{Spec} K \to X_R$ with $\pi a' = \iota$. A lift $h' \colon \operatorname{Spec} R \to X_R$ of the diagram for $\pi$ with $\pi h' = \mathrm{id}$ and $h' \iota = a'$ gives the lift $h = p h'$. The morphism $\pi$ is universally closed by Proposition P3.4.19, so it is closed.

Let $\xi$ be the image of $a'$, and $Z$ the closure of $\{\xi\}$. Then $\pi(\xi)$ is the generic point $(0)$ of $\operatorname{Spec} R$, whose closure is $\operatorname{Spec} R$. Since $\pi(Z)$ is closed and contains $(0)$, it contains the closed point $\mathfrak{m}$: choose $z \in Z$ with $\pi(z) = \mathfrak{m}$. Let $W = \operatorname{Spec} C$ be an affine open neighbourhood of $z$. Then $\xi \in W$, since otherwise $\xi$ would lie in the closed set $X_R \setminus W$ and so would $z$. Write $\xi = P$ and $z = Q$, primes of $C$ with $P \subseteq Q$. By Theorem P3.1.22, $a'$ corresponds to a ring map $\alpha \colon C \to K$, with kernel $P$, and $\pi|_W$ to a ring map $\beta \colon R \to C$, with $\alpha \beta$ the inclusion $R \subseteq K$.

Let $D = (C/P)_Q$, a local domain with fraction field $\operatorname{Frac}(C/P)$. The injective map $C/P \to K$ induced by $\alpha$ extends to the fraction field, so $D$ maps injectively to $K$; let $D' \subseteq K$ be its image. Then $R \subseteq D'$, since $\alpha \beta$ is the inclusion. The maximal ideal of $D'$ meets $R$ in $\beta^{-1}(Q) = \mathfrak{m}$, since $\pi(z) = \mathfrak{m}$. So $D'$ is a local ring of $K$ that dominates $R$. A valuation ring is maximal for domination: if $d \in D' \setminus R$, then $d^{-1} \in \mathfrak{m} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_{D'}$, so $1 = d \cdot d^{-1} \in \mathfrak{m}_{D'}$, which is absurd. So $D' = R$.

The ring map $\gamma \colon C \to C/P \to D \cong D' = R$ defines $h' \colon \operatorname{Spec} R \to W \subseteq X_R$. Its composite with $\pi$ corresponds to $\gamma \beta \colon R \to R$, which is the inclusion $R \subseteq D' = R$, the identity. The composite $h' \iota$ corresponds to $C \to R \subseteq K$, which is $\alpha$, so $h' \iota = a'$. So $h'$ is a lift, and $h = p h'$ is a lift of the original diagram.

Part 3 is not proved here. See the Lean line and the notes. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that every closed subscheme $V(F)$ of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ defined by a homogeneous polynomial is projective, and so proper, over $k$.
2. (A) Show that the image of $V(x_0 y_1 - x_1 y_0) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^1_k \times_k \mathbb{A}^2_k$, with $y_0, y_1$ the coordinates of $\mathbb{A}^2_k$, is all of $\mathbb{A}^2_k$, and describe its fibres. This is the blow-up of the plane at the origin, studied in Chapter 11.
3. (A) Let $A = k[s]$ and $S = \{s x_0 - x_1\} \subseteq A[x_0, x_1]$. Compute the image of $V(S) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^1_A$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A$, and check it against step 4 of the proof of Theorem P3.4.28.
4. (B) Show directly, with Proposition P3.2.13, that for a valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$ the map $\mathrm{Um}(R)/R^\times \to \mathrm{Um}(K)/K^\times$ is bijective. This is the valuative criterion for $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}$.
5. (B) Let $f \colon \mathbb{A}^1_k \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$ be the inclusion. Find a valuative diagram for $f$ with no lift.
6. (B) Let $X$ be projective over $k$ and $Y$ a separated $k$-scheme of finite type. Show that the graph of a morphism $X \to Y$ is closed in $X \times_k Y$ and that the projection $X \times_k Y \to Y$ is closed.
7. (C) Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field $k$, let $c \in C$ be a closed point, and let $\varphi \colon C \setminus \{c\} \to \mathbb{P}^n_k$ be a morphism. Using that $\mathcal{O}_{C, c}$ is a discrete valuation ring with fraction field the function field of $C$, and Theorem P3.4.32, show that $\varphi$ extends to a morphism $C \to \mathbb{P}^n_k$ near $c$. (Compare P0 Ch. 5, where the same statement is proved for curves in the classical language.)

### Notes and history

The main theorem of elimination theory is classical, and the proof here, by the rank of a matrix in each degree, follows Mumford's Red Book and Vakil. Hartshorne proves the properness of projective space with the valuative criterion, Section II.4, and proves the valuative criteria for Noetherian schemes. The general case is in EGA II, Section 7, and in the Stacks project. The literature state is theorem. Definition P3.4.29 is Hartshorne's; EGA defines projective morphisms with $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ for a quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{E}$, and the two agree when $Y$ has an ample line bundle, see Chapter 6. The minimal model program works with projective morphisms; proper non-projective varieties, which exist in dimension $3$ by examples of Hironaka, appear in the program only through algebraic spaces and Moishezon varieties.
