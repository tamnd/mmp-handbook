# Affine schemes

A ring $A$ is a ring of functions on a space. P1 Ch. 2 constructed the space: the prime spectrum $\operatorname{Spec} A$, with the Zariski topology. This chapter constructs the functions. It puts on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ a sheaf of rings $\mathcal{O}$ whose ring of sections over a basic open set $D(f)$ is the localization $A_f$, and whose stalk at a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ is the local ring $A_\mathfrak{p}$. The pair $(\operatorname{Spec} A, \mathcal{O})$ is a locally ringed space in the sense of P2 Ch. 6. It is the affine scheme of $A$.

Section 1.1 constructs the structure sheaf. The one real lemma is the sheaf property on the basis of the sets $D(f)$, and its proof is a partition of unity: if the sets $D(f_i)$ cover $\operatorname{Spec} A$, then $1 = \sum a_i f_i$. The same proof gives, for every $A$-module $M$, a sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ with sections $M_f$ over $D(f)$, and Chapter 5 starts from it. Section 1.2 computes the stalks, defines affine schemes, and proves that the open set $D(f)$ of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec} A_f$. Section 1.3 proves the main theorem of the chapter: for every locally ringed space $X$, the morphisms $X \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ are the ring maps $A \to \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$. So $A \mapsto \operatorname{Spec} A$ is an anti-equivalence from rings to affine schemes. Section 1.4 gives the first examples and the first closed subschemes: $\operatorname{Spec} A/I$ inside $\operatorname{Spec} A$, affine space over any ring, the plane cubics with a node and with a cusp, and the schemes with nilpotents.

The examples of the chapter come back in every later chapter. $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ is a curve whose points are the primes and one generic point. $\operatorname{Spec} k[x]$ is the affine line, with one closed point for each monic irreducible polynomial and one generic point. $\operatorname{Spec} k[x]/(x^2)$ has one point, but its ring of functions has a nilpotent $x$, a function that is zero at every point and is not zero. This scheme is a point with a tangent direction. The minimal model program uses non-reduced schemes in several places: the scheme-theoretic fibres of a contraction can be non-reduced, a divisor $mD$ with $m \geq 2$ is a non-reduced subscheme, and the deformation theory of rational curves behind bend-and-break (P8 Ch. 2) measures maps from $\operatorname{Spec} k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$.

The chapter follows Vakil, Chapters 3 and 4, and Hartshorne, Section II.2, with details from Görtz-Wedhorn, Chapters 2 and 3. It uses P1 Ch. 2 for localization and the prime spectrum, P2 Ch. 1 for the Yoneda lemma, and P2 Ch. 5 and 6 for sheaves on a base and for locally ringed spaces.
