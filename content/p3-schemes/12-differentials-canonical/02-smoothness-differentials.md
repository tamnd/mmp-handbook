## Smoothness and differentials

### Statement

Throughout, $k$ is a field, $X$ is a scheme locally of finite type over $k$, and $\Omega_X = \Omega_{X/k}$. Smoothness is Definition P3.7.21: $X$ is smooth of dimension $d$ at $x$ if $x$ has an open neighbourhood of pure dimension $d$ on which the Jacobian matrix of a presentation has rank $n - d$ at $x$. By Lemma P3.7.22, $\operatorname{rank} J(x) = n - \dim_{\kappa(x)} \Omega_X(x)$, where $\Omega_X(x) = \Omega_{X, x} \otimes \kappa(x)$ is the fibre of the sheaf of Theorem P3.12.2 at $x$.

**Theorem P3.12.9 (smoothness and differentials).** Let $x \in X$, and let $X$ have pure dimension $d$ on an open neighbourhood of $x$. The following are equivalent.

1. $X$ is smooth of dimension $d$ over $k$ at $x$.
2. $\dim_{\kappa(x)} \Omega_X(x) = d$.
3. $\Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $d$ on an open neighbourhood of $x$.

Lean: Mathlib defines `AlgebraicGeometry.SmoothOfRelativeDimension` through standard smooth ring maps (definition). The equivalence with the rank of $\Omega$ is not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P3.12.10 (smooth schemes are reduced).** If $X$ is smooth over $k$, then $X$ is reduced.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P3.12.11 (smooth means locally free differentials).** $X$ is smooth of dimension $d$ over $k$ at every point if and only if $X$ has pure dimension $d$ and $\Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $d$. In this case $\Omega_X^p = \bigwedge^p \Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $\binom{d}{p}$ for every $p$, and $\Omega_X^d$ is invertible.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.12.12 (generic smoothness).** Let $k$ be perfect and $X$ a variety over $k$ of dimension $d$. Then the smooth locus of $X$ is a dense open subset, and $\Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $d$ on it.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.dense_smoothLocus_of_perfectField`: the smooth locus of a reduced scheme locally of finite presentation over a perfect field is dense (proved in Mathlib, for Mathlib's smoothness).

**Proposition P3.12.13 (the smooth locus of a normal variety).** Let $k$ be perfect and $X$ a normal variety over $k$ of dimension $d$, with smooth locus $U$. Then $U$ contains every point $x$ with $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \leq 1$. So $X \setminus U$ has codimension at least $2$, and $U$ contains the generic point of every prime divisor.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P3.12.14 (smooth subvarieties).** Let $k$ be perfect, $X$ smooth over $k$ of pure dimension $n$, and $i \colon Y \to X$ a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$ that is smooth over $k$ of pure dimension $r$.

1. The conormal sequence is exact on the left:
$$
0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{C}_{Y/X} \xrightarrow{\ \delta\ } i^* \Omega_X \longrightarrow \Omega_Y \longrightarrow 0 ,
$$
and $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X}$ is locally free of rank $n - r$. Locally on $Y$ the sequence splits.
2. Every closed point $y \in Y$ has an open neighbourhood $W$ in $X$ on which $\mathcal{I}$ is generated by $n - r$ sections $f_1, \dots, f_{n-r}$ whose differentials are linearly independent in $\Omega_X(y)$.
3. If $r = n - 1$, then $Y$ is an effective Cartier divisor and $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X} \cong \mathcal{O}_X(-Y)|_Y$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The fibre of $\Omega_X$ at a point is the space of differentials at that point, and the Jacobian criterion of Chapter 7 already says that smoothness is the condition that this space has the dimension of $X$. The work is to pass from one point to a neighbourhood. The smooth locus is open, and on it the fibre dimension of $\Omega_X$ is constant. A sheaf of finite type with constant fibre dimension on a reduced scheme is locally free, and a smooth scheme is reduced because it becomes regular over the algebraic closure. Over a perfect field the generic point of a variety is smooth, because the function field is separably generated, and so is the generic point of a prime divisor on a normal variety, because there the local ring is a discrete valuation ring. For a smooth subvariety of a smooth variety, the local equations can be chosen to form part of a regular system of parameters, and then the conormal sheaf is free and maps isomorphically onto the kernel.

### Examples

**Example P3.12.15 (the quadric cone).** Let $X = V(xy - z^2) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3_k$, of pure dimension $2$, $X = \operatorname{Spec} B$. By the conormal sequence,
$$
\Omega_X = (B\,dx \oplus B\,dy \oplus B\,dz)/(y\,dx + x\,dy - 2z\,dz) .
$$
At the vertex the relation vanishes in the fibre, so $\dim \Omega_X(0) = 3 \neq 2$, and $X$ is not smooth there. At any other point one of $x, y$ is a unit, and $\Omega_X$ is free of rank $2$ near it. This agrees with Example P3.7.30. The cone is normal (Example P3.7.37), and its non-smooth locus is the vertex, of codimension $2$, as Proposition P3.12.13 requires.

**Example P3.12.16 (regular but not smooth).** Let $p > 2$, $k = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ and $X = V(y^2 - x^p + t) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2_k$, as in Example P3.7.29. Since $d(x^p) = 0$ and $dt = 0$ in $\Omega_{\mathbb{A}^2/k}$,
$$
\Omega_X = (B\,dx \oplus B\,dy)/(2y\,dy) .
$$
At a point where $y$ is a unit this is $B\,dx$, free of rank one. At the closed point $P = (y, x^p - t)$ the relation vanishes in the fibre, so $\dim \Omega_X(P) = 2$, while $X$ is regular of dimension $1$ at $P$. So over an imperfect field the sheaf of differentials detects smoothness, not regularity: part 2 of Theorem P1.12.23 has no analogue at $P$.

**Example P3.12.17 (a variety that is nowhere smooth).** Let $k = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ and $X = \operatorname{Spec} L$, $L = k[x]/(x^p - t) = k(t^{1/p})$. Then $X$ is a variety of dimension $0$, and $\Omega_X = L\,dx/(p x^{p-1}\,dx) = L\,dx$ has dimension $1 \neq 0$ at its only point. So $X$ is reduced and regular and not smooth anywhere, and Theorem P3.12.12 fails without the perfectness of $k$. Over $\bar k$ the scheme $X_{\bar k} = \operatorname{Spec} \bar k[x]/((x - t^{1/p})^p)$ is not reduced.

**Example P3.12.18 (the conormal sequence of a double point).** Let $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, $X = \mathbb{A}^1_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[x]$ and $Y = V(x^2)$, which is not smooth. Here $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X} = (x^2)/(x^4)$ is free of rank one over $k[x]/(x^2)$ with generator the class of $x^2$, and $\delta$ sends it to $d(x^2) = 2x\,dx$ in $i^* \Omega_X = (k[x]/(x^2))\,dx$. The class of $x^3 = x \cdot x^2$ is nonzero in $(x^2)/(x^4)$ and maps to $2x^2\,dx = 0$. So $\delta$ is not injective, and Theorem P3.12.14 needs $Y$ smooth. The cokernel is $\Omega_Y = (k[x]/(x^2))\,dx/(2x\,dx) = k\,dx$.

**Example P3.12.19 (a smooth plane curve).** Let $Y = V(f) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2_k$ with $f$ irreducible and $(f, f_x, f_y) = (1)$, so $Y$ is a smooth curve (Theorem P3.7.24). The conormal sheaf is $(f)/(f^2)$, free with generator $f$, and $\delta(f) = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy$, which is nowhere zero on $Y$. So $0 \to \mathcal{O}_Y \to \mathcal{O}_Y\,dx \oplus \mathcal{O}_Y\,dy \to \Omega_Y \to 0$ is exact, and $\Omega_Y$ is invertible. On the open set where $f_y$ is a unit, $\Omega_Y$ is free on $dx$; where $f_x$ is a unit it is free on $dy$; and $dx/f_y = -dy/f_x$ on the overlap, so this form is a nowhere vanishing generator of $\Omega_Y$, the residue form of Example P1.12.29.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem P3.12.9.* Let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open neighbourhood of $x$ of pure dimension $d$ with a presentation $A = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]/(f_1, \dots, f_r)$. On $V$, $\Omega_X$ is the sheaf of $\Omega_{A/k}$, so $\Omega_X(x) = \Omega_{A/k}(x)$.

(1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2). By Lemma P3.7.22, $\operatorname{rank} J(x) = n - \dim \Omega_X(x)$. So $\operatorname{rank} J(x) = n - d$ if and only if $\dim \Omega_X(x) = d$.

(3) $\Rightarrow$ (2). If $\Omega_X$ is free of rank $d$ near $x$, its fibre at $x$ has dimension $d$.

(1) $\Rightarrow$ (3). The smooth locus is open by part 1 of Theorem P3.7.24. Let $W \subseteq V$ be an open neighbourhood of $x$ in the smooth locus. Each $y \in W$ is a smooth point, and $W$ has pure dimension $d$, so $y$ is smooth of dimension $d$, and $\dim \Omega_X(y) = d$ by (1) $\Rightarrow$ (2) at $y$. So the fibre dimension of $\Omega_X$ is constant, equal to $d$, on $W$. The sheaf $\Omega_X$ is of finite type by part 3 of Theorem P3.12.2, and $W$ is reduced by Lemma P3.12.10. By part 4 of Proposition P3.5.25, $\Omega_X|_W$ is locally free, of rank $d$. $\square$

*Proof of Lemma P3.12.10.* Reducedness is local, so let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open, of pure dimension $d$, on which $X$ is smooth of dimension $d$. Let $\bar k$ be an algebraic closure of $k$. By part 4 of Theorem P3.7.24, $V_{\bar k} = \operatorname{Spec}(A \otimes_k \bar k)$ is smooth over $\bar k$, and $\bar k$ is perfect, so $V_{\bar k}$ is regular by part 3 of the same theorem. Its local rings are regular local rings, hence domains (Corollary P1.9.3), so $A \otimes_k \bar k$ has no nonzero nilpotents: a nilpotent element has nilpotent image in every local ring, so it is zero in every localization at a maximal ideal, so it is zero. Choose a $k$-linear complement $W$ of $k$ in $\bar k$, so $\bar k = k \oplus W$ as $k$-vector spaces. Then $A \otimes_k \bar k = A \oplus (A \otimes_k W)$, and $a \mapsto a \otimes 1$ is injective. A ring that embeds in a reduced ring is reduced, so $A$ is reduced. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary P3.12.11.* If $X$ is smooth of dimension $d$ at every point, then every point has a neighbourhood of pure dimension $d$, so $X$ has pure dimension $d$, and $\Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $d$ by Theorem P3.12.9. Conversely, if $X$ has pure dimension $d$ and $\Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $d$, then (3) of Theorem P3.12.9 holds at every point. The exterior powers of a free module of rank $d$ are free of rank $\binom{d}{p}$, and exterior powers commute with restriction to open subsets and with localization, so $\bigwedge^p \Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $\binom{d}{p}$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.12.12.* Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $X$, $K = \kappa(\eta)$ the function field, and $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ a nonempty affine open. By Proposition P1.12.8, $\Omega_{X, \eta} = \Omega_{A/k} \otimes_A K = \Omega_{K/k}$, and $\Omega_{K/k}$ has dimension $d$ by Corollary P1.12.15, so $\dim \Omega_X(\eta) = d$. By part 3 of Proposition P3.5.25 the set $Z = \{x : \dim \Omega_X(x) \geq d + 1\}$ is closed, and $\eta \notin Z$, so $U = X \setminus Z$ is a nonempty open subset, dense as $X$ is irreducible. A variety has pure dimension $d$ (Theorem P3.7.4), so $\dim \Omega_X(x) \geq d$ for every $x$ by part 2 of Lemma P3.7.22. So $\dim \Omega_X(x) = d$ for $x \in U$, and $X$ is smooth of dimension $d$ at every point of $U$ by Theorem P3.12.9. Conversely a smooth point lies in $U$ by the same theorem. So $U$ is the smooth locus, and $\Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $d$ on it by Theorem P3.12.9. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P3.12.13.* If $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X, x} = 0$, then $x$ is the generic point, which lies in $U$ by Theorem P3.12.12. Let $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X, x} = 1$, so the closure $D$ of $x$ is a prime divisor, an integral closed subscheme of dimension $d - 1$ with function field $\kappa(x)$. By part 4 of Proposition P3.7.32, $R = \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is a regular local ring of dimension $1$, a discrete valuation ring, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ and $\dim_{\kappa(x)} \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 = 1$. The conormal sequence (Theorem P1.12.6) for $k \to R \to R/\mathfrak{m} = \kappa(x)$ is
$$
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 \longrightarrow \Omega_{R/k} \otimes_R \kappa(x) \longrightarrow \Omega_{\kappa(x)/k} \longrightarrow 0 .
$$
Here $\Omega_{R/k} = \Omega_{X, x}$ by Proposition P1.12.8, and $\Omega_{\kappa(x)/k}$ has dimension $\operatorname{trdeg}_k \kappa(x) = d - 1$ by Corollary P1.12.15 applied to an affine open of $D$. So $\dim \Omega_X(x) \leq 1 + (d - 1) = d$. By part 2 of Lemma P3.7.22, $\dim \Omega_X(x) \geq d$. So $\dim \Omega_X(x) = d$, and $x \in U$ by Theorem P3.12.9. So every point of $X \setminus U$ has a local ring of dimension at least $2$, which is the statement on the codimension. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P3.12.14.* Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the kernel of $i^* \Omega_X \to \Omega_Y$. By Corollary P3.12.11, $i^* \Omega_X$ is locally free of rank $n$ and $\Omega_Y$ is locally free of rank $r$. Locally a surjection onto a free module splits, so $\mathcal{K}$ is locally a direct summand of $i^* \Omega_X$, locally free of rank $n - r$, and the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{K} \to i^* \Omega_X \to \Omega_Y \to 0$ is locally split. By Theorem P3.12.4, $\delta$ maps $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X}$ onto $\mathcal{K}$. It remains to show that $\delta \colon \mathcal{C}_{Y/X} \to \mathcal{K}$ is injective, and part 2.

Let $y \in Y$ be a closed point. Its residue field is finite over $k$ (Proposition P3.7.3), so separable, as $k$ is perfect. By part 2 of Theorem P3.7.24, $A = \mathcal{O}_{X, y}$ is a regular local ring of dimension $n$ and $A/J = \mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$ is a regular local ring of dimension $r$, where $J = \mathcal{I}_y$. Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be the maximal ideal of $A$. The surjection $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 \to \bar{\mathfrak{m}}/\bar{\mathfrak{m}}^2$ onto the cotangent space of $A/J$ has kernel $(J + \mathfrak{m}^2)/\mathfrak{m}^2$, of dimension $n - r$. Choose $f_1, \dots, f_{n-r} \in J$ whose images form a basis of this kernel. They are part of a regular system of parameters of $A$, so by Proposition P1.9.4 the ideal $J' = (f_1, \dots, f_{n-r})$ is prime and $A/J'$ is a regular local ring of dimension $r$. Now $J' \subseteq J$, and $J/J'$ is a prime ideal of the local domain $A/J'$ of dimension $r$ with quotient $A/J$ of dimension $r$. If $J/J' \neq 0$, a chain of primes of length $r$ in $A/J$ would lift to a chain in $A/J'$ starting at $J/J'$, and putting $0$ in front would give a chain of length $r + 1$, which is impossible. So $J = J'$.

The $f_i$ are sections of $\mathcal{I}$ on some open neighbourhood $W$ of $y$, and the inclusion of the subsheaf they generate into $\mathcal{I}|_W$ is an isomorphism at $y$. Both are coherent, so after shrinking $W$ it is an isomorphism (part 1 of Proposition P3.5.25 for the cokernel). So $\mathcal{I}|_W = (f_1, \dots, f_{n-r})$. For part 2, the conormal sequence (Theorem P1.12.6) for $k \to A \to A/\mathfrak{m} = \kappa(y)$ is $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 \to \Omega_X(y) \to \Omega_{\kappa(y)/k} \to 0$, and it sends the class of $f$ to $df(y)$. Here $\Omega_{\kappa(y)/k} = 0$, as $\kappa(y)/k$ is finite and separable (Theorem P1.12.13). So the first map is surjective, and it is an isomorphism, as both spaces have dimension $n$: the first because $A$ is regular of dimension $n$, the second by Theorem P3.12.9. The classes of the $f_i$ in $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ are linearly independent, so the $df_i(y)$ are linearly independent, which is part 2.

Now the stalk $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X, y} = J/J^2$ is generated by the $n - r$ classes of the $f_i$, and $\mathcal{K}_y$ is free of rank $n - r$ over $R = \mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$. So we have surjections $R^{n-r} \to J/J^2 \to \mathcal{K}_y \cong R^{n-r}$. A surjective endomorphism of $R^{n-r}$ over a local ring is an isomorphism: modulo the maximal ideal it is a surjective linear map of a vector space of dimension $n - r$ to itself, so its determinant is a unit. So both maps are isomorphisms, and $\delta_y$ is injective. The kernel of $\delta$ is coherent, and its support is closed (part 1 of Proposition P3.5.25) and contains no closed point. A nonempty closed subset of $Y$ contains a closed point of $Y$ (Proposition P3.7.3). So the kernel is zero, and part 1 holds, with $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X} \cong \mathcal{K}$ locally free of rank $n - r$.

(3) If $r = n - 1$, part 2 says that near every closed point of $Y$ the ideal $\mathcal{I}$ is generated by one element $f$, which is a nonzerodivisor as $\mathcal{O}_{X, y}$ is a domain and $f \neq 0$. The open set of points of $X$ near which $\mathcal{I}$ is locally principal and generated by a nonzerodivisor contains all closed points of $Y$ and all points outside $Y$, so it is all of $X$ (Proposition P3.7.3). So $Y$ is an effective Cartier divisor (Proposition P3.9.2), $\mathcal{I} = \mathcal{O}_X(-Y)$, and $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X} = \mathcal{I} \otimes \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_X(-Y)|_Y$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Use Theorem P3.12.9 to find the smooth locus of the Whitney umbrella $V(x^2 - y^2 z) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3_k$ for $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, and check that it is the complement of the line $x = y = 0$. Is the umbrella normal?
2. (A) Let $Y = V(y) \subseteq X = \mathbb{A}^2_k$, the $x$-axis. Write the conormal sequence of $Y$ and check that it is exact and split, with $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X}$ free on the class of $y$.
3. (A) Let $X$ be smooth over $k$, $x$ a $k$-point and $f \in \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ with $f(x) = 0$. Show that $V(f)$ is smooth of dimension $\dim_x X - 1$ at $x$ if and only if $f \notin \mathfrak{m}_x^2$, if and only if $df(x) \neq 0$ in $\Omega_X(x)$.
4. (B) Show that the converse of Lemma P3.12.10 fails in two ways: the node is reduced and not smooth, and Example P3.12.17 is reduced and regular and not smooth.
5. (B) Let $k$ be perfect and $X$ a reduced scheme of finite type over $k$. Show that the smooth locus of $X$ is dense. Hint: apply Theorem P3.12.12 to the irreducible components with their reduced structure and remove their intersections.
6. (C) Prove the converse of Theorem P3.12.14 (Hartshorne II.8.17): let $k$ be algebraically closed, $X$ smooth of pure dimension $n$, and $Y \subseteq X$ an irreducible closed subscheme of dimension $r$ such that $\Omega_Y$ is locally free and the conormal sequence is exact on the left with $\mathcal{C}_{Y/X}$ locally free of rank $n - r$. Show that $Y$ is smooth over $k$.
7. (C) Let $k$ have characteristic $p > 0$ and $F \colon \mathbb{A}^1_k \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$ the Frobenius morphism of Example P3.12.8. Show that every fibre of $F$ over a $k$-point is a non-reduced point, so the generic smoothness of morphisms in characteristic $0$ has no analogue in characteristic $p$.

### Lean

Mathlib's smoothness is defined for morphisms through smooth ring maps, `Algebra.Smooth`, which is `Algebra.FormallySmooth` together with finite presentation, and for schemes through `AlgebraicGeometry.Smooth` and `AlgebraicGeometry.SmoothOfRelativeDimension`. The smooth locus `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.smoothLocus` is open (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.isOpen_smoothLocus`) and dense over a perfect field for reduced schemes (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.dense_smoothLocus_of_perfectField`), both proved. Mathlib's `Algebra.FormallySmooth` has the field `projective_kaehlerDifferential`: the differentials of a smooth algebra are projective. The comparison with the Jacobian definition of Chapter 7 and Theorem P3.12.14 are not in Lean. The statements of mmp-lean use smoothness through Mathlib's `AlgebraicGeometry.Smooth`, and the handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The differential criterion is Hartshorne, Theorem II.8.15 over an algebraically closed field, and Vakil, Section 21.2. Generic smoothness over a perfect field is Hartshorne, Corollary II.8.16, and Theorem P3.12.14 with its converse is Hartshorne, Theorem II.8.17. The literature state is theorem. Over an imperfect field the three notions smooth, geometrically regular and regular differ only by the last, and the sheaf of differentials sees the first two: Example P3.12.16 is regular, not smooth, and $\Omega_X$ is not locally free at the bad point. Grothendieck's smooth morphisms, which are flat with smooth fibres, are characterized by the local freeness of $\Omega_{X/S}$ of the right rank together with flatness. Generic smoothness for a morphism of varieties in characteristic $0$, Hartshorne III.10.7, is the input of the Bertini theorems used in the minimal model program to choose general members of linear systems, and Exercise 7 is the reason why those choices need care in characteristic $p$.
