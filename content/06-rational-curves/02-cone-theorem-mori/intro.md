# The cone theorem by Mori's method

This chapter proves the cone theorem for a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field of any characteristic, with Mori's bound $n + 1$ on the anticanonical degree of the curves that span the extremal rays. The only input from outside the chapter is the list of tools in Theorem 6.1.2: the degree bound for rational curves, which rests on bend-and-break and in positive characteristic on the Frobenius, and the theorem that a rational curve of anticanonical degree at least $n + 2$ breaks. No vanishing theorem is used, which is why the proof works in every characteristic.

Section 1 proves the three ingredients. The degree bound passes from integral ample divisors to ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors. Every rational curve is a positive integral combination of rational curves that are either $K_X$-nonnegative or of anticanonical degree at most $n + 1$. Only finitely many classes of such curves are negative against $K_X + \varepsilon H$, and finite sums of closed subcones of the cone of curves are closed.

Section 2 proves the cone theorem, Theorem 6.2.9: the $K_X$-negative extremal rays are spanned by rational curves of anticanonical degree at most $n + 1$, which recovers Theorem P5.12.13; they are countable and discrete away from $K_X^\perp$; and with the $K_X$-nonnegative part they span the cone, with no closure. It deduces the length bound, the existence of a supporting nef divisor for each $K_X$-negative ray, and the polyhedrality of the cone of a Fano manifold.

Section 3 computes three cones with rational curves: $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^2$, with two rays of lengths $2$ and $3$; the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^n$ at a point, where the length $n - 1$ of the exceptional ray is the discrepancy of the exceptional divisor; and the blow-up at two points, where the strict transform of the line through the points spans an extremal ray on which $K_X$ is negative, zero or positive as $n = 2$, $n = 3$ or $n \ge 4$.
