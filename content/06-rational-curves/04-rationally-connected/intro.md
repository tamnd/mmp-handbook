# Rationally connected varieties

Chapters 2 and 3 produced rational curves in extremal rays, and the last of them through general points of a variety with a fibre type ray. This chapter asks how many rational curves there are: when two general points of $X$ are joined by one rational curve, or by a chain of them, and what is left of $X$ when the rational curves are collapsed. The notions of rational connectedness and rational chain connectedness, and the theorem of Kollár, Miyaoka and Mori and of Campana that a rationally chain connected smooth variety in characteristic $0$ is rationally connected, are in Section P8.3; this chapter uses them and proves what follows from the cone theorem.

Section 1 defines chains of curves of finitely many families and states the quotient by the equivalence relation they generate, cited. With it, a short divisor argument shows that dominating families whose classes span $N_1(X)$ join any two general points by a chain, and so a uniruled variety of Picard number one, for instance a Fano manifold of Picard number one, is rationally chain connected, and rationally connected in characteristic $0$.

Section 2 states the theorem of Kollár, Miyaoka and Mori and of Campana that a Fano manifold is rationally chain connected by chains of curves of anticanonical degree at most $n + 1$, cited, proves that images and products of rationally connected varieties are rationally connected, and proves that a variety of Picard number two with two fibre type rays is rationally connected. The examples are $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^2$, the flag threefold and a conic bundle threefold.

Section 3 states the maximal rationally connected fibration, cited, and proves from the theorem of Graber, Harris and Starr that its base is not uniruled. It follows that $X$ is uniruled exactly when the fibration has positive dimensional fibres. The section ends with Mori fibre spaces, the second possible outcome of the minimal model program: their general fibres are Fano and rationally connected, and by the theorem of Hacon and McKernan, cited, all their fibres are rationally chain connected.
