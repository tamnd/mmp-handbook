# Rational curves and bend-and-break

Mori's theorem of 1979 and 1982 found the curves that the minimal model program contracts. If the canonical class of a smooth projective variety $X$ of dimension $n$ is not nef, then $X$ contains a rational curve $\Gamma$ with $0 < -K_X \cdot \Gamma \le n + 1$, and the part of the closed cone of curves on which $K_X$ is negative is spanned by countably many such curves, locally finitely away from the hyperplane $K_X^\perp$. The proofs produce rational curves by deforming a curve with points held fixed until it breaks, and this volume is the volume of that method. Volume P8, Chapters 1 to 3, builds the tools: the Hom scheme and its dimension estimate, bend-and-break in its two forms, Mori's theorem on the existence of a rational curve, and the first facts on uniruled and rationally connected varieties. This volume cites those results by label and does not prove them again. It proves what the program takes from them: the cone theorem for smooth varieties, the length of extremal rays, the geometry of unsplit families, and the rational connectedness statements that describe the fibres of a Mori fibre space.

> **The discrepancy box.** For a projective birational $f \colon Y \to X$ and a pair $(X, \Delta)$, write $K_Y + \Delta_Y = f^*(K_X + \Delta)$ with $f_*\Delta_Y = \Delta$. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the *discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) = -\operatorname{mult}_E \Delta_Y$, and the *log discrepancy* is $a(E, X, \Delta) + 1$. The pair is terminal or canonical if $a > 0$ or $a \ge 0$ for every exceptional $E$ over $X$. It is klt or lc if $a > -1$ or $a \ge -1$ for every prime divisor $E$ over $X$, exceptional or not. For a divisor $E$ on $X$ itself, $a(E, X, \Delta)$ is minus the coefficient of $E$ in $\Delta$, so klt asks for coefficients less than 1 and lc for coefficients at most 1. The Lean code states the same conditions with the log discrepancy, over all divisorial valuations.

The box is the one of Volume 0, Chapter 3, and of Volumes 4 and 5, and in this volume it is mostly silent, which is itself the point to note. Every variety here is smooth and every boundary is zero, so every pair is terminal, every discrepancy of an exceptional divisor is a positive integer, and the canonical class $K_X$ is Cartier. Mori's method needs exactly this. The dimension estimate of Corollary P8.1.12 is a Riemann-Roch computation for the bundle $f^* T_X$, and it needs $X$ smooth along the curve; the bound $n + 1$ is the rank of $T_X$ plus one. On a klt pair $(X, \Delta)$ there is no tangent bundle to deform along, and the box is how the singular statements of Volume 7 measure what is lost: the length bound becomes $2n$ for $-(K_X + \Delta)$, by Kawamata's argument (Kaw91), which applies bend-and-break on a resolution of the exceptional locus of the contraction and compares $K_Y + \Delta_Y$ with $f^*(K_X + \Delta)$ through the discrepancies of the box. Blow-ups of points of $\mathbb{P}^n$ appear in every chapter, and the box gives their canonical class: the exceptional divisor of the blow-up of a point has discrepancy $n - 1$, so $K_{\tilde X} = \pi^* K_X + (n - 1) E$ (Example 1.5.15).

Volume 7 proves the cone theorem for klt pairs by the other method. It starts from the nonvanishing theorem and the base point free theorem, both proved with the vanishing theorems of Volume 5, then proves the rationality theorem, which says that the nef threshold of an ample divisor against $K_X + \Delta$ is rational with bounded denominator, and deduces the cone and contraction theorems from it. That method works for singular pairs in characteristic zero, and it constructs the contraction of an extremal ray, which Mori's method does not. It does not see rational curves: its extremal rays are spanned by classes of curves, and the length bound needs the separate argument of Kawamata, which again uses bend-and-break on a resolution. Mori's method is the only one of the two that works in characteristic $p$, where the vanishing theorems fail (Volume 5, Chapter 6), and it is the source of every statement in which a rational curve is produced: the length of extremal rays, the uniruledness of the fibres of a contraction, and the rational connectedness of Fano manifolds. The program needs the contraction, which only the second method gives, and the rational curves, which only the first gives; Volume 7 has a section that compares the two proofs.

Chapter 1 recalls the results of Volume P8 that the volume uses, with their labels, and adds the three facts on families that the later chapters need: every component of the Hom scheme has the expected dimension, a rational curve of anticanonical degree at least $n + 2$ breaks, and a curve of minimal degree in an extremal ray does not. It then works the two required examples. On $\mathbb{P}^n$ every rational curve is very free, the rational normal curve has restricted tangent bundle $\mathcal{O}(n + 1)^{\oplus n}$, and the lines through a point form a $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, the variety of minimal rational tangents. On a smooth cubic surface the 27 lines are rigid, unsplit and span the extremal rays, while the minimal rational curves through a general point are the conics of the 27 pencils residual to the lines.

Chapter 2 proves the cone theorem for a smooth projective variety in every characteristic by Mori's method: rational curves of anticanonical degree at most $n + 1$ span the $K_X$-negative part of the cone, every $K_X$-negative extremal ray is spanned by one of them, which is Theorem P5.12.13, and only finitely many of them are negative for $K_X + \varepsilon H$. It works the cones of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^2$ and of the blow-ups of $\mathbb{P}^n$ at one and two points, where an extremal ray on which $K_X$ is positive appears for $n \ge 4$. Chapter 3 studies extremal rays through their minimal rational curves: it proves the Ionescu-Wiśniewski inequality for an unsplit family, states Mori's classification of extremal contractions of smooth threefolds with the lengths of the rays, bounds the sum of the lengths of two fibre type rays, and treats Fano manifolds of Picard number 2. Chapter 4 turns to rational connectedness: chains of rational curves, the rational connectedness of Fano manifolds of Picard number one proved from the quotient by chains, the theorem of Kollár, Miyaoka and Mori in general, the maximal rationally connected fibration and the theorem of Graber, Harris and Starr that its base is not uniruled, and the fibres of Mori fibre spaces.

The spine is Debarre's book (Deb01), Kollár's book on rational curves (Kol96) and Kollár-Mori, Chapter 1 (KM98). Mori's papers (Mor79, Mor82) are the sources of Chapters 1 to 3, the papers of Kollár, Miyaoka and Mori and of Campana (KMM92a, KMM92b, Cam92) of Chapter 4, and the paper of Graber, Harris and Starr (GHS03) of its last section.
