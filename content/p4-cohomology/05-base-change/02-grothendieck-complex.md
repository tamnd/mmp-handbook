## The Grothendieck complex

### Statement

For a morphism $f \colon X \to \operatorname{Spec} A$, a quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ and an $A$-module $M$, write $\mathcal{F} \otimes_A M = \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} f^* \widetilde{M}$. On an affine open set $U \subseteq X$ its sections are $\mathcal{F}(U) \otimes_A M$, by part 2 of Proposition P3.5.32. For an $A$-algebra $B$ the pushforward of $\mathcal{F}_B$ along the affine morphism $X_B \to X$ is $\mathcal{F} \otimes_A B$. The *mapping cone* of a map of complexes is that of Definition P2.8.2.

**Lemma P4.5.6 (exact complexes of flat modules).** Let $E^0 \to E^1 \to \cdots \to E^n \to 0$ be a complex of flat $A$-modules that is exact in every degree $p \geq 1$, and let $Z^0 = \ker(E^0 \to E^1)$. Then $Z^0$ is flat, and for every $A$-module $M$ the complex
$$0 \to Z^0 \otimes_A M \to E^0 \otimes_A M \to E^1 \otimes_A M \to \cdots \to E^n \otimes_A M \to 0$$
is exact.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P4.5.7 (finite free approximation).** Let $A$ be Noetherian and $C^\bullet$ a complex of $A$-modules with $C^p = 0$ for $p < 0$ and $p > n$, whose cohomology modules $H^p(C^\bullet)$ are finitely generated. There are finitely generated free modules $K^1, \dots, K^n$, forming a complex $K^\bullet$ with $K^p = 0$ for $p \leq 0$ and $p > n$, and a map of complexes $\varphi \colon K^\bullet \to C^\bullet$, such that $H^p(\varphi)$ is an isomorphism for $p \geq 2$ and $Z^1(K^\bullet) = \ker(K^1 \to K^2) \to H^1(C^\bullet)$ is surjective.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P4.5.8 (the Grothendieck complex).** Let $A$ be Noetherian, $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A$, $f \colon X \to Y$ projective with $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_Y$, and $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent sheaf on $X$ that is flat over $Y$. There is a complex
$$L^\bullet \colon 0 \to L^0 \to L^1 \to \cdots \to L^n \to 0$$
of finitely generated flat $A$-modules, with $L^p$ free for $p \geq 1$, and isomorphisms
$$H^q(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_A M) \cong H^q(L^\bullet \otimes_A M)$$
for all $q$ and all $A$-modules $M$, natural in $M$. In particular $H^q(X_B, \mathcal{F}_B) \cong H^q(L^\bullet \otimes_A B)$ for every $A$-algebra $B$, naturally in $B$, and $H^q(X_y, \mathcal{F}_y) \cong H^q(L^\bullet \otimes_A \kappa(y))$ for every $y \in Y$. Every $L^p$ is locally free, and if $A$ is local, every $L^p$ is free.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

A complex with these properties is a *Grothendieck complex* for $\mathcal{F}$. It is not unique, but it turns every question about the cohomology of the fibres $\mathcal{F}_y$ into linear algebra over $A$: a bounded complex of matrices with entries in $A$, specialized at the points of $Y$. Over a local ring it can be simplified further.

**Lemma P4.5.9 (minimal complexes).** Let $(A, \mathfrak{m}, \kappa)$ be a local ring and $L^\bullet$ a bounded complex of finitely generated free $A$-modules. Then $L^\bullet = L'^\bullet \oplus T^\bullet$ as complexes, where $L'^\bullet$ is a complex of finitely generated free modules whose differentials have all entries in $\mathfrak{m}$, that is $d \otimes \kappa = 0$, and $T^\bullet$ is a finite direct sum of complexes $A \xrightarrow{1} A$ in two adjacent degrees. In particular $H^q(L^\bullet \otimes M) \cong H^q(L'^\bullet \otimes M)$ for every $A$-module $M$, naturally in $M$, and $\operatorname{rank} L'^q = \dim_\kappa H^q(L^\bullet \otimes \kappa)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

A complex of free modules with $d \otimes \kappa = 0$ is *minimal*. Over a local base, Theorem P4.5.8 and Lemma P4.5.9 give a minimal Grothendieck complex with $\operatorname{rank} L^q = h^q(y)$ at the closed point $y$.

### Proof in words

The Čech complex of $\mathcal{F}$ for the standard affine cover of $X$ already computes the cohomology of every $\mathcal{F} \otimes_A M$, and its terms are flat because $\mathcal{F}$ is flat over $A$, but they are enormous: modules like $\mathcal{F}(U_0)$ that are not finitely generated. The cohomology, however, is finitely generated by Serre's theorem. So one builds, from the top degree down, a complex of finitely generated free modules mapping to the Čech complex that has the same cohomology in degrees $\geq 1$. The mapping cone of this map is then exact in positive degrees, and its degree $0$ cocycles form a finitely generated flat module $L^0$ that repairs degree $0$. Because the cone is an exact complex of flat modules, it stays exact after tensoring with any $M$, and that says exactly that the small complex computes the same cohomology as the Čech complex after any base change. Over a local ring, any unit entry in a differential splits off a trivial complex $A \to A$, and repeating this leaves a minimal complex.

### Examples

**Example P4.5.10 (projective space).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n_A$ and $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}(m)$. By Theorem P4.2.1, applied over every $A$-algebra $B$, $H^0(X_B, \mathcal{O}(m)) = B[x_0, \dots, x_n]_m$, $H^n(X_B, \mathcal{O}(m))$ is free on the monomials with negative exponents of degree $m$, and the other groups vanish. Both are $H^q(X, \mathcal{O}(m)) \otimes_A B$. So $L^0 = H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(m))$, $L^n = H^n(X, \mathcal{O}(m))$ and $L^p = 0$ otherwise, with zero differentials, is a Grothendieck complex. Everything commutes with base change, and the fibre dimensions are constant.

**Example P4.5.11 (three points that become collinear).** In Example P4.5.3, with $A = k[t]$, $Z$ the three sections $p_1 = [1 : 0 : 0]$, $p_2 = [0 : 1 : 0]$, $p_3 = [1 : 1 : t]$ and $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{I}_Z(1)$, trivialize $\mathcal{O}_Z(1)$ on the three components by the coordinates $x$, $y$ and $x$. The sequence $0 \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{O}(1) \to \mathcal{O}_Z(1) \to 0$ has flat third term, so it stays exact after $\otimes_A M$ by Lemma P1.1.48, and since $Z$ is affine over $A$ and $H^q(\mathbb{P}^2_A, \mathcal{O}(1) \otimes_A M) = 0$ for $q > 0$, the long exact sequence gives
$$H^0(\mathcal{F} \otimes M) = \ker \Phi_M, \qquad H^1(\mathcal{F} \otimes M) = \operatorname{coker} \Phi_M, \qquad \Phi = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & t \end{pmatrix} \colon A^3 \to A^3,$$
the evaluation of $a x + b y + c z$ at the three points. So $L^\bullet = [A^3 \xrightarrow{\Phi} A^3]$ is a Grothendieck complex. Splitting off the two unit pivots as in Lemma P4.5.9 leaves the minimal complex $A \xrightarrow{t} A$ over $k[t]_{(t)}$. Hence $f_* \mathcal{F} = \ker(t) = 0$, $R^1 f_* \mathcal{F} = (A/tA)^\sim$, the skyscraper $k$ at the origin, and for a point $c \in \mathbb{A}^1$ the fibre complex is $k \xrightarrow{c} k$, with $h^0 = h^1 = 1$ for $c = 0$ and $h^0 = h^1 = 0$ otherwise.

**Example P4.5.12 (flatness is needed).** Let $A = k[t]$, $X = \mathbb{P}^1_A$, and $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}_{X_0}$ the structure sheaf of the fibre over $t = 0$, which is coherent but not flat over $A$. Then $\mathcal{F} \otimes_A M = \mathcal{O}_{X_0} \otimes_k M/tM$ and $H^0(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_A M) = M/tM$. No complex $L^\bullet$ of flat modules starting in degree $0$ has $H^0(L^\bullet \otimes M) \cong M/tM$ naturally in $M$: the functor $M \mapsto \ker(L^0 \otimes M \to L^1 \otimes M)$ is left exact when $L^0$ and $L^1$ are flat, while $M \mapsto M/tM$ takes the injection $t \colon A \to A$ to the zero map $A/tA \to A/tA$. The fibre dimensions $h^0(\mathcal{F}_y)$ are $1$ at $t = 0$ and $0$ elsewhere, upper semicontinuous here, but the Euler characteristic $\chi(\mathcal{F}_y)$ also jumps, from $0$ to $1$, which Theorem P4.5.13 below excludes for flat sheaves.

### Proof

*Lemma P4.5.6.* For $p \geq 0$ let $Z^p = \ker(E^p \to E^{p+1})$, with $E^{n+1} = 0$. Exactness in degree $p + 1$ gives short exact sequences $0 \to Z^p \to E^p \to Z^{p+1} \to 0$ for $0 \leq p \leq n$, with $Z^{n+1} = 0$. By descending induction on $p$, every $Z^p$ is flat. This holds for $Z^{n+1} = 0$. If $Z^{p+1}$ is flat and $N' \to N$ is injective, Lemma P1.1.48 makes $0 \to Z^p \otimes N' \to E^p \otimes N'$ exact, and $E^p \otimes N' \to E^p \otimes N$ is injective since $E^p$ is flat. So the composite $Z^p \otimes N' \to Z^p \otimes N \to E^p \otimes N$ is injective, and so is $Z^p \otimes N' \to Z^p \otimes N$. By Lemma P1.1.48 again, each sequence $0 \to Z^p \otimes M \to E^p \otimes M \to Z^{p+1} \otimes M \to 0$ is exact. The map $E^p \otimes M \to E^{p+1} \otimes M$ factors as the surjection $E^p \otimes M \to Z^{p+1} \otimes M$ followed by the injection $Z^{p+1} \otimes M \to E^{p+1} \otimes M$, so its kernel is $Z^p \otimes M$ and its image is $Z^{p+1} \otimes M$, the kernel of the next map. This is the exactness claimed. $\square$

*Lemma P4.5.7.* Descending induction on $p$ from $n$ to $1$. Suppose $K^{p+1} \to \cdots \to K^n$ and $\varphi$ in degrees $> p$ are constructed, a map of complexes in those degrees, with $H^q(\varphi)$ an isomorphism for $q \geq p + 2$ and $Z^{p+1}(K) \to H^{p+1}(C)$ surjective. (For $p = n$ there is nothing yet, and the conditions are empty.) Let
$$N = \ker\bigl(Z^{p+1}(K) \to H^{p+1}(C)\bigr),$$
a submodule of the finitely generated module $K^{p+1}$, hence finitely generated since $A$ is Noetherian. Choose generators $z_1, \dots, z_a$ of $N$, and for each $z_i$ an element $c_i \in C^p$ with $d c_i = \varphi(z_i)$. Choose cocycles $c'_1, \dots, c'_b \in Z^p(C)$ whose classes generate $H^p(C)$. Let $K^p$ be free on $e_1, \dots, e_a, e'_1, \dots, e'_b$, with
$$d e_i = z_i, \quad d e'_j = 0, \quad \varphi(e_i) = c_i, \quad \varphi(e'_j) = c'_j.$$
Then $d d e_i = d z_i = 0$, and $d \varphi(e_i) = d c_i = \varphi(z_i) = \varphi(d e_i)$, $d \varphi(e'_j) = 0 = \varphi(d e'_j)$, so $\varphi$ is a map of complexes in degrees $\geq p$. The map $H^{p+1}(K) \to H^{p+1}(C)$ is surjective by hypothesis, and injective because every $z \in N$ is a combination of the $z_i = d e_i$. The map $Z^p(K) \to H^p(C)$ is surjective because the $e'_j$ are cocycles mapping to generators. This completes the induction step, and after the step $p = 1$ the lemma holds. $\square$

*Theorem P4.5.8.* Let $U_i = X \cap D_+(x_i)$ for $0 \leq i \leq n$. All intersections $U_I$ are affine. Let $C^\bullet$ be the alternating Čech complex, $C^p = \prod_{|I| = p + 1} \mathcal{F}(U_I)$, which vanishes for $p > n$. Each $\mathcal{F}(U_I)$ is flat over $A$ by part 2 of Proposition P3.13.2, so each $C^p$ is flat. For an $A$-module $M$, the Čech complex of $\mathcal{F} \otimes_A M$ for the same cover is $C^\bullet \otimes_A M$, so by Corollary P4.1.9
$$H^q(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_A M) = H^q(C^\bullet \otimes_A M), \tag{1}$$
naturally in $M$. For an $A$-algebra $B$, the cover $U_i \times_A B$ of $X_B$ has Čech complex $C^\bullet \otimes_A B$ as well, which gives the statements for $B$ and for $B = \kappa(y)$. By Theorem P4.2.7, $H^p(C^\bullet) = H^p(X, \mathcal{F})$ is finitely generated.

Let $\varphi \colon K^\bullet \to C^\bullet$ be as in Lemma P4.5.7, and let $E^\bullet$ be the mapping cone, $E^p = K^{p+1} \oplus C^p$ with $d(k, c) = (-d k, \varphi(k) + d c)$. Its terms are flat, and $E^p = 0$ for $p < 0$ and $p > n$, since $K^0 = 0$. By Proposition P2.8.4 (or Exercise 4 of Section P2.3.1) there is an exact sequence $H^p(K) \to H^p(C) \to H^p(E) \to H^{p+1}(K) \to H^{p+1}(C)$. For $p \geq 1$ the first map is surjective and the last injective, so $H^p(E) = 0$. Let
$$L^0 = Z^0(E) = \{(k, c) \in K^1 \oplus C^0 : d k = 0, \ \varphi(k) + d c = 0\},$$
$L^p = K^p$ for $p \geq 1$, and $d \colon L^0 \to L^1$, $(k, c) \mapsto -k$. This is a complex, since $d(-k) = 0$. By Lemma P4.5.6, $L^0$ is flat. It is finitely generated: the projection $L^0 \to K^1$ has image in the finitely generated module $K^1$, and kernel $\{(0, c) : d c = 0\} = Z^0(C) = H^0(C)$, which is finitely generated.

Define $\psi \colon L^\bullet \to C^\bullet$ by $\psi^0(k, c) = c$ and $\psi^p = \varphi^p$ for $p \geq 1$. It is a map of complexes: $d \psi^0(k, c) = d c = -\varphi(k) = \psi^1(d(k, c))$. Its mapping cone has $L^0$ in degree $-1$ and $L^{p+1} \oplus C^p = E^p$ in degrees $p \geq 0$. The differential out of degree $-1$ is $(k, c) \mapsto (-d(k, c), \psi^0(k, c)) = (k, c)$, the inclusion $Z^0(E) \subseteq E^0$, and the other differentials are those of $E^\bullet$. So the cone of $\psi$ is the complex $0 \to Z^0(E) \to E^0 \to \cdots \to E^n \to 0$ of Lemma P4.5.6. The cone of $\psi \otimes M$ is this complex tensored with $M$, which is exact by Lemma P4.5.6. By Exercise 4 of Section P2.3.1, $\psi \otimes M$ is a quasi-isomorphism, so $H^q(L^\bullet \otimes M) \cong H^q(C^\bullet \otimes M)$, naturally in $M$. With (1) this is the theorem.

Finally $L^0$ is finitely generated over a Noetherian ring, so finitely presented, and its localizations are free by Theorem P1.1.50. If $L^0_\mathfrak{p} \cong A_\mathfrak{p}^m$, choose $A^m \to L^0$ inducing this isomorphism. Its kernel and cokernel are finitely generated and vanish at $\mathfrak{p}$, so they vanish on some $D(s)$ with $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$, and $L^0_s$ is free. So $L^0$ is locally free, and free when $A$ is local. $\square$

*Lemma P4.5.9.* Induction on $\sum_p \operatorname{rank} L^p$. If every differential has entries in $\mathfrak{m}$, take $L' = L$ and $T = 0$. Otherwise there are $p$ and a basis element $e$ of $L^p$ with $d e \notin \mathfrak{m} L^{p+1}$. The image of $d e$ in $L^{p+1} \otimes \kappa$ is nonzero, so it extends to a basis of $L^{p+1} \otimes \kappa$. Lifting, $d e$ and elements $f_2, \dots, f_s$ generate $L^{p+1}$ by Nakayama's lemma (Theorem P1.1.25), and $s = \operatorname{rank} L^{p+1}$, so they form a basis, since a surjection $A^s \to A^s$ is an isomorphism. Let $\pi \colon L^{p+1} \to A$ be the coordinate along $d e$, and set
$$T^p = A e, \quad T^{p+1} = A\, d e, \quad L'^p = \ker(\pi d), \quad L'^{p+1} = \ker \pi, \quad L'^q = L^q \text{ for } q \neq p, p+1.$$
Since $\pi d e = 1$, $L^p = A e \oplus \ker(\pi d)$ and $L^{p+1} = A\, de \oplus \ker \pi$. Both kernels are direct summands of free modules, hence finitely generated and flat, hence free by Theorem P1.1.50. The differential maps $T^p$ onto $T^{p+1}$ and kills $T^{p+1}$, maps $L'^p$ into $\ker \pi = L'^{p+1}$ by definition, and maps $L^{p-1}$ into $L'^p$, since $\pi d d = 0$. So $L^\bullet = L'^\bullet \oplus T^\bullet$ as complexes, with $T^\bullet \cong [A \xrightarrow{1} A]$, and induction applies to $L'^\bullet$. Finally $[A \xrightarrow{1} A] \otimes M = [M \xrightarrow{1} M]$ is exact, so $H^q(L \otimes M) = H^q(L' \otimes M)$, and $L' \otimes \kappa$ has zero differentials, so $H^q(L' \otimes \kappa) = L'^q \otimes \kappa$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $L^\bullet = [A \xrightarrow{t} A]$ over $A = k[t]$. Compute $H^0(L \otimes M)$ and $H^1(L \otimes M)$ for $M = A$, $A/t$, $A/(t - 1)$ and $k(t)$, and compare with Example P4.5.11.
2. (A) Show that the Euler characteristic $\sum_q (-1)^q \dim_\kappa H^q(L^\bullet \otimes \kappa)$ of a bounded complex of finitely generated free modules over a local ring equals $\sum_q (-1)^q \operatorname{rank} L^q$.
3. (A) Let $f \colon X \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $\mathcal{F}$ be as in Theorem P4.5.8. Show that $H^q(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_A M) = 0$ for all $q > n$ and all $M$, and that $H^n(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_A M) \cong H^n(X, \mathcal{F}) \otimes_A M$ for every $M$.
4. (B) Let $A$ be local and let $L^\bullet$, $L'^\bullet$ be two minimal complexes of finitely generated free modules with $H^q(L \otimes M) \cong H^q(L' \otimes M)$ naturally in $M$. Show that $\operatorname{rank} L^q = \operatorname{rank} L'^q$ for all $q$.
5. (B) Show that the functors $T^q(M) = H^q(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_A M)$ of Theorem P4.5.8 are *half exact*: a short exact sequence $0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$ gives a long exact sequence $\cdots \to T^q(M') \to T^q(M) \to T^q(M'') \to T^{q+1}(M') \to \cdots$. Show also that $T^q$ commutes with direct limits.
6. (C) Let $A$ be a Noetherian local ring and $L^\bullet$ a bounded complex of finitely generated free modules with $L^p = 0$ for $p > q$. Show that $H^q(L \otimes M) = H^q(L) \otimes M$ for every $M$, and that if $L$ is minimal, $H^q(L)$ is free if and only if $d^{q-1} = 0$. Deduce from Lemma P4.5.9 that a bounded complex of finitely generated free modules over a local ring is exact if and only if it is a direct sum of complexes $A \xrightarrow{1} A$.

### Lean

Bounded complexes of modules, the mapping cone and quasi-isomorphisms are in Mathlib (`CochainComplex.mappingCone`), as is the freeness of finitely generated flat modules over a local ring. The Grothendieck complex, which needs the Čech description of the cohomology of quasi-coherent sheaves, is not in Lean as stated. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Theorem P4.5.8 is Grothendieck's, EGA III.6.10.5 and III.7.7, in the form of Mumford, *Abelian varieties*, Section 5, and Hartshorne III.12.2. Mumford's presentation, by a finite complex of finitely generated flat modules, made the theorems on cohomology and base change into exercises on matrices. The literature state is theorem. In derived language the theorem says that $Rf_* \mathcal{F}$ is a perfect complex on $Y$ whose formation commutes with arbitrary base change. That perfectness is what the minimal model program uses when it applies Grothendieck duality to a family, in Chapter 9.
