# Grothendieck duality, dualizing complexes and Kempf's criterion

Chapters 7 and 8 proved two forms of duality. Serre duality on a projective scheme pairs $H^i(X, \mathcal{F})$ with $\operatorname{Ext}^{n-i}(\mathcal{F}, \omega^\circ_X)$, and it is perfect in every degree exactly when $X$ is Cohen-Macaulay. Local duality pairs $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M)$ with $\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R)$ over a quotient of a regular local ring. In both, a singular $X$ has several Ext sheaves or Ext modules where a Cohen-Macaulay one has a single one. This chapter collects them into one object, the dualizing complex, for which duality holds in every degree without hypotheses, and then uses it.

Section 9.1 defines dualizing complexes over a Noetherian ring and on a scheme, proves that they are unique up to shift and twist and that a complex $\omega^\bullet_X$ exists on every projective scheme, and reads off depth, the Cohen-Macaulay property and the canonical module from its cohomology sheaves. Section 9.2 proves duality for a morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ of projective schemes over a field: $Rf_*$ commutes with duality, $f^! = \mathbb{D}_X L f^* \mathbb{D}_Y$ is its right adjoint on coherent complexes, and $f^! \omega^\bullet_Y = \omega^\bullet_X$. The general form of Grothendieck duality, for proper morphisms of arbitrary Noetherian schemes, is a black box of the handbook, stated in Theorem P2.8.22; the projective case proved here is the one the minimal model program needs. Section 9.3 defines rational singularities and proves Kempf's criterion: a variety with a resolution $f \colon Y \to X$ has rational singularities if and only if it is normal, Cohen-Macaulay and $f_* \omega_Y = \omega_X$.

In the minimal model program, rational singularities are the class that cohomology sees. Klt singularities are rational (Volume 4), and so canonical, terminal and klt varieties are Cohen-Macaulay, the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem transfers along their resolutions, and cones over Fano varieties give the first examples. Kempf's criterion is also how Gorenstein rational singularities are recognized as canonical singularities. The proofs of 9.3 use two results from outside this volume: Hironaka's resolution of singularities, a black box stated in P8 Chapter 5, and the Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing theorem, proved in P7 and stated in Theorem P2.8.26.

The sources are Hartshorne, *Residues and duality* (1966), Chapters III and V, Hartshorne III.7, the Stacks project chapter on dualizing complexes, Kempf's chapter in *Toroidal embeddings I* (1973), and Kollár and Mori, Section 5.1.
