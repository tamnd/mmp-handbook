## The dualizing sheaf and Cohen-Macaulay duality

### Statement

Let $k$ be a field and $X$ a projective scheme over $k$ of dimension $n$. For a $k$-vector space $V$ write $V' = \operatorname{Hom}_k(V, k)$.

**Definition P4.7.14 (dualizing sheaf).** A *dualizing sheaf* for $X$ is a coherent sheaf $\omega^\circ_X$ with a linear map $t \colon H^n(X, \omega^\circ_X) \to k$, the *trace*, such that for every coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ the map
$$\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \omega^\circ_X) \to H^n(X, \mathcal{F})', \qquad \varphi \mapsto t \circ H^n(\varphi),$$
is an isomorphism.

By Theorem P4.7.8, $(\mathcal{O}(-N-1), t)$ is a dualizing sheaf for $\mathbb{P}^N_k$.

**Proposition P4.7.15 (uniqueness).** If $(\omega, t)$ and $(\omega_1, t_1)$ are dualizing sheaves for $X$, there is a unique isomorphism $\psi \colon \omega \to \omega_1$ with $t_1 \circ H^n(\psi) = t$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

For the rest of the section fix a closed immersion $X \subseteq P = \mathbb{P}^N_k$, let $r = N - n$, and $\omega_P = \mathcal{O}_P(-N-1)$. Coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules are identified with coherent $\mathcal{O}_P$-modules killed by the ideal of $X$, through the pushforward, which does not change cohomology (Proposition P2.7.4).

**Lemma P4.7.16 (low Ext sheaves vanish).** $\mathcal{E}xt^i_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P) = 0$ for every $i < r$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P4.7.17 (existence of the dualizing sheaf).** Let $\omega^\circ_X = \mathcal{E}xt^r_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$, a coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. For every coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$ there are isomorphisms, natural in $\mathcal{F}$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \omega^\circ_X) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^r_P(\mathcal{F}, \omega_P) \cong H^n(X, \mathcal{F})'.$$
So $\omega^\circ_X$, with the trace $t$ corresponding to $\operatorname{id}_{\omega^\circ_X}$, is a dualizing sheaf for $X$. Every projective scheme over $k$ has a dualizing sheaf, unique by Proposition P4.7.15.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

Duality in degree $n$ always holds. In the other degrees, the trace and the long exact sequences give maps
$$\theta^i \colon \operatorname{Ext}^i_X(\mathcal{F}, \omega^\circ_X) \to H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{F})',$$
and whether they are isomorphisms depends on the singularities of $X$.

**Theorem P4.7.18 (Serre duality on a Cohen-Macaulay scheme).** Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_k$ be a closed subscheme that is equidimensional of dimension $n$, that is, every irreducible component has dimension $n$. There are unique maps $\theta^i$ as above, natural in $\mathcal{F}$, compatible with connecting maps and equal to the duality isomorphism for $i = 0$. The following are equivalent:

1. $X$ is Cohen-Macaulay;
2. $\mathcal{E}xt^j_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P) = 0$ for every $j \neq r$;
3. for every locally free sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ of finite rank on $X$, $H^i(X, \mathcal{E}(-q)) = 0$ for $i < n$ and $q$ large enough;
4. the same holds for $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{O}_X$;
5. $\theta^i$ is an isomorphism for every $i$ and every coherent $\mathcal{F}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P4.7.19 (duality for vector bundles).** If $X$ is Cohen-Macaulay and equidimensional of dimension $n$, then for every locally free sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ of finite rank and every $i$,
$$H^i(X, \mathcal{E}) \cong H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{E}^\vee \otimes \omega^\circ_X)'.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P4.7.20 (local complete intersections).** Suppose that the ideal $\mathcal{I}$ of $X$ in $P$ is locally generated by a regular sequence of length $r$. Then the conormal sheaf $\mathcal{C}_{X/P} = \mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^2$ is locally free of rank $r$, $X$ is Cohen-Macaulay and equidimensional of dimension $n$, and
$$\omega^\circ_X \cong \omega_P|_X \otimes \det \mathcal{N}_{X/P}, \qquad \mathcal{N}_{X/P} = \mathcal{C}_{X/P}^\vee.$$
In particular $\omega^\circ_X$ is invertible.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P4.7.21 (smooth projective varieties).** Let $k$ be perfect and $X$ smooth and projective over $k$ of pure dimension $n$. Then $\omega^\circ_X \cong \omega_X = \bigwedge^n \Omega_X$, and for every locally free $\mathcal{E}$ of finite rank,
$$H^i(X, \mathcal{E}) \cong H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{E}^\vee \otimes \omega_X)'.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P4.7.22 (normal projective varieties).** Let $k$ be perfect and $X$ a normal projective variety over $k$, with smooth locus $j \colon U \to X$. Then $\omega^\circ_X$ is torsion-free of rank one, $\operatorname{depth} \omega^\circ_{X,x} \geq \min(2, \dim \mathcal{O}_{X,x})$ for every $x \in X$, and $\omega^\circ_X \cong j_* \omega_U$. So $\omega^\circ_X$ is the canonical sheaf $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}_X(K_X)$ of Proposition P3.12.29.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

So for the varieties of the minimal model program, the canonical sheaf of Volume P3 and the dualizing sheaf of this section are the same, and Serre duality in all degrees holds on the Cohen-Macaulay ones.

### Proof in words

The dualizing sheaf is the object that represents the functor $\mathcal{F} \mapsto H^n(X, \mathcal{F})'$, which makes it unique. To construct it, compute $H^n(X, \mathcal{F}) = H^n(P, \mathcal{F})$ by duality on $P$ as the dual of $\operatorname{Ext}^r_P(\mathcal{F}, \omega_P)$. The low Ext sheaves of $\mathcal{O}_X$ vanish because, after a high twist, their sections are dual to cohomology groups of $X$ above its dimension. That vanishing lets $\operatorname{Ext}^r_P(\mathcal{F}, \omega_P)$ be computed as $\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, -)$ applied to the first nonvanishing Ext sheaf. For the other degrees, both $\operatorname{Ext}^i_X(-, \omega^\circ_X)$ and $H^{n-i}(X, -)'$ are $\delta$-functors, and the first is universal. The second is universal, so that $\theta$ is an isomorphism, exactly when the groups $H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-q))$ vanish for $q$ large. By duality on $P$ these groups are the duals of the sections of the twisted Ext sheaves $\mathcal{E}xt^{r+i}_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$, and by the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula the stalks of these Ext sheaves vanish exactly when $X$ is Cohen-Macaulay. For a local complete intersection the Koszul complex computes the one nonzero Ext sheaf and gives the determinant of the normal bundle. For a smooth variety the conormal sequence identifies $\omega_P|_X \otimes \det \mathcal{N}_{X/P}$ with $\bigwedge^n \Omega_X$.

### Examples

**Example P4.7.23 (plane curves and their genus).** Let $C = V_+(F) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ be any curve of degree $d$, possibly singular or non-reduced. It is a hypersurface, so Theorem P4.7.20 applies with $\mathcal{N}_{C/P} = \mathcal{O}_C(d)$, and $\omega^\circ_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C(d - 3)$. By Corollary P4.7.19,
$$h^1(C, \mathcal{O}_C) = h^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C(d - 3)) = \binom{d - 1}{2},$$
the last from $0 \to \mathcal{O}_P(-3) \to \mathcal{O}_P(d-3) \to \mathcal{O}_C(d-3) \to 0$ and Theorem P4.2.1. This is the arithmetic genus of Example P4.3.12. For a smooth cubic, $\omega_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C$, and for a smooth quartic, $\omega_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C(1)$: the canonical embedding of a non-hyperelliptic curve of genus $3$.

**Example P4.7.24 (complete intersections).** Let $X = V_+(F_1, \ldots, F_r) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_k$ with $\dim X = N - r$ and $\deg F_i = d_i$. The $F_i$ form a regular sequence locally, since the local rings of $P$ are Cohen-Macaulay and the codimension is $r$ (Corollary P1.10.15), so $\mathcal{C}_{X/P} = \bigoplus_i \mathcal{O}_X(-d_i)$ and
$$\omega^\circ_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X\Big(\sum_i d_i - N - 1\Big).$$
For a complete intersection of a quadric and a cubic in $\mathbb{P}^3$, $\omega^\circ_X = \mathcal{O}_X(1)$, a canonical curve of genus $4$. For a quartic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$, $\omega^\circ_X = \mathcal{O}_X$, a K3 surface when smooth. For $\sum d_i < N + 1$, $\omega^\circ_X$ is negative, and for $\sum d_i > N + 1$ it is ample, the three cases of the trichotomy of Volume 1.

**Example P4.7.25 (two planes meeting in a point).** Let $X = P_1 \cup P_2 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^4_k$ be the union of the planes $P_1 = V_+(x_3, x_4)$ and $P_2 = V_+(x_1, x_2)$, which meet in the single point $p = [1 : 0 : 0 : 0 : 0]$. It is equidimensional of dimension $2$, and its local ring at $p$ has depth $1$ (Example P1.10.11), so $X$ is not Cohen-Macaulay. The sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_{P_1} \oplus \mathcal{O}_{P_2} \to k(p) \to 0$ twisted by $-q$, with Theorem P4.2.1 on the planes, gives
$$H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-q)) \cong H^0(k(p)) = k \quad \text{for every } q > 0.$$
So condition 4 of Theorem P4.7.18 fails for $i = 1$, and $\theta^1 \colon \operatorname{Ext}^1_X(\mathcal{O}_X(-q), \omega^\circ_X) \to H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-q))'$ is not surjective for $q$ large, since its source $H^1(X, \omega^\circ_X(q))$ vanishes. By Theorem P4.7.18, the sheaf $\mathcal{E}xt^3_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ is nonzero, and it is supported at $p$. The derived category view of this example is Example P2.8.29.

### Proof

*Proposition P4.7.15.* The isomorphisms $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F}, \omega) \cong H^n(\mathcal{F})' \cong \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F}, \omega_1)$ are natural in $\mathcal{F}$. So they give a natural isomorphism of the functors $\operatorname{Hom}(-, \omega)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(-, \omega_1)$ on coherent sheaves, which by the Yoneda lemma comes from a unique isomorphism $\psi \colon \omega \to \omega_1$. Applied to $\mathcal{F} = \omega$ and $\operatorname{id}_\omega$, it says $t_1 \circ H^n(\psi) = t$. Any $\psi$ with this property induces the given natural isomorphism, so it is unique. $\square$

*Lemma P4.7.16.* The sheaf $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{E}xt^i_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ is coherent by Proposition P4.7.3. For $q$ large, using Proposition P4.7.4, part 4 of Proposition P4.7.2, and Theorem P4.7.8,
$$\Gamma(P, \mathcal{G}(q)) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^i_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P(q)) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^i_P(\mathcal{O}_X(-q), \omega_P) \cong H^{N-i}(P, \mathcal{O}_X(-q))' = H^{N-i}(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-q))'.$$
For $i < r$, $N - i > n = \dim X$, so the last group vanishes by Theorem P2.7.13. By Theorem P3.6.23, $\mathcal{G}(q)$ is generated by its global sections for $q$ large, so $\mathcal{G}(q) = 0$ and $\mathcal{G} = 0$. $\square$

*Theorem P4.7.17.* The sheaf $\omega^\circ_X$ is coherent by Proposition P4.7.3 and killed by the ideal of $X$, since $\mathcal{O}_X$ acts on $\mathcal{E}xt_P(\mathcal{O}_X, -)$ through the first variable. So it is a coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.

*Step 1: an injective resolution on $X$.* Let $\omega_P \to \mathcal{I}^\bullet$ be an injective resolution in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_P)$ and $\mathcal{J}^i = \mathcal{H}om_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{I}^i)$, the subsheaf of $\mathcal{I}^i$ killed by the ideal of $X$, an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. For an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{J}^i) = \operatorname{Hom}_P(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{I}^i),$$
since a map from $\mathcal{F}$ lands in the sections killed by the ideal. The right side is exact in $\mathcal{F}$, so $\mathcal{J}^i$ is an injective $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. The cohomology sheaves of $\mathcal{J}^\bullet$ are $\mathcal{E}xt^i_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$, which vanish for $i < r$ by Lemma P4.7.16, and $\mathcal{H}^r(\mathcal{J}^\bullet) = \omega^\circ_X$.

*Step 2: splitting off the exact part.* Let $\mathcal{B}^i = \operatorname{im}(\mathcal{J}^{i-1} \to \mathcal{J}^i)$ and $\mathcal{Z}^i = \ker(\mathcal{J}^i \to \mathcal{J}^{i+1})$, with $\mathcal{B}^0 = 0$. By induction on $i \leq r$, $\mathcal{B}^i$ is injective: $\mathcal{B}^0 = 0$, and for $1 \leq i \leq r$ the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{Z}^{i-1} \to \mathcal{J}^{i-1} \to \mathcal{B}^i \to 0$ has $\mathcal{Z}^{i-1} = \mathcal{B}^{i-1}$ injective by exactness in degree $i - 1 < r$, so it splits and $\mathcal{B}^i$ is a direct summand of $\mathcal{J}^{i-1}$. Since $\mathcal{B}^r$ is injective, $\mathcal{Z}^r = \mathcal{B}^r \oplus \mathcal{Z}'$ with $\mathcal{Z}' \cong \mathcal{Z}^r/\mathcal{B}^r = \omega^\circ_X$, and $\mathcal{J}^{r-1} \to \mathcal{B}^r$ is split surjective, for $r \geq 1$.

*Step 3.* For a coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$, by Step 1,
$$\operatorname{Ext}^r_P(\mathcal{F}, \omega_P) = H^r(\operatorname{Hom}_P(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{I}^\bullet)) = H^r(\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{J}^\bullet)) = \frac{\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{Z}^r)}{\operatorname{im} \operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{J}^{r-1})}.$$
By Step 2, the image is $\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{B}^r)$, and the quotient is $\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{Z}') = \operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \omega^\circ_X)$. For $r = 0$ the same holds with $\mathcal{B}^0 = 0$. By Theorem P4.7.8, $\operatorname{Ext}^r_P(\mathcal{F}, \omega_P) \cong H^{N-r}(P, \mathcal{F})' = H^n(X, \mathcal{F})'$. All the maps are natural in $\mathcal{F}$.

*Step 4: the trace.* Let $t \in H^n(X, \omega^\circ_X)'$ correspond to $\operatorname{id}$. For $\varphi \colon \mathcal{F} \to \omega^\circ_X$, naturality of the isomorphism $\alpha_\mathcal{F} \colon \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F}, \omega^\circ_X) \to H^n(\mathcal{F})'$ applied to $\varphi$ gives $\alpha_\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{id} \circ \varphi) = \alpha_{\omega^\circ}(\operatorname{id}) \circ H^n(\varphi) = t \circ H^n(\varphi)$. So $\alpha$ is the map of Definition P4.7.14, and $(\omega^\circ_X, t)$ is dualizing. $\square$

*Theorem P4.7.18.* *The maps $\theta^i$.* On the abelian category $\mathsf{Coh}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}$, $S^i = \operatorname{Ext}^i_X(-, \omega^\circ_X)$ and $T^i = H^{n-i}(X, -)'$ are $\delta$-functors, as in the proof of Theorem P4.7.8. The functor $S$ is effaceable: every coherent $\mathcal{F}$ is a quotient of $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{O}_X(-q)^{\oplus m}$ for every $q$ large enough (Theorem P3.6.23), and $S^i(\mathcal{M}) = H^i(X, \omega^\circ_X(q))^{\oplus m} = 0$ for $i > 0$ and $q$ large, by Proposition P4.7.2 and Theorem P4.2.7. Only $i \leq n$ matters, since $T^i = 0$ for $i > n$, so one $q$ works for all relevant $i$. So $S$ is universal, as in the proof of Theorem P4.7.8, and the isomorphism $\theta^0$ of Theorem P4.7.17 extends uniquely to a morphism $\theta \colon S \to T$.

*(1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2).* The sheaves $\mathcal{E}xt^j_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ are coherent and supported on $X$, so they vanish if and only if their stalks vanish at all closed points of $X$, since every nonempty closed subset of $X$ contains a closed point. Let $x \in X$ be closed, $R = \mathcal{O}_{P,x}$, a regular local ring of dimension $N$, and $A = \mathcal{O}_{X,x} = R/I$. Since $X$ is equidimensional of dimension $n$ and of finite type over $k$, $\dim A = n$. Since $\omega_P$ is invertible, by Proposition P4.7.3 the stalk of $\mathcal{E}xt^j_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ at $x$ is $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R)$, up to the choice of a generator of $\omega_{P,x}$. By Theorem P1.9.21, $p = \operatorname{pd}_R A$ is finite, and by Theorem P1.10.8, $p = N - \operatorname{depth} A$. Take a minimal free resolution $0 \to F_p \to \cdots \to F_0 \to A \to 0$ (Lemma P1.9.19). Then $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R) = 0$ for $j > p$, and $\operatorname{Ext}^p_R(A, R)$ is the cokernel of $F_{p-1}^\vee \to F_p^\vee$, whose image lies in $\mathfrak{m}_R F_p^\vee$. So it is nonzero by Nakayama's lemma. So $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R) = 0$ for all $j > r$ if and only if $p \leq r$, that is, $\operatorname{depth} A \geq N - r = n = \dim A$. Since $\operatorname{depth} A \leq \dim A$ (Proposition P1.10.6), this says $A$ is Cohen-Macaulay. With Lemma P4.7.16 for $j < r$, condition 2 at $x$ is equivalent to $A$ being Cohen-Macaulay. By Definition P1.10.13, $X$ is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if this holds at all closed points.

*(2) $\Rightarrow$ (3).* Let $\mathcal{E}$ be locally free on $X$ and $i < n$. As in the proof of Lemma P4.7.16, for $q$ large,
$$H^i(X, \mathcal{E}(-q))' \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_P(\mathcal{E}(-q), \omega_P) \cong \Gamma\big(P, \mathcal{E}xt^{N-i}_P(\mathcal{E}, \omega_P)(q)\big).$$
On an open $V \subseteq P$ where $\mathcal{E} \cong \mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus m}$, part 2 of Proposition P4.7.2 gives $\mathcal{E}xt^{N-i}_P(\mathcal{E}, \omega_P)|_V \cong \mathcal{E}xt^{N-i}_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)^{\oplus m}|_V$, which is $0$ by condition 2, since $N - i > r$. So the group vanishes.

*(3) $\Rightarrow$ (4)* is the case $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{O}_X$.

*(4) $\Rightarrow$ (2).* By the displayed isomorphism for $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{O}_X$, condition 4 says that $\Gamma(\mathcal{E}xt^j_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)(q)) = 0$ for $r < j \leq N$ and $q$ large, so these coherent sheaves vanish, as in the proof of Lemma P4.7.16. For $j > N$ the stalks $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R)$ vanish since $\operatorname{gl.dim} R = N$ (Theorem P1.9.21), and for $j < r$ use Lemma P4.7.16.

*(4) $\Leftrightarrow$ (5).* If (4) holds, $T^i(\mathcal{O}_X(-q)^{\oplus m}) = H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-q))'^{\oplus m} = 0$ for $0 < i \leq n$ and $q$ large, so $T$ is effaceable by the same surjections as $S$, hence universal, and $\theta$ is an isomorphism as in the proof of Theorem P4.7.8. Conversely, if every $\theta^i$ is an isomorphism, then for $0 < i \leq n$ and $q$ large, $H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-q))' \cong S^i(\mathcal{O}_X(-q)) = 0$, which is (4). $\square$

*Corollary P4.7.19.* By part 4 of Proposition P4.7.2, $\operatorname{Ext}^{n-i}_X(\mathcal{E}, \omega^\circ_X) = H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{E}^\vee \otimes \omega^\circ_X)$, and by Theorem P4.7.18 it is $H^i(X, \mathcal{E})'$. $\square$

*Theorem P4.7.20.* *Local structure.* Let $V \subseteq P$ be an affine open on which $\mathcal{I}$ is generated by a regular sequence $f_1, \ldots, f_r$, with $B = \mathcal{O}(V)$. By Lemma P1.9.20 the Koszul complex $K_\bullet = K(f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ is a free resolution of $B/I$, so $I/I^2$ is free over $B/I$ on the classes of the $f_i$ (Exercise 5), and $\mathcal{C}_{X/P}$ is locally free of rank $r$. At a closed point $x$ of $X \cap V$, the $f_i$ form an $R$-sequence in $R = \mathcal{O}_{P,x}$, so $\dim R/I = N - r = n$ by part 1 of Theorem P1.10.14, and $R/I$ is Cohen-Macaulay by part 4. Since this holds at every closed point and every irreducible component of $X$ contains one, $X$ is Cohen-Macaulay and equidimensional of dimension $n$.

*The Ext sheaf.* On $V$, by Proposition P4.7.3, $\mathcal{E}xt^j_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_P)$ is the cohomology of $\operatorname{Hom}_B(K_\bullet, B)$, which is the Koszul complex again with the indices reversed (Exercise 6). In degree $r$, with $K_r = B \cdot e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_r$, the cokernel of $\operatorname{Hom}(K_{r-1}, B) \to \operatorname{Hom}(K_r, B)$ is $B/I$, generated by the dual basis vector $(e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_r)^*$. Define
$$\lambda_f \colon \mathcal{E}xt^r_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_P)|_V \to \mathcal{H}om_X(\det \mathcal{C}_{X/P}, \mathcal{O}_X)|_V, \qquad [\varphi] \mapsto \big(\bar f_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \bar f_r \mapsto \varphi(e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_r) \bmod I\big).$$
It sends the generator $(e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_r)^*$ to the generator dual to $\bar f_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \bar f_r$, so it is an isomorphism.

*Independence of the generators.* If $f'_i = \sum_j g_{ij} f_j$ is another regular sequence generating $I$ on $V$, with $g = (g_{ij})$ a matrix over $B$, then $e'_i \mapsto \sum_j g_{ij} e_j$ extends to a map of Koszul complexes $K(f') \to K(f)$ lifting the identity of $B/I$, given in degree $r$ by multiplication by $\det g$. So the identification of $\mathcal{E}xt^r$ with the cokernel changes by $\det g$ in degree $r$, and $\bar f'_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \bar f'_r = \det(\bar g) \, \bar f_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \bar f_r$ in $\det \mathcal{C}_{X/P}$. The two factors of $\det g$ cancel, so $\lambda_{f'} = \lambda_f$. Here $\det \bar g$ is a unit on $X \cap V$, since both sequences generate $I/I^2$ freely. So the local isomorphisms glue to $\mathcal{E}xt^r_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_P) \cong \det \mathcal{N}_{X/P}$ on all of $X$. Twisting by the invertible sheaf $\omega_P$ with part 4 of Proposition P4.7.2 gives $\omega^\circ_X = \mathcal{E}xt^r_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P) \cong \omega_P|_X \otimes \det \mathcal{N}_{X/P}$. $\square$

*Corollary P4.7.21.* Let $x \in X$ be a closed point, $R = \mathcal{O}_{P,x}$ and $A = \mathcal{O}_{X,x} = R/I$, regular of dimension $n$ by part 3 of Theorem P3.7.24. The kernel of $\mathfrak{m}_R/\mathfrak{m}_R^2 \to \mathfrak{m}_A/\mathfrak{m}_A^2$ has dimension $N - n = r$ and is the image of $I$. Choose $f_1, \ldots, f_r \in I$ whose classes form a basis of it. By Proposition P1.9.4, $R/(f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ is regular of dimension $n$, so a domain (Corollary P1.9.3), and the $f_i$ form a regular sequence. The surjection $R/(f_1, \ldots, f_r) \to A$ of domains of the same dimension is an isomorphism, since a nonzero kernel would lower the dimension. So $\mathcal{I}$ is generated near $x$ by a regular sequence of length $r$, and the same holds on a neighbourhood of $x$, since the generators extend and the Koszul complex is exact near $x$ (its homology is coherent and vanishes at $x$). Since every point of $X$ specializes to a closed point, $X$ is a local complete intersection in $P$, and Theorem P4.7.20 applies.

By part 1 of Theorem P3.12.14 the conormal sequence $0 \to \mathcal{C}_{X/P} \to \Omega_P|_X \to \Omega_X \to 0$ is exact, with locally free terms of ranks $r$, $N$ and $n$. By Lemma P3.12.21, $\omega_P|_X = \det(\Omega_P|_X) \cong \det \mathcal{C}_{X/P} \otimes \omega_X$, so $\omega_X \cong \omega_P|_X \otimes \det \mathcal{N}_{X/P} \cong \omega^\circ_X$. The duality statement is Corollary P4.7.19. $\square$

*Proposition P4.7.22.* *Local form.* Let $x \in X$, not necessarily closed, $R = \mathcal{O}_{P,x}$, regular of dimension $N_x$ by Corollary P1.9.22, $A = \mathcal{O}_{X,x} = R/I$, a normal domain of dimension $c$, and $r = N_x - c$, which equals $N - n$ by the dimension theory of varieties. Since $R$ is Cohen-Macaulay and $\operatorname{ht} I = r$, $I$ contains an $R$-sequence $g_1, \ldots, g_r$, by part 3 of Theorem P1.10.14 applied to a system of parameters of $R$ extending them. Let $D = R/(g_1, \ldots, g_r)$, Cohen-Macaulay of dimension $c$ by parts 1 and 4 of Theorem P1.10.14. Applying Lemma P1.10.3 $r$ times, as in the proof of Theorem P1.10.4, with $N = A$ killed by each $g_i$,
$$\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R) = 0 \text{ for } j < r, \qquad \operatorname{Ext}^r_R(A, R) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(A, D).$$
So $\omega^\circ_{X,x} \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(A, D)$, after choosing a generator of $\omega_{P,x}$.

*Torsion-free of rank one.* Every associated prime of $\operatorname{Hom}_R(A, D)$ is an associated prime of $D$ containing $I$. Since $D$ is Cohen-Macaulay, its associated primes are minimal, of dimension $c$ (part 2 of Theorem P1.10.14). A prime of dimension $c$ containing $I$ is $I$ itself, since $A = R/I$ is a domain of dimension $c$. So the only associated prime of $\omega^\circ_{X,x}$, as an $A$-module, is $0$, and $\omega^\circ_X$ is torsion-free. On the smooth locus $U$, $\omega^\circ_X|_U \cong \omega_U$ by Theorem P4.7.20 and the proof of Corollary P4.7.21, which are local on $X$. So $\omega^\circ_X$ has rank one.

*Depth.* If $c \geq 2$, choose $y, z \in \mathfrak{m}_R$ forming a $D$-sequence, which exists since $\operatorname{depth} D = c \geq 2$. Then $y$ is a nonzerodivisor on $H = \operatorname{Hom}_R(A, D)$, since it is one on $D$. The sequence $0 \to D \xrightarrow{y} D \to D/yD$ gives an injection $H/yH \to \operatorname{Hom}_R(A, D/yD)$, and $z$ is a nonzerodivisor on $D/yD$, hence on $\operatorname{Hom}_R(A, D/yD)$ and on its submodule $H/yH$. The images of $y$, $z$ lie in $\mathfrak{m}_A$, and they act on $H$ through $A$, so $\operatorname{depth}_A \omega^\circ_{X,x} \geq 2$. If $c = 1$, $y$ alone shows depth at least $1$.

*Comparison with $j_* \omega_U$.* The map $\omega^\circ_X \to j_* (\omega^\circ_X|_U) \cong j_* \omega_U$ is injective since $\omega^\circ_X$ is torsion-free. To show it is surjective, let $W = \operatorname{Spec} A_0 \subseteq X$ be an affine open, $M = \omega^\circ_X(W)$, and $s \in \omega_U(W \cap U)$, an element of $M \otimes K$, regular at every height one prime of $A_0$, since $X \setminus U$ has codimension at least $2$ (Proposition P3.12.13). So the module $Q = (M + A_0 s)/M$ satisfies $Q_\mathfrak{p} = 0$ for every prime of height at most one. If $Q \neq 0$, let $\mathfrak{q}$ be an associated prime of $Q$, of height at least $2$, and localize at $\mathfrak{q}$, with residue field $\kappa$. The sequence $0 \to M_\mathfrak{q} \to M_\mathfrak{q} + A_\mathfrak{q} s \to Q_\mathfrak{q} \to 0$ gives an exact sequence
$$\operatorname{Hom}(\kappa, M_\mathfrak{q} + A_\mathfrak{q} s) \to \operatorname{Hom}(\kappa, Q_\mathfrak{q}) \to \operatorname{Ext}^1(\kappa, M_\mathfrak{q}).$$
The right term vanishes by Theorem P1.10.4, since $\operatorname{depth} M_\mathfrak{q} \geq 2$. The left term vanishes, since $M_\mathfrak{q} + A_\mathfrak{q} s$ is a torsion-free module over a domain that is not a field. But the middle term is nonzero, since $\mathfrak{q}$ is associated to $Q$. This contradiction shows $Q = 0$ and $s \in M$. So $\omega^\circ_X \cong j_* \omega_U$, which is $\omega_X$ by Proposition P3.12.29. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\omega^\circ_X$ does not depend on the embedding $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_k$, up to unique isomorphism compatible with the traces.
2. (A) Let $X$ be a Cohen-Macaulay projective curve over $k$. Show that $h^0(\omega^\circ_X) = h^1(\mathcal{O}_X)$ and $h^1(\omega^\circ_X) = h^0(\mathcal{O}_X)$.
3. (B) Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$ be the twisted cubic. Show that $X$ is not a complete intersection, but that it is a local complete intersection, and compute $\omega^\circ_X \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-2)$ by Theorem P4.7.20 and the normal bundle $\mathcal{N}_{X/P} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(5)^{\oplus 2}$.
4. (B) Let $X = V_+(x_0 x_1) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ be two lines meeting in a point. Show that $\omega^\circ_X \cong \mathcal{O}_X(-1)$, that its restriction to each line is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)$, and explain the difference from $\omega_{\mathbb{P}^1} = \mathcal{O}(-2)$ through the node.
5. (B) Let $f_1, \ldots, f_r$ be a regular sequence in a ring $B$, with $I = (f_1, \ldots, f_r)$. Show that $I/I^2$ is a free $B/I$-module with basis the classes of the $f_i$.
6. (C) Show that $\operatorname{Hom}_B(K_\bullet(f_1, \ldots, f_r), B) \cong K_\bullet(f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ with the indices reversed, $j \mapsto r - j$, through the pairing $\bigwedge^j B^r \times \bigwedge^{r-j} B^r \to \bigwedge^r B^r \cong B$. Deduce that $\operatorname{Ext}^j_B(B/I, B) = 0$ for $j \neq r$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^r_B(B/I, B) \cong B/I$ for a regular sequence.

### Lean

Mathlib has regular sequences (`RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular`), depth and Krull dimension as definitions, and exterior powers. Dualizing sheaves and the duality theorems above are not in Mathlib. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

The dualizing sheaf of a projective scheme, as the object representing $\mathcal{F} \mapsto H^n(X, \mathcal{F})'$, is due to Grothendieck, who presented the projective case at the Séminaire Bourbaki in 1957, *Théorèmes de dualité pour les faisceaux algébriques cohérents*. The characterization of full duality by the Cohen-Macaulay property and the computation for local complete intersections are in that talk and in Altman and Kleiman's *Introduction to Grothendieck duality theory* (1970). The presentation follows Hartshorne III.7.1 to III.7.12. The proof of Proposition P4.7.22 is the standard argument that a sheaf with depth two in codimension two is determined by its restriction to a big open set. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program, Proposition P4.7.22 is the bridge between the divisor $K_X$, which the program manipulates, and the sheaf $\omega^\circ_X$, which the cohomological tools see, and Theorem P4.7.18 is the reason that Cohen-Macaulay singularities, and in particular rational singularities, are the natural class for Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing in its dual form.
