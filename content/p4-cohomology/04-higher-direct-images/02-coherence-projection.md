## Coherence and the projection formula

### Statement

A projective morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ (Definition P3.4.29) comes with a closed immersion $i \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^n_Y$ over $Y$, and $\mathcal{O}_X(1) = i^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ and $\mathcal{F}(m) = \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(1)^{\otimes m}$ depend on $i$. A projective morphism is proper (Corollary P3.4.30), in particular quasi-compact and separated, so Theorem P4.4.1 applies to it.

**Theorem P4.4.8 (coherence of higher direct images).** Let $Y$ be locally Noetherian, $f \colon X \to Y$ projective with $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_Y$, and $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent sheaf on $X$. Then $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}$ is coherent for every $q$, and $R^q f_* \mathcal{F} = 0$ for $q > n$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P4.4.9 (relative Serre vanishing and generation).** Let $Y$ be Noetherian, $f \colon X \to Y$ projective with $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_Y$, and $\mathcal{F}$ coherent on $X$. There is $m_0$ such that for every $m \geq m_0$:

1. $R^q f_* (\mathcal{F}(m)) = 0$ for all $q > 0$;
2. the counit $f^* f_* (\mathcal{F}(m)) \to \mathcal{F}(m)$ is surjective.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P4.4.10 (projection formula).** Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a morphism of ringed spaces, $\mathcal{F}$ an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module and $\mathcal{E}$ a locally free $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module of finite rank. For every $q \geq 0$ there is a natural isomorphism
$$R^q f_* \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y} \mathcal{E} \cong R^q f_* (\mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} f^* \mathcal{E}).$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

For $q = 0$ this is Theorem P2.6.30. Theorem P4.4.9 says that, after a large twist, the higher direct images vanish and the sheaf is a quotient of the pullback of a coherent sheaf on $Y$. Over a field it is Theorem P4.2.7 together with Theorem P3.6.23.

### Proof in words

Coherence and vanishing are local on $Y$, so the base can be taken affine and Noetherian. There Theorem P4.4.1 says that $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}$ is the sheaf of the module $H^q(X, \mathcal{F})$, which is finitely generated by Serre's finiteness theorem and zero above $n$. For the relative Serre theorem, a Noetherian $Y$ has a finite affine cover, and the absolute theorems on each piece give finitely many thresholds, of which the largest works everywhere. The projection formula is local on $Y$ too: where $\mathcal{E}$ is free of rank $s$, both sides are $s$ copies of $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}$, and the only work is to write down a map that is defined globally and is this identification locally.

### Examples

**Example P4.4.11 (the twists on projective space over a base).** For $\pi \colon \mathbb{P}^r_Y \to Y$ and $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}(a)$, Example P4.4.4 shows that the conditions of Theorem P4.4.9 hold exactly for $m \geq -a$: the higher direct images of $\mathcal{O}(a + m)$ vanish for $a + m \geq -r$, and $\pi^* \pi_* \mathcal{O}(a + m) \to \mathcal{O}(a + m)$ is surjective for $a + m \geq 0$ and is the zero map from the zero sheaf for $a + m < 0$. For a locally free $\mathcal{E}$ on $Y$, Proposition P4.4.10 gives $R^r \pi_* (\pi^* \mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-r-1)) \cong \mathcal{E}$.

**Example P4.4.12 (properness is needed).** The structure morphism $f \colon \mathbb{A}^1_k \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is affine and of finite type, and $f_* \mathcal{O} = k[t]^\sim$ is not coherent. The open immersion of Example P4.4.5 is quasi-projective, and $R^1 j_* \mathcal{O}_U$ is not coherent. In both cases the failure is that a non-proper morphism has fibres or preimages with infinite-dimensional cohomology over the base.

**Example P4.4.13 (a double cover of the line).** Let $k$ be a field with $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$ and $f \colon X = V_+(y^2 - x_0 x_1) \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$, the plane conic in $\mathbb{P}^2_k$ with coordinates $x_0, x_1, y$, and $f([x_0 : x_1 : y]) = [x_0 : x_1]$. The morphism $f$ is finite of degree $2$, and on $D_+(x_0)$ it is $k[t] \to k[t, w]/(w^2 - t)$ with $t = x_1/x_0$, $w = y/x_0$. So $f_* \mathcal{O}_X|_{D_+(x_0)}$ is free on $1, w$, and since $y$ has degree $1$, $f_* \mathcal{O}_X \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1} \oplus \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)$, as in Example P3.5.41 with $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{O}(1)$. Corollary P4.4.3 gives $H^q(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \cong H^q(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1))$, so $h^0 = 1$ and $h^1 = 0$, in agreement with $X \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$.

### Proof

*Theorem P4.4.8.* Both claims are local on $Y$, so let $V = \operatorname{Spec} A \subseteq Y$ be affine, with $A$ Noetherian. Then $f^{-1}(V) = X \times_Y V$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^n_A$, and $\mathcal{F}|_{f^{-1}V}$ is coherent. By Theorem P4.4.1, $(R^q f_* \mathcal{F})|_V = H^q(f^{-1}V, \mathcal{F})^\sim$, and by part 1 of Theorem P4.2.7 the module $H^q(f^{-1}V, \mathcal{F})$ is finitely generated over $A$ and is $0$ for $q > n$. A quasi-coherent sheaf on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ whose module is finitely generated is coherent (Proposition P3.5.22). $\square$

*Theorem P4.4.9.* Since $Y$ is Noetherian, it has a finite cover by affine open sets $V_j = \operatorname{Spec} A_j$, $j = 1, \dots, s$, with $A_j$ Noetherian. Let $X_j = f^{-1}(V_j)$ with the closed immersion $i_j \colon X_j \to \mathbb{P}^n_{A_j}$. By part 2 of Theorem P4.2.7 there is $m_j$ with $H^q(X_j, \mathcal{F}(m)) = 0$ for $q > 0$ and $m \geq m_j$, so $(R^q f_* \mathcal{F}(m))|_{V_j} = 0$ by Theorem P4.4.1. The sheaf $i_{j*} \mathcal{F}$ is coherent on $\mathbb{P}^n_{A_j} = \operatorname{Proj} A_j[x_0, \dots, x_n]$, and $(i_{j*} \mathcal{F})(m) = i_{j*} (\mathcal{F}(m))$ by Theorem P2.6.30 for the closed immersion $i_j$. By Theorem P3.6.23 there is $m_j'$ such that $i_{j*}(\mathcal{F}(m))$ is generated by global sections for $m \geq m_j'$, and then so is $\mathcal{F}(m)|_{X_j}$, by the same sections $\Gamma(X_j, \mathcal{F}(m)) = (f_* \mathcal{F}(m))(V_j)$. A section $t \in (f_* \mathcal{F}(m))(V_j)$ is the image under the counit of the section $f^* t$ of $f^* f_* \mathcal{F}(m)$ over $X_j$, so the counit is surjective on $X_j$. Take $m_0 = \max_j \max(m_j, m_j')$. $\square$

*Proposition P4.4.10.* Restriction to an open $V \subseteq Y$ commutes with $R^q f_*$, by part 4 of Theorem P2.7.3, and $R^q f_*$ is an additive functor, so it commutes with finite direct sums. Let $V \subseteq Y$ be open and $e \in \mathcal{E}(V)$. The map $\mu_e \colon \mathcal{F}|_{f^{-1}V} \to (\mathcal{F} \otimes f^* \mathcal{E})|_{f^{-1}V}$, $s \mapsto s \otimes f^* e$, is $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear, and $R^q f_*(\mu_e)$ is a map $(R^q f_* \mathcal{F})|_V \to R^q f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes f^* \mathcal{E})|_V$. Define
$$\Phi_V \colon (R^q f_* \mathcal{F})(V) \times \mathcal{E}(V) \to R^q f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes f^* \mathcal{E})(V), \qquad (\xi, e) \mapsto R^q f_*(\mu_e)(\xi).$$
It is additive in both variables, since $\mu_{e + e'} = \mu_e + \mu_{e'}$ and $R^q f_*$ is additive, and it is $\mathcal{O}_Y(V)$-balanced, since $\mu_{a e} = f^\#(a) \mu_e$ and the $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module structure on $R^q f_*$ is induced by $f^\#$ (part 4 of Theorem P2.7.11). The maps $\Phi_V$ are compatible with restriction, so they define a morphism from the presheaf tensor product to $R^q f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes f^* \mathcal{E})$, and by sheafification a morphism
$$\Phi \colon R^q f_* \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{E} \to R^q f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes f^* \mathcal{E}).$$
It is natural in $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{E}$. To see that it is an isomorphism, let $V$ be an open set on which $\mathcal{E}|_V$ is free with basis $e_1, \dots, e_s$. Then $(\mu_{e_1}, \dots, \mu_{e_s}) \colon \mathcal{F}^{\oplus s} \to \mathcal{F} \otimes f^* \mathcal{E}$ is an isomorphism over $f^{-1}(V)$, and $\Phi|_V$ is the composite of the isomorphism $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}|_V \otimes \mathcal{O}_V^{\oplus s} \cong (R^q f_* \mathcal{F})^{\oplus s}|_V$ with $R^q f_*$ of this isomorphism. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be projective with $Y$ Noetherian and $\mathcal{F}$ coherent on $X$, with $f_* \mathcal{F} = 0$. Show that $\mathcal{F} = 0$ is not forced, using $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1_Y$, but that $f_*(\mathcal{F}(m)) = 0$ for all $m \gg 0$ forces $\mathcal{F} = 0$.
2. (A) Let $\mathcal{L}$ be an invertible sheaf on $Y$ and $\pi \colon \mathbb{P}^r_Y \to Y$. Compute $R^q \pi_* (\pi^* \mathcal{L} \otimes \mathcal{O}(n))$ for all $q$ and $n$, and deduce $H^q(\mathbb{P}^r_k \times_k \mathbb{P}^s_k, \mathcal{O}(a, b))$ for $q = 0$ and $b \geq 0$.
3. (A) Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be projective, $Y$ locally Noetherian, and $\mathcal{F}$ coherent on $X$. Show that the support of $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}$ is closed and contained in $f(\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F})$, which is closed because $f$ is proper. Show by an example on $\mathbb{P}^1_Y \to Y$ that the support of $f_* \mathcal{F}$ can be strictly smaller than $f(\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F})$.
4. (B) Let $j \colon U = \mathbb{A}^2_k \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{A}^2_k$ be the inclusion and $\mathcal{E} = k(0)$ the skyscraper sheaf at the origin, a coherent sheaf that is not locally free. Show that $j^* \mathcal{E} = 0$, that $j_* \mathcal{O}_U \otimes \mathcal{E} \cong k(0)$, and that $R^1 j_* \mathcal{O}_U \otimes \mathcal{E} = 0$, using Example P4.4.5. So the natural map of Proposition P4.4.10 is not an isomorphism for $q = 0$ and is one for $q = 1$.
5. (B) Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be projective, $Y$ Noetherian, and $0 \to \mathcal{F}' \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}'' \to 0$ exact with coherent terms. Show that $0 \to f_* \mathcal{F}'(m) \to f_* \mathcal{F}(m) \to f_* \mathcal{F}''(m) \to 0$ is exact for $m \gg 0$, and deduce that every coherent $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ is a quotient of $f^* \mathcal{G} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(-m)$ for some coherent $\mathcal{G}$ on $Y$ and some $m$.
6. (C) Let $Y$ be Noetherian and $f \colon X \to Y$ projective. Show that $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is $f$-ample in the sense that for every coherent $\mathcal{F}$ and every affine open $V \subseteq Y$, $\mathcal{F}(m)|_{f^{-1}V}$ is generated by global sections for $m \gg 0$. Conversely, let $\mathcal{L}$ be invertible on $X$ with $R^1 f_* (\mathcal{I} \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}) = 0$ for every coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$ and $m \gg 0$. Adapt the proof of Theorem P4.2.10 to show that $\mathcal{L}|_{f^{-1}V}$ is ample for every affine $V$.

### Notes and history

Theorem P4.4.8 is Hartshorne III.8.8 and EGA III.3.2.1, where it is proved for proper morphisms by Chow's lemma and dévissage. The projective case suffices for the rest of this volume. Theorem P4.4.9 is EGA III.2.2.1, and the projection formula is Hartshorne Exercise III.8.3. The literature state is theorem. The minimal model program works with $f$-ample and $f$-nef divisors for a contraction $f$, and Theorem P4.4.9 is the cohomological meaning of relative ampleness. The relative Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem has the form $R^q f_* \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) = 0$ for $q > 0$ and $L$ that is $f$-nef and $f$-big, a sharp version of part 1 of Theorem P4.4.9 for adjoint bundles.
