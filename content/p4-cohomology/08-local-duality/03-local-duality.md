## Matlis duality and local duality

### Statement

Throughout, $(A, \mathfrak{m}, k)$ is a Noetherian local ring. An extension of modules $L \subseteq M$ is *essential* if every nonzero submodule of $M$ meets $L$ in a nonzero element.

**Proposition P4.8.18 (the injective hull of the residue field).**

1. There is an injective $A$-module $E = E_A(k)$ containing $k$ as an essential submodule. If $E'$ is another such module, there is an isomorphism $E \cong E'$ that is the identity on $k$.
2. Every element of $E$ is killed by a power of $\mathfrak{m}$, and $\operatorname{Soc}(E) = (0 :_E \mathfrak{m}) = k$.
3. For an ideal $I$ of $A$, $(0 :_E I) = \operatorname{Hom}_A(A/I, E)$ is the injective hull $E_{A/I}(k)$ of $k$ over $A/I$.
4. The *Matlis dual* $N^\vee = \operatorname{Hom}_A(N, E)$ is an exact functor, and $N^\vee = 0$ only for $N = 0$. If $N$ has finite length, then $\ell(N^\vee) = \ell(N)$ and the evaluation map $N \to N^{\vee\vee}$ is an isomorphism.
5. The submodules $E_n = (0 :_E \mathfrak{m}^n)$ have finite length, $E = \bigcup_n E_n$, and the action of $A$ on $E$ extends to an isomorphism $\hat{A} \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(E, E)$. The module $E$ is Artinian.

Lean: Mathlib has `Module.Injective` and Baer's criterion `Module.Baer.injective`. Injective hulls and Matlis duality are not in Mathlib.

**Theorem P4.8.19 (Matlis duality).** Let $A$ be complete.

1. If $M$ is finitely generated, then $M^\vee$ is Artinian and $M \to M^{\vee\vee}$ is an isomorphism.
2. If $N$ is Artinian, then $N^\vee$ is finitely generated and $N \to N^{\vee\vee}$ is an isomorphism.

So $N \mapsto N^\vee$ is an anti-equivalence between finitely generated and Artinian $A$-modules, which exchanges $A$ and $E$ and is the identity on modules of finite length up to isomorphism of lengths.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

**Theorem P4.8.20 (local duality).** Let $(R, \mathfrak{n}, k)$ be a regular local ring of dimension $N$ and $E = E_R(k)$.

1. $H^i_\mathfrak{n}(R) = 0$ for $i \neq N$, and $H^N_\mathfrak{n}(R) \cong E$.
2. For every finitely generated $R$-module $M$ and every $i$ there is an isomorphism, natural in $M$,
$$H^i_\mathfrak{n}(M) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R), E).$$
3. If $R$ is complete, then $\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R) \cong H^i_\mathfrak{n}(M)^\vee$.

Lean: not in Mathlib. Mathlib has local cohomology, `localCohomology`, as a colimit of Ext modules, without this computation.

**Corollary P4.8.21 (local cohomology over a quotient of a regular ring).** Let $A = R/J$ with $R$ as in Theorem P4.8.20, $\mathfrak{m} = \mathfrak{n}/J$, and $E_A = (0 :_E J)$. Let $M$ be a finitely generated $A$-module of dimension $d$.

1. $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R), E_A)$ for every $i$.
2. $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M)$ is Artinian for every $i$. This holds for every Noetherian local ring $A$, not only for quotients of regular ones.
3. $H^d_\mathfrak{m}(M) \neq 0$. This also holds for every Noetherian local ring, which completes the proof of Theorem P4.8.10.
4. $M$ is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(M, R) = 0$ for every $j \neq N - d$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

**Definition P4.8.22 (canonical module).** Let $A = R/J$ be as in Corollary P4.8.21, of dimension $d$. The *canonical module* of $A$ is the finitely generated $A$-module
$$K_A = \operatorname{Ext}^{N-d}_R(A, R).$$

**Proposition P4.8.23 (the canonical module).** Let $A = R/J$ be as in Definition P4.8.22.

1. $H^d_\mathfrak{m}(A) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(K_A, E_A)$, and $K_A \otimes_A \hat{A} \cong H^d_\mathfrak{m}(A)^\vee$. So $K_A \otimes_A \hat{A}$ depends only on $A$, not on the choice of $R$ and $J$.
2. Let $X \subseteq P = \mathbb{P}^m_k$ be a closed subscheme, equidimensional of dimension $n$, with dualizing sheaf $\omega^\circ_X = \mathcal{E}xt^{m-n}_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ (Theorem P4.7.17). For a closed point $x \in X$, with $A = \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ written as a quotient of $R = \mathcal{O}_{P,x}$, there is an isomorphism $K_A \cong \omega^\circ_{X,x}$.
3. If $J$ is generated by an $R$-regular sequence $f_1, \dots, f_c$, then $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R) = 0$ for $j \neq c$ and $K_A \cong A$. In particular a complete intersection has $H^d_\mathfrak{m}(A) \cong E_A$.

Lean: not in Mathlib.

Part 4 of Corollary P4.8.21 with $M = A$ is the local form of the equivalence of parts 1 and 2 in Theorem P4.7.18: by part 2 of Proposition P4.8.23 and Proposition P4.7.3, the stalk of $\mathcal{E}xt^j_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ at a closed point $x$ is $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R)$, up to the invertible factor $\omega_{P,x}$. If $X$ is a normal projective variety over a perfect field, Proposition P4.7.22 identifies $\omega^\circ_X$ with the canonical sheaf $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}_X(K_X)$, so $K_A$ is the canonical module $\omega_A$ of Definition P1.12.25.

### Proof in words

The module $E$ is the smallest injective module containing $k$. Its dual theory is that of finite length modules, where $\operatorname{Hom}(-, E)$ behaves like the dual of a vector space, and the colimit of these finite pieces gives $\operatorname{Hom}(E, E) = \hat{A}$. Over a regular local ring $R$, the local cohomology $H^N_\mathfrak{n}(R)$ is $\mathfrak{n}$-torsion, it is injective because $R$ has global dimension $N$, and its socle is $\operatorname{Ext}^N_R(k, R) = k$. So it is $E$. The local Čech complex of $R$ is then a flat resolution of $E[-N]$, and tensoring it with a finite free resolution of $M$ turns local cohomology into $E \otimes_R F_\bullet = \operatorname{Hom}_R(F_\bullet^*, E)$, whose cohomology is the Matlis dual of $\operatorname{Ext}^*_R(M, R)$. The nonvanishing in degree $\dim M$ comes from localizing at a prime of maximal dimension in the support, where local duality in degree $0$ forces an Ext module to be nonzero.

### Examples

**Example P4.8.24 (the power series ring in one variable).** Let $R = k[[x]]$, so $N = 1$. The local Čech complex is $R \to R_x = k((x))$, and $H^1_{(x)}(R) = k((x))/k[[x]]$, with basis the classes of $x^{-1}, x^{-2}, \dots$. The element $x^{-1}$ spans the socle, every nonzero submodule contains it, and $H^1_{(x)}(R) \cong E$ as Theorem P4.8.20 predicts. The submodule $E_n = (0 :_E x^n)$ is spanned by $x^{-1}, \dots, x^{-n}$, and it is the Matlis dual of $R/(x^n)$. An endomorphism of $E$ is multiplication by a power series, which is part 5 of Proposition P4.8.18. For $M = R/(x^n)$, local duality says $H^0_{(x)}(M) = M$ is dual to $\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(M, R) \cong R/(x^n)$, which is part 4 of the same proposition.

**Example P4.8.25 (two planes meeting in a point).** Let $R = k[[x, y, z, w]]$ and $A = R/((x, y) \cap (z, w))$, the completion of the local ring of Example P4.8.14, with $N = 4$ and $d = 2$. Apply $\operatorname{Ext}_R(-, R)$ to the exact sequence $0 \to A \to R/(z, w) \oplus R/(x, y) \to k \to 0$. By part 3 of Proposition P4.8.23, $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(R/(z, w), R)$ is $R/(z, w)$ for $j = 2$ and zero otherwise, and the same holds for $R/(x, y)$ and, with $j = 4$, for $k = R/(x, y, z, w)$. The long exact sequence gives
$$K_A = \operatorname{Ext}^2_R(A, R) \cong R/(z, w) \oplus R/(x, y), \qquad \operatorname{Ext}^3_R(A, R) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^4_R(k, R) = k,$$
and $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R) = 0$ for the other $j$. By part 4 of Corollary P4.8.21, $A$ is not Cohen-Macaulay, and by local duality $H^1_\mathfrak{m}(A) \cong k^\vee = k$, as in Example P4.8.14. The canonical module is the canonical module of the normalization $R/(z, w) \times R/(x, y)$, and it is not cyclic. The extra module $\operatorname{Ext}^3_R(A, R) = k$ is the stalk of the sheaf $\mathcal{E}xt^3_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ at the singular point in Example P4.7.25.

**Example P4.8.26 (the cusp).** Let $A = k[[t^2, t^3]] \cong k[[x, y]]/(y^2 - x^3)$. It is a complete intersection of dimension $1$, so $K_A \cong A$ and $H^1_\mathfrak{m}(A) \cong E_A$ by part 3 of Proposition P4.8.23. The local Čech complex for the parameter $x = t^2$ gives $H^1_\mathfrak{m}(A) = A_x/A = k((t))/k[[t^2, t^3]]$, which contains the class of $t$, killed by $\mathfrak{m} = (t^2, t^3)$. This class spans the socle of $E_A$. The normalization $k[[t]]$ has canonical module $k[[t]]\,dt$, while $K_A$ is the module of forms $f\,dt/t^2$ with $f \in A$, the module of Rosenlicht differentials of the cusp.

### Proof

*Proposition P4.8.18, part 1.* Embed $k$ in an injective module $J$ (Theorem P2.2.35). The submodules $L \subseteq J$ with $k \subseteq L$ essential form a set ordered by inclusion in which every chain has its union as an upper bound, so by Zorn's lemma there is a maximal one, $E$. Choose by Zorn's lemma a submodule $Q \subseteq J$ maximal with $Q \cap E = 0$. Then $E \to J/Q$ is essential: if $L/Q$ meets the image of $E$ in zero, then $L \cap E \subseteq Q \cap E = 0$, so $L = Q$ by maximality. Since $J$ is injective, the inclusion $E \subseteq J$ extends along $E \to J/Q$ to a map $\varphi \colon J/Q \to J$. Its kernel meets $E$ in zero, so $\varphi$ is injective, and $\varphi(J/Q)$ is an essential extension of $E$, hence of $k$, inside $J$. By maximality $\varphi(J/Q) = E$, so $J \to J/Q \to E$ is a retraction of $E \subseteq J$, and $E$ is a direct summand of $J$, hence injective. If $E'$ is another injective module with $k \subseteq E'$ essential, the inclusion $k \subseteq E'$ extends to $\psi \colon E \to E'$, which is injective because its kernel meets $k$ in zero. Its image is injective, so $E' = \psi(E) \oplus C$, and $C \cap k = 0$ forces $C = 0$.

*Part 2.* If $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Ass}(E)$, then $A/\mathfrak{p} \subseteq E$ meets $k$, and every nonzero element of the domain $A/\mathfrak{p}$ has annihilator $\mathfrak{p}$, while every nonzero element of $k$ has annihilator $\mathfrak{m}$. So $\operatorname{Ass}(E) = \{\mathfrak{m}\}$. For $e \in E$, the cyclic module $Ae$ has $\operatorname{Ass}(Ae) \subseteq \{\mathfrak{m}\}$, so it has finite length and is killed by a power of $\mathfrak{m}$. The socle is a $k$-vector space containing $k$, and a line in it that meets $k$ in zero would contradict essentiality.

*Part 3.* The module $(0 :_E I)$ is an $A/I$-module containing $k$ essentially, since every submodule of it is a submodule of $E$. For an $A/I$-module $N$, $\operatorname{Hom}_{A/I}(N, \operatorname{Hom}_A(A/I, E)) = \operatorname{Hom}_A(N, E)$, which is exact in $N$, so $(0 :_E I)$ is an injective $A/I$-module. Part 1 over $A/I$ identifies it with $E_{A/I}(k)$.

*Part 4.* The functor is exact since $E$ is injective. If $N \neq 0$, choose $0 \neq u \in N$. Then $Au \cong A/I$ with $I \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$, so there is a nonzero map $Au \to k \subseteq E$, which extends to $N$. So $N^\vee \neq 0$. By part 2, $k^\vee = \operatorname{Soc}(E) = k$, and the evaluation map $k \to k^{\vee\vee}$ is nonzero, hence an isomorphism. For $N$ of finite length, choose $0 \to N' \to N \to k \to 0$ and use induction on the length: exactness gives $\ell(N^\vee) = \ell(N'^\vee) + 1$, and the five lemma applied to the evaluation maps gives $N \cong N^{\vee\vee}$.

*Part 5.* Let $y_1, \dots, y_s$ generate $\mathfrak{m}^j$. The map $(0 :_E \mathfrak{m}^{j+1}) \to E^s$, $u \mapsto (y_1 u, \dots, y_s u)$, lands in $\operatorname{Soc}(E)^s = k^s$ and has kernel $(0 :_E \mathfrak{m}^j)$. By induction on $j$, each $E_n$ has finite length. By part 2, $E = \bigcup_n E_n$. Now $E_n = (A/\mathfrak{m}^n)^\vee$, so part 4 gives $\operatorname{Hom}_A(E_n, E) = (A/\mathfrak{m}^n)^{\vee\vee} = A/\mathfrak{m}^n$, where $a$ corresponds to multiplication by $a$. These isomorphisms are compatible with the restrictions $\operatorname{Hom}_A(E_{n+1}, E) \to \operatorname{Hom}_A(E_n, E)$ and the projections $A/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1} \to A/\mathfrak{m}^n$, by naturality of the evaluation map. Taking the limit, $\operatorname{Hom}_A(E, E) = \varprojlim_n \operatorname{Hom}_A(E_n, E) = \varprojlim_n A/\mathfrak{m}^n = \hat{A}$. For a descending chain $N_1 \supseteq N_2 \supseteq \dots$ of submodules of $E$, the duals $(E/N_i)^\vee \subseteq E^\vee = \hat{A}$ are ideals of $\hat{A}$, stable under composition with endomorphisms of $E$, and they form an ascending chain. Since $\hat{A}$ is Noetherian (Theorem P1.6.16), the chain stabilizes, and by exactness $(N_i/N_{i+1})^\vee = 0$ for large $i$, so $N_i = N_{i+1}$.

*Theorem P4.8.19.* By part 5 of Proposition P4.8.18, the map $A \to A^{\vee\vee} = \operatorname{Hom}_A(E, E)$ is the isomorphism $A = \hat{A} \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(E, E)$, and $E \to E^{\vee\vee} = \operatorname{Hom}_A(A, E) = E$ is the identity. For finitely generated $M$, choose a presentation $A^b \to A^a \to M \to 0$. Dualizing gives $0 \to M^\vee \to E^a \to E^b$, so $M^\vee$ is a submodule of the Artinian module $E^a$, hence Artinian. The double dual is exact, so the evaluation maps on the presentation form a map of exact sequences that is an isomorphism on $A^b$ and $A^a$, and the five lemma gives $M \cong M^{\vee\vee}$.

Let $N$ be Artinian. Each $u \in N$ is killed by a power of $\mathfrak{m}$: the chain $\mathfrak{m}^j u$ stabilizes, and $\mathfrak{m}^j u = \mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathfrak{m}^j u$ gives $\mathfrak{m}^j u = 0$ by Nakayama's lemma (Lemma P1.1.25). So, as in the proof of Proposition P1.10.25, every nonzero submodule of $N$ meets $\operatorname{Soc}(N)$, which is an Artinian $k$-vector space, of finite dimension $s$. The inclusion $\operatorname{Soc}(N) = k^s \subseteq E^s$ extends to $N \to E^s$, which is injective because its kernel meets the socle in zero. The cokernel $E^s/N$ is again Artinian, so it embeds in some $E^{s'}$ in the same way, which gives an exact sequence $0 \to N \to E^s \to E^{s'}$. Dualizing gives $A^{s'} \to A^s \to N^\vee \to 0$, so $N^\vee$ is finitely generated, and the five lemma applied to the evaluation maps gives $N \cong N^{\vee\vee}$.

*Theorem P4.8.20, part 1.* The ring $R$ is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension $N$ (Corollary P1.10.15), so $H^i_\mathfrak{n}(R) = 0$ for $i \neq N$ by part 1 of Theorem P4.8.10. Let $H = H^N_\mathfrak{n}(R)$ and choose an injective resolution $R \to I^\bullet$. By Lemma P4.8.3, $\Gamma_\mathfrak{n}(I^\bullet)$ is a complex of injective modules, and its only cohomology is $H$ in degree $N$. In degrees below $N$ it is exact and consists of injectives, so it splits off, and what remains in degrees $\geq N$ is an injective resolution of $H$. Since $\operatorname{Hom}_R(L, \Gamma_\mathfrak{n}(I^j)) = \operatorname{Hom}_R(L, I^j)$ for an $\mathfrak{n}$-torsion module $L$, this gives
$$\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(L, H) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{j+N}_R(L, R) \quad \text{for } \mathfrak{n}\text{-torsion } L.$$
Let $x_1, \dots, x_N$ be a regular system of parameters. It is an $R$-regular sequence (Proposition P1.9.4), so the Koszul complex is a free resolution of $k$ (Lemma P1.9.20). It is isomorphic to its own dual, so $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(k, R)$ is $k$ for $j = N$ and zero otherwise. Combined with the displayed isomorphism, $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(L, R) = 0$ for $j < N$ and every $\mathfrak{n}$-torsion $L$, and $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(L, R) = 0$ for $j > N$ and every finitely generated $L$, since $R$ has global dimension $N$ (Theorem P1.9.21). So on modules of finite length the functor $L \mapsto \operatorname{Ext}^N_R(L, R)$ is exact, and it takes $k$ to $k$. By induction on the length, $\ell(\operatorname{Ext}^N_R(L, R)) = \ell(L)$ for every $L$ of finite length.

With $L = k$ and $j = 0$, $\operatorname{Soc}(H) = \operatorname{Hom}_R(k, H) \cong k$. Every nonzero submodule of the $\mathfrak{n}$-torsion module $H$ meets its socle, so $k \subseteq H$ is essential. Extend $k \subseteq E$ to a map $\iota \colon H \to E$, using that $E$ is injective. Its kernel meets $k$ in zero, so $\iota$ is injective. It maps $(0 :_H \mathfrak{n}^t)$ into $E_t = (0 :_E \mathfrak{n}^t)$. With $L = R/\mathfrak{n}^t$ and $j = 0$, the first module is $\operatorname{Hom}_R(R/\mathfrak{n}^t, H) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^N_R(R/\mathfrak{n}^t, R)$, of length $\ell(R/\mathfrak{n}^t)$, and the second is $(R/\mathfrak{n}^t)^\vee$, of the same length by part 4 of Proposition P4.8.18. So $\iota$ maps $(0 :_H \mathfrak{n}^t)$ onto $E_t$ for every $t$. Both modules are the unions of these pieces, so $\iota \colon H \to E$ is an isomorphism.

*Part 2.* Let $\check{C} = \check{C}(x_1, \dots, x_N; R)$ be the local Čech complex, $0 \to R \to \bigoplus_i R_{x_i} \to \dots \to R_{x_1 \cdots x_N} \to 0$ in degrees $0$ to $N$. Its terms are flat, and by part 1 of Theorem P4.8.4 and part 1 above it has cohomology $E$ in degree $N$ and nowhere else. Choose a free resolution $0 \to F_N \to \dots \to F_0 \to M \to 0$ of length at most $N$ by finitely generated free modules (Theorem P1.9.21). The double complex $\check{C}^p \otimes_R F_{N-q}$, with $0 \leq p, q \leq N$, lies in the first quadrant. For fixed $p$, the column $\check{C}^p \otimes F_\bullet$ is a resolution of $\check{C}^p \otimes M$, because $\check{C}^p$ is flat. For fixed $q$, the row $\check{C}^\bullet \otimes F_{N-q}$ has cohomology $E \otimes F_{N-q}$ in degree $N$ only, because $F_{N-q}$ is free. By Theorem P2.4.15, both spectral sequences degenerate, and the cohomology of the total complex is
$$H^i(\check{C} \otimes_R M) = H^i_\mathfrak{n}(M) \quad \text{and} \quad H_{N-i}(E \otimes_R F_\bullet).$$
For a free module $F$ of finite rank with dual $F^* = \operatorname{Hom}_R(F, R)$, $E \otimes_R F \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(F^*, E)$, naturally in $F$. So $E \otimes_R F_\bullet = \operatorname{Hom}_R(F^*_\bullet, E)$, and the complex $F^*_\bullet$ computes $\operatorname{Ext}^\bullet_R(M, R)$. The functor $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-, E)$ is exact, so it commutes with cohomology, and $H_{N-i}(E \otimes_R F_\bullet) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R), E)$. The isomorphism does not depend on the resolution, since two resolutions are homotopy equivalent, and it is natural in $M$.

*Part 3.* The module $\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R)$ is finitely generated, so by part 1 of Theorem P4.8.19 it is the dual of its dual, which is $H^i_\mathfrak{n}(M)^\vee$ by part 2.

*Corollary P4.8.21, part 1.* By part 4 of Theorem P4.8.4, $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M) \cong H^i_\mathfrak{n}(M)$. The module $\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R)$ is killed by $J$, so every map from it to $E$ lands in $(0 :_E J) = E_A$, by part 3 of Proposition P4.8.18. Apply Theorem P4.8.20.

*Part 2.* For $A = R/J$, choose a surjection $R^a \to \operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R)$. Its dual embeds $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M)$ in $E^a$, which is Artinian by part 5 of Proposition P4.8.18. For a general Noetherian local ring $A$, part 5 of Theorem P4.8.4 gives $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M) \cong H^i_{\hat{\mathfrak{m}}}(\hat{M})$. Every element of this module is killed by some $\mathfrak{m}^n$, and $\hat{A}/\hat{\mathfrak{m}}^n = A/\mathfrak{m}^n$, so its $A$-submodules and its $\hat{A}$-submodules are the same. By the Cohen structure theorem (Theorem P1.6.27), $\hat{A}$ is a quotient of a power series ring over a field or over a complete discrete valuation ring, which is a regular local ring, so the first case applies to $\hat{A}$.

*Part 3.* First let $A = R/J$. Choose $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Supp} M$ with $\dim R/\mathfrak{p} = d$, and let $h = \operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p}$. Then $\mathfrak{p}$ is minimal in $\operatorname{Supp} M$, so $M_\mathfrak{p}$ is a nonzero $R_\mathfrak{p}$-module of finite length. The ring $R_\mathfrak{p}$ is regular local of dimension $h$ (Corollary P1.9.22). Theorem P4.8.20 over $R_\mathfrak{p}$ with $i = 0$ gives
$$0 \neq M_\mathfrak{p} = H^0_{\mathfrak{p}R_\mathfrak{p}}(M_\mathfrak{p}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Ext}^h_{R_\mathfrak{p}}(M_\mathfrak{p}, R_\mathfrak{p}), E_{R_\mathfrak{p}}),$$
so $\operatorname{Ext}^h_R(M, R)_\mathfrak{p} \neq 0$, and $\operatorname{Ext}^h_R(M, R) \neq 0$. By part 1 and part 4 of Proposition P4.8.18, $H^{N-h}_\mathfrak{m}(M) \neq 0$, so $N - h \leq d$ by part 1 of Theorem P4.8.10. On the other hand $h + \dim R/\mathfrak{p} \leq \dim R$ gives $N - h \geq d$. So $N - h = d$ and $H^d_\mathfrak{m}(M) \neq 0$. For a general Noetherian local ring, $H^d_\mathfrak{m}(M) \cong H^d_{\hat{\mathfrak{m}}}(\hat{M})$, and $\dim \hat{M} = d$ because $\ell(M/\mathfrak{m}^n M) = \ell(\hat{M}/\hat{\mathfrak{m}}^n \hat{M})$ and the dimension is the degree of this function (Theorem P1.5.35). As in part 2, $\hat{A}$ is a quotient of a regular local ring.

*Part 4.* By part 1 and part 4 of Proposition P4.8.18, $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M) = 0$ if and only if $\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R) = 0$. Apply Corollary P4.8.11.

*Proposition P4.8.23, part 1.* The first isomorphism is part 1 of Corollary P4.8.21 with $M = A$ and $i = d$. The ring $\hat{R}$ is regular (Proposition P1.9.5) and flat over $R$, and $\hat{A} = \hat{R}/J\hat{R}$. Ext of finitely generated modules commutes with flat base change, so $K_A \otimes_A \hat{A} = \operatorname{Ext}^{N-d}_{\hat{R}}(\hat{A}, \hat{R})$, which is $H^d_{\hat{\mathfrak{m}}}(\hat{A})^\vee = H^d_\mathfrak{m}(A)^\vee$ by part 3 of Theorem P4.8.20 and part 5 of Theorem P4.8.4.

*Part 2.* The ring $R = \mathcal{O}_{P,x}$ is regular of dimension $m$, and $A = \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ has dimension $n$, since $X$ is equidimensional and $x$ is closed (Theorem P3.7.4). By Proposition P4.7.3, the stalk of $\mathcal{E}xt^{m-n}_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ at $x$ is $\operatorname{Ext}^{m-n}_R(A, \omega_{P,x})$, and $\omega_{P,x} \cong R$ since $\omega_P$ is invertible. This is $K_A$.

*Part 3.* By Lemma P1.9.20, the Koszul complex $K(f_1, \dots, f_c)$ is a free resolution of $A$. Its dual $\operatorname{Hom}_R(K(f), R)$ is isomorphic to $K(f)$ with the degrees reversed, so its cohomology is $A$ in degree $c$ and zero elsewhere. Here $\dim A = N - c$ (Proposition P1.9.4 and Theorem P1.5.35), so $K_A = \operatorname{Ext}^c_R(A, R) \cong A$, and part 1 gives $H^d_\mathfrak{m}(A) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(A, E_A) = E_A$.

### Exercises

3. (B) Let $R = k[[x_1, \dots, x_N]]$. Show that $E_R(k)$ is the $k$-vector space with basis the monomials $x_1^{-a_1} \cdots x_N^{-a_N}$, $a_i \geq 1$, on which $x_j$ acts by multiplication followed by deleting monomials with an exponent $\geq 0$. Compare with the Čech description of $H^N_\mathfrak{n}(R)$.
4. (B) Let $A$ be the completed local ring at the vertex of the cone over two skew lines in $\mathbb{P}^3_k$, as in Exercise 4 of Section 8.2, written as a quotient of $R = k[[x, y, z, w]]$. Compute $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(A, R)$ for all $j$, and identify $K_A$.
5. (B) Let $M$ be a finitely generated module over a regular local ring $R$ of dimension $N$. Show that $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(M, R) = 0$ for $j < N - \dim M$ and for $j > N - \operatorname{depth} M$, and that it is nonzero for $j = N - \dim M$ and $j = N - \operatorname{depth} M$.
6. (C) Let $A = R/J$ be Cohen-Macaulay of dimension $d$. Show that a minimal free resolution of $A$ over $R$ has length $N - d$ (Theorem P1.10.8), that its dual is a free resolution of $K_A$, and deduce that $K_A$ is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension $d$ with $\operatorname{Ext}^{N-d}_R(K_A, R) \cong A$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_A(K_A, K_A) \cong A$.

### Lean

Mathlib has injective modules, `Module.Injective`, Baer's criterion, `Module.Baer.injective`, and local cohomology, `localCohomology`, defined as a colimit of Ext modules. Injective hulls, Matlis duality and local duality are not in Mathlib. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Matlis proved the duality of Theorem P4.8.19 in *Injective modules over Noetherian rings* (1958), together with the structure theory of injective modules over Noetherian rings. Local duality is due to Grothendieck, in the 1961 seminar written up as *Local cohomology* (1967), where it is derived from duality on projective space; the proof here, by the Čech complex and a free resolution, follows Hartshorne III.7 and the presentation in Bruns and Herzog. The canonical module of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring was studied by Herzog and Kunz (1971), who proved that $A$ is Gorenstein if and only if $K_A \cong A$. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program, the canonical module $K_A$ is the local form of $\omega_X$, and local duality is the tool that converts the vanishing of Ext sheaves into the vanishing of local cohomology, which is how the Cohen-Macaulay property of klt singularities is proved in Volume 4 from the Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing theorem.
