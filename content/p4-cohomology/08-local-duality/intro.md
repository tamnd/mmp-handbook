# Ext sheaves and local duality

Chapter 7 proved Serre duality on a projective scheme. Its answer to the question of when full duality holds was local: at every closed point, the sheaves $\mathcal{E}xt^j_P(\mathcal{O}_X, \omega_P)$ must vanish outside one degree, which is the Cohen-Macaulay condition on the local rings of $X$. This chapter develops the local theory behind that answer. The local invariant is the local cohomology $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M)$ of a module over a Noetherian local ring $(A, \mathfrak{m})$. It sees the depth and the dimension of $M$, and when $A$ is a quotient of a regular local ring $R$ it is dual to the modules $\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(M, R)$. This is Grothendieck's local duality theorem.

Section 8.1 defines cohomology with supports $H^i_Z(X, \mathcal{F})$ for a closed subset $Z$ of a space, the local cohomology modules $H^i_I(M)$ of a module, and the local cohomology sheaves $\mathcal{H}^i_Z(\mathcal{F})$. It computes $H^i_I(M)$ by a Čech complex and as the colimit of $\operatorname{Ext}^i_A(A/I^n, M)$, and it relates the local cohomology at the vertex of a cone to the cohomology of the twists $\mathcal{F}(n)$ on the base. Section 8.2 proves Grothendieck's theorem that the depth of $M$ is the first degree and the dimension of $M$ the last degree in which $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M)$ is nonzero. So $M$ is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if its local cohomology lives in one degree, and this gives a criterion for the vertex of a cone to be Cohen-Macaulay in terms of the cohomology of the base. Section 8.3 constructs the injective hull $E$ of the residue field, proves Matlis duality, and proves local duality $H^i_\mathfrak{m}(M) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(\operatorname{Ext}^{N-i}_R(M, R), E)$ over a quotient of a regular local ring of dimension $N$. The nonvanishing of $H^{\dim M}_\mathfrak{m}(M)$ follows, and so does the local form of Theorem P4.7.18.

In the minimal model program these statements are used every time a singularity is tested for the Cohen-Macaulay property, and that test is constant: rational singularities are Cohen-Macaulay (Chapter 9), log terminal singularities are rational, and the cone over an abelian surface (Example P4.8.16) is the standard log canonical singularity that fails it. The canonical module $K_A = \operatorname{Ext}^{N-d}_R(A, R)$ of Section 8.3 is the stalk of the dualizing sheaf $\omega^\circ_X$ of Chapter 7, and Chapter 9 places both in the dualizing complex.

The sources are Hartshorne III.2, III.3 and III.7 with their exercises, Vakil Chapter 30, Görtz-Wedhorn II, and the Stacks project chapters on local cohomology and dualizing complexes. Section P2.8.4 stated local duality in the language of derived categories, and Section 8.3 proves the form needed there.
