# Formal functions, Zariski's main theorem and Stein factorization

Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a projective morphism and $y \in Y$. The higher direct images $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}$ near $y$ are not determined by the fibre $X_y$ alone, but they are determined by the infinitesimal neighbourhoods of the fibre, the closed subschemes $X_n$ cut out by the powers of the maximal ideal of $y$. This is Zariski's theorem on formal functions, and it is the main tool for studying a morphism along one of its fibres. This chapter proves it and draws its classical consequences: the vanishing of $R^q f_*$ above the dimension of the fibres, the connectedness of the fibres of a morphism with $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y$, Zariski's main theorem for birational morphisms to normal schemes, and the Stein factorization of a projective morphism into a morphism with connected fibres and a finite morphism.

Section 6.1 proves the theorem on formal functions with the Rees algebra and the Artin-Rees argument, and computes $R^1 \pi_* \mathcal{O}$ for the blow-up of a point of the plane and for the cone over a plane cubic. Section 6.2 proves Zariski's connectedness theorem and Zariski's main theorem, in the form that a projective birational morphism to a normal scheme has connected fibres and is an isomorphism over the points with finite fibres. Section 6.3 constructs the Stein factorization and defines contractions.

In the minimal model program every step is a contraction: a projective morphism $f$ with $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y$. The cone and contraction theorem produces contractions, the Stein factorization turns any projective morphism into one, and Zariski's main theorem separates divisorial contractions, small contractions and fibre spaces by the dimension of their fibres. The theorem on formal functions is how the higher direct images $R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ are computed in examples, and how rational singularities are recognized. The running examples are the blow-up of a point of the plane, the cone over an elliptic curve and the small contraction of the quadric cone in $\mathbb{A}^4$.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Sections III.11 and II.4, Vakil, Chapters 28 and 29, and Görtz-Wedhorn II, with details from the Stacks project. It uses Chapters 2, 4 and 5, the completion and the Artin-Rees lemma of P1 Ch. 5 and Ch. 6, normality and finite morphisms from P3 Ch. 7, and blow-ups from P3 Ch. 11.
