# Cohomology and duality

This volume computes the cohomology of coherent sheaves and proves the duality theorems that the minimal model program uses. Volume P2 defined sheaf cohomology as a derived functor and gave the tools to compute it: flasque resolutions, Čech complexes and Leray's theorem. Volume P3 defined schemes and quasi-coherent sheaves. Here the two meet. The vanishing theorems of Volume 5, the base point free theorem and the cone theorem of Volume 7, and the construction of flips in Volume 8 are all statements about the groups $H^i(X, \mathcal{F})$ and $R^i f_* \mathcal{F}$ of coherent sheaves, and they are proved with the results of this volume.

The volume has nine chapters in three groups.

- **Cohomology on schemes, Chapters 1 to 3.** Chapter 1 proves that a quasi-coherent sheaf on an affine scheme has no higher cohomology, so that Čech complexes of affine covers compute the cohomology of quasi-coherent sheaves on a separated scheme. It proves Serre's criterion for a scheme to be affine and the flat base change of cohomology over an affine base. Chapter 2 computes the cohomology of the sheaves $\mathcal{O}(n)$ on projective space and deduces the finiteness of cohomology of coherent sheaves on projective schemes and Serre vanishing. Chapter 3 defines the Euler characteristic, proves that it is additive, and identifies the Hilbert polynomial with $n \mapsto \chi(\mathcal{F}(n))$.
- **Cohomology in families, Chapters 4 to 6.** Chapter 4 treats higher direct images: their quasi-coherence, their computation over an affine base, the Leray spectral sequence and the projection formula. Chapter 5 proves flat base change for higher direct images, the theorem on cohomology and base change, semicontinuity and Grauert's theorem. Chapter 6 proves the theorem on formal functions and deduces Zariski's main theorem and the Stein factorization. These are the tools behind the contractions of the program: a contraction is a proper morphism $f$ with $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z$, and its fibres are connected.
- **Duality, Chapters 7 to 9.** Chapter 7 proves Serre duality on projective space and on Cohen-Macaulay projective schemes, with the canonical sheaf as the dualizing sheaf. Chapter 8 treats Ext sheaves, local cohomology, depth and local duality. Chapter 9 introduces dualizing complexes and the functor $f^!$ of Grothendieck duality, defines rational singularities, and proves Kempf's criterion: a variety with a resolution $f \colon Y \to X$ has rational singularities if and only if it is Cohen-Macaulay and $f_* \omega_Y = \omega_X$.

The spine is Hartshorne's *Algebraic geometry*, Chapter III (Har77), Vakil's *The rising sea* (Vak25), Görtz-Wedhorn, *Algebraic geometry II* (GW23), and the Stacks project (Stacks). The order follows Hartshorne. The proofs of the first two chapters use Čech complexes, as in Serre's paper *Faisceaux algébriques cohérents*, and the later chapters use the derived functor language of P2. Each section lists in `reads_with` the places of the spine texts that read in parallel.

The volume needs P1 for commutative algebra, P2 Ch. 3, 4, 7 and 8 for derived functors, spectral sequences, sheaf cohomology and derived categories, and P3 for schemes and quasi-coherent sheaves. Rings are commutative with $1$, and $k$ is a field. The text says when $k$ must be algebraically closed.
