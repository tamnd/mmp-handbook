# Cohomology of quasi-coherent sheaves

Sheaf cohomology is defined by injective resolutions, and injective sheaves are too large to write down. This chapter proves the theorem that makes the cohomology of quasi-coherent sheaves computable: on an affine scheme, a quasi-coherent sheaf has no higher cohomology. With Leray's theorem of P2 Ch. 7, the cohomology of a quasi-coherent sheaf on a separated scheme is then the cohomology of the Čech complex of any affine cover. This is a complex of modules over the rings of the affine pieces, built from localizations, and in the examples of the handbook it is a finite computation.

Section 1.1 defines the Čech complex of a module for a family of elements $f_1, \dots, f_r$ of a ring, the Čech complex of the sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ for the cover by the basic open sets $D(f_i)$. It proves that this complex is exact when the $f_i$ generate the unit ideal. The proof localizes at one $f_j$, where the complex becomes contractible. Section 1.2 proves Cartan's criterion, which turns the vanishing of Čech cohomology for enough covers into the vanishing of sheaf cohomology. It deduces Serre's vanishing theorem on affine schemes, the comparison of Čech cohomology and sheaf cohomology on separated schemes, the bound on cohomological dimension given by the number of affine pieces, and the fact that an extension of two quasi-coherent sheaves is quasi-coherent. Section 1.3 proves Serre's criterion: a Noetherian scheme is affine if and only if the first cohomology of every coherent ideal sheaf vanishes. It also proves that cohomology commutes with flat base change over an affine base, with direct sums, and with pushforward along affine morphisms. Section 1.4 computes: the Mayer-Vietoris sequence for two affine pieces, the punctured plane, the affine line with a doubled origin, and a sheaf of modules that is not quasi-coherent and has nonzero cohomology on the affine line.

In the minimal model program the results of this chapter are used without comment. The vanishing on affine schemes is the reason why statements about $R^i f_* \mathcal{F}$ are local on the base. Serre's criterion recognizes affine morphisms in the construction of flips, and the comparison with Čech cohomology is how the cohomology groups in the examples of the later volumes are computed.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Sections III.3 and III.4, Vakil, Chapter 18, and the chapter of Görtz-Wedhorn II on the cohomology of quasi-coherent modules, with details from the Stacks project, chapter Cohomology of schemes. It uses P2 Ch. 7, the quasi-coherent sheaves of P3 Ch. 5 and the localization of P1 Ch. 2.
