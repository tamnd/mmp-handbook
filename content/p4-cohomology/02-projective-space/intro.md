# Cohomology of projective space

Chapter 1 made the cohomology of a quasi-coherent sheaf on a separated scheme computable by Čech complexes of affine covers. This chapter carries out the computation that the rest of the handbook rests on: the cohomology of the line bundles $\mathcal{O}(n)$ on projective space $\mathbb{P}^r_A$ over an arbitrary ring. The answer is Serre's. The sheaf $\mathcal{O}(n)$ has cohomology only in degrees $0$ and $r$, the module $H^0$ is the space of forms of degree $n$, the module $H^r$ is spanned by the inverse monomials of degree $n$, and the two are dual to each other under multiplication into $H^r(\mathcal{O}(-r-1)) \cong A$.

Section 2.1 proves this by grading the Čech complex of the standard cover by monomials, so that the complex splits into pieces indexed by the exponent vectors, each of which is the cochain complex of a simplex and is either contractible or concentrated in one degree. It deduces the dimension formulas over a field and computes the cohomology of hypersurfaces, which already contains the genus formula for plane curves. Section 2.2 proves Serre's theorems on a projective scheme over a Noetherian ring: the cohomology of a coherent sheaf is finitely generated, and it vanishes in positive degrees after a sufficiently large twist. It then proves Serre's cohomological criterion for ampleness on a proper scheme. Section 2.3 introduces the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of a coherent sheaf on projective space over a field and proves Mumford's theorem, which turns the vanishing of finitely many cohomology groups into an explicit bound for the twists that are globally generated and have no higher cohomology.

In the minimal model program the vanishing theorem of Serre is the starting point of every vanishing theorem: the Kodaira and Kawamata-Viehweg theorems of the later volumes say that a twist by an ample line bundle that is not large, but adjoint to the canonical bundle, already has no higher cohomology. The ampleness criterion is the form of ampleness that passes to closed subschemes and finite covers, and the regularity theorem is the source of the effective bounds behind the boundedness statements for families of varieties.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Section III.5, Vakil, Chapter 18, and Lazarsfeld, Section 1.8, with details from the Stacks project. It uses Chapter 1, the Proj construction and ampleness of P3 Ch. 6, and the associated primes of P1 Ch. 2.
