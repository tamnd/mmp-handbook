## Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity

### Statement

Serre's vanishing theorem says that $H^p(\mathcal{F}(n)) = 0$ for $p > 0$ and $n$ large, without saying how large. Regularity gives a bound: the vanishing of finitely many groups, one in each degree, propagates to all larger twists and forces global generation. Let $k$ be a field, $r \geq 0$, $X = \mathbb{P}^r_k$ and $V = H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(1))$, the space of linear forms.

**Definition P4.2.15 (regularity).** Let $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. A coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ is *$m$-regular* if
$$H^p(X, \mathcal{F}(m - p)) = 0 \quad \text{for all } p > 0.$$
The *regularity* $\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{F})$ is the infimum of the $m$ for which $\mathcal{F}$ is $m$-regular, an integer or $-\infty$.

Only $p = 1, \dots, r$ matter, by Corollary P4.1.10. The regularity is finite or $-\infty$, since by Theorem P4.2.7 the sheaf is $m$-regular for $m$ large.

**Theorem P4.2.16 (Mumford).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be an $m$-regular coherent sheaf on $X = \mathbb{P}^r_k$. Then:

1. $\mathcal{F}$ is $m'$-regular for every $m' \geq m$.
2. The multiplication map $V \otimes_k H^0(X, \mathcal{F}(n)) \to H^0(X, \mathcal{F}(n+1))$ is surjective for every $n \geq m$.
3. $\mathcal{F}(m)$ is generated by global sections.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P4.2.17 (effective Serre vanishing).** If $\mathcal{F}$ is $m$-regular, then $H^p(X, \mathcal{F}(n)) = 0$ for all $p > 0$ and $n \geq m - p$, the sheaf $\mathcal{F}(n)$ is generated by global sections for $n \geq m$, and the graded module $\bigoplus_{n \geq m} H^0(X, \mathcal{F}(n))$ is generated over $k[x_0, \dots, x_r]$ by its part of degree $m$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

The proof cuts $\mathcal{F}$ by a hyperplane and inducts on $r$. It needs a hyperplane that does not contain any associated point of $\mathcal{F}$, which exists when $k$ is infinite.

**Lemma P4.2.18 (a hyperplane in general position).** Let $k$ be infinite, $r \geq 1$ and $\mathcal{F}$ coherent on $X = \mathbb{P}^r_k$. There is a linear form $h \in V$ such that multiplication by $h$ is injective on $\mathcal{F}(-1) \to \mathcal{F}$. For such $h$, with $H = V_+(h) \cong \mathbb{P}^{r-1}_k$ and $\mathcal{F}_H = \mathcal{F}/h\mathcal{F}(-1)$, a coherent sheaf on $H$, there are exact sequences
$$0 \to \mathcal{F}(n - 1) \xrightarrow{\ h\ } \mathcal{F}(n) \to \mathcal{F}_H(n) \to 0 \qquad (n \in \mathbb{Z}),$$
where $\mathcal{F}_H(n)$ is the twist by $\mathcal{O}_H(1) = \mathcal{O}(1)|_H$, and $H^p(H, \mathcal{F}_H(n)) = H^p(X, \mathcal{F}_H(n))$ for all $p$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

If $\mathcal{F}$ is $m$-regular and $h$ is a general hyperplane, the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{F}(-1) \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}_H \to 0$ shows that $\mathcal{F}_H$ is $m$-regular on a projective space of one dimension less. By induction $\mathcal{F}_H$ is $(m+1)$-regular, and the same sequence lifts this to $\mathcal{F}$. For the surjectivity of multiplication, a section of $\mathcal{F}(n+1)$ restricts to $H$, where it is a combination of products by induction. The products lift to $X$ because $H^1(\mathcal{F}(n)) = 0$, and the difference vanishes on $H$, so it is divisible by $h$. Global generation follows by comparing $\mathcal{F}(m)$ with the subsheaf generated by its sections: after a large twist they have the same sections, which generate by Serre's theorem. A finite field is replaced by an infinite extension, which changes no dimension by flat base change.

### Examples

**Example P4.2.19 (line bundles).** By Theorem P4.2.1, $H^p(\mathbb{P}^r, \mathcal{O}(-p)) = 0$ for $0 < p < r$ and $H^r(\mathbb{P}^r, \mathcal{O}(-r)) = 0$, while $H^r(\mathbb{P}^r, \mathcal{O}(-1 - r)) \neq 0$ for $r \geq 1$. So $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^r}$ is $0$-regular and not $(-1)$-regular, and $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{O}(a) = -a$. Theorem P4.2.16 then says that $\mathcal{O}(a)$ is generated by global sections for $a \geq 0$, which is sharp, and that $V \otimes S_n \to S_{n+1}$ is surjective for $n \geq 0$, which is clear.

**Example P4.2.20 (sheaves with finite support).** If $\operatorname{Supp} \mathcal{F}$ is finite, it lies in an affine open set $D_+(\ell)$ for a suitable linear form $\ell$ when $k$ is infinite, and in any case $\mathcal{F} = i_* \mathcal{G}$ for the inclusion $i$ of a finite closed subscheme, which is affine. By Theorem P4.1.8 and Proposition P4.1.19, all higher cohomology of all twists of $\mathcal{F}$ vanishes. So $\mathcal{F}$ is $m$-regular for every $m$ and $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{F} = -\infty$. Theorem P4.2.16 then says that $\mathcal{F}(m)$ is generated by global sections for all $m$, which is the statement that a coherent sheaf on an affine scheme is generated by global sections.

**Example P4.2.21 (collinear points).** Let $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ be a line, $d \geq 1$, and $Z = \{P_1, \dots, P_d\} \subseteq L$ a set of $d$ distinct $k$-points with the reduced structure and ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}_Z$. From $0 \to \mathcal{I}_Z \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}_Z \to 0$ and $H^p(\mathcal{O}_Z(n)) = 0$ for $p > 0$,
$$H^1(\mathcal{I}_Z(n)) = \operatorname{coker}\bigl(H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(n)) \to H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z(n)) \cong k^d\bigr), \qquad H^2(\mathcal{I}_Z(n)) = H^2(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(n)).$$
The restriction $H^0(\mathbb{P}^2, \mathcal{O}(n)) \to H^0(L, \mathcal{O}_L(n))$ is surjective, and the evaluation of binary forms of degree $n$ at $d$ distinct points is surjective if and only if $n \geq d - 1$, by the Vandermonde determinant. So $H^1(\mathcal{I}_Z(n)) = 0$ if and only if $n \geq d - 1$. Since $H^2(\mathcal{O}(d-2)) = 0$, the sheaf $\mathcal{I}_Z$ is $d$-regular, and it is not $(d-1)$-regular, because $H^1(\mathcal{I}_Z(d - 2)) \neq 0$. So $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{I}_Z = d$, and indeed the ideal of $Z$ needs a generator of degree $d$: it is generated by the linear form of $L$ and a form of degree $d$ that cuts out $Z$ on $L$. Theorem P4.2.16 says that $\mathcal{I}_Z(d)$ is generated by global sections, and the example shows that this cannot be improved.

**Example P4.2.22 (a plane curve).** Let $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ be a curve of degree $d \geq 1$. By Example P4.2.12, $H^1(C, \mathcal{O}_C(n)) = 0$ for $n \geq d - 2$ and $H^1(C, \mathcal{O}_C(d - 3)) \cong k$, and $H^2(C, \mathcal{O}_C(n)) = 0$ for all $n$ by Grothendieck vanishing, Theorem P2.7.13. So $\mathcal{O}_C$ is $(d-1)$-regular and not $(d-2)$-regular, hence by part 1 of Theorem P4.2.16 not $m$-regular for any $m \leq d - 2$, and $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{O}_C = d - 1$. Its ideal sheaf is $\mathcal{O}(-d)$, with regularity $d$.

### Proof

*Lemma P4.2.18.* Let $U_i = D_+(x_i) = \operatorname{Spec} B_i$ and $\mathcal{F}|_{U_i} = \widetilde{M_i}$ with $M_i$ a finitely generated $B_i$-module, by Proposition P3.5.22. By Theorem P1.2.46 each $\operatorname{Ass}(M_i)$ is finite. Let $\Sigma \subseteq X$ be the finite set of points corresponding to these primes, for all $i$. For $P \in \Sigma$ the linear forms vanishing at $P$ form a subspace $W_P \subsetneq V$, proper because some $x_i$ does not vanish at $P$.

A vector space $V$ over an infinite field is not a finite union of proper subspaces $W_1, \dots, W_s$. By induction on $s$ choose $u \notin W_1 \cup \dots \cup W_{s-1}$, and $w \notin W_s$. If $u \notin W_s$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, consider $u + tw$ for $t \in k$. If two different values of $t$ give elements of $W_i$ for some $i < s$, then $w \in W_i$ and $u \in W_i$, which is excluded, so each $W_i$, $i < s$, contains $u + tw$ for at most one $t$. And $u + tw \in W_s$ forces $tw \in W_s$, so $t = 0$. Since $k$ is infinite, some $u + tw$ lies in no $W_i$.

Choose $h \in V \setminus \bigcup_{P \in \Sigma} W_P$. On $U_i$, trivialize $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ by $x_i^{-1}$, so that multiplication by $h$ from $\mathcal{F}(-1)$ to $\mathcal{F}$ becomes multiplication by $h/x_i \in B_i$ on $M_i$. For a prime $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Ass}(M_i)$ with point $P$, $h/x_i \in \mathfrak{p}$ would say that $h$ vanishes at $P$, which is excluded. By Proposition P1.2.44 the element $h/x_i$ is a nonzerodivisor on $M_i$, and multiplication by $h$ is injective on every $U_i$.

Twisting by $\mathcal{O}(n)$ gives the exact sequences. The quotient $\mathcal{F}_H$ is coherent and annihilated by $h$, so it is $i_* $ of a coherent sheaf on $H$, also written $\mathcal{F}_H$, and $(i_* \mathcal{F}_H)(n) = i_*(\mathcal{F}_H(n))$. A linear change of coordinates moves $h$ to $x_r$, and identifies $H$ with $\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_k = \operatorname{Proj} k[x_0, \dots, x_{r-1}]$ with $\mathcal{O}(1)|_H = \mathcal{O}_H(1)$, by Corollary P3.6.15. The comparison of cohomology is Proposition P4.1.19 for the closed immersion $i$. $\square$

*Theorem P4.2.16, reduction to an infinite field.* Let $k' = k(t)$, $X' = \mathbb{P}^r_{k'}$ and $\mathcal{F}'$ the pullback of $\mathcal{F}$, so $\mathcal{F}'(n)$ is the pullback of $\mathcal{F}(n)$. By Theorem P4.1.17, $H^p(X', \mathcal{F}'(n)) = H^p(X, \mathcal{F}(n)) \otimes_k k'$, compatibly with multiplication by linear forms. So $\mathcal{F}'$ is $m$-regular, and parts 1 and 2 for $\mathcal{F}$ follow from parts 1 and 2 for $\mathcal{F}'$, because a $k$-linear map is surjective, or zero, if and only if it is after $\otimes_k k'$. For part 3, let $\mathcal{C}$ be the cokernel of $H^0(X, \mathcal{F}(m)) \otimes_k \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{F}(m)$. Pullback is right exact, so the pullback of $\mathcal{C}$ is the cokernel of the corresponding map for $\mathcal{F}'$, which is $0$ by part 3 for $\mathcal{F}'$. On $U_i$, $\mathcal{C} = \widetilde{N}$ and its pullback is $(N \otimes_k k')^\sim$, and $N \otimes_k k' = 0$ forces $N = 0$, because $k'$ is a nonzero free $k$-module. So assume $k$ infinite.

*Induction on $r$.* For $r = 0$, $X = \operatorname{Spec} k$, every coherent sheaf is a finite-dimensional vector space with no higher cohomology, $\mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}$, and $V = k x_0$ acts by isomorphisms, so all three parts hold. Let $r \geq 1$, assume the theorem on $\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_k$, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be $m$-regular. Choose $h$ by Lemma P4.2.18.

*$\mathcal{F}_H$ is $m$-regular.* For $p > 0$ the long exact sequence of $0 \to \mathcal{F}(m-p-1) \to \mathcal{F}(m-p) \to \mathcal{F}_H(m-p) \to 0$ contains
$$H^p(\mathcal{F}(m-p)) \to H^p(\mathcal{F}_H(m-p)) \to H^{p+1}(\mathcal{F}(m-(p+1))),$$
and both outer terms vanish by $m$-regularity. So by the induction hypothesis, parts 1 to 3 hold for $\mathcal{F}_H$ on $H$.

*Part 1.* It suffices to show that $\mathcal{F}$ is $(m+1)$-regular. For $p > 0$ the sequence for $n = m + 1 - p$ contains
$$H^p(\mathcal{F}(m-p)) \to H^p(\mathcal{F}(m+1-p)) \to H^p(\mathcal{F}_H(m+1-p)).$$
The left term vanishes by $m$-regularity of $\mathcal{F}$, and the right term vanishes because $\mathcal{F}_H$ is $(m+1)$-regular by part 1 for $\mathcal{F}_H$. So $H^p(\mathcal{F}(m+1-p)) = 0$.

*Part 2.* Let $n \geq m$. By part 1, $\mathcal{F}$ is $(n+1)$-regular, so $H^1(\mathcal{F}(n)) = 0$ and $H^1(\mathcal{F}(n-1)) = 0$, the second because $\mathcal{F}$ is $n$-regular. The sequences for $n$ and $n + 1$ then show that the restrictions
$$H^0(\mathcal{F}(n)) \to H^0(\mathcal{F}_H(n)), \qquad H^0(\mathcal{F}(n+1)) \to H^0(\mathcal{F}_H(n+1))$$
are surjective. The restriction $V \to V_H = H^0(H, \mathcal{O}_H(1))$ is surjective, with kernel $k h$. Let $s \in H^0(\mathcal{F}(n+1))$ with restriction $\bar{s}$. By part 2 for $\mathcal{F}_H$, $\bar{s} = \sum_j \bar{v}_j \bar{t}_j$ with $\bar{v}_j \in V_H$ and $\bar{t}_j \in H^0(\mathcal{F}_H(n))$. Lift $\bar{v}_j$ to $v_j \in V$ and $\bar{t}_j$ to $t_j \in H^0(\mathcal{F}(n))$. Then $s - \sum_j v_j t_j$ restricts to $0$ on $H$, so by exactness at $H^0(\mathcal{F}(n+1))$ it is $h u$ for some $u \in H^0(\mathcal{F}(n))$, and $s = \sum_j v_j t_j + h u$ is in the image of $V \otimes H^0(\mathcal{F}(n))$.

*Part 3.* Let $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}(m)$ be the image of $H^0(\mathcal{F}(m)) \otimes_k \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{F}(m)$, the subsheaf generated by global sections. For $N \geq m$, part 2 applied $N - m$ times shows that every element of $H^0(\mathcal{F}(N))$ is a sum of products $g \, t$ with $g$ a form of degree $N - m$ and $t \in H^0(\mathcal{F}(m))$, so it is a section of the subsheaf $\mathcal{G}(N - m) \subseteq \mathcal{F}(N)$. By Theorem P3.6.23 there is $N \geq m$ such that $\mathcal{F}(N)$ is generated by global sections. These sections lie in $\mathcal{G}(N - m)$, so $\mathcal{G}(N-m) = \mathcal{F}(N)$, and twisting by $m - N$ gives $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{F}(m)$. $\square$

*Corollary P4.2.17.* For $p > 0$ and $n \geq m - p$, $\mathcal{F}$ is $(n + p)$-regular by part 1, so $H^p(\mathcal{F}(n)) = H^p(\mathcal{F}((n+p) - p)) = 0$. For $n \geq m$, $\mathcal{F}$ is $n$-regular, and part 3 gives global generation of $\mathcal{F}(n)$. The last statement is part 2 applied repeatedly. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $P \in \mathbb{P}^r_k$ be a $k$-point, $r \geq 1$. Show that $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{I}_P = 1$, and identify the generating sections of $\mathcal{I}_P(1)$ given by Theorem P4.2.16.
2. (A) Let $0 \to \mathcal{F}' \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}'' \to 0$ be exact. Show that if $\mathcal{F}'$ and $\mathcal{F}''$ are $m$-regular then $\mathcal{F}$ is $m$-regular, and that if $\mathcal{F}$ is $m$-regular and $\mathcal{F}'$ is $(m+1)$-regular then $\mathcal{F}''$ is $m$-regular.
3. (B) Let $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3_k$ be the twisted cubic, the image of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ under $[s : t] \mapsto [s^3 : s^2 t : s t^2 : t^3]$, with $\mathcal{O}_C(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(3)$. Show that $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{I}_C = 2$, and deduce that $\mathcal{I}_C(2)$ is generated by the three quadrics $x_0 x_2 - x_1^2$, $x_1 x_3 - x_2^2$ and $x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2$.
4. (B) Show that $\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{F} \oplus \mathcal{G}) = \max(\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{F}, \operatorname{reg} \mathcal{G})$, and that $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{F}(a) = \operatorname{reg} \mathcal{F} - a$. Compute the regularity of $\mathcal{O}(a_1) \oplus \dots \oplus \mathcal{O}(a_s)$.
5. (C) Let $k$ be infinite and $Z \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ a set of $d \geq 1$ distinct $k$-points. Show that $\operatorname{reg} \mathcal{I}_Z \leq d$: for each $i$, a product of $d - 1$ linear forms, each through one $P_j$ with $j \neq i$ and not through $P_i$, is a form of degree $d - 1$ that separates $P_i$ from the other points. Compare with Example P4.2.21, which shows that the bound is attained by collinear points.

### Notes and history

The idea of cutting by a general hyperplane and lifting sections goes back to Castelnuovo's work on linear systems on curves in the 1890s. Mumford isolated the notion of $m$-regularity and proved Theorem P4.2.16 in *Lectures on curves on an algebraic surface* (1966), where it is used to construct the Hilbert and Picard schemes. The algebraic counterpart, for graded modules, was developed by Eisenbud and Goto through minimal free resolutions and local cohomology, and Lazarsfeld's Section 1.8 surveys both. The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program regularity turns vanishing into boundedness. A family of sheaves with bounded regularity has bounded Hilbert polynomials and is parametrized by a scheme of finite type, which is how Matsusaka's theorem, the boundedness of Fano varieties and the effective statements of the later volumes are reduced to cohomology.
