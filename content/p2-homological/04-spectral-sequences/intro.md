# Spectral sequences

A spectral sequence is a bookkeeping device for a computation in several steps. A complex with a filtration has graded pieces whose cohomology is often easy to find, and a spectral sequence passes from this cohomology to the cohomology of the whole complex, one correction at a time. The answer comes as a filtration of the limit with known graded pieces. In most applications in geometry only a few corrections can be nonzero, and the spectral sequence reduces to an isomorphism or to a short exact sequence in low degrees.

Section 4.1 defines spectral sequences and their convergence, constructs the spectral sequence of a filtered complex, and proves the facts used most often: the edge maps, degeneration, the five-term exact sequence and the comparison of spectral sequences. Section 4.2 applies this to the two filtrations of a double complex. It proves that a double complex with exact rows has the cohomology of its first column, and uses this to give a second proof of the balance of $\operatorname{Ext}$. Section 4.3 introduces Cartan-Eilenberg resolutions and hypercohomology, and proves the Grothendieck spectral sequence $R^p F(R^q G(A)) \Rightarrow R^{p+q}(F G)(A)$ for a composite of functors. Its main case is the Leray spectral sequence $H^p(Y, R^q f_* \mathcal{F}) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X, \mathcal{F})$ of a map $f: X \to Y$.

The minimal model program uses the Leray spectral sequence in its simplest form. If the higher direct images $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}$ vanish, then $H^n(X, \mathcal{F}) = H^n(Y, f_* \mathcal{F})$. The vanishing comes from the relative Kawamata-Viehweg theorem (P7 Ch. 8, Volume 5). For a contraction $f: X \to Z$ of a $(K_X + \Delta)$-negative extremal ray of a klt pair (Volume 7) it gives $R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_X = 0$ for $i > 0$, hence $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = H^i(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z)$. Klt singularities are rational, so the same identification holds for a resolution of singularities, and $h^i(\mathcal{O})$ does not change along the program. The higher direct images and their base change (P4 Ch. 4 and 5) are compared with cohomology on the fibres through this spectral sequence. The local to global spectral sequence for $\operatorname{Ext}$ (P4 Ch. 8) is a Grothendieck spectral sequence and enters Serre duality (P4 Ch. 7) and the deformation theory of the Hom and Hilbert schemes (P8 Ch. 1). The Čech to derived functor spectral sequence of Section 4.2 is how Chapter 7 proves that Čech cohomology of an affine cover computes the cohomology of a quasi-coherent sheaf. Finally, the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of Section 4.3 is a hypercohomology spectral sequence. Its degeneration is the input of the proofs of Kodaira and Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing by Deligne and Illusie and by Esnault and Viehweg (P7 Ch. 8 and 11).

The chapter follows Weibel, Chapter 5, and Grothendieck's Tohoku paper, Chapter II. Proofs are given in full, except for the identification of the pages of the spectral sequence of a filtered complex and the existence and uniqueness of Cartan-Eilenberg resolutions, which are sketched and cited to Weibel. The chapter uses Chapter 3.
