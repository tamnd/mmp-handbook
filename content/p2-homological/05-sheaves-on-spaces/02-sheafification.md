## Sheafification

### Statement

Throughout, $X$ is a topological space, and presheaves take values in sets or in abelian groups. Write $\mathsf{PSh}(X)$ for the category of presheaves of sets.

**Theorem P2.5.12 (sheafification).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a presheaf on $X$. There is a sheaf $\mathcal{F}^+$ and a morphism $\theta: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}^+$ with two properties.

1. (Universal property.) For every sheaf $\mathcal{G}$ and every morphism $\varphi: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$ there is a unique morphism $\psi: \mathcal{F}^+ \to \mathcal{G}$ with $\psi \theta = \varphi$.
2. (Stalks.) For every $x \in X$ the map $\theta_x: \mathcal{F}_x \to \mathcal{F}^+_x$ is bijective.

If $\mathcal{F}$ is a presheaf of abelian groups, $\mathcal{F}^+$ is a sheaf of abelian groups and $\theta$ is a morphism of presheaves of abelian groups, with the universal property for sheaves of abelian groups.

Lean: Mathlib `TopCat.Presheaf.sheafify`, `TopCat.Presheaf.toSheafify` and `TopCat.Presheaf.sheafifyStalkIso` for presheaves of types; `CategoryTheory.presheafToSheaf` and `CategoryTheory.sheafificationAdjunction` in general.

The sheaf $\mathcal{F}^+$ is the *sheafification* of $\mathcal{F}$. By the universal property it is unique up to unique isomorphism, and $\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}^+$ is a functor $\mathsf{PSh}(X) \to \mathsf{Sh}(X)$, left adjoint to the inclusion (Section 1.3).

**Corollary P2.5.13 (properties of sheafification).**

1. If $\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf, then $\theta: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}^+$ is an isomorphism.
2. A morphism of presheaves $\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}'$ that is bijective on all stalks induces an isomorphism $\mathcal{F}^+ \to \mathcal{F}'^+$.
3. The sheafification of the constant presheaf with value $A$ is the constant sheaf $\underline{A}$ of locally constant functions.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P2.5.14 (étalé space).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a presheaf of sets. Its *étalé space* is the set $\operatorname{Et}(\mathcal{F}) = \bigsqcup_{x \in X} \mathcal{F}_x$, with the map $\pi: \operatorname{Et}(\mathcal{F}) \to X$ that sends $\mathcal{F}_x$ to $x$, and the topology generated by the sets $\{ t_y : y \in V \}$ for $V$ open and $t \in \mathcal{F}(V)$.

Lean: Mathlib `TopCat.Presheaf.EtaleSpace`.

The map $\pi$ is a local homeomorphism, and $\mathcal{F}^+(U)$ is the set of continuous sections of $\pi$ over $U$ (Exercise 4). This is the form of the definition used by Leray and Godement.

### Proof in words

A sheaf is determined by its stalks, so the sheafification must have the same stalks as $\mathcal{F}$. The construction takes this literally. A section of $\mathcal{F}^+$ over $U$ is a choice of a germ at every point of $U$ that is locally the family of germs of one section of $\mathcal{F}$. Such choices satisfy locality and gluing for free, because the condition is local and the data is pointwise. A section of $\mathcal{F}$ gives its family of germs, and this is $\theta$. The stalks do not change, because a germ of a section of $\mathcal{F}^+$ at $x$ is the germ of a section of $\mathcal{F}$. For the universal property, a section of $\mathcal{F}^+$ is locally the image of sections of $\mathcal{F}$. Their images in $\mathcal{G}$ agree on overlaps because they have the same germs, and they glue because $\mathcal{G}$ is a sheaf.

### Examples

**Example P2.5.15 (the image of the exponential).** On $X = \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$, the morphism $\exp: \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}^\times$ of sheaves of abelian groups has a presheaf image $U \mapsto \exp(\mathcal{O}(U))$. This presheaf is not a sheaf. The function $z$ is not in its value on $X$, but it is in its value on each disc not containing $0$, since a logarithm exists there. The sheafification of the presheaf image is all of $\mathcal{O}^\times$. Section 5.3 defines the image of a morphism of sheaves as this sheafification, and so $\exp$ is an epimorphism of sheaves.

**Example P2.5.16 (higher direct images).** Let $f: X \to Y$ be continuous and $\mathcal{F}$ a sheaf of abelian groups on $X$. The higher direct image $R^q f_* \mathcal{F}$ of Chapter 7 and P4 Ch. 4 is the sheafification of the presheaf $V \mapsto H^q(f^{-1}(V), \mathcal{F})$. This presheaf is not a sheaf in general. For $f = 1_X$ its value on $X$ is $H^q(X, \mathcal{F})$, but its sheafification is $0$ for $q > 0$, since every cohomology class vanishes locally (Chapter 7). The vanishing theorems of the minimal model program in relative form, $R^q f_* \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) = 0$, are statements about such sheafifications (P7 Ch. 8).

**Example P2.5.17 (the constant sheaf).** On $X = \{a, b\}$ with the discrete topology, the constant presheaf with value $\mathbb{Z}$ has $\mathcal{F}(X) = \mathbb{Z}$, while the constant sheaf has $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}(X) = \mathbb{Z}^2$. The map $\theta_X$ is the diagonal $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}^2$. It is injective but not surjective.

**Example P2.5.18 (a presheaf with zero sheafification).** Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathcal{F}(U) = C(U, \mathbb{R}) / C_b(U, \mathbb{R})$, continuous functions modulo bounded continuous functions. Every point has a neighbourhood with compact closure, and every continuous function on $\mathbb{R}$ is bounded near each point. So every stalk of $\mathcal{F}$ is $0$, and $\mathcal{F}^+ = 0$, although $\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}) \neq 0$.

### Proof

*Proof of the theorem.* For an open $U$, let $\mathcal{F}^+(U)$ be the set of functions $s: U \to \bigsqcup_{x \in U} \mathcal{F}_x$ with $s(x) \in \mathcal{F}_x$, such that each $x \in U$ has an open neighbourhood $V \subseteq U$ and a section $t \in \mathcal{F}(V)$ with $s(y) = t_y$ for all $y \in V$. Restriction is restriction of functions. This is a presheaf, since the condition is inherited by smaller open sets.

$\mathcal{F}^+$ is a sheaf. Let $U = \bigcup U_i$. Locality holds because sections of $\mathcal{F}^+$ are functions on $U$, and two functions that agree on each $U_i$ are equal. For gluing, let $s_i \in \mathcal{F}^+(U_i)$ agree on overlaps. They define one function $s$ on $U$. The local condition at $x \in U_i$ holds for $s$ because it holds for $s_i$ on a neighbourhood $V \subseteq U_i$ of $x$. So $s \in \mathcal{F}^+(U)$.

Define $\theta_U: \mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}^+(U)$ by $\theta_U(t)(x) = t_x$. It commutes with restriction.

Stalks. Surjectivity of $\theta_x$: an element of $\mathcal{F}^+_x$ is the germ of some $s \in \mathcal{F}^+(U)$. By definition, $s = \theta_V(t)$ on a neighbourhood $V$ of $x$, for some $t \in \mathcal{F}(V)$. So the germ of $s$ is $\theta_x(t_x)$. Injectivity: if $\theta_x(t_x) = \theta_x(t'_x)$ for $t, t' \in \mathcal{F}(V)$, then $\theta(t)$ and $\theta(t')$ agree on a neighbourhood $W$ of $x$. In particular $t_x = \theta(t)(x) = \theta(t')(x) = t'_x$.

Universal property. Let $\varphi: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$ with $\mathcal{G}$ a sheaf. Uniqueness: if $\psi \theta = \varphi = \psi' \theta$, then $\psi_x \theta_x = \psi'_x \theta_x$ on stalks. Since $\theta_x$ is bijective, $\psi_x = \psi'_x$ for all $x$, and $\psi = \psi'$ by part 3 of the theorem of Section 5.1. Existence: let $s \in \mathcal{F}^+(U)$. Choose an open cover $U = \bigcup V_i$ and $t_i \in \mathcal{F}(V_i)$ with $s|_{V_i} = \theta(t_i)$. For $y \in V_i \cap V_j$, $(t_i)_y = s(y) = (t_j)_y$. So $\varphi(t_i)_y = \varphi_y((t_i)_y) = \varphi_y((t_j)_y) = \varphi(t_j)_y$, and by the proposition of Section 5.1, applied to the sheaf $\mathcal{G}$, $\varphi(t_i)$ and $\varphi(t_j)$ agree on $V_i \cap V_j$. They glue to a section $\psi_U(s) \in \mathcal{G}(U)$. Its germ at each $y \in V_i$ is $\varphi_y(s(y))$, so it does not depend on the choice of cover and of the $t_i$. The same description of germs shows that $\psi$ commutes with restriction and that $\psi \theta = \varphi$.

For presheaves of abelian groups, add the functions in $\mathcal{F}^+(U)$ pointwise in the groups $\mathcal{F}_x$. The local condition is preserved by sums, since the sum of $\theta(t)$ and $\theta(t')$ on $V$ is $\theta(t + t')$. The maps $\theta_U$ are homomorphisms, and so are the maps $\psi_U$ built from a homomorphism $\varphi$, since they are determined by the homomorphisms $\varphi_y$ on stalks. $\square$

*Proof of the corollary.* For part 1, $\theta$ is a morphism of sheaves that is bijective on stalks, so it is an isomorphism by the theorem of Section 5.1. For part 2, the induced morphism $\mathcal{F}^+ \to \mathcal{F}'^+$ is, on stalks, the composite of the bijections $\mathcal{F}^+_x \cong \mathcal{F}_x \to \mathcal{F}'_x \cong \mathcal{F}'^+_x$. So it is an isomorphism, again by Section 5.1. For part 3, the constant presheaf has all stalks equal to $A$, and a section of its sheafification over $U$ is a function $U \to A$ that is locally constant. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\theta_U: \mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}^+(U)$ is injective for all $U$ if and only if $\mathcal{F}$ satisfies locality. Such presheaves are called *separated*.
2. (A) Show that sheafification is a functor and that it is left adjoint to the inclusion $\mathsf{Sh}(X) \to \mathsf{PSh}(X)$.
3. (A) Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a presheaf and $\mathcal{G}$ a sheaf with a morphism $\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$ that is bijective on stalks. Show that $\mathcal{G} \cong \mathcal{F}^+$.
4. (B) Prove that $\pi: \operatorname{Et}(\mathcal{F}) \to X$ is a local homeomorphism, and that $\mathcal{F}^+(U)$ is the set of continuous sections of $\pi$ over $U$. Deduce that $\mathsf{Sh}(X)$ is equivalent to the category of local homeomorphisms $E \to X$.
5. (B) Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a presheaf of abelian groups whose stalks are all $0$. Show that $\mathcal{F}^+ = 0$. Find such an $\mathcal{F}$ with $\mathcal{F}(X) \neq 0$ on a space $X$ different from the example.
6. (B) Show that sheafification commutes with finite limits: if $\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{H} \leftarrow \mathcal{G}$ are presheaves, then $(\mathcal{F} \times_{\mathcal{H}} \mathcal{G})^+ \cong \mathcal{F}^+ \times_{\mathcal{H}^+} \mathcal{G}^+$.
7. (C) For a presheaf $\mathcal{F}$ and open $U$, let $\mathcal{F}^\dagger(U) = \operatorname{colim}_{\mathcal{U}} \check{H}^0(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})$, the colimit over the open covers $\mathcal{U}$ of $U$, ordered by refinement, of the sets of compatible families. Show that $\mathcal{F}^\dagger$ is separated, that $\mathcal{F}^\dagger$ is a sheaf if $\mathcal{F}$ is separated, and that $(\mathcal{F}^\dagger)^\dagger \cong \mathcal{F}^+$.

### Lean

Mathlib constructs the sheafification of a presheaf of types on a topological space as `TopCat.Presheaf.sheafify`, through local predicates on the family of stalks, which is the construction above. The morphism $\theta$ is `TopCat.Presheaf.toSheafify`, and `TopCat.Presheaf.sheafifyStalkIso` is part 2 of the theorem. For presheaves with values in a general category on a general site, sheafification is `CategoryTheory.presheafToSheaf`, with the adjunction `CategoryTheory.sheafificationAdjunction`. The construction of Exercise 7 on a general site is `CategoryTheory.GrothendieckTopology.plusObj`. The étalé space is `TopCat.Presheaf.EtaleSpace`. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Leray and Cartan defined sheaves through étalé spaces, so sheafification was built in. Godement's book of 1958 gives the construction of the sheaf associated with a presheaf. Grothendieck's construction $(\mathcal{F}^\dagger)^\dagger$ of Exercise 7 works on any site and is the one used for the étale topology (SGA 4).
