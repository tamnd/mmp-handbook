# Sheaves on spaces

A sheaf records data that is defined locally on a space and glues: functions, sections of a line bundle, solutions of a differential equation. The value of a sheaf on an open set is determined by its values on the members of any open cover, and its germs at the points determine its sections. This chapter sets up sheaves of sets and of abelian groups on a topological space and proves the properties that cohomology in Chapter 7 needs.

Section 5.1 defines presheaves, sheaves and stalks, and proves that morphisms of sheaves are injective, bijective or equal if they are so on all stalks. Section 5.2 constructs the sheafification of a presheaf as the sheaf of compatible germs and proves its universal property. Section 5.3 proves that the sheaves of abelian groups form an abelian category with enough injectives, that exactness is checked on stalks, and that global sections form a left exact functor that is not exact. Section 5.4 treats direct image, inverse image and extension by zero, the adjunction $f^{-1} \dashv f_*$, sheaves on a basis of the topology and the gluing of sheaves.

The minimal model program uses these ideas everywhere, mostly without comment. The sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ of a Weil divisor on a normal variety (Section 5.1) is the object of the cone, contraction and base point free theorems (Volume 7). Restriction sequences $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD - S) \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \to i_* \mathcal{O}_S(mD|_S) \to 0$ are exact as sequences of sheaves, but not on global sections, and the base point free theorem lifts sections from $S$ to $X$ by killing the obstruction with a vanishing theorem (P7 Ch. 8, Volume 5). The exponential sequence of Section 5.3 links line bundles to $H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})$ and so to the Néron-Severi group in which the cone theorem is stated. The adjunction $f^{-1} \dashv f_*$ shows that $f_*$ keeps injectives, which is the hypothesis of the Leray spectral sequence of Section 4.3 and of the higher direct images of P4 Ch. 4. Sheaves on a base and gluing define the structure sheaf of a scheme and its quasi-coherent sheaves (P3 and P4 Ch. 1).

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Chapter II, Section 1, and Vakil, Chapter 2, with the exactness results of Grothendieck's Tohoku paper. Proofs are given in full. The chapter uses Chapter 1 for adjunctions and filtered colimits and Chapter 2 for abelian categories.
