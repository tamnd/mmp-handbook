# Sheaves of modules

A variety carries its sheaf of regular functions $\mathcal{O}_X$, and most sheaves that geometry meets are modules over it: ideal sheaves, sections of vector bundles, the sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ of divisors, differential forms and the canonical sheaf. This chapter puts the sheaves of Chapter 5 together with the modules of P1 Ch. 1. A space with a sheaf of rings is a ringed space, and a sheaf of modules over that sheaf of rings is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. The operations of commutative algebra, tensor product, Hom, extension and restriction of scalars, then become operations on sheaves.

Section 6.1 defines ringed spaces, locally ringed spaces and their morphisms, and proves that the $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules form an abelian category in which exactness is checked on stalks. Section 6.2 constructs the tensor product and the sheaf $\mathcal{H}om$, computes the stalks of the tensor product and proves the adjunction between them. Section 6.3 defines the pushforward $f_*$ and the pullback $f^*$ along a morphism of ringed spaces, proves the adjunction $f^* \dashv f_*$ announced in Chapter 1, and computes the stalks of $f^*$. Section 6.4 treats sheaves of finite type and of finite presentation, quasi-coherent sheaves on a ringed space in general terms, locally free sheaves and their transition functions, line bundles and the Picard group, and the projection formula.

The minimal model program uses this chapter in its basic notation. The canonical sheaf $\omega_X$ and the sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X(mK_X)$ are $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules. A Weil divisor $D$ on a normal variety is Cartier exactly when $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ is a line bundle, and $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier when some $\mathcal{O}_X(mD)$ with $m > 0$ is one. Example P2.6.35 shows a cone singularity where $D$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier but not Cartier. The hypothesis "$K_X + \Delta$ is $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier" in the definition of klt pairs (Volume 1) is of this kind. The Picard group of Section 6.4 maps onto the Néron-Severi group in which the cone theorem is stated (Volume 7). The projection formula gives $H^0(Y, f^* L) = H^0(X, L)$ for a contraction $f: Y \to X$ with $f_* \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_X$. This is how sections move between a variety and its contractions in the base point free theorem (Volume 7) and in the definition of the canonical model.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Chapter II, Section 5, and Vakil, Chapter 2, with details from the Stacks project, chapter Sheaves of modules. Proofs are given in full. The chapter uses Chapter 5 for sheaves and P1 Ch. 1 for tensor products and Hom of modules. Schemes appear only in examples, with the facts they need quoted from P3.
