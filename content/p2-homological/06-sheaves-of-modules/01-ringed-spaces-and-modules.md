## Ringed spaces and sheaves of modules

### Statement

Rings are commutative with $1$. A sheaf of rings on a space $X$ is a sheaf with values in commutative rings, and its stalks are rings (Section 5.1).

**Definition P2.6.1 (ringed space).** A *ringed space* is a pair $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ of a topological space $X$ and a sheaf of rings $\mathcal{O}_X$ on $X$, the *structure sheaf*. A *morphism of ringed spaces* $(f, f^\#): (X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to (Y, \mathcal{O}_Y)$ is a continuous map $f: X \to Y$ with a morphism of sheaves of rings $f^\#: \mathcal{O}_Y \to f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ on $Y$. The ringed space is *locally ringed* if every stalk $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is a local ring, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_x$ and residue field $\kappa(x) = \mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_x$. A morphism of locally ringed spaces is a morphism of ringed spaces such that, for every $x \in X$, the induced map of stalks
$$f^\#_x: \mathcal{O}_{Y, f(x)} \to \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$$
is a local homomorphism, that is, $f^\#_x(\mathfrak{m}_{f(x)}) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x$.

Lean: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.RingedSpace` and `AlgebraicGeometry.LocallyRingedSpace`.

The map of stalks is the composite $\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)} \to (f_* \mathcal{O}_X)_{f(x)} \to \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$, where the second map sends the germ at $f(x)$ of a section over $f^{-1}(V)$ to its germ at $x$. By the adjunction $f^{-1} \dashv f_*$ of Section 5.4, the datum $f^\#$ is the same as a morphism of sheaves of rings $f^{-1} \mathcal{O}_Y \to \mathcal{O}_X$ on $X$. Section 6.3 uses this form.

**Definition P2.6.2 (sheaf of modules).** Let $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ be a ringed space. An *$\mathcal{O}_X$-module* is a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ of abelian groups on $X$ with an $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$-module structure on each $\mathcal{F}(U)$, such that $(a s)|_V = a|_V \, s|_V$ for $V \subseteq U$, $a \in \mathcal{O}_X(U)$ and $s \in \mathcal{F}(U)$. A morphism of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules is a morphism of sheaves $\varphi$ such that each $\varphi_U$ is $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$-linear. Write $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$ for this category and $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})$ for its sets of maps. A *presheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules* is defined in the same way, with $\mathcal{F}$ a presheaf. An *ideal sheaf* is a sub-$\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$.

Lean: Mathlib `SheafOfModules` and `PresheafOfModules`, and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules` for a scheme.

The stalk $\mathcal{F}_x$ of an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module is an $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-module, with $a_x s_x = (a s)_x$. This is well defined because both germs can be represented on a common neighbourhood. The same holds for any sheaf of rings $\mathcal{A}$ in place of $\mathcal{O}_X$, and Section 6.3 uses the case $\mathcal{A} = f^{-1} \mathcal{O}_Y$.

**Theorem P2.6.3 ($\mathcal{O}_X$-modules form an abelian category).** Let $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ be a ringed space.

1. The sheafification $\mathcal{F}^+$ of a presheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules $\mathcal{F}$ has a unique structure of $\mathcal{O}_X$-module for which $\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}^+$ is linear. Every linear map from $\mathcal{F}$ to an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module factors uniquely through a linear map from $\mathcal{F}^+$.
2. The category $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$ is abelian. The kernel, cokernel and image of a morphism are those of the underlying morphism in $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$, with their induced module structures. So the forgetful functor $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X) \to \mathsf{Ab}(X)$ is exact and faithful.
3. A sequence of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules is exact if and only if it is exact on the stalk at every point.

Lean: Mathlib `PresheafOfModules.sheafification` for part 1; the instance `Abelian (SheafOfModules R)` in `Mathlib/Algebra/Category/ModuleCat/Sheaf/Abelian.lean` for part 2. Part 3 is not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P2.6.4 (maps from free modules).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module and $I$ a set. Write $\mathcal{O}_X^{(I)}$ for the direct sum of copies of $\mathcal{O}_X$ indexed by $I$, the *free* module on $I$, and $\mathcal{O}_X^n$ when $I$ has $n$ elements. Then $\varphi \mapsto \varphi_X(1)$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{F}) \cong \mathcal{F}(X)$, and so
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\mathcal{O}_X^{(I)}, \mathcal{F}) \cong \prod_{i \in I} \mathcal{F}(X).$$

Lean: Mathlib `SheafOfModules.unitHomEquiv` and `SheafOfModules.freeHomEquiv`, with `SheafOfModules.free` for the free module.

### Proof in words

All the constructions of Chapter 5 respect a module structure. Sheafification replaces a section by a compatible family of germs, and a function can multiply germs one point at a time. Kernels are computed open set by open set, and a kernel of a linear map is a submodule. Cokernels are sheafifications of presheaf cokernels, which are presheaves of modules. So the abelian category $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$ of Section 5.3 already contains every kernel and cokernel that $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$ needs, and the axioms of an abelian category follow from those of $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$, because a linear morphism that is invertible as a morphism of sheaves has a linear inverse. Exactness is then a question about the underlying sheaves of abelian groups, and Section 5.3 checks it on stalks.

The proposition is the sheaf form of the fact that a map from $A$ to a module is determined by the image of $1$.

### Examples

**Example P2.6.5 (ringed spaces from geometry).** A topological space with its sheaf of continuous real functions, a smooth manifold with its smooth functions and a complex manifold with its holomorphic functions are locally ringed spaces. The stalk at $x$ is local, with maximal ideal the germs that vanish at $x$, because a function with $f(x) \neq 0$ is invertible near $x$. A variety, or more generally a scheme, with its structure sheaf is a locally ringed space (P3 Ch. 1 and 2). A continuous map $f: X \to Y$ of manifolds is smooth if and only if composition with $f$ sends smooth functions to smooth functions, and then $f^\#(g) = g \circ f$ makes it a morphism of locally ringed spaces.

**Example P2.6.6 (ringed spaces that are not locally ringed).** A one point space with a ring $A$ is a ringed space whose modules are the $A$-modules. It is locally ringed only if $A$ is local. Every space $X$ with the constant sheaf $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$ is a ringed space, whose modules are the sheaves of abelian groups. So $\mathsf{Mod}(\underline{\mathbb{Z}}) = \mathsf{Ab}(X)$, and the theorem contains Theorem P2.5.20. The stalks are $\mathbb{Z}$, which is not local.

**Example P2.6.7 (vector bundles).** Let $X$ be a smooth manifold, $C^\infty_X$ its sheaf of smooth real functions and $\pi: E \to X$ a smooth real vector bundle of rank $r$. Its smooth sections $\mathcal{E}(U) = \{ s: U \to E \text{ smooth} : \pi s = 1_U \}$ form a sheaf, by the argument of Exercise 2 of Section 5.1. Sections can be added and multiplied by smooth functions fibre by fibre, so $\mathcal{E}$ is a $C^\infty_X$-module. On an open set $U$ where $E|_U \cong U \times \mathbb{R}^r$, the sheaf $\mathcal{E}|_U$ is isomorphic to $(C^\infty_U)^r$. This is the model of a locally free sheaf (Section 6.4). The bundle map $(x, v) \mapsto (x, xv)$ of $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ of Example P2.2.8 has no kernel among vector bundles, but it has a kernel and a cokernel in $\mathsf{Mod}(C^\infty_{\mathbb{R}})$. On sections it is multiplication by the function $x$. This is injective on every open set, because a continuous function $f$ with $x f = 0$ vanishes off $0$ and so vanishes everywhere. So the kernel is $0$. The cokernel has stalk $C^\infty_{\mathbb{R}, 0}/(x)$ at $0$ and $0$ elsewhere. By Hadamard's lemma a smooth germ $f$ with $f(0) = 0$ has the form $f = x g$ with $g$ smooth, so this stalk is $\mathbb{R}$, through $f \mapsto f(0)$. The next example names this cokernel.

**Example P2.6.8 (skyscraper modules and the sheaf of a point).** Let $x \in X$ and let $M$ be an $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-module. The skyscraper sheaf $x_* M$ of Section 5.1 is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module: for an open $U \ni x$, an element $a \in \mathcal{O}_X(U)$ acts on $x_* M(U) = M$ through its germ $a_x$. If $X$ is locally ringed and $x$ is a closed point, the *sheaf of the point* is $k(x) = x_* \kappa(x)$. For a variety over an algebraically closed field $k$ and a closed point $x$, $\kappa(x) = k$, and there is an exact sequence
$$0 \to \mathfrak{m}_x \to \mathcal{O}_X \to k(x) \to 0,$$
where $\mathfrak{m}_x$ is the ideal sheaf of functions that vanish at $x$ and $\mathcal{O}_X \to k(x)$ is evaluation at $x$. Exactness is checked on stalks: at $x$ it is $0 \to \mathfrak{m}_x \to \mathcal{O}_{X,x} \to k \to 0$, and at $y \neq x$ it is $0 \to \mathcal{O}_{X,y} \to \mathcal{O}_{X,y} \to 0 \to 0$, because $y$ has a neighbourhood that does not contain the closed point $x$. In the same way, on any locally ringed space with a closed point $x$, evaluation gives an exact sequence $0 \to \mathfrak{m}_x \to \mathcal{O}_X \to k(x) \to 0$. In Example P2.6.7 the cokernel of multiplication by $x$ on $C^\infty_{\mathbb{R}}$ is the sheaf $k(0)$ of the point $0$.

**Example P2.6.9 (the line bundles $\mathcal{O}(n)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$).** Let $k$ be a field and cover $\mathbb{P}^1$ by $U_0 = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$ and $U_1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[s]$, glued along $U_{01} = \operatorname{Spec} k[t, t^{-1}]$ by $s = t^{-1}$. For an integer $n$, glue the modules $\mathcal{O}_{U_0}$ and $\mathcal{O}_{U_1}$ along the isomorphism $\varphi_{01}: \mathcal{O}_{U_1}|_{U_{01}} \to \mathcal{O}_{U_0}|_{U_{01}}$ given by multiplication by $t^n$. The cocycle condition is empty for two open sets, so Theorem P2.5.35 gives a sheaf, and the gluing is $\mathcal{O}$-linear, so it is an $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}$-module $\mathcal{O}(n)$. A global section is a pair $(f, g)$ with $f \in k[t]$, $g \in k[s]$ and $f = t^n g(t^{-1})$ in $k[t, t^{-1}]$. If $n \geq 0$, then $g$ has degree at most $n$ and $f$ is determined by $g$, so
$$\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(n)) \cong \{ g \in k[s] : \deg g \leq n \}, \qquad \dim_k \Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(n)) = n + 1.$$
If $n < 0$, then $t^n g(t^{-1})$ has only negative powers of $t$, so it is a polynomial in $t$ only if $g = 0$, and $\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(n)) = 0$. The module $\mathcal{O}(n)$ is the sheaf $\mathcal{O}(n \cdot \infty)$ of the divisor $n$ times the point $\infty$ (Exercise 3 of Section 5.1). Chapter 7 computes its higher cohomology.

**Example P2.6.10 (the sheaf of a divisor).** Let $X$ be a normal variety and $D$ a Weil divisor. The sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ of Example P2.5.10 is a sub-$\mathcal{O}_X$-module of the constant sheaf $\underline{k(X)}$, since the condition $\operatorname{div} f + D \geq 0$ on $U$ is kept under multiplication by a regular function on $U$. If $D \geq 0$, then $\mathcal{O}_X(-D) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ is an ideal sheaf. If $D$ is a prime divisor, $\mathcal{O}_X(-D)$ is the ideal sheaf of the functions that vanish on $D$.

### Proof

*Proof of the theorem.* Part 1. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a presheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules. A section of $\mathcal{F}^+$ over $U$ is a family of germs $(\sigma_x)_{x \in U}$, $\sigma_x \in \mathcal{F}_x$, that is locally represented by sections of $\mathcal{F}$ (Section 5.2). For $a \in \mathcal{O}_X(U)$ put $a \cdot (\sigma_x) = (a_x \sigma_x)$. If $\sigma_y = s_y$ for $y \in V$, with $s \in \mathcal{F}(V)$, then $a_y \sigma_y = (a|_V s)_y$ for $y \in V$, so the new family is again locally represented. The module axioms hold germ by germ, and restriction commutes with the action. The map $\mathcal{F}(U) \to \mathcal{F}^+(U)$, $s \mapsto (s_x)$, is linear by the definition of the action on stalks. The structure is unique: two module structures for which this map is linear agree on the image of $\mathcal{F}$, and every section of $\mathcal{F}^+$ is locally in this image, so they agree by locality. For the universal property, let $\alpha: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$ be linear, with $\mathcal{G}$ an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module, and $\alpha^+: \mathcal{F}^+ \to \mathcal{G}$ the unique morphism of sheaves of Theorem P2.5.12 that extends it. On stalks $\alpha^+_x = \alpha_x$, which is $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-linear. For $a \in \mathcal{O}_X(U)$ and $\sigma \in \mathcal{F}^+(U)$, the sections $\alpha^+(a \sigma)$ and $a \alpha^+(\sigma)$ have the same germ $a_x \alpha_x(\sigma_x)$ at every $x$, so they are equal by Proposition P2.5.4. So $\alpha^+$ is linear.

Part 2. Let $\varphi: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$ be a morphism of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules. The kernel $(\ker \varphi)(U) = \ker \varphi_U$ is a submodule of $\mathcal{F}(U)$, so $\ker \varphi$ is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. A linear $\psi: \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{F}$ with $\varphi \psi = 0$ factors through $\ker \varphi$ in $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$, and the factorization is linear because it is $\psi$ on sections. So $\ker \varphi$ is the kernel in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$. The presheaf cokernel $U \mapsto \mathcal{G}(U)/\varphi_U(\mathcal{F}(U))$ is a presheaf of modules, and its sheafification is $\operatorname{coker} \varphi$ of $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$. By part 1 it is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module and the projection $\mathcal{G} \to \operatorname{coker} \varphi$ is linear. A linear map $\mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{H}$ that kills $\varphi$ factors linearly through the presheaf cokernel, and then through its sheafification by part 1. So $\operatorname{coker} \varphi$ is the cokernel in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$. The category is additive, with sums $(\mathcal{F} \oplus \mathcal{G})(U) = \mathcal{F}(U) \oplus \mathcal{G}(U)$ and the sum of linear maps as addition.

It remains to check the axioms of Definition P2.2.10. Let $\varphi$ be a monomorphism in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$. Then $\ker \varphi = 0$, since $\ker \varphi \to \mathcal{F}$ is linear and composes to $0$ with $\varphi$. Its kernel in $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$ is the same sheaf, so $\varphi$ is a monomorphism in $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$, and by Theorem P2.5.20 the natural morphism $\mathcal{F} \to \ker(\mathcal{G} \to \operatorname{coker} \varphi)$ is an isomorphism of sheaves. It is linear, and the inverse of a bijective linear map is linear, so it is an isomorphism of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules. So $\varphi$ is the kernel of its cokernel in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$. The dual statement for epimorphisms has the same proof. The forgetful functor keeps kernels and cokernels by construction, so it is exact, and it is faithful since a morphism of modules is determined by its maps of sections.

Part 3. By part 2 a sequence is exact in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$ if and only if it is exact in $\mathsf{Ab}(X)$, and Corollary P2.5.21 checks this on stalks. $\square$

*Proof of the proposition.* The section $1 \in \mathcal{O}_X(X)$ restricts to $1$ on every $U$. A linear $\varphi: \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{F}$ satisfies $\varphi_U(a) = a \varphi_U(1|_U) = a \cdot \varphi_X(1)|_U$, so it is determined by $s = \varphi_X(1)$. Conversely, for $s \in \mathcal{F}(X)$, the maps $a \mapsto a \, s|_U$ commute with restriction and are linear. The direct sum $\mathcal{O}_X^{(I)}$ is a coproduct in $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X)$: it is the sheafification of the presheaf direct sum (Exercise 6 of Section 5.3), which is a coproduct of presheaves of modules, and by part 1 of the theorem a linear map out of the sheafification is a linear map out of the presheaf. So $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_X^{(I)}, \mathcal{F}) \cong \prod_I \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{F}) \cong \prod_I \mathcal{F}(X)$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $(f, f^\#): X \to Y$ and $(g, g^\#): Y \to Z$ be morphisms of ringed spaces. Define their composite, and show that a composite of morphisms of locally ringed spaces is a morphism of locally ringed spaces.
2. (A) Let $U \subseteq X$ be open. Show that $(U, \mathcal{O}_X|_U)$ is a ringed space and that the inclusion is a morphism of locally ringed spaces when $X$ is locally ringed. Show that the restriction $\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}|_U$ is an exact functor $\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_X) \to \mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_U)$.
3. (A) Let $X$ be a locally ringed space, $\mathcal{F}$ an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module and $x \in X$. The *fibre* of $\mathcal{F}$ at $x$ is the $\kappa(x)$-vector space $\mathcal{F}(x) = \mathcal{F}_x \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}} \kappa(x)$. Show that the fibre of the vector bundle sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ of Example P2.6.7 at $x$ is the fibre $E_x$ of the bundle, and that the fibre of $C^\infty_X$ at $x$ is $\mathbb{R}$.
4. (B) Let $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ be an ideal sheaf and $Z = \{ x : \mathcal{I}_x \neq \mathcal{O}_{X,x} \}$. Show that $Z$ is closed, since $1 \in \mathcal{I}_x$ implies $1 \in \mathcal{I}(V)$ for a neighbourhood $V$ of $x$. Show that $Z$ is the support $\{ x : \mathcal{F}_x \neq 0 \}$ of $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{I}$. On $X = \mathbb{R}$ with its continuous functions, show that the support of the module $\bigoplus_{n \geq 1} (1/n)_* \mathbb{R}$ is not closed.
5. (B) A morphism of ringed spaces $(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to (\{\mathrm{pt}\}, A)$ to a point with a ring $A$ is the same as a ring map $A \to \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$. Prove this, and deduce that for a locally ringed space $X$ the morphisms to $(\{\mathrm{pt}\}, k)$, for a field $k$, are the $k$-algebra structures on $\mathcal{O}_X$.
6. (B) Let $X = \{a, b\}$ with open sets $\emptyset$, $\{a\}$ and $X$, and put $\mathcal{O}_X(X) = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, $\mathcal{O}_X(\{a\}) = \mathbb{Q}$. Show that $X$ is a locally ringed space, that it is $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, and that an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module is the same as a $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-module $M$, a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space $V$ and a $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-linear map $M \to V$. Which of these modules come from a $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-module $M$ with $V = M \otimes \mathbb{Q}$?

### Lean

Mathlib defines ringed spaces as sheafed spaces with values in commutative rings, `AlgebraicGeometry.RingedSpace`, and locally ringed spaces as `AlgebraicGeometry.LocallyRingedSpace`, whose morphisms have local maps on stalks. Sheaves of modules are defined on any site with a sheaf of rings, as `SheafOfModules`. The abelian structure is an instance in `Mathlib/Algebra/Category/ModuleCat/Sheaf/Abelian.lean`, deduced from the sheafification adjunction `PresheafOfModules.sheafificationAdjunction`. Free modules and maps from them are `SheafOfModules.free`, `SheafOfModules.unitHomEquiv` and `SheafOfModules.freeHomEquiv`. For a scheme, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules` is the category of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules. The check of exactness on stalks for modules is not stated separately. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Ringed spaces appear in Cartan's seminar and in Serre's paper *Faisceaux algébriques cohérents* of 1955, which defines a variety as a space with a sheaf of functions and studies sheaves of modules over it. Grothendieck's EGA made locally ringed spaces the setting of scheme theory: a scheme is a locally ringed space locally isomorphic to a spectrum, and a morphism of schemes is a morphism of locally ringed spaces. The condition that maps of stalks are local is what makes the value of a function at a point behave well under pullback.
