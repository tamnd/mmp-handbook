# Sheaf cohomology and Čech cohomology

The global sections functor is left exact but not exact. A surjection of sheaves is surjective only locally, and local preimages need not glue. Sheaf cohomology measures this failure. This chapter defines the groups $H^i(X, \mathcal{F})$ as the right derived functors of $\Gamma(X, -)$, with the machinery of Chapter 3, and gives two ways to compute them: resolutions by flasque sheaves and Čech complexes of open covers.

Section 7.1 proves that the $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules have enough injectives, defines sheaf cohomology and the higher direct images $R^i f_*$, and gives the long exact sequence and its first uses. Section 7.2 treats flasque sheaves: injective sheaves are flasque, flasque sheaves have no higher cohomology, pushforward keeps them, and so cohomology computed with $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules agrees with cohomology computed with sheaves of abelian groups. Section 7.3 defines Čech cohomology, proves that the Čech complex of a flasque sheaf is exact in positive degrees, proves Leray's theorem on acyclic covers and identifies $\check{H}^1$ inside $H^1$. It computes $H^i(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(n))$ by a Čech complex. Section 7.4 interprets $H^1$ as the group of torsors, proves $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^\times)$, computes $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^1) = \mathbb{Z}$, and explains how $H^1$ controls the lifting of sections.

The minimal model program uses this chapter in almost every proof. The vanishing theorems of P7 Ch. 8 and 9, Kodaira, Kawamata-Viehweg and Nadel vanishing, are statements $H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0$ for $i > 0$. The base point free theorem (Volume 7) lifts sections from a divisor $S$ to $X$ through the long exact sequence of $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD - S) \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \to \mathcal{O}_S(mD) \to 0$, once $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - S)) = 0$. The higher direct images $R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_Y$ of a resolution define rational singularities (Volume 4). The identification $\operatorname{Pic}(X) = H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^\times)$ and the exponential sequence give the first Chern class into $H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})$, whose image is the Néron-Severi group in which the cone theorem lives (Volume 7).

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Chapter III, Sections 1 to 4, and Godement, Chapter II, with details from the Stacks project, chapter Cohomology of sheaves. Proofs are given in full, except Grothendieck's vanishing theorem, which is stated with a reference. The chapter uses Chapters 3 and 4 for derived functors and double complexes, and Chapters 5 and 6 for sheaves. The cohomology of quasi-coherent sheaves on affine schemes, which feeds Leray's theorem in the examples, is quoted from P4 Ch. 1.
