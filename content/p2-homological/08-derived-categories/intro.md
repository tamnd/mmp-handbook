# Derived categories

Chapters 3 and 7 work with one cohomology group at a time. A resolution is a complex, but only its cohomology objects survive, and the long exact sequences and spectral sequences relate these objects after the fact. The derived category keeps the whole complex. Its objects are complexes, and a quasi-isomorphism becomes an isomorphism. A left exact functor then has one total derived functor $RF$, and the groups $R^i F$ are the cohomology objects of $RF$. Compositions of derived functors become compositions of functors, and many identities between spectral sequences become isomorphisms between complexes.

Section 8.1 builds the homotopy category $K(\mathcal{A})$, the mapping cone and distinguished triangles, and proves the long exact sequence of a triangle. Section 8.2 localizes at quasi-isomorphisms to get $D(\mathcal{A})$, describes its morphisms by roofs, proves that bounded below complexes of injectives compute morphisms, and identifies $\operatorname{Ext}^i(A, B)$ with $\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal{A})}(A, B[i])$. Section 8.3 defines total derived functors and the functors of geometry: $R\Gamma$, $Rf_*$, $Lf^*$, $\otimes^L$, $R\operatorname{Hom}$ and $R\mathcal{H}om$, with the adjunction $Lf^* \dashv Rf_*$ and the projection formula. Section 8.4 states Grothendieck duality, defines the dualizing complex, and explains how it gives the canonical sheaf $\omega_X$, Serre duality, the Cohen-Macaulay condition, and the Grauert-Riemenschneider and Kempf criteria for rational singularities.

The minimal model program uses this chapter in three places. Rational singularities are defined by the condition $\mathcal{O}_X \cong Rf_* \mathcal{O}_Y$ for a resolution $f: Y \to X$ (Volume 4). The canonical sheaf $\omega_X$ of a normal variety, and so the canonical divisor $K_X$ on which the whole program turns, is the lowest cohomology sheaf of the dualizing complex. Duality turns the vanishing of $R^i f_* \omega_Y$ into statements about $Rf_* \mathcal{O}_Y$, and these are the tools of P4 Ch. 9 and of Volumes 4 and 5.

The chapter follows Weibel, Chapter 10, for triangulated and derived categories, Hartshorne's *Residues and duality* and Conrad's book for duality, and Huybrechts, Chapters 2 and 3, for the functors of geometry. The proofs that use only cones and homotopies are given in full. The octahedral axiom, the existence of unbounded derived functors and Grothendieck duality are long, and they are stated with a sketch and a reference. P4 Ch. 9 develops the duality theory for the applications.
