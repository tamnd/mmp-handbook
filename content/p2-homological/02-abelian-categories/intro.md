# Abelian categories

The category of modules over a ring has kernels, cokernels and exact sequences, and so does the category of sheaves of abelian groups on a space. The two categories look very different. A module is a set with operations, while a sheaf is a rule that assigns a group to each open set. But the proofs of homological algebra use only the kernels, the cokernels and the addition of maps. An abelian category is a category with these structures and the axioms that make them behave as they do for modules. This chapter defines abelian categories and proves the results about exact sequences that the rest of the volume uses.

Section 2.1 defines additive categories: categories in which the maps between two objects form an abelian group and finite direct sums exist. Section 2.2 defines abelian categories, proves that in such a category the image and the coimage of a map agree, and states the embedding theorem of Freyd and Mitchell, which says that a small abelian category is a full subcategory of a category of modules. Section 2.3 proves the five lemma and the snake lemma. Section 2.4 is about exact functors and about projective and injective objects. It proves Baer's criterion and that every module embeds in an injective module.

The minimal model program uses this chapter through sheaves. The coherent sheaves on a variety form an abelian category (P3 Ch. 5), and so do the sheaves of abelian groups (Chapter 5). The snake lemma of Section 2.3 is the source of every long exact sequence of cohomology. In the proof of the base point free theorem in Volume 7, the long exact sequence of $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD - S) \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \to \mathcal{O}_S(mD) \to 0$ for a divisor $S$ lifts sections from $S$ to $X$ once a vanishing theorem kills $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - S))$. Injective objects, from Section 2.4, are what Chapter 7 uses to define sheaf cohomology.

The chapter follows Weibel, Chapter 1 and Appendix A, and Vakil, Chapter 1. Freyd's book *Abelian categories* and Grothendieck's Tohoku paper are the sources of the theory. The chapter uses Chapter 1 and P1 Ch. 1.
