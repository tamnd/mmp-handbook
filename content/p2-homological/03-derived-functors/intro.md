# Derived functors

A left exact functor keeps the kernel of a map but loses the cokernel. The global sections functor on sheaves is the example that matters most for geometry: an onto map of sheaves need not be onto on global sections. Derived functors measure this loss. For a left exact functor $F$ they give functors $R^i F$ and, for each short exact sequence $0 \to A' \to A \to A'' \to 0$, a long exact sequence
$$0 \to F(A') \to F(A) \to F(A'') \to R^1 F(A') \to R^1 F(A) \to R^1 F(A'') \to R^2 F(A') \to \cdots.$$
So $F(A) \to F(A'')$ is onto as soon as $R^1 F(A') = 0$. This one statement is behind most uses of cohomology in the minimal model program.

Section 3.1 defines complexes, their cohomology and homotopies, and proves that a short exact sequence of complexes gives a long exact sequence of cohomology. Section 3.2 constructs injective resolutions, proves that they are unique up to homotopy, and defines the right derived functors $R^i F$ and the left derived functors $L_i F$. Section 3.3 introduces $\delta$-functors and proves Grothendieck's theorem that the derived functors are the universal $\delta$-functor. A consequence is that any resolution by $F$-acyclic objects computes $R^i F$. Section 3.4 applies this to the two derived functors of algebra, $\operatorname{Ext}$ and $\operatorname{Tor}$. It proves that $\operatorname{Ext}$ can be computed from either variable, that $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ classifies extensions, and the criterion of flatness by $\operatorname{Tor}$ that P1 Ch. 1 announced.

The minimal model program uses this chapter at every step. Sheaf cohomology is $H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) = R^i \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ (Chapter 7), and the higher direct images $R^i f_* \mathcal{F}$ of a morphism $f$ are the derived functors of $f_*$ (P4 Ch. 4). The vanishing theorems of P7 Ch. 8 and 9 and of Volume 5 are statements that some $R^i$ is zero. Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing gives $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L)) = 0$ for $i > 0$, $X$ smooth projective over a field of characteristic $0$ and $L$ nef and big, and the relative form gives $R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) = 0$. The proof of the base point free theorem in Volume 7 uses exactly the long exact sequence above, with $R^1 F(A') = H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - S)) = 0$, to lift sections from a divisor $S$ to $X$. The acyclic resolutions of Section 3.3 are how Chapter 7 compares sheaf cohomology with Čech cohomology. $\operatorname{Ext}$ sheaves and Serre duality (P4 Ch. 7 and 8) are built on Section 3.4, and $\operatorname{Tor}$ is the language of flat families and base change (P3 Ch. 13, P4 Ch. 5), which the Hilbert and Hom schemes of P8 Ch. 1 use.

The chapter follows Weibel, Chapters 1 to 3, and Hartshorne, Chapter III, Section 1. The theory of $\delta$-functors is from Grothendieck's Tohoku paper. The chapter uses Chapter 2.
