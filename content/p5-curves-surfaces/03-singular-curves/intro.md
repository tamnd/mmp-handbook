# Singular curves

An integral projective curve $C$ over an algebraically closed field has a normalization $\nu \colon \widetilde{C} \to C$, a smooth projective curve that is finite and birational over $C$. The failure of $C$ to be smooth at a point $P$ is measured by the *$\delta$-invariant*, the length of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_P / \mathcal{O}_P$. The arithmetic genus of $C$ is the genus of $\widetilde{C}$ plus the sum of the $\delta$-invariants, so a plane curve of degree $d$ has geometric genus at most $(d-1)(d-2)/2$, with the deficit measured by its singular points.

Section 3.1 proves the genus formula, computes $\delta$ for nodes, cusps and the singularities $y^a = x^b$, and proves the bound $\delta_P \geq m_P(m_P - 1)/2$ for a point of multiplicity $m_P$ on a plane curve. Section 3.2 describes the dualizing sheaf of a singular curve through the normalization. Its consequences are Riemann-Roch for invertible sheaves on any integral projective curve, Serre duality in the form $h^1(L) = h^0(\omega^\circ_C \otimes L^{-1})$ on a Gorenstein curve, and Serre's formula $\ell(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_P / \mathfrak{c}_P) = 2 \delta_P$ for the conductor of a Gorenstein point.

Singular curves arise in the minimal model program in three ways. A stable or semistable curve, with only nodes, is the one-dimensional case of a semi-log-canonical variety (Volume 4), and moduli of stable curves are the first example of a compact moduli space built from the program. The rational curves produced by bend and break (Volume 6) are images of $\mathbb{P}^1$ and may be singular, and the bound on their degree is a bound on the arithmetic genus of their images. The adjunction formula $K_C = (K_S + C)|_C$ for a curve on a surface (Chapter 5) gives $\omega^\circ_C$ even when $C$ is singular, and this is the starting point of adjunction theory in higher dimension.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Exercises IV.1.8 and V.3.9, Serre's *Algebraic groups and class fields*, Chapter IV, and Liu, Section 7.5.
