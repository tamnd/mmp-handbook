# Elliptic curves and bundles on the projective line

This chapter treats the two simplest classes of curves and sheaves in detail: curves of genus $1$, and vector bundles on curves of genus $0$.

A smooth projective curve of genus $1$ with a chosen point $O$ is an *elliptic curve*. By Riemann-Roch it is a plane cubic in Weierstrass form, and the bijection $P \mapsto [P - O]$ onto $\operatorname{Pic}^0$ of Chapter 1 makes it a group. Section 4.1 proves that the group law is a morphism, so that an elliptic curve is a one-dimensional abelian variety. It also classifies elliptic curves by the $j$-invariant and describes the surface $E \times E$ through the group law. Section 4.2 proves Grothendieck's theorem: every vector bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ is a direct sum of line bundles $\mathcal{O}(a_i)$, with the integers $a_i$ determined by the bundle.

Both results recur throughout the minimal model program. Elliptic curves are the general fibres of elliptic fibrations, which are the Iitaka fibrations of surfaces of Kodaira dimension $1$ (Chapter 11) and the subject of the canonical bundle formula in Volume 10. They are also the curves on which $K_X$ restricts trivially in the abundance problem. Grothendieck's theorem is the reason rational curves are the right test curves. For a morphism $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to X$, the pullback $f^* T_X$ splits as $\bigoplus \mathcal{O}(a_i)$, and the signs of the $a_i$ control the deformations of $f$. This is the starting point of bend and break and of the theory of rationally connected varieties in Volume 6.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Section IV.4 and Exercise V.2.6, Silverman, Chapters III and IV, and Kollár's *Rational curves on algebraic varieties*, Section II.3.
