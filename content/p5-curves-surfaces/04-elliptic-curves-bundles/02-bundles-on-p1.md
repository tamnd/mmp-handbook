## Vector bundles on the projective line

### Statement

In this section $k$ is any field, and a *vector bundle* on a scheme is a locally free sheaf of finite rank. On $\mathbb{P}^1 = \mathbb{P}^1_k$, $h^0(\mathcal{O}(n)) = \max(n + 1, 0)$ and $h^1(\mathcal{O}(n)) = \max(-n - 1, 0)$ by Theorem P4.2.1.

**Theorem P5.4.10 (Grothendieck).** Every vector bundle $\mathcal{E}$ of rank $r$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$ is isomorphic to
$$\mathcal{O}(a_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathcal{O}(a_r), \qquad a_1 \geq a_2 \geq \cdots \geq a_r,$$
and the integers $a_i$, the *splitting type* of $\mathcal{E}$, are determined by $\mathcal{E}$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P5.4.11.** Let $\mathcal{E} \cong \bigoplus_i \mathcal{O}(a_i)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$.

1. $\deg \mathcal{E} = \deg \det \mathcal{E} = \sum_i a_i$, and $h^0(\mathcal{E}(n)) = \sum_i \max(a_i + n + 1, 0)$.
2. $\mathcal{E}$ is generated by global sections if and only if all $a_i \geq 0$, if and only if $H^1(\mathcal{E}(-1)) = 0$.
3. All $a_i \geq 1$ if and only if $H^1(\mathcal{E}(-2)) = 0$, if and only if $\mathcal{E}(-1)$ is generated by global sections.
4. $\mathcal{E}$ is determined up to isomorphism by the function $n \mapsto h^0(\mathcal{E}(n))$, and in particular by its rank and the dimensions $h^0(\mathcal{E}(n))$ for $-a_1 - 1 \leq n \leq -a_r$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P5.4.12 (free and very free rational curves).** Let $X$ be a smooth variety and $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ a nonconstant morphism, with $f^* T_X \cong \bigoplus_i \mathcal{O}(a_i)$. The curve $f$ is *free* if all $a_i \geq 0$ and *very free* if all $a_i \geq 1$. The differential is a map $T_{\mathbb{P}^1} = \mathcal{O}(2) \to f^* T_X$. When it is nonzero, for instance always in characteristic $0$, it has a nonzero component $\mathcal{O}(2) \to \mathcal{O}(a_i)$, so $a_1 \geq 2$.

### Proof in words

Twist $\mathcal{E}$ down until it has no more sections. The last twist with a nonzero section, say $\mathcal{E}(-a)$, has a section that vanishes nowhere, since otherwise it would be a section of $\mathcal{E}(-a-1)$. So $\mathcal{O}(a)$ is a subbundle, and the quotient splits by induction on the rank. The extension of the quotient by $\mathcal{O}(a)$ splits because the quotient has no summand of degree greater than $a$, and $H^1(\mathcal{O}(d)) = 0$ for $d \geq -1$. The splitting type is read off from the dimensions $h^0(\mathcal{E}(n))$.

### Examples

**Example P5.4.13 (the tangent bundle of projective space on a line).** Let $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be a line. Restricting the Euler sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}(1)^{n+1} \to T_{\mathbb{P}^n} \to 0$ of Theorem P3.12.20 to $L$ gives a quotient of $\mathcal{O}(1)^{n+1}$ of rank $n$ and degree $n + 1$, containing $T_L = \mathcal{O}(2)$. By Theorem P5.4.10, $T_{\mathbb{P}^n}|_L \cong \bigoplus \mathcal{O}(a_i)$, and all $a_i \geq 1$: twisting by $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ makes $T_{\mathbb{P}^n}|_L(-1)$ a quotient of $\mathcal{O}^{n+1}$, so generated by global sections, and part 3 of Corollary P5.4.11 applies. With $\sum a_i = n + 1$ and $a_1 \geq 2$, the only possibility is
$$T_{\mathbb{P}^n}|_L \cong \mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(1)^{n-1}.$$
So lines in $\mathbb{P}^n$ are very free, and the normal bundle is $N_{L/\mathbb{P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(1)^{n-1}$.

**Example P5.4.14 (a conic and a rational normal curve).** Let $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ be a smooth conic, $\nu \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to C$ an isomorphism, so $\nu^* \mathcal{O}(1) = \mathcal{O}(2)$. Then $\nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2}$ is a quotient of $\mathcal{O}(2)^3$ of rank $2$ and degree $6$ containing $\mathcal{O}(2)$, so its splitting type $(a_1, a_2)$ has $a_1 + a_2 = 6$ and $a_2 \geq 2$. To decide between $(4, 2)$ and $(3, 3)$, twist the restricted Euler sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}(2)^3 \to \nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \to 0$ by $\mathcal{O}(-4)$. Since $H^0(\mathcal{O}(-2)^3) = 0$, $H^0(\nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-4))$ is the kernel of the map $H^1(\mathcal{O}(-4)) \to H^1(\mathcal{O}(-2))^3$ given by the three quadrics $s^2, st, t^2$ that define $\nu$. By Serre duality its transpose is $k^3 = H^0(\mathcal{O})^3 \to H^0(\mathcal{O}(2))$, $(c_1, c_2, c_3) \mapsto c_1 s^2 + c_2 st + c_3 t^2$, an isomorphism. So $h^0(\nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-4)) = 0$, $a_1 \leq 3$, and $\nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \cong \mathcal{O}(3) \oplus \mathcal{O}(3)$. The normal bundle is $\mathcal{O}_C(C) \cong \mathcal{O}(4)$, the quotient by $T_C = \mathcal{O}(2)$, so the sequence $0 \to T_C \to \nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \to N_C \to 0$ does not split. For the rational normal curve of degree $d$ in $\mathbb{P}^d$, the same argument with the $d + 1$ monomials of degree $d$ gives $\nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^d} \cong \mathcal{O}(d + 1)^d$.

**Example P5.4.15 (a jump of splitting type).** Extensions $0 \to \mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{O}(1) \to 0$ are classified by $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathcal{O}(1), \mathcal{O}(-1)) = H^1(\mathcal{O}(-2)) \cong k$ (Proposition P4.7.2). The split extension is $\mathcal{O}(1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$. A nonsplit extension has $\deg \mathcal{E} = 0$, and $\mathcal{E} \not\cong \mathcal{O}(1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$. It has $h^0(\mathcal{E}(-1)) = 0$, since $H^0(\mathcal{O}(-2)) = 0$ and the connecting map $H^0(\mathcal{O}) \to H^1(\mathcal{O}(-2))$ is the extension class, nonzero. So $a_1 \leq 0$, and $a_1 + a_2 = 0$ forces $\mathcal{E} \cong \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}$. Scaling the class by $t \in k$ gives a family over $\mathbb{A}^1$ with splitting type $(0, 0)$ for $t \neq 0$ and $(1, -1)$ at $t = 0$. The splitting type is upper semicontinuous: it can only become less balanced under specialization, as Theorem P4.5.13 predicts for $h^0(\mathcal{E}(-1))$.

**Example P5.4.16 (Grothendieck's theorem fails in genus $1$).** On an elliptic curve $E$, $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathcal{O}, \mathcal{O}) = H^1(\mathcal{O}_E) \cong k$, and a nonsplit extension $0 \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{F}_2 \to \mathcal{O} \to 0$ is not a direct sum of line bundles. Suppose $\mathcal{F}_2 \cong \mathcal{L} \oplus \mathcal{M}$, so $\deg \mathcal{L} + \deg \mathcal{M} = 0$. If $\deg \mathcal{L} > 0$, then $\mathcal{L} \to \mathcal{F}_2 \to \mathcal{O}$ is zero, so $\mathcal{L}$ maps into the subsheaf $\mathcal{O}$, which is impossible for degree reasons. So $\deg \mathcal{L} = \deg \mathcal{M} = 0$. The inclusion $\mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{F}_2$ has a nonzero component $\mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{L}$ or $\mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{M}$, and a nonzero map of invertible sheaves of degree $0$ on a curve is an isomorphism. So $\mathcal{O}$ is a direct summand of $\mathcal{F}_2$ and the sequence splits, a contradiction. This is Atiyah's bundle $\mathcal{F}_2$, the first of the indecomposable bundles of his classification of vector bundles on elliptic curves.

### Proof

*Theorem P5.4.10, existence.* Induct on $r$, the case $r = 1$ being $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (Corollary P3.9.11). Let $r \geq 2$. By Theorem P4.2.7, $H^0(\mathcal{E}(n)) \neq 0$ for $n$ large. By Corollary P4.7.19, $H^0(\mathcal{E}(-n)) \cong H^1(\mathcal{E}^\vee(n - 2))'$, which vanishes for $n$ large by Theorem P4.2.7 again. So
$$a = \max\{ n : H^0(\mathcal{E}(-n)) \neq 0 \}$$
exists. Let $s \in H^0(\mathcal{E}(-a))$ be nonzero. If $s$ vanished at a closed point $p$, with $\mathcal{O}(-p) \cong \mathcal{O}(-\deg p)$, then $s$ would be a section of $\mathcal{E}(-a) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-p) \subseteq \mathcal{E}(-a-1)$, which is zero. So $s$ is nowhere zero: at every point its image in the fibre $\mathcal{E}(-a) \otimes \kappa(p)$ is nonzero, and $s \colon \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{E}(-a)$ is locally a split injection, by Nakayama's lemma applied to a basis of the fibre extending $s(p)$. So $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{E}(-a)/\mathcal{O}$ is a vector bundle of rank $r - 1$, and by induction $\mathcal{F} \cong \bigoplus_{j=2}^r \mathcal{O}(b_j)$.

From $0 \to \mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathcal{E}(-a-1) \to \mathcal{F}(-1) \to 0$ and $H^0(\mathcal{E}(-a-1)) = 0$, $H^1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = 0$, we get $H^0(\mathcal{F}(-1)) = 0$, so every $b_j \leq 0$. The extension $0 \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{E}(-a) \to \mathcal{F} \to 0$ has class in
$$\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{O}) \cong H^1(\mathcal{F}^\vee) = \bigoplus_j H^1(\mathcal{O}(-b_j)) = 0,$$
by Proposition P4.7.2 and $-b_j \geq 0$. So $\mathcal{E}(-a) \cong \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{F}$, and $\mathcal{E} \cong \mathcal{O}(a) \oplus \bigoplus_j \mathcal{O}(a + b_j)$, with $a \geq a + b_j$.

*Uniqueness and Corollary P5.4.11.* Part 1 is additivity of $\det$ and of $h^0$ on direct sums. For part 4, the function $\phi(n) = h^0(\mathcal{E}(n)) = \sum_i \max(a_i + n + 1, 0)$ has second difference $\phi(n) - 2\phi(n-1) + \phi(n-2) = \#\{ i : a_i = -n \}$, for every $n$. So the multiset of the $a_i$ is determined by $\phi$, which proves uniqueness in Theorem P5.4.10. Parts 2 and 3: $\mathcal{O}(a)$ is generated by global sections if and only if $a \geq 0$, and $H^1(\mathcal{O}(a - 1)) = 0$ if and only if $a - 1 \geq -1$; similarly with $a - 2$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^n}|_L \cong \mathcal{O}(-2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)^{n-1}$ for a line $L$, and that $N_{L/\mathbb{P}^n}$ has $h^0 = 2(n-1)$, the dimension of the Grassmannian of lines.
2. (A) Find the splitting type of $\mathcal{O}(a) \otimes \mathcal{E}$, of $\mathcal{E}^\vee$, of $\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(1))$ and of $\bigwedge^2 (\mathcal{O}(1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(5))$.
3. (B) Show that a vector bundle $\mathcal{E}$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$ is trivial if and only if $H^0(\mathcal{E}(-1)) = H^1(\mathcal{E}(-1)) = 0$. Find the splitting type of the kernel of the surjection $\mathcal{O}^3 \to \mathcal{O}(2)$ given by $s^2, st, t^2$.
4. (B) Complete Example P5.4.14 for a rational normal curve $\nu \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^d$: show that $\nu^* T_{\mathbb{P}^d} \cong \mathcal{O}(d+1)^d$ and that the normal bundle is $\mathcal{O}(d+2)^{d-1}$.
5. (B) Show that a vector bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ that is generated by global sections and has degree $0$ is trivial, and that a subbundle of a trivial bundle with a trivial quotient is trivial.
6. (C) Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$ and $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ a free rational curve. Show that $H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, f^* T_X) = 0$, so that by deformation theory (Volume 6) $f$ deforms in a family of dimension $h^0(f^* T_X) = -K_X \cdot f_* \mathbb{P}^1 + \dim X$, and that the images of the deformations cover a dense subset of $X$.

### Lean

Mathlib does not formalize vector bundles on $\mathbb{P}^1$ as locally free sheaves, or Grothendieck's splitting theorem. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Grothendieck proved the splitting theorem in 1957, in the form that every principal $G$-bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ reduces to a maximal torus, for a reductive group $G$. The case of vector bundles was known earlier through the classification of matrices over $k[t]$ and $k[t^{-1}]$ by Dedekind and Weber (1882) and Birkhoff (1909). The literature state is theorem. In the minimal model program, the splitting type of $f^* T_X$ for a rational curve is the basic invariant of the theory of rational curves. Mori's bend and break produces rational curves with $-K_X \cdot C \leq \dim X + 1$, and a variety is rationally connected exactly when it contains a very free rational curve, the theorem of Kollár, Miyaoka and Mori in Volume 6.
