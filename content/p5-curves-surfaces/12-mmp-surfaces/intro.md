# The minimal model program for surfaces

The minimal model program for a smooth projective surface $S$ is a finite sequence of contractions of $(-1)$-curves that ends either at a surface with nef canonical class or at a fibration whose fibres are negative for the canonical class. This chapter derives the program from the geometry of the cone of curves, the way Mori's theory does in every dimension, so that each step is the contraction of a $K$-negative extremal ray. The tools are the intersection theory of Chapter 5, the blow-ups of Chapter 6, Castelnuovo's contraction theorem and the minimal models of Chapter 11. Two results are cited rather than proved: the existence of rational curves in $K$-negative extremal rays, which is Mori's bend-and-break theorem, and the fact that a surface with Picard number $1$ and ample anticanonical class is $\mathbb{P}^2$.

Section 12.1 sets up the real vector space $N^1(S) = \operatorname{Num}(S) \otimes \mathbb{R}$, the closed cone of curves $\overline{NE}(S)$, the nef cone and the positive cone. The Hodge index theorem of Chapter 5 puts the positive cone inside $\overline{NE}(S)$ and the nef cone inside the closed positive cone, and the Nakai-Moishezon criterion then gives Kleiman's criterion: a divisor is ample exactly when it is positive on $\overline{NE}(S) \setminus \{0\}$, and the nef cone and $\overline{NE}(S)$ are dual. The examples are the plane blown up at a point, the Hirzebruch surfaces, the round cone of $E \times E$ and surfaces with trivial canonical class.

Section 12.2 proves the cone theorem for surfaces: $\overline{NE}(S)$ is the part where $K_S \geq 0$ plus a sum of rays spanned by rational curves $C_i$ with $0 < -K_S \cdot C_i \leq 3$, and these rays are locally finite away from the hyperplane $K_S^\perp$. Riemann-Roch and the Hodge index theorem then classify the $K$-negative extremal rays. A ray of negative square is spanned by a $(-1)$-curve, which contracts by Castelnuovo's theorem. A ray of square zero gives a morphism onto a curve all of whose fibres are $\mathbb{P}^1$, and forces $\rho(S) = 2$. A ray of positive square forces $\rho(S) = 1$ and $S \cong \mathbb{P}^2$.

Section 12.3 runs the program. Each divisorial step lowers the Picard number by $1$, so the program stops after at most $\rho(S) - 1$ steps, at a minimal model or at a Mori fibre space, and the two outcomes exclude each other. The explicit runs on the plane blown up at one, two and six points show that the minimal model, when it exists, is reached by contracting $(-1)$-curves, while the Mori fibre space at the end of a run is not unique: the cubic surface has runs ending at $\mathbb{P}^2$ in two different ways, at $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ and at $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$. This is the surface case of the program of Volume 2, where flips, terminal singularities and the termination problem appear.

The chapter follows Kollár and Mori, Chapter 1, Hartshorne, Section V.1, Reid's *Chapters on algebraic surfaces* and Matsuki's *Introduction to the Mori program*.
