# Ruled, rational and del Pezzo surfaces

The surfaces of this chapter are the surfaces with $K$ negative in some direction. On a projective bundle $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ over a curve the canonical class has degree $-2$ on every fibre, on a rational surface $K$ is never nef, and on a del Pezzo surface $-K$ is ample. In the language of the minimal model program they are the outcomes of the program that are not minimal models: Mori fibre spaces over a curve, Mori fibre spaces over a point, and the surfaces from which a sequence of contractions of $(-1)$-curves leads to them.

Section 9.1 studies a geometrically ruled surface $\pi \colon X \to C$, a surface whose closed fibres are projective lines and which has a section. Every such surface is the projective bundle $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ of a vector bundle of rank $2$ on $C$, determined up to a twist by a line bundle. The Picard group is $\mathbb{Z} h \oplus \pi^* \operatorname{Pic}(C)$, the intersection form on $\operatorname{Num}(X) = \mathbb{Z} h \oplus \mathbb{Z} f$ is unimodular, and $K \equiv -2h + (2g - 2 + \deg \mathcal{E}) f$, so $K^2 = 8(1 - g)$. A normalization of $\mathcal{E}$ gives the invariant $e$, the negative of the smallest self-intersection of a section, with $e \geq -2g$, and $e \leq 2g - 2$ unless $\mathcal{E}$ splits. Over $\mathbb{P}^1$ every ruled surface is a Hirzebruch surface $F_n$, the integer $n = e$ determines $F_n$ up to isomorphism, and an elementary transformation, the blow-up of a point followed by the contraction of the strict transform of its fibre, passes from $F_n$ to $F_{n \pm 1}$.

Section 9.2 proves the Noether-Enriques theorem: a surface with a morphism to a curve whose general fibre is $\mathbb{P}^1$ is birational to $B \times \mathbb{P}^1$. The proof contracts the $(-1)$-curves in the fibres, embeds the resulting conic bundle by $-K$ into a $\mathbb{P}^2$-bundle, and uses Tsen's theorem to find a section. For a rational surface the invariants are $q = p_g = 0$ and $K^2 + \rho = 10$, and every minimal rational surface is $\mathbb{P}^2$ or $F_n$ with $n \neq 1$. The last statement uses the cone theorem P5.12.14 and the classification of $K$-negative extremal rays in Theorem P5.12.15, and its proof identifies a rational surface with $\rho = 1$ and $-K$ ample with $\mathbb{P}^2$ directly; it gives the surfaces $S_0$ of the classification in Theorem P5.11.26 explicitly in the rational case.

Section 9.3 treats del Pezzo surfaces. The blow-up $S_r$ of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in $r \leq 8$ points in general position has $-K$ ample and degree $K^2 = 9 - r$. Its $(-1)$-curves are the classes $D$ with $D^2 = K \cdot D = -1$, and their numbers $1, 3, 6, 10, 16, 27, 56, 240$ come out of an arithmetic enumeration. For $r \leq 6$ the anticanonical system embeds $S_r$ in $\mathbb{P}^{9 - r}$ as a surface of degree $9 - r$: a cubic surface for $r = 6$, treated in Chapter 10, and an intersection of two quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^4$ for $r = 5$.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Sections V.2 and V.4, Beauville, Chapters III, IV and V, and the third and fourth chapters of Reid's *Chapters on algebraic surfaces*.
