## The Noether-Enriques theorem and minimal rational surfaces

### Statement

Let $S$ be a surface and $B$ a smooth projective curve. A *fibration* $p \colon S \to B$ is a surjective morphism. For a closed point $b \in B$ write $S_b = p^* b$ for the fibre, an effective divisor whose underlying closed subscheme is the scheme-theoretic fibre, and $F$ for the class of a fibre once all fibres are known to be numerically equivalent. As in Section 9.1, a section of $p$ is a morphism $\sigma \colon B \to S$ with $p \sigma = \mathrm{id}$, and $\rho(S)$ is the rank of $\operatorname{Num}(S)$.

**Definition P5.9.15 (rational, ruled and minimal surfaces).** A surface $S$ is *rational* if it is birational to $\mathbb{P}^2$, and *birationally ruled* over a curve $B$ if it is birational to $B \times \mathbb{P}^1$; these are the notions of Definition P5.11.23. A surface is *minimal* if it contains no $(-1)$-curve (Definition P5.7.1), as in Definition P5.11.13. A *minimal rational surface* is a rational surface that is minimal.

**Proposition P5.9.16 (fibrations with a rational fibre).** Let $p \colon S \to B$ be a fibration with $S_{b_0} \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ for one closed point $b_0$.

1. $p$ is flat, $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_B$, and every fibre is connected.
2. All closed fibres are numerically equivalent, $F^2 = 0$ and $K \cdot F = -2$.
3. An integral fibre $S_b$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$.
4. If $S_b$ is not integral, then $S_b = \sum n_i C_i$ has at least two components, every component has $C_i^2 < 0$, and some component is a $(-1)$-curve.
5. Let $E$ be a $(-1)$-curve contained in a fibre and $\psi \colon S \to S'$ its contraction (Theorem P5.7.4). There is a fibration $p' \colon S' \to B$ with $p = p' \psi$, it has $S'_{b_0} \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, and $\rho(S') = \rho(S) - 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P5.9.17 (relatively minimal models of a rational fibration).** Let $p \colon S \to B$ be a fibration with one closed fibre isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$. There is a composite $\psi \colon S \to S'$ of at most $\rho(S) - 2$ contractions of $(-1)$-curves contained in fibres and a fibration $p' \colon S' \to B$ with $p = p' \psi$, all of whose closed fibres are isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$. The morphism $\psi$ is an isomorphism over the complement of finitely many closed points of $B$. If $S$ has a divisor $D$ with $D \cdot F = 1$, then $S'$ is a geometrically ruled surface $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ over $B$, and $\rho(S') = 2$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P5.9.18 (Noether-Enriques).** Let $p \colon S \to B$ be a fibration with one closed fibre isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$. Then there is a nonempty open $U \subseteq B$ with $p^{-1}(U) \cong U \times \mathbb{P}^1$ over $U$. In particular $S$ is birationally ruled over $B$, $p$ has a section, and $S$ has a birational morphism over $B$ onto a geometrically ruled surface over $B$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P5.9.19 (invariants of rational surfaces).** Let $S$ be a rational surface.

1. $h^1(\mathcal{O}_S) = h^2(\mathcal{O}_S) = 0$, so $q = p_g = 0$ and $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 1$, and $K_S^2 + \rho(S) = 10$.
2. $\operatorname{Pic}(S) \to \operatorname{Num}(S)$ is an isomorphism, and the intersection form on $\operatorname{Num}(S)$ is unimodular.
3. $K_S$ is not nef.
4. If $p \colon S \to B$ is a fibration with $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_B$, then $B \cong \mathbb{P}^1$.
5. If $p \colon S \to B$ is a fibration with one closed fibre isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, then the fibre class $F$ is primitive in $\operatorname{Num}(S)$, and there is a divisor $D$ with $D \cdot F = 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P5.9.20 (minimal rational surfaces).**

1. $\mathbb{P}^2$ and the $F_n$ with $n \geq 0$, $n \neq 1$, are minimal rational surfaces, and no two of them are isomorphic. $F_1$ is not minimal.
2. Every minimal rational surface is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^2$ or to $F_n$ for some $n \neq 1$.
3. Every rational surface has a birational morphism onto $\mathbb{P}^2$ or onto some $F_n$ with $n \neq 1$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A fibration with one fibre a line is flat, so all its fibres have the numbers of a line: square $0$ and canonical degree $-2$. A fibre that is not a line breaks up, and the parity of $D \cdot (D + K)$ forbids a double line, so it has several components, each of negative square, and one of them is a $(-1)$-curve. Contracting it keeps the fibration, and after finitely many steps every fibre is a line. Then $-K$ has degree $2$ on every fibre and maps the surface into a bundle of planes, with fibres conics. Tsen's theorem gives the generic conic a rational point, whose closure is a section, and a fibration by lines with a section is a projective bundle, locally a product.

For a rational surface, the invariants that do not change under blow-ups are those of $\mathbb{P}^2$, so $q = p_g = 0$ and $K^2 + \rho = 10$, and the lattice of curves is an orthogonal summand of the odd unimodular lattice $\langle 1 \rangle \oplus \langle -1 \rangle^n$ of an iterated blow-up of the plane. A line in the plane, pulled back, shows that $K$ is not nef. The cone theorem P5.12.14 and Theorem P5.12.15 then leaves two possibilities for a minimal rational surface: a fibration by lines over a curve, which is rational and so $\mathbb{P}^1$, and is then some $F_n$ by Section 9.1, or $\rho = 1$ with $-K$ ample. In the second case the unimodular lattice $\mathbb{Z} H$ has $H^2 = 1$ and $K = -3H$, and the three sections of $H$ map the surface isomorphically to $\mathbb{P}^2$.

### Examples

**Example P5.9.21 (the pencil of conics through four points).** Let $p_1, \dots, p_4 \in \mathbb{P}^2$ be four points, no three collinear, and $S = \operatorname{Bl}_{p_1, \dots, p_4} \mathbb{P}^2$, with $\operatorname{Pic}(S) = \mathbb{Z} \ell \oplus \bigoplus \mathbb{Z} E_i$ by Theorem P5.6.3. The conics through the four points form a pencil, and two distinct members meet in exactly the four points, transversally, since their intersection number is $4$ (Example P5.5.8). So the strict transforms of the members of the pencil are disjoint members of $|F|$, $F = 2\ell - \sum E_i$, with $F^2 = 4 - 4 = 0$ and $K \cdot F = -6 + 4 = -2$, and the pencil is a fibration $p \colon S \to \mathbb{P}^1$ whose smooth fibres are conics. The singular members are the three line pairs $p_i p_j \cup p_k p_l$, which give the reducible fibres $(\ell - E_i - E_j) + (\ell - E_k - E_l)$ for $\{i, j, k, l\} = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, each component a $(-1)$-curve and the two components meeting once, as $(\ell - E_i - E_j) \cdot (\ell - E_k - E_l) = 1$. Each $E_i$ has $E_i \cdot F = 1$, so it is a section. By Proposition P5.9.16 the count $\rho(S) = 5 = 2 + 3$ matches the three reducible fibres with two components each. Contract $G_j = \ell - E_1 - E_j$ for $j = 2, 3, 4$, one component in each reducible fibre; they are pairwise disjoint, since $G_j \cdot G_{j'} = 1 - 1 = 0$. The result $S'$ has all fibres $\mathbb{P}^1$ and the sections $E_1, \dots, E_4$, so it is some $F_n$ by Proposition P5.9.9. With $\psi \colon S \to S'$, the images satisfy $\psi^* E_1' = E_1 + G_2 + G_3 + G_4$ and $\psi^* E_2' = E_2 + G_2$, so $E_1'^2 = -1 + 6 - 3 = 2$ and $E_2'^2 = -1 + 2 - 1 = 0$. By part 2 of Proposition P5.9.8, on $F_n$ with $n \geq 1$ every section other than $C_-$ has square at least $n > 0$, and $C_-^2 = -n < 0$; so $E_2'^2 = 0$ forces $n = 0$ and $S' \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$.

**Example P5.9.22 (a fibre with a double component).** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be the first projection, $F$ a fibre and $x \in F$. On $S_1 = \operatorname{Bl}_x X$ the fibre is $F_1 + E_1$, with $F_1$ the strict transform of $F$ and $F_1^2 = E_1^2 = -1$ by Theorem P5.6.3, meeting transversally in the point $y = F_1 \cap E_1$. Let $S_2 = \operatorname{Bl}_y S_1$ with exceptional curve $E_2$, and $F_1', E_1'$ the strict transforms. Both $F_1$ and $E_1$ pass through $y$ with multiplicity $1$, so the fibre of $S_2$ is $F_1' + E_1' + 2E_2$, with $F_1'^2 = E_1'^2 = -2$, $E_2^2 = -1$, $F_1' \cdot E_1' = 0$ and $F_1' \cdot E_2 = E_1' \cdot E_2 = 1$ by parts 3 and 4 of Proposition P5.6.4, as $F_1$ and $E_1$ have distinct tangent directions at $y$. The checks of Proposition P5.9.16 read $(F_1' + E_1' + 2E_2)^2 = -2 - 2 - 4 + 2(0 + 2 + 2) = 0$ and $K \cdot (F_1' + E_1' + 2E_2) = 0 + 0 + 2(-1) = -2$, by the genus formula $K \cdot C = -2 - C^2$ on each component. So fibres need not be reduced, and the multiple component is the $(-1)$-curve found in part 4: the components of square $-2$ have $K \cdot C = 0$. Contracting $E_2$ returns $S_1$, where $F_1$ and $E_1$ are again $(-1)$-curves, and contracting either of them gives a geometrically ruled surface, $X$ or the surface $S' \cong F_1$ of Example P5.9.23 below.

**Example P5.9.23 (two ruled surfaces from one fibre).** Let $C$ be a curve of genus $g$, $X = C \times \mathbb{P}^1$, $x \in X$ a closed point over $y \in C$, and $S = \operatorname{Bl}_x X$. The fibre of $S \to C$ over $y$ is $\tilde X_y + E$, with $\tilde X_y^2 = E^2 = -1$ by Theorem P5.6.3, and both are $(-1)$-curves meeting once. Contracting $E$ gives back $X$, and contracting $\tilde X_y$ gives a surface $S'$ over $C$ with all fibres $\mathbb{P}^1$. The section $C_0 = C \times \{t\}$ through $x$ has strict transform $\tilde C_0$ with $\tilde C_0^2 = -1$, meeting $E$ and not $\tilde X_y$, so its image $D'$ in $S'$ is a section with $D'^2 = -1$. So $S' \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}')$ by Proposition P5.9.5, and $D'^2 = 2 \deg \mathcal{N} - \deg \mathcal{E}'$ by Theorem P5.9.4 shows $\deg \mathcal{E}'$ odd. Twisting changes the degree by even numbers, so the normalized bundle of $S'$ has odd degree, $e(S')$ is odd, and $S' \not\cong X$ over $C$, as $e(X) = 0$. For $C = \mathbb{P}^1$ this is the passage from $F_0$ to $F_1$ of Proposition P5.9.10.

### Proof

*Proof of Proposition P5.9.16.* (1) As $S$ is integral and $p$ is dominant, $\mathcal{O}_{S,s}$ is torsion free over the discrete valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_{B, p(s)}$, so $p$ is flat by Theorem P3.13.4. Let $S \xrightarrow{p'} B' \xrightarrow{g} B$ be the Stein factorization (Theorem P4.6.15). As in Step 2 of the proof of Theorem P5.12.15, $B'$ is a smooth projective curve and $g$ is finite, of some degree $d$. The fibre $S_{b_0} \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ is connected, so $g^{-1}(b_0)$ is a single point $b'$, and $g^* b_0 = d \, b'$ by Proposition P5.1.4. Then $p^* b_0 = p'^*(g^* b_0) = d \, p'^* b'$, with $p'^* b'$ a nonzero effective divisor. As $S_{b_0}$ is reduced, $d = 1$. So $g$ is finite and birational onto the normal curve $B$, and $\mathcal{O}_B \to g_* \mathcal{O}_{B'}$ is a finite extension of sheaves of normal domains with the same fraction field, hence an isomorphism. So $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_B$, and the fibres are connected by Theorem P4.6.7.

(2) For a divisor $L$, Proposition P5.5.12 gives $L \cdot S_b = \chi(\mathcal{O}_{S_b}(L)) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_{S_b})$, and both terms are independent of $b$ by Theorem P4.5.13, since $p$ is flat and projective. So $S_b \equiv S_{b_0}$. Distinct fibres are disjoint, so $F^2 = S_b \cdot S_{b'} = 0$, and the genus formula (Theorem P5.5.14) on $S_{b_0} \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ gives $K \cdot F = -2 - 0 = -2$.

(3) By (2), $S_b^2 + K \cdot S_b = -2$, so an integral fibre is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ by part 1 of Corollary P5.5.15.

(4) Suppose first $S_b = n C$ with $C$ integral and $n \geq 2$. Then $n^2 C^2 = 0$ and $n K \cdot C = -2$, so $n = 2$, $C^2 = 0$ and $K \cdot C = -1$. But $C^2 + K \cdot C = 2 p_a(C) - 2$ is even by Theorem P5.5.14, while here it is $-1$. So $S_b$ has at least two components. Each component $C_i$ has $C_i \cdot S_b = C_i \cdot S_{b'} = 0$ for $b' \neq b$, by (2). As the support of $S_b$ is connected by (1), $C_i$ meets another component, and $C_i \cdot \sum_{j \neq i} n_j C_j > 0$ by Proposition P5.5.5. So $n_i C_i^2 = -C_i \cdot \sum_{j \neq i} n_j C_j < 0$. Finally $\sum_i n_i K \cdot C_i = K \cdot S_b = -2$, so some $K \cdot C_i < 0$, and $C_i$ is a $(-1)$-curve by part 2 of Corollary P5.5.15.

(5) By Theorem P5.7.4, $\psi_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_{S'}$, $\psi$ is projective, and it is an isomorphism off $E$, which maps to a point $s'$. As $p(E)$ is a point, Lemma P5.7.2 gives $p'$ with $p = p' \psi$, and $p'$ is surjective. The point $s'$ lies over $b = p(E)$, and $b \neq b_0$ since $S_{b_0}$ is integral and is not $E$, as $S_{b_0}^2 = 0$. So $\psi$ is an isomorphism over a neighbourhood of $b_0$, and $S'_{b_0} \cong S_{b_0} \cong \mathbb{P}^1$. Finally $\rho(S') = \rho(S) - 1$ by part 4 of Theorem P5.6.3. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P5.9.17.* If some closed fibre is not integral, it contains a $(-1)$-curve by part 4 of Proposition P5.9.16, and part 5 contracts it, keeping the hypotheses and lowering $\rho$ by $1$. The fibre class $F$ and a curve $H$ with $H \cdot F > 0$, for instance an ample divisor, are independent in $\operatorname{Num}$, so $\rho \geq 2$ throughout, and the process stops after at most $\rho(S) - 2$ steps, at a fibration $p' \colon S' \to B$ with all closed fibres integral, hence isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ by part 3. The contracted curves lie in finitely many fibres, and $\psi$ is an isomorphism over their complement. Let $D$ be a divisor with $D \cdot F = 1$. For one contraction $\psi \colon S \to S'$ of a $(-1)$-curve $E$ in a fibre, let $D' = \psi_* D$ be the image divisor, so that $D = \psi^* D' + a E$ by Theorem P5.6.3, and let $F'$ be a fibre of $S'$ not through $\psi(E)$, so that $\psi^* F'$ is a fibre $F$ of $S$ disjoint from $E$. Then $D' \cdot F' = \psi^* D' \cdot \psi^* F' = (D - aE) \cdot F = 1$. By induction the image of $D$ in $S'$ has degree $1$ on the fibres, and Proposition P5.9.5 shows $S' \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ over $B$. Then $\rho(S') = 2$ by Theorem P5.9.4. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P5.9.18.* Let $\psi \colon S \to S'$ and $p' \colon S' \to B$ be as in Theorem P5.9.17. It suffices to find a section of $p'$: then $S' \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$ by Proposition P5.9.5, $\mathcal{E}$ is free over a nonempty open $U \subseteq B$, so $p'^{-1}(U) \cong \mathbb{P}^1_U$ by part 3 of Proposition P3.6.35, and shrinking $U$ so that $\psi$ is an isomorphism over $U$ gives the claim, and a section of $p$ over $U$ extends to $B$ by Proposition P5.1.2 and the properness of $p$.

Let $\mathcal{L} = \omega_{S'}^{-1}$. By part 2 of Proposition P5.9.16, $\mathcal{L}$ has degree $2$ on every closed fibre, so $\mathcal{L}|_{S'_b} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(2)$, with $h^0 = 3$ and $h^1 = 0$. As in the proof of Proposition P5.9.5, $p'$ is flat and projective, and Theorem P4.5.13, Corollary P4.5.15 and Theorem P4.5.16 show that $\mathcal{V} = p'_* \mathcal{L}$ is locally free of rank $3$ with $\mathcal{V}(b) \cong H^0(S'_b, \mathcal{L}|_{S'_b})$, and that $p'^* \mathcal{V} \to \mathcal{L}$ is surjective. By Theorem P3.6.34 this gives a morphism $\psi' \colon S' \to P = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})$ over $B$ with $\psi'^* \mathcal{O}_P(1) \cong \mathcal{L}$, which on the fibre over $b$ is the morphism $\mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ of the complete linear system $|\mathcal{O}(2)|$, the conic of Theorem P3.10.19, a closed immersion. As in the proof of Proposition P5.9.5, $\psi'$ is projective with fibres empty or one reduced point, hence finite by Corollary P4.6.17. Let $Y \subseteq P$ be its image with the reduced structure. Then $S' \to Y$ is finite with fibres $\operatorname{Spec} k$ over the closed points of $Y$, so it is an isomorphism by Lemma P5.9.2, and $\psi'$ is a closed immersion.

Let $K = k(B)$ with generic point $\eta$. The generic fibre $Y_\eta \subseteq P_\eta = \mathbb{P}^2_K$ is an integral closed subscheme of dimension $1$, the localization of the integral surface $Y$. Since $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^2_K) = \mathbb{Z}$ (Corollary P3.9.11), $Y_\eta = V_+(G)$ for an irreducible form $G$ of some degree $d \geq 1$, and the exact sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}(n - d) \to \mathcal{O}(n) \to \mathcal{O}_{Y_\eta}(n) \to 0$ with Theorem P4.2.1 gives
$$\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y_\eta}(n)) = \binom{n + 2}{2} - \binom{n - d + 2}{2} = dn - \frac{d(d - 3)}{2}.$$
On the other hand $\mathcal{O}_{Y_\eta}(n) = \mathcal{L}^n|_{S'_\eta}$, and by Theorem P4.5.13 its Euler characteristic equals that on a closed fibre, $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(2n)) = 2n + 1$. Comparing coefficients, $d = 2$, so $Y_\eta$ is a conic $V_+(q) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_K$ with $q$ a quadratic form in three variables. By Tsen's theorem (Serre, *Galois cohomology*, Section II.3.3), the function field $K$ of a curve over an algebraically closed field is $C_1$: every form of degree $d$ in more than $d$ variables over $K$ has a nontrivial zero. So $q$ has a nontrivial zero, and $S'_\eta$ has a $K$-point $\xi$. Its closure $D \subseteq S'$ is an integral curve with generic point $\xi$ mapping to $\eta$, so $p'|_D \colon D \to B$ is surjective, projective, with finite fibres, hence finite by Corollary P4.6.17, and birational, as $k(D) = \kappa(\xi) = K$. As $B$ is normal, $\mathcal{O}_B \to (p'|_D)_* \mathcal{O}_D$ is an isomorphism, as in the proof of part 1 of Proposition P5.9.16, and $D \to B$ is an isomorphism. Its inverse is a section of $p'$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition P5.9.19.* By Theorem P5.7.13 there are a surface $Z$ and composites of point blow-ups $g \colon Z \to S$ and $h \colon Z \to \mathbb{P}^2$.

(1) By part 2 of Corollary P5.7.14, $h^i(\mathcal{O}_S) = h^i(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2})$, which is $0$ for $i = 1, 2$ by Theorem P4.2.1, and $K_S^2 + \rho(S) = K_{\mathbb{P}^2}^2 + \rho(\mathbb{P}^2) = 9 + 1 = 10$. By Serre duality $p_g = h^2(\mathcal{O}_S) = 0$.

(2) By part 2 of Theorem P5.7.12 applied to $h$, $\operatorname{Pic}(Z) = \mathbb{Z} h^* \ell \oplus \bigoplus_{i=1}^n \mathbb{Z} \varepsilon_i$ with Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1, -1, \dots, -1)$. This form is nondegenerate, so $\operatorname{Pic}(Z) \to \operatorname{Num}(Z)$ is injective, hence an isomorphism, and the form is unimodular. Applied to $g$, the same theorem gives the orthogonal decomposition $\operatorname{Num}(Z) = g^* \operatorname{Num}(S) \oplus \bigoplus_j \mathbb{Z} \varepsilon'_j$, with $g^*$ an isometry onto the first summand. The determinant of the Gram matrix of $\operatorname{Num}(Z)$ is the product of those of the summands, so $\operatorname{Num}(S)$ is unimodular. If $L \in \operatorname{Pic}(S)$ is numerically trivial, then $g^* L$ is numerically trivial, since $g^* L \cdot \varepsilon'_j = 0$ and $g^* L \cdot g^* M = L \cdot M = 0$; so $g^* L = 0$ and $L = 0$, as $g^*$ is injective on $\operatorname{Pic}$.

(3) Suppose $K_S$ nef. By part 3 of Theorem P5.7.12, $K_Z = g^* K_S + \sum_j \varepsilon'_j$ with each $\varepsilon'_j$ effective, supported on the finitely many curves contracted by $g$, and $K_Z = -3 h^* \ell + \sum_i \varepsilon_i$. Choose a line $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ avoiding the finitely many points over which $h$ is not an isomorphism and such that $L' = h^{-1}(L) \cong L$ is not one of the curves contracted by $g$. Then $L' \cdot \varepsilon_i = 0$ and $K_Z \cdot L' = -3$, while $\varepsilon'_j \cdot L' \geq 0$ by Proposition P5.5.5. So $g^* K_S \cdot L' \leq -3$. But $g$ maps $L'$ birationally onto the curve $g(L')$, so $g^* K_S \cdot L' = \deg (g^* \mathcal{O}_S(K_S)|_{L'}) = K_S \cdot g(L') \geq 0$ by Theorem P5.5.6, a contradiction.

(4) By part 2 of Corollary P2.4.27 with $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_B$, $H^1(B, \mathcal{O}_B)$ injects into $H^1(S, \mathcal{O}_S) = 0$, so $g(B) = 0$ and $B \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ by Proposition P5.1.12.

(5) If $F \equiv m G$ with $m \geq 2$, then $K \cdot F = -2$ gives $m = 2$, $K \cdot G = -1$ and $G^2 = 0$. But $D^2 + K \cdot D$ is even for every divisor $D$, since $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S(-D)) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = \frac{1}{2} D \cdot (D + K)$ is an integer by Theorem P5.5.13. So $F$ is primitive. A primitive vector of a unimodular lattice $\Lambda$ pairs to $1$ with some vector: the map $\Lambda \to \operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda, \mathbb{Z})$ is an isomorphism, and a primitive $F$ extends to a basis of $\Lambda$, whose dual basis contains a functional sending $F$ to $1$. By (2) this functional is $D \cdot (-)$ for a divisor $D$, so $D \cdot F = 1$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem P5.9.20.* (1) $\mathbb{P}^2$ has $\rho = 1$ and no curve of negative square. The $F_n$ are rational by part 1 of Theorem P3.11.25. On $F_0$ every integral curve has square $2ab \geq 0$ by Proposition P5.9.8, and on $F_n$ with $n \geq 2$ the only integral curve of negative square is $C_-$, with $C_-^2 = -n \neq -1$. So these surfaces are minimal, and $F_1$ is not, as $C_-$ is a $(-1)$-curve. $\mathbb{P}^2$ has $\rho = 1$ and the $F_n$ have $\rho = 2$, and the $F_n$ are pairwise non-isomorphic by part 3 of Proposition P5.9.9.

(2) Let $S$ be a minimal rational surface. By part 3 of Proposition P5.9.19, $K_S$ is not nef, so by the cone theorem P5.12.14 there is a $K$-negative extremal ray $R$, and Theorem P5.12.15 applies to it. Case 1 of that theorem does not occur, as $S$ has no $(-1)$-curve. In case 2 there is a fibration $p \colon S \to B$ with $p_* \mathcal{O}_S = \mathcal{O}_B$ and all fibres isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$. By parts 4 and 5 of Proposition P5.9.19, $B \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ and there is a divisor $D$ with $D \cdot F = 1$, so $S$ is geometrically ruled over $\mathbb{P}^1$ by Proposition P5.9.5, hence $S \cong F_n$ by Proposition P5.9.9, with $n \neq 1$ by minimality.

In case 3, $\rho(S) = 1$ and $-K$ is ample. These two facts are proved in the case $x^2 > 0$ of the proof of Theorem P5.12.15 without the classification of surfaces, and the rest of the argument is as follows. By part 2 of Proposition P5.9.19, $\operatorname{Pic}(S) = \mathbb{Z} H$ with $H^2 = \pm 1$, and $H^2 = 1$ for the ample generator $H$. Write $K = aH$. Then $a^2 = K^2 = 10 - 1 = 9$ by part 1 of Proposition P5.9.19, and $a < 0$ as $-K$ is ample, so $K = -3H$. By Riemann-Roch (Theorem P5.5.13),
$$h^0(H) - h^1(H) + h^2(H) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2} H \cdot (H - K) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + 3) = 3,$$
and $h^2(H) = h^0(K - H) = h^0(-4H) = 0$, so $h^0(H) \geq 3$. Every curve $C \in |H|$ is integral, since its components have positive degree against $H$ by Lemma P5.5.20 and $C \cdot H = 1$, and $C^2 + K \cdot C = 1 - 3 = -2$, so $C \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ by Corollary P5.5.15. The sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_S \to \mathcal{O}_S(H) \to \mathcal{O}_C(H) \to 0$, with $\mathcal{O}_C(H) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)$ and $h^1(\mathcal{O}_S) = 0$, shows that $H^0(\mathcal{O}_S(H)) \to H^0(\mathcal{O}_C(1))$ is surjective and $h^0(H) = 1 + 2 = 3$. A base point of $|H|$ would lie on $C$ and be a base point of $|\mathcal{O}_C(1)|$, which has none. So $|H|$ defines a morphism $\varphi \colon S \to \mathbb{P}^2$ with $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_S(H)$ (Theorem P3.10.9). It contracts no curve $\Gamma$, since $\varphi^* \mathcal{O}(1)$ would be trivial on $\Gamma$ while $H \cdot \Gamma > 0$, so $\varphi$ is finite by Corollary P4.6.17, and surjective, as its image is closed of dimension $2$. For a closed point $z \in \mathbb{P}^2$ choose two distinct lines $L, L'$ through $z$, with $L \cap L' = \{z\}$ scheme-theoretically. Then $\varphi^{-1}(z) \subseteq \varphi^{-1}(L) \cap \varphi^{-1}(L') = C \cap C'$ with $C, C' \in |H|$ distinct integral curves, and $C \cdot C' = 1$, so $C \cap C'$ is one point with $\dim_k \mathcal{O}_{S,s}/(f, f') = 1$ by Proposition P5.5.5, that is, $C \cap C' \cong \operatorname{Spec} k$. So the nonempty fibre $\varphi^{-1}(z)$ is $\operatorname{Spec} k$, and $\varphi$ is an isomorphism by Lemma P5.9.2.

(3) By part 3 of Corollary P5.7.14 every rational surface has a birational morphism onto a minimal surface, which is rational, and (2) applies. If that surface is $F_1$, compose with $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Check $K^2 + \rho = 10$ for $F_n$, for $\operatorname{Bl}_{p_1, \dots, p_r} \mathbb{P}^2$, and for a smooth cubic surface, using $K^2 = 3$ and $\rho = 7$.
2. (A) Let $p \in \mathbb{P}^2$. Show that the pencil of lines through $p$ defines a fibration $\operatorname{Bl}_p \mathbb{P}^2 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ with all fibres $\mathbb{P}^1$ and the section $E$, and identify it with $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ via Theorem P3.11.26.
3. (B) Let $p \colon S \to B$ be a fibration with one fibre isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, and $n_b$ the number of components of $S_b$. Show that $\sum_b (n_b - 1)$ is finite and that $\rho(S) = 2 + \sum_b (n_b - 1)$ if $p$ has a section.
4. (B) In the situation of Proposition P5.9.16, let $S_b = C_1 + C_2$ have two components. Show that $C_1$ and $C_2$ are $(-1)$-curves meeting transversally in one point, and that a fibre with three components contains a curve of square $-2$ or two disjoint $(-1)$-curves.
5. (B) Let $S$ be birationally ruled over a curve $B$ of genus $g \geq 1$. Show that $q(S) = g$, and that every morphism $\mathbb{P}^1 \to B$ is constant. Deduce that every rational curve on $S$ lies in a fibre of any fibration $S \to B$, so that the fibration with general fibre $\mathbb{P}^1$ is unique up to an automorphism of $B$.
6. (C) Let $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$ and $a, b, c \in k[t]$ nonzero. Prove directly that $a x^2 + b y^2 + c z^2$ has a nontrivial zero in $k(t)^3$: count the coefficients of polynomials $x, y, z$ of degree at most $N$, compare with the number of conditions imposed by $a x^2 + b y^2 + c z^2 = 0$, and use that a system of $m$ homogeneous equations in more than $m$ unknowns over $k$ has a nonzero solution in $k$.

### Notes and history

The theorem that a surface with a pencil of rational curves is birationally ruled is due to Max Noether (1870) for rational pencils on rational surfaces and to Enriques (1897) in general; the modern proof by a section of the generic conic uses Tsen's theorem of 1933, and the argument here is that of Beauville, Theorem III.4, and Hartshorne, Section V.2. Tsen's theorem is the statement that the function field of a curve over an algebraically closed field is quasi-algebraically closed, in Lang's terminology. The classification of minimal rational surfaces as $\mathbb{P}^2$ and the $F_n$, $n \neq 1$, goes back to Enriques and was proved in modern form by Nagata in 1960; Beauville, Theorem V.10, deduces it from Noether's lemma that a rational surface not $\mathbb{P}^2$ has a pencil of rational curves. The literature state is theorem. In the language of the minimal model program, Theorem P5.9.20 says that the Mori fibre spaces of Definition P5.12.20 that occur as outcomes of a run on a rational surface are $\mathbb{P}^2 \to \mathrm{pt}$ and the $F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1$ with $n \neq 1$, and Example P5.9.22 shows that the outcome is not determined by the surface; the Sarkisov program relates the outcomes by elementary links such as Proposition P5.9.10 and the blow-down $F_1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$. The proof of case 3 above makes the identification of a surface with $\rho = 1$ and $-K$ ample with $\mathbb{P}^2$ independent of the classification, for rational surfaces; Kollár and Mori, Theorem 1.28, and Mori 1982, Theorem 2.1, give the statement for all smooth projective surfaces.
