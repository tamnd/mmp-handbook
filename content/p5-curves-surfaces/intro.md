# Curves and surfaces

The minimal model program starts from the classical theory of surfaces. On a smooth projective surface, a curve $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ with $E^2 = -1$ can be contracted to a smooth point, by Castelnuovo's criterion. Contracting these curves one after another ends at a minimal surface, or at a surface that is ruled. The Enriques-Kodaira classification then sorts the minimal surfaces by Kodaira dimension. Mori's cone theorem rewrites this picture in terms of the canonical divisor and the cone of curves, and the higher-dimensional program extends it. This volume builds the theory of curves and surfaces that Volume 2 runs the program on, and that the later volumes use as the source of their examples.

The volume has twelve chapters in three groups.

- **Curves, Chapters 1 to 4.** Chapter 1 proves that a smooth projective curve is determined by its function field, proves the Riemann-Roch theorem with the canonical divisor, and studies linear systems on curves: base points, very ampleness, hyperelliptic curves and the canonical embedding. Chapter 2 treats finite morphisms of curves and the Hurwitz formula. Chapter 3 treats singular curves: the normalization, the delta invariant and the genus formula for plane curves. Chapter 4 treats elliptic curves, vector bundles on $\mathbb{P}^1$ and the surface $E \times E$, whose nef cone is round.
- **Surfaces and birational maps, Chapters 5 to 8.** Chapter 5 defines the intersection pairing on a smooth projective surface and proves the adjunction formula, Riemann-Roch for surfaces and the Hodge index theorem. Chapter 6 studies blow-ups of points. Chapter 7 proves Castelnuovo's criterion and the factorization of birational morphisms into blow-ups. Chapter 8 proves the negativity lemma, the contraction criteria of Grauert and Artin, and the Zariski decomposition, which are the surface cases of results of Volumes 4, 7 and 10.
- **Classification, Chapters 9 to 12.** Chapter 9 treats ruled and rational surfaces, the Hirzebruch surfaces $\mathbb{F}_n$ and del Pezzo surfaces. Chapter 10 treats the 27 lines on a cubic surface and the plane blown up in nine points. Chapter 11 defines the Kodaira dimension and states the Enriques-Kodaira classification. Chapter 12 runs the minimal model program on a smooth projective surface, as a preview of Volume 2.

The spine is Hartshorne's *Algebraic geometry*, Chapters IV and V (Har77), Beauville's *Complex algebraic surfaces* (Bea96), Reid's *Chapters on algebraic surfaces* (Rei97) and Vakil's *The rising sea* (Vak25). The curve chapters follow Hartshorne IV and Vakil. The surface chapters follow Hartshorne V and Beauville, with the point of view of Reid, who presents the classification as the first case of the minimal model program. Each section lists in `reads_with` the places of the spine texts that read in parallel.

The volume needs P3 for schemes, divisors, linear systems, blow-ups and differentials, and P4 Ch. 1 to 7 for cohomology, Euler characteristics and Serre duality. A *curve* is an integral separated scheme of finite type over a field $k$ of dimension $1$, and a *surface* is one of dimension $2$. Unless the text says otherwise, $k$ is algebraically closed. The characteristic is arbitrary unless the text says otherwise; where a proof needs characteristic $0$, it says so.
