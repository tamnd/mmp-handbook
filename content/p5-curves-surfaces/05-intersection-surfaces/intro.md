# Intersection theory on surfaces

The basic numerical invariant of a pair of curves $C$, $D$ on a smooth projective surface $S$ is the number of points in which they meet, counted with multiplicity. This chapter makes that number into a symmetric bilinear form on $\operatorname{Pic}(S)$, the *intersection pairing*. The number depends only on the classes of $C$ and $D$, so it is defined even when $C = D$. The self-intersection $C^2$ is then the degree of the normal bundle of $C$, and it can be negative.

Section 5.1 defines the pairing through Euler characteristics, following Beauville, and proves that it agrees with the count of points with local multiplicities. It also proves the form of Bertini's theorem that the rest of the volume uses: a sufficiently ample linear system on a smooth surface contains smooth irreducible curves. Section 5.2 proves Riemann-Roch for divisors on a surface, $\chi(D) = \chi(\mathcal{O}_S) + \frac{1}{2} D \cdot (D - K_S)$, and the adjunction formula, which computes the arithmetic genus of every curve on $S$ from $C^2$ and $K_S \cdot C$. Section 5.3 proves the Hodge index theorem: modulo numerical equivalence, the pairing is negative definite on the orthogonal complement of an ample class. On a product $C \times C$ over a finite field this yields Weil's bound on the number of rational points of a curve.

These are the tools of the whole minimal model program in dimension $2$. The sign of $K_S \cdot C$ decides which curves the program contracts, and the adjunction formula identifies them: a curve with $C^2 < 0$ and $K_S \cdot C < 0$ is a smooth rational curve with $C^2 = -1$. The cone of curves, the negativity lemma and the Zariski decomposition of Chapters 8 and 12 are statements about the intersection pairing, and the Hodge index theorem is the reason the nef cone of a surface lies in one half of a light cone.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Section V.1, Beauville, Chapter I, and the first chapter of Reid's *Chapters on algebraic surfaces*.
