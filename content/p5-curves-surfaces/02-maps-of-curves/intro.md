# Maps of curves

A nonconstant morphism $f \colon C \to D$ of smooth projective curves is finite, and its degree is the degree of the extension of function fields. This chapter compares the genera of $C$ and $D$. When the extension is separable, the pullback of differentials is injective, and its cokernel is supported on the points where $f$ is ramified. Comparing degrees of the canonical divisors gives the Hurwitz formula
$$2g(C) - 2 = \deg f \cdot (2g(D) - 2) + \deg R.$$
When the extension is purely inseparable, $f$ is a power of the Frobenius morphism, which does not change the genus.

Section 2.1 defines ramification, proves the exact sequence of differentials of a separable morphism, distinguishes tame from wild ramification, and proves the Hurwitz formula. Its consequences are that the genus does not increase along a separable map, that $\mathbb{P}^1$ has no nontrivial unramified covers, and that a map between curves of the same genus $g \geq 2$ is an isomorphism. The examples include hyperelliptic curves, projections of plane curves, and the Artin-Schreier curves, where the ramification is wild. Section 2.2 defines the Frobenius morphism, shows that every morphism of curves is a power of Frobenius followed by a separable morphism, and proves Lüroth's theorem: every subfield of $k(t)$ strictly containing $k$ is generated by one element.

In the minimal model program the Hurwitz formula is the curve case of the formula $K_X = f^* K_Y + R$ for a finite morphism, which in Volume 4 compares the singularities of $X$ and $Y$ through their discrepancies. Lüroth's theorem says that a curve dominated by $\mathbb{P}^1$ is rational. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the same holds for surfaces, by Castelnuovo's rationality criterion (Chapter 11), and it fails in dimension $3$, by the theorems of Clemens-Griffiths and Iskovskikh-Manin.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Sections IV.2 and IV.4, and Vakil, Chapter 21, with Silverman for the Frobenius morphism.
