# Contractions, negativity and Zariski decomposition

Chapter 7 contracts a single $(-1)$-curve and stays among smooth surfaces. This chapter asks which configurations of curves on a smooth projective surface can be contracted at all, to a point of a normal surface, and what the intersection form says about divisors supported on such configurations. The answer to both questions starts from negative definiteness, and the same linear algebra produces the Zariski decomposition of a pseudoeffective divisor into a nef part and a negative definite part.

Section 8.1 proves the negativity lemma for symmetric matrices with nonnegative entries off the diagonal and Mumford's theorem that the exceptional curves of every birational morphism onto a normal surface have a negative definite intersection matrix. It deduces the negativity lemma for a birational morphism and Mumford's rational pullback of Weil divisors, with the chain $A_n$ and the quadric cone as running examples. Section 8.2 turns to the converse. Grauert's analytic criterion is cited; the fundamental cycle, Laufer's algorithm and the necessity of Artin's condition $p_a(Z) \leq 0$ for rational singularities are proved, Artin's contractibility theorem is cited, and configurations of $(-2)$-curves are shown to contract to rational double points. Section 8.3 proves the existence and uniqueness of the Zariski decomposition by Zariski's algorithm, identifies the positive part of an effective divisor as its largest nef subdivisor, and shows that the negative part lies in the base locus of every multiple.

In the minimal model program the negativity lemma is the tool that compares discrepancies on the two sides of a divisorial contraction or a flip, in every dimension. Du Val and rational singularities are the singularities that the program produces on surfaces, and the Zariski decomposition is the surface model of the decompositions of divisors into a movable part and a fixed part that run through the higher dimensional theory.

The chapter follows Kollár and Mori, Sections 3.6 and 4.1, Artin's paper on rational singularities, Beauville, Chapter II, and Lazarsfeld, Section 2.3.E.
