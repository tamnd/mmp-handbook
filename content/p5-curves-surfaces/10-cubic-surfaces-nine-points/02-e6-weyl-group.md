## The lattice of the cubic surface and the Weyl group of E6

### Statement

The intersection form of $X_r$ depends only on $r$, so it can be studied as a lattice. The roots of this lattice act by reflections on $\operatorname{Pic}(X_r)$, and for $r = 6$ they permute the $27$ lines of Section 10.1. The group they generate is the symmetry group of the configuration of Proposition P5.10.6.

**Definition P5.10.10 (the lattice $I^{1,r}$, roots and reflections).** Let $I^{1,r} = \mathbb{Z} \ell \oplus \mathbb{Z} E_1 \oplus \dots \oplus \mathbb{Z} E_r$ with the symmetric bilinear form $\ell^2 = 1$, $E_i^2 = -1$, all other products $0$, and let $k_r = -3\ell + E_1 + \dots + E_r$. By Example P5.6.8, $I^{1,r}$ is $\operatorname{Pic}(X_r)$ with its intersection form and $k_r = K_{X_r}$, for any $r$ distinct points. An *exceptional class* is $D \in I^{1,r}$ with $D^2 = D \cdot k_r = -1$, as in Definition P5.10.4. A *root* is $\alpha \in I^{1,r}$ with $\alpha^2 = -2$ and $\alpha \cdot k_r = 0$. The *reflection* in a root $\alpha$ is
$$s_\alpha(x) = x + (x \cdot \alpha) \, \alpha .$$
The *Weyl group* $W_r$ is the subgroup of the orthogonal group $O(I^{1,r})$ generated by the reflections in all roots, and $O(I^{1,r})_k$ is the group of isometries $g$ with $g(k_r) = k_r$.

From now on in this section $r = 6$, $k = k_6$ and $W = W_6$, and $E_i$, $F_{ij} = \ell - E_i - E_j$, $G_j = 2\ell - \sum_{k \neq j} E_k$ are the $27$ exceptional classes of Theorem P5.10.5. Its proof used only the lattice, so these are the exceptional classes of $I^{1,6}$. A *six* is an ordered sequence $(L_1, \dots, L_6)$ of pairwise orthogonal exceptional classes.

**Proposition P5.10.11 (the root system $E_6$).**

1. Each reflection $s_\alpha$ is an isometry of $I^{1,6}$ with $s_\alpha(k) = k$, $s_\alpha(\alpha) = -\alpha$ and $s_\alpha^2 = 1$. It permutes the $27$ exceptional classes.
2. The orthogonal complement $k^\perp = \{x : x \cdot k = 0\}$ is negative definite of rank $6$. It contains exactly $72$ roots: the $30$ classes $E_i - E_j$ with $i \neq j$, the $40$ classes $\pm(\ell - E_i - E_j - E_k)$ with $i < j < k$, and $\pm(2\ell - E_1 - \dots - E_6)$.
3. The roots $\alpha_0 = \ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3$ and $\alpha_i = E_i - E_{i+1}$ for $1 \leq i \leq 5$ form a basis of $k^\perp$. In the order $\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_5$ the matrix $(-\alpha_a \cdot \alpha_b)$ is
$$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 & -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 & -1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & -1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix},$$
the Cartan matrix of the Dynkin diagram $E_6$: a chain $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_5$ with $\alpha_0$ attached to the middle vertex $\alpha_3$. Its determinant is $3$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P5.10.12 (the Weyl group of $E_6$).**

1. $W = O(I^{1,6})_k$. Every element of $W$ permutes the $27$ exceptional classes and preserves their intersection numbers.
2. $W$ acts simply transitively on the set of sixes. In particular $W$ acts faithfully and transitively on the $27$ exceptional classes.
3. $|W| = 27 \cdot 16 \cdot 10 \cdot 6 \cdot 2 = 51840 = 2^7 \cdot 3^4 \cdot 5$. There are exactly $72$ sets of six pairwise disjoint lines on the cubic surface $Y$ of Theorem P5.10.3, and the stabilizer in $W$ of one exceptional class has order $1920$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P5.10.13 (other presentations as a blown-up plane).** Let $(L_1, \dots, L_6)$ be a six. Then $\ell' = \frac{1}{3}(L_1 + \dots + L_6 - k)$ lies in $I^{1,6}$, and $\ell', L_1, \dots, L_6$ is a basis of $I^{1,6}$ with $\ell'^2 = 1$, $L_i^2 = -1$, all other products $0$, and $k = -3\ell' + L_1 + \dots + L_6$. On $X_6$, for six points in general position, the $L_i$ are the classes of six pairwise disjoint $(-1)$-curves.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The complement of $k$ is negative definite by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, so it has only finitely many vectors of square $-2$, and solving the two equations gives the $72$ roots. Reflections in roots preserve $k$ and the form, hence they permute the $27$ classes. To compute the group, look at sixes of disjoint lines: an isometry fixing $k$ and a six $E_1, \dots, E_6$ fixes $3\ell = \sum E_i - k$, hence everything. Conversely reflections move any six to the standard one, one line at a time, using at each step only roots orthogonal to the lines already placed. Counting sixes step by step gives $27 \cdot 16 \cdot 10 \cdot 6 \cdot 2$.

### Examples

**Example P5.10.14 (the Cremona transformation).** The reflection in $\alpha_0 = \ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3$ is
$$s_{\alpha_0}(\ell) = \ell + \alpha_0 = 2\ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3, \qquad s_{\alpha_0}(E_1) = E_1 + \alpha_0 = \ell - E_2 - E_3,$$
and similarly for $E_2$ and $E_3$, while $s_{\alpha_0}(E_i) = E_i$ for $i \geq 4$. On the $27$ classes it exchanges $E_1 \leftrightarrow F_{23}$, $E_2 \leftrightarrow F_{13}$, $E_3 \leftrightarrow F_{12}$ and $F_{56} \leftrightarrow G_4$, $F_{46} \leftrightarrow G_5$, $F_{45} \leftrightarrow G_6$, and it fixes the other $15$: $E_4, E_5, E_6$, the $9$ classes $F_{ij}$ with $i \leq 3 < j$, and $G_1, G_2, G_3$. For instance $F_{56} \cdot \alpha_0 = 1$, so $s_{\alpha_0}(F_{56}) = F_{56} + \alpha_0 = 2\ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3 - E_5 - E_6 = G_4$, and $G_1 \cdot \alpha_0 = 2 - 2 = 0$. Geometrically, let $P_1, P_2, P_3$ be the coordinate points of $\mathbb{P}^2$ and $\sigma [x : y : z] = [yz : xz : xy]$ the standard quadratic transformation. The pullback by $\sigma$ of a line $ax + by + cz = 0$ is the conic $ayz + bxz + cxy = 0$ through the three points, and $\sigma \circ \sigma = xyz \cdot \operatorname{id}$, so $\sigma$ is a birational involution. By Hartshorne, Example V.4.2.3, $\sigma$ lifts to an automorphism of the blow-up of the three points that exchanges $E_i$ with the strict transform of the coordinate line through the other two. Its action on $\operatorname{Pic}$ sends $\ell$ to $2\ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3$ and $E_1$ to $\ell - E_2 - E_3$, so it is $s_{\alpha_0}$ restricted to $I^{1,3}$. If $P_4, P_5, P_6$ are further points off the coordinate lines, the lift identifies the blow-up of $P_1, \dots, P_6$ with the blow-up of $P_1, P_2, P_3, \sigma(P_4), \sigma(P_5), \sigma(P_6)$, and the identification acts on $I^{1,6}$ as $s_{\alpha_0}$.

**Example P5.10.15 (roots as differences of disjoint lines).** If $L$ and $L'$ are orthogonal exceptional classes, then $(L - L')^2 = -1 - 1 = -2$ and $(L - L') \cdot k = -1 + 1 = 0$, so $L - L'$ is a root. Each root arises from exactly $6$ ordered pairs. For $E_1 - E_2$ these are $(E_1, E_2)$, the four pairs $(F_{2j}, F_{1j})$ with $j \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, since $(\ell - E_2 - E_j) - (\ell - E_1 - E_j) = E_1 - E_2$, and $(G_1, G_2)$, since $G_1 - G_2 = -E_2 + E_1$. By Theorem P5.10.12, $W$ is transitive on ordered pairs of disjoint lines, of which there are $27 \cdot 16 = 432 = 72 \cdot 6$, so the count $6$ holds for every root. A computer enumeration of the $432$ pairs confirms that every root occurs exactly $6$ times.

**Example P5.10.16 (the 36 double sixes).** A *double six* is a pair of sixes $(L_1, \dots, L_6)$, $(L'_1, \dots, L'_6)$ with $L_i \cdot L'_j = 1 - \delta_{ij}$. Every six extends to exactly one double six. For the standard six, an exceptional class $D = a\ell - \sum b_i E_i$ with $D \cdot E_i = 1$ for $i \neq j$ and $D \cdot E_j = 0$ has $b_i = 1$ for $i \neq j$ and $b_j = 0$, so $3a - 1 = 5$, $a = 2$ and $D = G_j$. By Theorem P5.10.12 every six is $w(E_1, \dots, E_6)$ for some $w \in W$, and its partner is $w(G_1, \dots, G_6)$. The partner of $(G_1, \dots, G_6)$ is $(E_1, \dots, E_6)$, by the symmetry of the condition. So the $72$ unordered sixes fall into $36$ double sixes. A computer enumeration of all sets of six pairwise disjoint lines confirms the counts $72$ and $36$.

### Proof

*Proposition P5.10.11.* (1) For $x, y \in I^{1,6}$,
$$s_\alpha(x) \cdot s_\alpha(y) = x \cdot y + 2 (x \cdot \alpha)(y \cdot \alpha) + (x \cdot \alpha)(y \cdot \alpha) \alpha^2 = x \cdot y,$$
since $\alpha^2 = -2$. Also $s_\alpha(k) = k$ because $\alpha \cdot k = 0$, $s_\alpha(\alpha) = \alpha - 2\alpha = -\alpha$, and $s_\alpha(x) \cdot \alpha = -x \cdot \alpha$ gives $s_\alpha(s_\alpha(x)) = x$. So $s_\alpha$ is an isometry fixing $k$, and it maps exceptional classes to exceptional classes. It is injective, so it permutes the finite set of $27$ exceptional classes.

(2) Let $x = a\ell - \sum b_i E_i$ with $x \cdot k = 0$, that is, $\sum b_i = 3a$. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality $9a^2 = (\sum b_i)^2 \leq 6 \sum b_i^2$, so
$$x^2 = a^2 - \sum b_i^2 \leq a^2 - \tfrac{3}{2} a^2 = -\tfrac{1}{2} a^2 .$$
If $a \neq 0$ then $x^2 < 0$, and if $a = 0$ then $x^2 = -\sum b_i^2 < 0$ unless $x = 0$. So $k^\perp$ is negative definite, and it has rank $6$ because $k \neq 0$ and the form is nondegenerate. A root has $\sum b_i = 3a$ and $\sum b_i^2 = a^2 + 2$, so $9a^2 \leq 6(a^2 + 2)$ and $|a| \leq 2$, and $\sum b_i (b_i - 1) = a^2 + 2 - 3a = (a - 1)(a - 2)$. For $a = 1$ and $a = 2$ every $b_i$ lies in $\{0, 1\}$, which gives the $20$ classes $\ell - E_i - E_j - E_k$ and the class $2\ell - \sum E_i$. Replacing $x$ by $-x$ gives $a = -1$ and $a = -2$. For $a = 0$, $\sum b_i = 0$ and $\sum b_i^2 = 2$, so $x = E_i - E_j$ with $i \neq j$. In total $30 + 20 + 20 + 1 + 1 = 72$.

(3) For $x = a\ell - \sum b_i E_i \in k^\perp$, the class $x - a \alpha_0 = \sum_i c_i E_i$ satisfies $(x - a\alpha_0) \cdot k = -\sum c_i = 0$, and the classes $\sum c_i E_i$ with $\sum c_i = 0$ are the integer combinations of $E_1 - E_2, \dots, E_5 - E_6$. So the six roots generate $k^\perp$, which has rank $6$, and they form a basis. The products are $\alpha_i^2 = -2$, $\alpha_i \cdot \alpha_{i+1} = -E_{i+1} \cdot E_{i+1} = 1$, $\alpha_i \cdot \alpha_j = 0$ for $|i - j| \geq 2$ and $i, j \geq 1$, and $\alpha_0 \cdot E_j = 1$ for $j \leq 3$ and $0$ for $j \geq 4$, so $\alpha_0 \cdot \alpha_i = [i \leq 3] - [i + 1 \leq 3]$, which is $1$ for $i = 3$ and $0$ otherwise. This is the matrix displayed. For the determinant, the map $x \mapsto x \cdot k \bmod 3$ from $I^{1,6}$ to $\mathbb{Z}/3$ is surjective, since $E_1 \cdot k = -1$, and its kernel is $k^\perp \oplus \mathbb{Z} k$: if $x \cdot k = 3m$, then $x = (x - mk) + mk$ with $x - mk \in k^\perp$, because $k^2 = 9 - 6 = 3$. So $k^\perp \oplus \mathbb{Z} k$ has index $3$ in the lattice $I^{1,6}$, whose Gram determinant is $\pm 1$, and the Gram determinants satisfy $|\det(k^\perp)| \cdot k^2 = 3^2 \cdot 1$. Hence $|\det(k^\perp)| = 3$. The displayed matrix is positive definite, by part 2, so its determinant is $3$. An exact computation of the $6 \times 6$ determinant over $\mathbb{Q}$ confirms the value. $\square$

*Theorem P5.10.12.* By part 1 of Proposition P5.10.11, $W \subseteq O(I^{1,6})_k$, and every $g \in O(I^{1,6})_k$ preserves the conditions $D^2 = D \cdot k = -1$, so it permutes the exceptional classes and maps sixes to sixes.

*Freeness.* Let $g \in O(I^{1,6})_k$ fix $E_1, \dots, E_6$. Then $g$ fixes $3\ell = E_1 + \dots + E_6 - k$, and since $I^{1,6}$ is torsion free, $g(\ell) = \ell$. So $g$ fixes a basis and $g = 1$. Applied to $w^{-1} w'$, this shows that $w \mapsto w(E_1, \dots, E_6)$ is injective on $O(I^{1,6})_k$.

*Transitivity.* Let $(L_1, \dots, L_6)$ be a six. Each reflection $s_\alpha$ with $\alpha \cdot E_j = 0$ fixes $E_j$. The following computations use $s_\alpha(D) = D - \alpha$ whenever $D \cdot \alpha = -1$.

Step 1: some $w_1 \in W$ maps $L_6$ to $E_6$. Indeed $s_{E_i - E_6}$ maps $E_i$ to $E_6$, for $\alpha = \ell - E_i - E_j - E_k$ one has $F_{ij} \cdot \alpha = -1$ and $s_\alpha(F_{ij}) = E_k$, and for $\alpha = 2\ell - \sum E_k$ one has $G_j \cdot \alpha = -1$ and $s_\alpha(G_j) = E_j$. So every exceptional class is in the orbit of $E_6$.

Step 2: the classes orthogonal to $E_6$ are those with $b_6 = 0$: $E_1, \dots, E_5$, the $10$ classes $F_{ij}$ with $i, j \leq 5$, and $G_6$, in total $16$. The reflections in the roots $E_i - E_j$ and $\ell - E_i - E_j - E_k$ with all indices at most $5$ fix $E_6$, and they map every one of the $16$ classes to $E_5$: $F_{ij} \mapsto E_k$ by $s_{\ell - E_i - E_j - E_k}$ for $k \leq 5$ outside $\{i, j\}$, $G_6 \cdot (\ell - E_l - E_m - E_n) = 2 - 3 = -1$ gives $G_6 \mapsto G_6 - \ell + E_l + E_m + E_n = F_{ij}$ with $\{i, j, l, m, n\} = \{1, \dots, 5\}$, and transpositions $s_{E_i - E_j}$ move the $E_i$. So some $w_2 \in W$ fixing $E_6$ maps $w_1(L_5)$ to $E_5$.

Step 3: the classes orthogonal to $E_5$ and $E_6$ are $E_1, \dots, E_4$ and the $6$ classes $F_{ij}$ with $i, j \leq 4$, in total $10$; no $G_j$ qualifies, since $G_j$ has $b_5 = 1$ or $b_6 = 1$. The reflections in $\ell - E_i - E_j - E_k$ and $E_i - E_j$ with indices at most $4$ fix $E_5$ and $E_6$ and move all $10$ classes to $E_4$.

Step 4: the classes orthogonal to $E_4, E_5, E_6$ are $E_1, E_2, E_3, F_{12}, F_{13}, F_{23}$, and $s_{\ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3}$ maps $F_{ij}$ to the third $E_k$, while fixing $E_4, E_5, E_6$. So the $6$ classes are moved to $E_3$.

Step 5: the classes orthogonal to $E_3, \dots, E_6$ are $E_1, E_2$ and $F_{12}$. Since $F_{12}$ meets both $E_1$ and $E_2$, the last two members of a six that begins with $E_3, \dots, E_6$ in reverse order are $E_1$ and $E_2$ in some order, and $s_{E_1 - E_2}$ fixes $E_3, \dots, E_6$ and exchanges them.

Composing, some $w \in W$ maps $(L_1, \dots, L_6)$ to $(E_1, \dots, E_6)$.

*Conclusion.* By transitivity and freeness, $w \mapsto w(E_1, \dots, E_6)$ is a bijection from $W$, and also from $O(I^{1,6})_k$, to the set of sixes. So $W = O(I^{1,6})_k$. An element fixing all $27$ classes fixes $E_1, \dots, E_6$, so the action on the $27$ classes is faithful, and it is transitive by Step 1. The steps also count the sixes: by transitivity there are $27$ choices of $L_6$, then the $16$ classes orthogonal to it, then $10$, then $6$, each of which extends to a six by Steps 2 to 4, then $2$ choices of $L_2$ and $1$ of $L_1$ by Step 5. So $|W| = 27 \cdot 16 \cdot 10 \cdot 6 \cdot 2 = 51840$. Every ordering of a set of six pairwise disjoint lines is a six, so there are $51840 / 720 = 72$ such sets, and by Theorem P5.10.5 these are the sets of six disjoint lines on $Y$. The stabilizer of one class has index $27$, hence order $51840 / 27 = 1920$. $\square$

*Corollary P5.10.13.* By Theorem P5.10.12 there is $w \in W$ with $w(E_i) = L_i$, and $w(k) = k$. Then $w(\ell) = \frac{1}{3}(w(E_1) + \dots + w(E_6) - w(k)) = \ell'$, so $\ell' \in I^{1,6}$, and $w$ maps the basis $\ell, E_1, \dots, E_6$ to $\ell', L_1, \dots, L_6$, preserving the products. By Theorem P5.10.5 each $L_i$ is the class of a $(-1)$-curve, and two of these curves are disjoint because their intersection number is $0$ and distinct integral curves meet with positive local multiplicities (Proposition P5.5.5). $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute the reflection in $\alpha = 2\ell - E_1 - \dots - E_6$. Show that it exchanges $E_i$ and $G_i$ for every $i$ and fixes the $15$ classes $F_{ij}$.
2. (A) List the roots orthogonal to $E_6$ and show that there are $40$ of them. These are the roots of the system $D_5$, and the reflections in them generate a subgroup of the stabilizer of $E_6$ of Theorem P5.10.12.
3. (B) Show that each exceptional class lies in exactly $16$ of the $72$ sets of six disjoint lines, and each pair of disjoint lines in exactly $5$ of them.
4. (B) Compute the image of the triangle $\{E_1, F_{12}, G_2\}$ under $s_\alpha$ for $\alpha = \ell - E_1 - E_3 - E_4$, and show that it is $\{F_{34}, F_{12}, F_{56}\}$. Using that the transpositions $s_{E_i - E_j}$ generate a copy of the symmetric group $S_6$ in $W$, deduce that $W$ acts transitively on the $45$ triangles of Proposition P5.10.6, with stabilizers of order $1152$.
5. (B) Show that the discriminant group $(k^\perp)^* / k^\perp$ is cyclic of order $3$, generated by the image of the functional $x \mapsto x \cdot E_1$ restricted to $k^\perp$, and that the class $E_1 + \tfrac{1}{3} k$ of $(k^\perp) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ represents it. Compute its square.
6. (C) Let $W' \subseteq W$ be the subgroup generated by the six reflections $s_{\alpha_0}, \dots, s_{\alpha_5}$. Show that every root is $w(\alpha_1)$ for some $w \in W'$, using that $s_{w(\alpha)} = w s_\alpha w^{-1}$, and deduce that $W' = W$.

### Lean

Mathlib has finite permutation groups (`Equiv.Perm`) and determinants of matrices over commutative rings (`Matrix.det`), so the determinant of Proposition P5.10.11 and the action of the six simple reflections on the $27$ classes are finite computations that could be checked by evaluation. The lattice $I^{1,6}$, its exceptional classes and Theorem P5.10.12 are not formalized. The handbook adds no `MinimalModel` node here.

### Notes and history

Jordan identified the group of the $27$ lines in 1870 as a group of order $51840$, with a simple subgroup of index $2$. Its description as the Weyl group of $E_6$ acting on $\operatorname{Pic}$ of the blown-up plane goes back to Coble and Du Val, and Manin's book *Cubic forms* develops the lattice-theoretic view for all del Pezzo surfaces: for $X_r$ with $3 \leq r \leq 8$ and points in general position, the roots of $K^\perp$ form the root systems $A_2 \times A_1$, $A_4$, $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, and the Weyl group acts on the exceptional classes. The argument above follows the elementary approach of Beauville, Chapter IV, through the action on sixes. That $W$ is generated by the reflections in the simple roots is a general theorem on root systems (Bourbaki, *Groupes et algèbres de Lie*, Chapter VI), proved for $E_6$ in Exercise 6. The literature state is theorem. In the language of the minimal model program, Corollary P5.10.13 says that the $72$ sixes are the $72$ ways to run the program on the cubic surface down to $\mathbb{P}^2$ by contracting disjoint $(-1)$-curves, once the contractibility criterion of Chapter 7 shows that the contraction of six disjoint $(-1)$-curves exists; Chapter 12 describes these runs as sequences of extremal contractions.
