# Cubic surfaces and the plane blown up in nine points

Blow up $r$ distinct points of the projective plane. Example P5.6.8 computes the Picard group of the result $X_r$: it is the lattice $\mathbb{Z} \ell \oplus \mathbb{Z} E_1 \oplus \dots \oplus \mathbb{Z} E_r$ with $\ell^2 = 1$, $E_i^2 = -1$, and the canonical class is $K = -3\ell + E_1 + \dots + E_r$ with $K^2 = 9 - r$. This chapter studies the classes $D$ with $D^2 = K \cdot D = -1$, the numerical shadow of the exceptional curves, for $r = 6$, $8$ and $9$. The answer changes character at each step: $27$ classes and an embedding as a cubic surface for $r = 6$, $240$ classes and an elliptic pencil with one base point for $r = 8$, and infinitely many classes and an elliptic fibration for $r = 9$.

Section 10.1 embeds $X_6$, for six points in general position, into $\mathbb{P}^3$ by the cubics through the points. The image is a smooth cubic surface, and its lines are the images of the $27$ curves $E_i$, the strict transforms of the $15$ lines through two of the points, and the strict transforms of the $6$ conics through five of them. The intersection numbers give the classical configuration: each line meets exactly $10$ others, in $5$ coplanar pairs, and there are $45$ tritangent planes. The Fermat cubic shows the $27$ lines in coordinates.

Section 10.2 studies the lattice $\operatorname{Pic}(X_6)$ for its own sake. The orthogonal complement of $K$ is negative definite, its $72$ vectors of square $-2$ form the root system $E_6$, and the reflections in these roots generate a group $W$ that permutes the $27$ classes. The group $W$ acts simply transitively on ordered sixes of disjoint lines, which gives $|W| = 27 \cdot 16 \cdot 10 \cdot 6 \cdot 2 = 51840$, and it is the full group of isometries fixing $K$. Every six of disjoint lines is the set of exceptional curves of another presentation of the same surface as a blown-up plane, and the Cremona transformation is the reflection in the root $\ell - E_1 - E_2 - E_3$.

Section 10.3 treats $r = 8$ and $r = 9$. For every $r \leq 9$ the classes with $D^2 = K \cdot D = -1$ are effective, by Riemann-Roch. For $r = 8$ and $-K$ ample there are $240$ of them, matching the roots of $E_8$, and $|-K|$ is a pencil of curves of arithmetic genus $1$ with one base point. For nine base points of a pencil of plane cubics, the anticanonical system is base point free and gives an elliptic fibration $X_9 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ whose fibres are the cubics of the pencil. Its $(-1)$-curves are exactly its sections, and they are in bijection with the $E_8$ lattice, so there are infinitely many of them. As a consequence the nef cone of $X_9$ is not polyhedral. The section ends with Zariski's example of a nef anticanonical class that is not semiample and with the state of the Nagata conjecture.

For the minimal model program, the chapter gives the basic examples behind Chapter 12. On $X_6$ and on a del Pezzo surface of degree $1$ there are only finitely many $(-1)$-curves, each with $K \cdot C = -1$. On $X_9$, where $-K$ is nef with $(-K)^2 = 0$, there are infinitely many $(-1)$-curves, and their classes accumulate at the class of $-K$, which lies on the hyperplane $K^\perp$. This is the picture that the cone theorem of Chapter 12 describes in general: the $K$-negative curves can be infinite in number, but they can accumulate only along $K^\perp$.

The chapter follows Hartshorne, Section V.4, Beauville, Chapter IV, and Reid's *Chapters on algebraic surfaces*, with the elliptic pencil from the literature on rational elliptic surfaces cited in Section 10.3.
