# The secondary fan and Gale duality

Chapters 4 to 6 studied one projective simplicial toric variety at a time: its cone of curves, its extremal contractions, its flips and the program they make. This chapter fixes the list of ray generators instead and looks at all the projective simplicial toric varieties whose rays are among them at once. Their nef cones all live in one vector space, the space of classes $G_U$ of the Gale dual configuration, and they fit together into a decomposition of the effective cone. The steps of the toric minimal model program become the passages from one piece to the next.

Section 1 sets up Gale duality. To a list $U = (u_1, \dots, u_r)$ of vectors that span $N_{\mathbb{R}}$ it attaches the quotient $G_U = \mathbb{R}^r / M_{\mathbb{R}}$ and the images $\beta_\rho$ of the basis vectors. Independence and spanning are exchanged under complements, and for a complete simplicial fan with rays $U$ the nef cone, the ample cone and the effective cone are read off from the $\beta_\rho$. The examples are the weighted projective spaces, the Hirzebruch surfaces, and the circuits of rank one: the Atiyah flop with weights $(1, -1, 1, -1)$, the Francia flip with weights $(1, 1, -1, -2)$, and the cones $\frac12(1,1)$ and $\frac13(1,1)$ as the circuits $(1, 1, -2)$ and $(1, 1, -3)$. Section 2 defines the GKZ cone of a fan whose rays are among the $u_\rho$, which records its nef cone together with the vectors it does not use, and proves that the GKZ cones of the projective such fans have disjoint interiors and cover the cone spanned by the $\beta_\rho$. The proof builds the fan of a general class from the polytope of that class. The full fan structure of the secondary fan is stated with references and not proved. Section 3 shows that the facets of a chamber are the extremal rays of its variety, that the chamber of a flip or of a divisorial contraction is the one across the corresponding facet, and that the facet of a ray of fibre type lies on the boundary of the effective cone. So a toric $D$-MMP is a walk through chambers toward the fixed class of $D$, and it ends with a minimal model exactly when $D$ is pseudo-effective. The weighted blow-ups of $\mathbb{P}^3$ with weights $(1, 1, 2)$ and $(1, 2, 3)$ from Chapter 6 are drawn in this picture.

The spine is Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapters 14 and 15, for the secondary fan and its relation to the toric MMP, and Chapter 4 for divisors and sections. The proofs were written for the handbook from the results of the earlier chapters. No node of the map covers this chapter, and none of it is in the Lean library.
