## The secondary fan

### Statement

Fix the list $U$ of vectors and let the fan vary. Every complete simplicial fan whose rays are among the $u_\rho$ has a cone of classes in the single space $G_U$, its GKZ cone, which records its nef cone together with the vectors it leaves out. This section proves that the GKZ cones of the projective such fans have disjoint interiors and cover the cone $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ spanned by the Gale dual. So the effective cone is cut into chambers, one for each projective simplicial toric variety with rays among the $u_\rho$, and a class in general position picks out exactly one of them.

In this section $\operatorname{Cone}(U) = N_{\mathbb{R}}$. We keep the notation of Section 1: $G_U$, $\beta_\rho$, $B$, $R(U)$ and the map $\partial$.

**Definition 3.7.7 (U-fans and GKZ cones).** A *$U$-fan* is a complete simplicial fan $\Sigma$ in $N_{\mathbb{R}}$ each of whose rays is spanned by one of the $u_\rho$. Write $S(\Sigma) = \{\rho : \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} u_\rho \in \Sigma\}$ for the set of *used* indices, so that $u_\rho$ is the minimal generator of its ray for $\rho \in S(\Sigma)$; the other indices are *unused*. The *GKZ cone* of $\Sigma$ is

$$\Gamma_\Sigma = \bigcap_{\sigma} \operatorname{Cone}(\beta_\rho : \rho \notin \sigma(1)) \subseteq G_U,$$

the intersection over the maximal cones $\sigma$ of $\Sigma$, where $\sigma(1) \subseteq S(\Sigma)$ is the set of rays of $\sigma$ and the complement is taken in $\{1, \dots, r\}$. For $a \in \mathbb{R}^r$ let $\varphi_a$ be the support function of $\Sigma$ with $\varphi_a(u_\rho) = -a_\rho$ for $\rho \in S(\Sigma)$ (Definition 3.4.11), with forms $m_\sigma$ on the maximal cones, and let $D_a = \sum_{\rho \in S(\Sigma)} a_\rho D_\rho$ on $X_\Sigma$.

When $S(\Sigma) = \{1, \dots, r\}$, $\Gamma_\Sigma$ is $\operatorname{Nef}(X_\Sigma)$ by Theorem 3.7.3. In general it also records the unused vectors. Replacing $a$ by $a + \partial(m)$ replaces $\varphi_a$ by $\varphi_a - m$ and $m_\sigma$ by $m_\sigma - m$, so all the conditions below depend only on the class $[a]$.

**Theorem 3.7.8 (the GKZ cone of a fan).** Let $\Sigma$ be a $U$-fan, $S = S(\Sigma)$, and $a \in \mathbb{R}^r$.

1. $[a] \in \Gamma_\Sigma$ if and only if $\varphi_a$ is convex and $\varphi_a(u_\rho) \ge -a_\rho$ for every unused $\rho$.
2. The following are equivalent: (a) $[a]$ lies in the interior of $\Gamma_\Sigma$; (b) $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle > -a_\rho$ for every maximal cone $\sigma$ and every $\rho \notin \sigma(1)$; (c) $\varphi_a$ is strictly convex and $\varphi_a(u_\rho) > -a_\rho$ for every unused $\rho$.
3. $\Gamma_\Sigma$ has dimension $r - n$ if and only if $X_\Sigma$ is projective. In that case $D_a$ is ample for every $[a]$ in the interior of $\Gamma_\Sigma$.
4. Let $\iota_\Sigma \colon N^1(X_\Sigma) \to G_U$ send the class of $D = \sum_{\rho \in S} a_\rho D_\rho$ to the class of the vector $(-\varphi_D(u_\rho))_{\rho = 1, \dots, r}$. It is a well defined injective linear map, $G_U = \iota_\Sigma(N^1(X_\Sigma)) \oplus \operatorname{span}(\beta_\rho : \rho \notin S)$, and
$$\Gamma_\Sigma = \iota_\Sigma(\operatorname{Nef}(X_\Sigma)) + \operatorname{Cone}(\beta_\rho : \rho \notin S).$$
5. Let $[a]$ lie in the interior of $\Gamma_\Sigma$. Put $P_a = \{m \in M_{\mathbb{R}} : \langle m, u_\rho \rangle \ge -a_\rho \text{ for all } \rho\}$ and $\psi_a(x) = \min_{m \in P_a} \langle m, x \rangle$. Then $\psi_a = \varphi_a$, the maximal cones of $\Sigma$ are the sets $\{x : \psi_a(x) = \langle m, x \rangle\}$, $m \in P_a$, that have dimension $n$, and $S = \{\rho : \psi_a(u_\rho) = -a_\rho\}$. So $\Sigma$ is determined by any class in the interior of $\Gamma_\Sigma$, and different $U$-fans have GKZ cones with disjoint interiors.

Part 4 says that $\Gamma_\Sigma$ is the product of the nef cone of $X_\Sigma$ and an orthant, one coordinate $c_\rho = a_\rho + \varphi_a(u_\rho)$ for each unused vector. The map $\iota_\Sigma$ is the pullback: if $\Sigma'$ is a $U$-fan that refines $\Sigma$ and uses all the $u_\rho$, the support function of the pullback of $D$ to $X_{\Sigma'}$ is $\varphi_D$ (Section 1 of Chapter 6), so $\iota_\Sigma[D]$ is the class of that pullback. The coordinate $c_\rho$ is the coefficient of the exceptional divisor $D_\rho$ in $D'$ minus the pullback, for a divisor $D'$ on $X_{\Sigma'}$ of class $[a]$.

**Theorem 3.7.9 (the chambers cover the effective cone).** Let $\operatorname{Cone}(U) = N_{\mathbb{R}}$.

1. $\Gamma_\Sigma \subseteq \operatorname{Cone}(B)$ for every $U$-fan $\Sigma$.
2. There are finitely many proper linear subspaces $H_1, \dots, H_k$ of $G_U$ such that every class in the interior of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ and outside $H_1 \cup \dots \cup H_k$ lies in the interior of $\Gamma_\Sigma$ for exactly one $U$-fan $\Sigma$, and this $\Sigma$ is projective.
3. $\operatorname{Cone}(B) = \bigcup_\Sigma \Gamma_\Sigma$, the union over the projective $U$-fans, which are finitely many.

**Definition 3.7.10 (chambers and the secondary fan).** The GKZ cones $\Gamma_\Sigma$ of the projective $U$-fans are the *chambers* of $U$. Gelfand, Kapranov and Zelevinsky, and independently Oda and Park, and Billera, Filliman and Sturmfels, showed that the chambers and all their faces form a fan in $G_U$ with support $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, the *secondary fan* or *GKZ fan* of $U$ (see Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapters 14 and 15). Its cones of lower dimension correspond to fans that need not be simplicial. We prove only Theorems 3.7.8 and 3.7.9, that is, that the chambers have disjoint interiors and cover $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, and in Section 3 that two chambers that meet along a wall of the toric MMP meet along a common facet. That the faces of two chambers always fit together is not proved here.

For a complete simplicial fan $\Sigma_0$ with $U$ its ray generators, $\operatorname{Cone}(B) = \operatorname{Eff}(X_{\Sigma_0})$ by Theorem 3.7.3, so Theorem 3.7.9 cuts the effective cone of $X_{\Sigma_0}$ into the nef cones of all projective simplicial toric varieties with rays among those of $\Sigma_0$, each extended by its unused vectors.

### Proof in words

A class $[a]$ gives a polytope $P_a$, cut out by the inequalities $\langle m, u_\rho \rangle \ge -a_\rho$, and its minimum function $\psi_a$. When $[a]$ is in general position, at each vertex of $P_a$ exactly $n$ of the inequalities are equalities, and the corresponding $u_\rho$ form a basis. These $n$ vectors span a simplicial cone, the cone on which $\psi_a$ agrees with that vertex. The cones for the different vertices fit together into a complete simplicial fan, the normal fan of $P_a$, whose rays are the $u_\rho$ that define facets. The other $u_\rho$ are unused, and the inequalities that they define are strict on all of $P_a$. In Gale coordinates these strict inequalities are exactly the conditions for $[a]$ to lie in the interior of the GKZ cone of this fan.

Conversely, a class in the interior of a GKZ cone determines the fan, because the fan is the fan of linearity domains of $\psi_a$. So the chambers do not overlap, and the classes in general position lie in exactly one of them. The chambers are closed and finite in number, and the classes in general position are dense, so the chambers cover the whole cone $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$.

### Examples

**Example 3.7.11 (the chambers of the Hirzebruch configuration).** Let $U = (u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4) = ((1, 0), (0, 1), (-1, a), (0, -1))$ with $a \ge 1$, the rays of $\mathbb{F}_a$, with the Gale dual of Example 3.7.5: $\beta_1 = \beta_3 = (1, 0)$, $\beta_2 = (-a, 1)$, $\beta_4 = (0, 1)$. A complete fan in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is determined by its rays, and a set of rays is the set of rays of a complete fan if and only if its generators span $\mathbb{R}^2$ positively. Of the subsets of $U$, only $\{u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4\}$ and $\{u_1, u_3, u_4\}$ do: the other subsets of three vectors lie in a closed half-plane, $y \ge 0$, $x \ge 0$ or $x \le 0$. So there are two $U$-fans, the fan of $\mathbb{F}_a$ and the fan with rays $u_1, u_3, u_4$ and the relation $u_1 + u_3 + a u_4 = 0$, which is the fan of $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, a)$. Both are projective. Their GKZ cones are

$$\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_a} = \operatorname{Cone}(\beta_1, \beta_4), \qquad \Gamma_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,a)} = \operatorname{Cone}((-a, 1), (0, 1)) \cap \operatorname{Cone}((1, 0), (-a, 1)) = \operatorname{Cone}(\beta_4, \beta_2),$$

the second from the maximal cones $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_3)$, $\operatorname{Cone}(u_3, u_4)$, $\operatorname{Cone}(u_4, u_1)$ with the complements $\{2, 4\}$, $\{1, 2\}$, $\{2, 3\}$. Their union is $\operatorname{Cone}(\beta_1, \beta_2) = \operatorname{Eff}(\mathbb{F}_a)$, as Theorem 3.7.9 says, and they meet along the ray of $\beta_4$. In part 4 of Theorem 3.7.8, $\iota(\operatorname{Nef}(\mathbb{P}(1,1,a)))$ is the ray of $\beta_4$, the class of the section $D_4$ with $D_4^2 = a$, which is the pullback of the ample generator, and the unused vector $u_2$ contributes the edge $\beta_2 = [D_2]$.

The class $\beta_4$ itself is not in general position. With $a = e_4$, the polytope $P_a$ is cut out by $m_1 \ge 0$, $m_2 \ge 0$, $-m_1 + a m_2 \ge 0$ and $m_2 \le 1$. It is the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(a, 1)$, and at the vertex $(0, 0)$ the three inequalities of $u_1$, $u_2$, $u_3$ are equalities. The domains of $\psi_a$ are the three cones of the fan of $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, a)$, and $\psi_a(u_2) = 0 = -a_2$: the vector $u_2$ is on the boundary between used and unused, as it must be for a class on the common face of the two chambers. For $a = 1$ the two chambers are the nef cones of $\mathbb{F}_1$ and of $\mathbb{P}^2$, and the wall between them is the blow-down of the $(-1)$-curve.

**Example 3.7.12 (a non-simplicial domain fan).** Let $U = ((1, 0), (0, 1), (-1, 0), (0, -1))$, the rays of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$. Here $\beta_1 = \beta_3$ and $\beta_2 = \beta_4$ form a basis of $G_U = \mathbb{R}^2$, $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ is the quadrant, and there is one $U$-fan, with one chamber, the whole quadrant. The class $\beta_1$ on its boundary gives the polytope $P_a = \{0 \le m_1 \le 1, m_2 = 0\}$, a segment, and the domains of $\psi_a$ are the two half-planes $x \ge 0$ and $x \le 0$. These are not strongly convex: the class $\beta_1$ is the pullback from the factor $\mathbb{P}^1$, and its domains form the domain fan of Proposition 3.5.5, part 4, whose cones contain the line $L = \{x_1 = 0\}$, the fibre direction of the projection.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem 3.7.8.* Part 1. By part 4 of Lemma 3.7.2 with $I = \sigma(1)$, $[a] \in \operatorname{Cone}(\beta_\rho : \rho \notin \sigma(1))$ if and only if $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle \ge -a_\rho$ for all $\rho$, since the form of that lemma must be $m_\sigma$. Assume this for all maximal $\sigma$. For the used $\rho$ it says $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle \ge \varphi_a(u_\rho)$, and the computation in the proof of Theorem 3.7.3, part 1, shows that $\varphi_a$ is convex. For an unused $\rho$, pick a maximal $\sigma$ with $u_\rho \in \sigma$; then $\varphi_a(u_\rho) = \langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle \ge -a_\rho$. Conversely, if $\varphi_a$ is convex, Proposition 3.4.12, part 2, gives $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle \ge \varphi_a(u_\rho)$ for all $\sigma$ and $\rho$. This is $-a_\rho$ for used $\rho$, and at least $-a_\rho$ for unused $\rho$ by hypothesis.

Part 2. The interior of the finite intersection $\Gamma_\Sigma$ is the intersection of the interiors of the cones $\operatorname{Cone}(\beta_\rho : \rho \notin \sigma(1))$. Each is simplicial of full dimension, by part 3 of Lemma 3.7.2, and its interior is the set where all coordinates $a_\rho + \langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle$, $\rho \notin \sigma(1)$, are positive. So (a) and (b) are equivalent.

(b) implies (c). Let $(\sigma, \sigma')$ be a wall pair with the standard labels. The ray $u_{n+1}$ of $\sigma'$ is not a ray of $\sigma$, so (b) gives $a_{n+1} + \langle m_\sigma, u_{n+1} \rangle > 0$, and by Theorem 3.4.4 $D_a \cdot V(\tau)$ is a positive multiple of this number. So all wall degrees are positive, and $\varphi_a$ is strictly convex by Theorem 3.4.13. For an unused $\rho$, choose $\sigma$ with $u_\rho \in \sigma$; then $\rho \notin \sigma(1)$ and $\varphi_a(u_\rho) = \langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle > -a_\rho$.

(c) implies (b). Let $\sigma$ be maximal and $\rho \notin \sigma(1)$. If $\rho$ is unused, $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle \ge \varphi_a(u_\rho) > -a_\rho$ by Proposition 3.4.12. Let $\rho$ be used, and suppose $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle = \varphi_a(u_\rho)$. The function $h = m_\sigma - \varphi_a$ is nonnegative by Proposition 3.4.12 and convex in the usual sense, since $\varphi_a$ is concave in the usual sense. So its zero set $Z$ is convex, and it is closed under positive multiples. It contains $\sigma$ and $u_\rho$, hence $K = \operatorname{Cone}(\sigma \cup \{u_\rho\})$. The vector $u_\rho$ is not in $\sigma$: otherwise the ray $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} u_\rho \in \Sigma$ meets $\sigma$ in a face of $\sigma$ (Proposition 3.1.20), which would be a ray of $\sigma$, so $\rho \in \sigma(1)$. So $K \not\subseteq \sigma$. Since $K$ is a convex cone of dimension $n$, it is the closure of its interior, so the interior of $K$ is not contained in $\sigma$, and $O = \operatorname{int}(K) \setminus \sigma$ is a nonempty open set. It is covered by the finitely many closed sets $\sigma'$, $\sigma' \neq \sigma$ maximal, since $\Sigma$ is complete. A finite union of closed sets with empty interior has empty interior, so some $\sigma' \neq \sigma$ contains a nonempty open subset $O'$ of $O$. On $O'$, $\langle m_{\sigma'}, x \rangle = \varphi_a(x) = \langle m_\sigma, x \rangle$, so $m_{\sigma'} = m_\sigma$, which contradicts strict convexity. So $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle > \varphi_a(u_\rho) = -a_\rho$.

Part 3. If $\Gamma_\Sigma$ has an interior point, the set of $a \in \mathbb{R}^r$ that satisfy (b) is open and nonempty, so it contains a rational $a$. Then $\varphi_a$ is strictly convex by (c), with rational forms $m_\sigma$, and the $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D_a$ on the complete variety $X_\Sigma$ is ample by Cox-Little-Schenck, Theorem 6.1.14. So $X_\Sigma$ is projective. Conversely, let $X_\Sigma$ be projective and $A = \sum_{\rho \in S} a_\rho D_\rho$ an ample torus-invariant $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor (Theorem 3.3.4 gives a torus-invariant divisor in each class). Its support function is strictly convex (Theorem 3.4.19). Extend $a$ to $\mathbb{R}^r$ by $a_\rho = 1 - \varphi_a(u_\rho)$ for unused $\rho$. Then (c) holds, and $[a]$ is an interior point. For the last claim, if $[a]$ is interior, $\varphi_a$ is strictly convex by (c), and $D_a$ is ample by Theorem 3.4.19.

Part 4. If $D$ is replaced by $D + \operatorname{div}(\chi^m)$, $\varphi_D$ is replaced by $\varphi_D - m$ and the vector by its sum with $\partial(m)$, so $\iota_\Sigma$ is well defined. It is linear, since $\varphi_D$ depends linearly on the coefficients of $D$. If $\iota_\Sigma[D] = 0$, then $(-\varphi_D(u_\rho))_\rho = \partial(m)$ for some $m$, so $a_\rho = \langle m, u_\rho \rangle$ for $\rho \in S$, and $D = \operatorname{div}(\chi^m)$ has class $0$ by Theorem 3.4.20, part 1. For $a \in \mathbb{R}^r$ put $c_\rho = a_\rho + \varphi_a(u_\rho)$ for unused $\rho$. Then
$$a = (-\varphi_a(u_\rho))_{\rho} + \sum_{\rho \notin S} c_\rho e_\rho, \qquad [a] = \iota_\Sigma[D_a] + \sum_{\rho \notin S} c_\rho \beta_\rho,$$
since the two vectors agree at the used indices, where $\varphi_a(u_\rho) = -a_\rho$, and at the unused ones. So $G_U$ is the sum of the two subspaces. The dimensions are $|S| - n$ (Theorem 3.4.20) and at most $r - |S|$, with total at most $r - n = \dim G_U$, so the sum is direct and the $\beta_\rho$, $\rho \notin S$, are independent. By part 1, $[a] \in \Gamma_\Sigma$ if and only if $\varphi_a$ is convex, that is, $D_a$ is nef (Theorem 3.4.19), and all $c_\rho \ge 0$. Since the decomposition is unique, this is the claimed formula.

Part 5. By (b), each $m_\sigma$ lies in $P_a$. For $m \in P_a$ and $x = \sum_{\rho \in \sigma(1)} \lambda_\rho u_\rho \in \sigma$ with $\lambda_\rho \ge 0$,
$$\langle m, x \rangle = \sum_{\rho \in \sigma(1)} \lambda_\rho \langle m, u_\rho \rangle \ge -\sum_{\rho \in \sigma(1)} \lambda_\rho a_\rho = \langle m_\sigma, x \rangle,$$
so $\psi_a(x) = \langle m_\sigma, x \rangle = \varphi_a(x)$ on $\sigma$, and $\psi_a = \varphi_a$. If $x \notin \sigma$, then $x$ lies in some other maximal cone $\sigma'$ and $x = \sum_{\rho \in \sigma'(1)} \lambda_\rho u_\rho$ with $\lambda_\rho > 0$ for some $\rho \notin \sigma(1)$, since otherwise $x$ would lie in $\operatorname{Cone}(u_\rho : \rho \in \sigma(1) \cap \sigma'(1)) \subseteq \sigma$. Then (b) gives $\langle m_\sigma, x \rangle > -\sum_\rho \lambda_\rho a_\rho = \varphi_a(x)$. So $\{x : \psi_a(x) = \langle m_\sigma, x \rangle\} = \sigma$. If $m \in P_a$ and $C_m = \{x : \psi_a(x) = \langle m, x \rangle\}$ has dimension $n$, then $C_m$ is convex, since it is the set where the concave function $\psi_a - m$ attains its maximum $0$, so it has interior points, and by the argument of part 2 it contains a nonempty open subset of some maximal $\sigma$. There $m = m_\sigma$, so $C_m = \sigma$. The description of $S$ is $\varphi_a(u_\rho) = -a_\rho$ for used $\rho$ and (c) for unused ones. Replacing $a$ by $a + \partial(m')$ replaces $P_a$ by $P_a - m'$ and $\psi_a$ by $\psi_a - m'$, which changes none of these sets. So the maximal cones of $\Sigma$ are determined by $[a]$, and so is $\Sigma$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 3.7.9.* Part 1. $\Gamma_\Sigma \subseteq \operatorname{Cone}(\beta_\rho : \rho \notin \sigma(1)) \subseteq \operatorname{Cone}(B)$ for any maximal $\sigma$.

Part 2. For each $I \subseteq \{1, \dots, r\}$ such that $u_I$ is linearly dependent, let $A_I \subseteq \mathbb{R}^r$ be the set of $a$ for which some $m$ has $\langle m, u_\rho \rangle = -a_\rho$ for all $\rho \in I$. It is the set of $a$ whose restriction to $I$ lies in the image of $M_{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathbb{R}^I$, a subspace of dimension $\operatorname{rank}(u_I) < |I|$. So $A_I$ is a subspace of codimension at least one that contains $\partial(M_{\mathbb{R}})$, and its image $H_I$ in $G_U$ is a proper subspace. Let $[a]$ lie in the interior of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ and outside all the $H_I$.

First, $[a] = \sum_\rho c_\rho \beta_\rho$ with all $c_\rho > 0$. Indeed, $g = [a] - \varepsilon \sum_\rho \beta_\rho$ lies in $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ for small $\varepsilon > 0$, since $[a]$ is interior, so $g = \sum_\rho c'_\rho \beta_\rho$ with $c' \ge 0$, and $c = c' + \varepsilon$ works. Replace $a$ by $c$, so that all $a_\rho > 0$. Then $0 \in P_a$, and $P_a$ is a closed convex set. It is bounded: otherwise there are $m_k \in P_a$ with $|m_k| \to \infty$, a subsequence of $m_k / |m_k|$ converges to some $y$ with $|y| = 1$, and $\langle y, u_\rho \rangle \ge \lim (-a_\rho / |m_k|) = 0$ for all $\rho$, so $y \in \operatorname{Cone}(U)^\vee = 0$, a contradiction. For $m \in M_{\mathbb{R}}$ let $I(m) = \{\rho : \langle m, u_\rho \rangle = -a_\rho\}$. By the choice of $[a]$, $u_{I(m)}$ is linearly independent for every $m$, since otherwise $a \in A_{I(m)}$. Call $m \in P_a$ a *vertex* if $u_{I(m)}$ spans $N_{\mathbb{R}}$; then $u_{I(m)}$ is a basis, and we put $\sigma_m = \operatorname{Cone}(u_{I(m)})$, a simplicial cone of dimension $n$.

(i) For each $m \in P_a$ there is a vertex $m'$ with $I(m) \subseteq I(m')$. Among the $m' \in P_a$ with $I(m') \supseteq I(m)$, choose one with $|I(m')|$ maximal. If $u_{I(m')}$ does not span, pick $y \neq 0$ with $\langle y, u_\rho \rangle = 0$ for $\rho \in I(m')$. The set $T = \{t \in \mathbb{R} : m' + t y \in P_a\}$ is a compact interval, since $P_a$ is compact and convex. It contains a neighbourhood of $0$, since the inequalities for $\rho \notin I(m')$ are strict at $m'$. At its right end $t_1 > 0$, some inequality for $\rho \notin I(m')$ must be an equality, since otherwise slightly larger $t$ would lie in $T$. So $I(m' + t_1 y) \supsetneq I(m')$, which contradicts the maximality.

(ii) Every $x \in N_{\mathbb{R}}$ lies in some $\sigma_{m'}$. The linear function $\langle \cdot, x \rangle$ attains its minimum on the compact set $P_a$ at some $m$. Let $\tau = \operatorname{Cone}(u_{I(m)})$. If $x \notin \tau$, then by Theorem 3.1.3, part 2, there is $y \in \tau^\vee$ with $\langle y, x \rangle < 0$. For small $\varepsilon > 0$, $m + \varepsilon y \in P_a$, since $\langle y, u_\rho \rangle \ge 0$ for $\rho \in I(m)$ and the other inequalities are strict at $m$, and $\langle m + \varepsilon y, x \rangle < \langle m, x \rangle$, a contradiction. So $x \in \tau \subseteq \sigma_{m'}$ for a vertex $m'$ as in (i).

(iii) For a vertex $m$ and $x \in \sigma_m$, $\psi_a(x) = \langle m, x \rangle$, by the computation in the proof of part 5 of Theorem 3.7.8.

(iv) For vertices $m, m'$, $\sigma_m \cap \sigma_{m'} = \operatorname{Cone}(u_K)$ with $K = I(m) \cap I(m')$. The inclusion $\supseteq$ is clear. Let $x \in \sigma_m \cap \sigma_{m'}$ and $x = \sum_{\rho \in I(m)} \lambda_\rho u_\rho$ with $\lambda_\rho \ge 0$. By (iii), $\langle m', x \rangle = \psi_a(x) = \langle m, x \rangle = -\sum \lambda_\rho a_\rho$, while $\langle m', x \rangle = \sum \lambda_\rho \langle m', u_\rho \rangle \ge -\sum \lambda_\rho a_\rho$ with equality only if $\lambda_\rho = 0$ for every $\rho \notin I(m')$. So $x \in \operatorname{Cone}(u_K)$. Since $u_{I(m)}$ is a basis, two cones $\operatorname{Cone}(u_J)$, $\operatorname{Cone}(u_{J'})$ with $J, J' \subseteq I(m)$ meet in $\operatorname{Cone}(u_{J \cap J'})$, by the uniqueness of coordinates, and these cones are the faces of $\sigma_m$.

Let $\Sigma_a$ be the set of the faces of the cones $\sigma_m$, $m$ a vertex. By (iv) and the remark after it, two of its cones meet in a common face: for $J \subseteq I(m)$ and $J' \subseteq I(m')$, $\operatorname{Cone}(u_J) \cap \operatorname{Cone}(u_{J'}) = \operatorname{Cone}(u_J) \cap \operatorname{Cone}(u_K) \cap \operatorname{Cone}(u_{J'}) = \operatorname{Cone}(u_{J \cap J' \cap K})$, computed inside $\sigma_m$ and inside $\sigma_{m'}$. Faces of faces are faces. So $\Sigma_a$ is a simplicial fan, complete by (ii), whose rays are spanned by vectors of $U$: a $U$-fan. Different vertices give different cones, since $\sigma_m$ determines $I(m)$, the set of indices of its rays, and $I(m)$ determines $m$.

The support function $\varphi_a$ of $\Sigma_a$ has the form $m$ on $\sigma_m$, since $\langle m, u_\rho \rangle = -a_\rho$ for $\rho \in I(m)$. For $\rho \notin I(m)$, $\langle m, u_\rho \rangle > -a_\rho$, since $m \in P_a$ and $\rho \notin I(m)$. This is condition (b) of Theorem 3.7.8, part 2, so $[a]$ is in the interior of $\Gamma_{\Sigma_a}$, and $\Sigma_a$ is projective by part 3. By part 5 of Theorem 3.7.8, no other $U$-fan has $[a]$ in the interior of its GKZ cone.

Part 3. A $U$-fan is determined by its maximal cones, each of which is $\operatorname{Cone}(u_I)$ for a subset $I$, so there are finitely many $U$-fans. By part 1 the union is contained in $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$. The cone $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ has dimension $r - n$ (part 1 of Lemma 3.7.2), so it is the closure of its interior, and the interior minus the finite union of proper subspaces $H_I$ is dense in it. By part 2 that dense set lies in the union of the finitely many closed sets $\Gamma_\Sigma$, $\Sigma$ projective, so the union is all of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$. $\square$

2. (A) In Example 3.7.11, compute $P_a$ and the fan $\Sigma_a$ of the proof of Theorem 3.7.9 for the vectors $(1, 1, 1, 1)$ and $(1, 2a + 1, 1, 1)$ of $\mathbb{R}^4$, and check which chamber contains each class.
3. (B) Show that $\beta_\rho$ lies in a proper face of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ if and only if $\operatorname{Cone}(u_{\rho'} : \rho' \neq \rho)$ contains a line, and check this for $\beta_2$ in Example 3.7.11.
4. (B) Let $b \in R(U)$ be a nonzero relation with all coordinates nonnegative. Show that $\operatorname{Cone}(B) \cap b^\perp$ is a proper face of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, and that for every class $[a]$ in it the polytope $P_a$ has dimension less than $n$.
5. (C) Show that the union of the chambers of the $U$-fans that use all the $u_\rho$ is $\bigcap_\rho \operatorname{Cone}(\beta_{\rho'} : \rho' \neq \rho)$, when that cone has dimension $r - n$. (Hint: use part 4 of Lemma 3.7.2 with $I = \{\rho\}$ and part 5 of Theorem 3.7.8.)

### Notes and history

The secondary polytope and its normal fan were introduced by Gelfand, Kapranov and Zelevinsky in their work on discriminants, and the secondary fan of a vector configuration, with its relation to the projective simplicial toric varieties on given rays, was developed by Oda and Park and by Billera, Filliman and Sturmfels around 1990. Cox-Little-Schenck treat it in Chapters 14 and 15, where the chambers are called GKZ cones; this section follows their point of view but its proofs were written for the handbook and use only Chapter 4 and Section 1, with the polytope $P_a$ as the one external tool. The full statement that the cones form a fan is not proved here; the parts used in the minimal model program are the disjointness and covering of Theorem 3.7.9 and the wall crossing of Section 3. In the language of Mori theory, the chambers of the $U$-fans that use all the $u_\rho$ are the nef cones of the small $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial modifications of one of them, and their union is the movable cone (Exercise 5); toric varieties are Mori dream spaces in this sense.
