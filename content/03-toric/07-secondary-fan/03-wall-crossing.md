## Wall crossing and the MMP in the secondary fan

### Statement

The chambers of Section 2 are the nef cones of the projective simplicial toric varieties with rays among the $u_\rho$, all drawn in one space $G_U$. This section shows that the steps of the toric MMP are the passages from one chamber to an adjacent one. The facets of a chamber are the extremal rays of the variety. Across the facet of a small ray lies the chamber of the flip, across the facet of a divisorial ray lies the chamber of the contracted variety, and the facet of a ray of fibre type lies on the boundary of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, with no chamber behind it. A toric $D$-MMP is then a walk through chambers. The class of $D$ does not move, and each step crosses a wall toward it. The walk ends in the chamber that contains the class of $D$, if there is one, and otherwise on the boundary of the effective cone.

We keep the notation of Sections 1 and 2, with $\operatorname{Cone}(U) = N_{\mathbb{R}}$. For a $U$-fan $\Sigma$ we extend relations by zero: $R(\Sigma) \subseteq R(U)$ is the space of relations supported on the used indices. For a wall $\tau$ of $\Sigma$, $b^\tau \in R(\Sigma)$ is its wall relation (Theorem 3.4.5), so that $D_a \cdot V(\tau) = \sum_\rho a_\rho b^\tau_\rho = \langle [a], b^\tau \rangle$. For an unused index $\rho$, let $\gamma$ be the smallest cone of $\Sigma$ that contains $u_\rho$, write $u_\rho = \sum_{i \in \gamma(1)} \lambda_i u_i$ with $\lambda_i > 0$, and put $c^\rho = e_\rho - \sum_{i \in \gamma(1)} \lambda_i e_i \in R(U)$. The coefficients are unique because $\gamma$ is simplicial, and the same coefficients express $u_\rho$ in the rays of every maximal cone that contains it.

**Theorem 3.7.13 (the walls of a chamber).** Let $\Sigma$ be a projective $U$-fan with used set $S$, and $X = X_\Sigma$.

1. $\Gamma_\Sigma = \{ g \in G_U : \langle g, b^\tau \rangle \ge 0 \text{ for every wall } \tau, \ \langle g, c^\rho \rangle \ge 0 \text{ for every } \rho \notin S \}$.
2. For each extremal ray $R$ of $NE(X)$, with relation $b^R$ (Lemma 3.5.2), $F_R = \Gamma_\Sigma \cap (b^R)^\perp$ is a facet of $\Gamma_\Sigma$. Every facet of $\Gamma_\Sigma$ is of this form or is $\Gamma_\Sigma \cap (c^\rho)^\perp$ for an unused $\rho$.
3. Let $R$ be small, with flip $\Sigma^+$ (Theorem 3.5.20). Then $\Sigma^+$ is a projective $U$-fan with used set $S$, $\Gamma_{\Sigma^+} \subseteq \{ g : \langle g, b^R \rangle \le 0 \}$, and $\Gamma_\Sigma \cap \Gamma_{\Sigma^+} = F_R$.
4. Let $R$ be divisorial with $J_- = \{k\}$ and contraction fan $\Sigma_R$ (Theorem 3.5.15). Then $\Sigma_R$ is a projective $U$-fan with used set $S \setminus \{k\}$, $c^k = -b^R / |b^R_k|$ for $\Sigma_R$, $\Gamma_{\Sigma_R} \subseteq \{ g : \langle g, b^R \rangle \le 0 \}$, and $\Gamma_\Sigma \cap \Gamma_{\Sigma_R} = F_R$.
5. Let $R$ be of fibre type. Then $\langle g, b^R \rangle \ge 0$ for all $g \in \operatorname{Cone}(B)$, $F_R$ lies in the proper face $\operatorname{Cone}(B) \cap (b^R)^\perp$ of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, and no chamber contains a point $g$ with $\langle g, b^R \rangle < 0$.

So a chamber is a polyhedral cone whose facets are labelled by the extremal rays of its variety and by its unused vectors. Parts 3 and 4 say that two chambers that come from one step of the MMP lie on the two sides of one hyperplane and share the facet there. The last sentence of the paragraph after Theorem 3.5.20, that the flip of $R^+$ is $X$ again, is the statement that one may cross the same facet back.

**Corollary 3.7.14 (the toric MMP as a walk through chambers).** Let $\Sigma_0$ be a projective $U$-fan that uses every $u_\rho$, $X = X_{\Sigma_0}$, and $D = \sum_\rho d_\rho D_\rho$ a torus-invariant $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with class $d = [(d_\rho)] \in G_U = N^1(X)$. Let $(X_k, D_k)$, $k = 0, \dots, \ell$, be a toric $D$-MMP (Definition 3.6.7), $X_k = X_{\Sigma_k}$.

1. Each $\Sigma_k$ is a projective $U$-fan, and $D_k = \sum_{\rho \in S(\Sigma_k)} d_\rho D_\rho$, so the class $d$ is the same at every step.
2. $\varphi_{D_{k+1}} \ge \varphi_{D_k}$ on $N_{\mathbb{R}}$, and $\varphi_{D_k}(u_\rho) > -d_\rho$ for every $\rho$ unused by $\Sigma_k$.
3. $D_k$ is nef if and only if $d \in \Gamma_{\Sigma_k}$.
4. If $D_k$ is not nef and $R_k$ is the ray of the $k$-th step, then $\langle d, b^{R_k} \rangle < 0$. If the step is a flip or a divisorial contraction, $\Gamma_{\Sigma_{k+1}}$ is the chamber across the facet $F_{R_k}$ of $\Gamma_{\Sigma_k}$, on the side of $d$.
5. The $D$-MMP ends with a minimal model if and only if $D$ is pseudo-effective, that is, $d \in \operatorname{Cone}(B) = \operatorname{Eff}(X)$. Then the minimal model $X_\ell$ is a variety whose chamber contains $d$. Otherwise it ends with a Mori fibre space, whose ray $R_\ell$ has $b^{R_\ell} \ge 0$, and $b^{R_\ell}$ defines a face of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ that separates $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ from $d$.

Part 5 is the toric form of a principle of the general theory, that the MMP of a divisor that is not pseudo-effective ends with a Mori fibre space. In the toric case every effective divisor also has a minimal model, and every toric $D$-MMP reaches one. Since $K_X$ is never pseudo-effective on a complete toric variety ($-K_X = \sum_\rho D_\rho$ and $\sum_\rho \beta_\rho$ is an interior point of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, which contains no line), part 5 recovers Corollary 3.6.9 for $\Delta = 0$.

### Proof in words

The inequalities that cut out a chamber are those of Theorem 3.7.8, part 1. Convexity of the support function is the wall inequality at every wall, which is positivity against the wall relations, and the condition at an unused vector is positivity against the relation that expresses it in the cone that contains it. A supporting divisor of an extremal ray, plus a little of each unused vector, satisfies all of these inequalities strictly, except those of the walls of the ray, which are proportional to one another. So the ray cuts out a facet.

Take a class on this facet. Its support function is convex and linear across the walls of the ray, so it is linear on the cones of the contraction fan. The fan on the other side, the flip or the contracted fan, refines the contraction fan, so the support function there is the same function, still convex, and the class lies in both chambers. On the other side the walls of the ray have the opposite relation $-b^R$, or the contracted vector has the relation $-b^R / |b^R_k|$, so the new chamber lies on the other side of the hyperplane. For a ray of fibre type the relation $b^R$ is nonnegative, every generator $\beta_\rho$ pairs nonnegatively with it, and the facet is part of the boundary of the effective cone.

In the MMP the class does not move, because the coefficients of the divisor do not change. The divisor is negative on the ray it contracts, so the class is on the far side of the facet, and the step moves into the chamber on that side. Pseudo-effectivity decides how the walk ends.

### Examples

**Example 3.7.15 (the chambers of the $(1, 1, 2)$ blow-up).** Let $X$ be the blow-up of Proposition 3.6.14: $U = (e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3, v)$ in $N = \mathbb{Z}^3$, with $e_0 = -e_1 - e_2 - e_3$ and $v = e_1 + e_2 + 2 e_3$, and $R(U)$ has the basis $r_1 = (1, 1, 1, 1, 0)$, $r_2 = (0, -1, -1, -2, 1)$. The map $g \mapsto (\langle g, r_1 \rangle, \langle g, r_2 \rangle)$ identifies $G_U$ with $\mathbb{R}^2$, and the Gale dual is

$$\beta_0 = (1, 0), \quad \beta_1 = \beta_2 = (1, -1), \quad \beta_3 = (1, -2), \quad \beta_v = (0, 1).$$

So $\operatorname{Cone}(B) = \operatorname{Cone}((0, 1), (1, -2))$. There are three chambers:

- $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}^3} = \operatorname{Cone}((0, 1), (1, 0))$. The fan of $\mathbb{P}^3$ leaves out $v$. The hyperplane class $H = D_0$ has $\varphi_H = 0$ on $\operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_2, e_3) \ni v$, so $\iota(H) = \beta_0$, and the unused vector contributes $\beta_v$ (Theorem 3.7.8, part 4).
- $\Gamma_X = \operatorname{Cone}((1, 0), (1, -1))$, the dual of $NE(X) = \operatorname{Cone}(r_1 + r_2, -r_2)$ from Proposition 3.6.14. Its facet on the ray of $(1, 0)$ is $F_\ell$, with $b = -r_2$, and its facet on the ray of $(1, -1)$ is $F_{C_{13}}$, with $b = r_1 + r_2$.
- $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,2)} = \operatorname{Cone}((1, -1), (1, -2))$. The fan leaves out $e_3$, which contributes $\beta_3$. Its relation $2 e_0 + e_1 + e_2 + v = 0$ is $2 r_1 + r_2$, which vanishes on $(1, -2)$.

These cover $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, as Theorem 3.7.9 says, and so $X$, $\mathbb{P}^3$ and $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 1, 2)$ are the only projective simplicial toric varieties with rays among those of $X$. The facets are as Theorem 3.7.13 predicts. Crossing $F_\ell$ from $\Gamma_X$ leads to $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}^3}$, the blow-down of $E = D_v$, and crossing $F_{C_{13}}$ leads to $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,2)}$. The ray of $(0, 1)$ is the facet of the fibre-type ray $r_1$ of $\mathbb{P}^3$, the ray of $(1, -2)$ is the facet of the fibre-type ray $2 r_1 + r_2$ of $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 1, 2)$, and both lie on the boundary of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$.

The anticanonical class is $-K = \sum_\rho \beta_\rho = (4, -3)$. It lies in the interior of $\Gamma_X$, so $X$ is Fano, in accordance with $K \cdot C_{13} = -1$ and $K \cdot \ell = -3$. The class $K = (-4, 3)$ has negative first coordinate, so it is not in $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, and both $K$-MMPs of Proposition 3.6.14 end with a Mori fibre space. For $D = D_3$, with class $(1, -2)$, one has $\langle d, r_1 + r_2 \rangle = -1 < 0$ and $\langle d, -r_2 \rangle = 2 > 0$, so the $D_3$-MMP contracts $C_{13}$, and it stops on $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 1, 2)$, where the pushforward of $D_3$ is $0$ and $d = \beta_3$ is the edge of the unused vector.

**Example 3.7.16 (the chambers of the $(1, 2, 3)$ blow-up).** Let $X$ be the blow-up of Proposition 3.6.15, with $v = e_1 + 2 e_2 + 3 e_3$, $r_1 = (1, 1, 1, 1, 0)$, $r_2 = (0, -1, -2, -3, 1)$ and the same coordinates. Now

$$\beta_0 = (1, 0), \quad \beta_1 = (1, -1), \quad \beta_2 = (1, -2), \quad \beta_3 = (1, -3), \quad \beta_v = (0, 1),$$

$\operatorname{Cone}(B) = \operatorname{Cone}((0, 1), (1, -3))$, and there are four chambers, in order of the slope:

$$\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}^3} = \operatorname{Cone}((0, 1), (1, 0)), \quad \Gamma_X = \operatorname{Cone}((1, 0), (1, -1)), \quad \Gamma_{X^+} = \operatorname{Cone}((1, -1), (1, -2)), \quad \Gamma_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,2,3)} = \operatorname{Cone}((1, -2), (1, -3)).$$

The first is computed as in Example 3.7.15. The second is the dual of $NE(X) = \operatorname{Cone}(r_1 + r_2, -r_2)$, and the third is the dual of $NE(X^+) = \operatorname{Cone}(-(r_1 + r_2), 2 r_1 + r_2)$, the rays of $C^+$ and $C^+_{13}$ in Proposition 3.6.15 up to positive factors: the conditions $-x - y \ge 0$ and $2 x + y \ge 0$ cut out the cone between $(1, -1)$ and $(1, -2)$. The fourth contains the edge $\beta_3$ of the unused vector $e_3$, and its other edge $(1, -2)$ is the common facet with $\Gamma_{X^+}$. Since the four cones cover $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, Theorem 3.7.9 shows that $X$ and $X^+$ are the only projective simplicial toric varieties with all five rays, and that the only others with rays among these are $\mathbb{P}^3$ and $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, 2, 3)$.

The chamber walk shows each step of Proposition 3.6.15:

- Between $\Gamma_X$ and $\Gamma_{X^+}$ lies the ray of $(1, -1)$, the facet $F_{C_{23}}$ with $b = r_1 + r_2$. It is a wall between two chambers that use all five vectors: the flip.
- Between $\Gamma_{X^+}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,2,3)}$ lies the ray of $(1, -2)$, with $b = 2 r_1 + r_2$, proportional to the ray of $C^+_{13}$: the divisorial contraction of $D^+_3$.
- The union $\Gamma_X \cup \Gamma_{X^+} = \operatorname{Cone}((1, 0), (1, -2))$ is the cone of classes of the $U$-fans that use all the vectors, the movable cone of Exercise 6 of Section 1.

The anticanonical class is $-K = (4, -5)$. It satisfies $-x - y = 1 > 0$ and $2x + y = 3 > 0$, so it lies in the interior of $\Gamma_{X^+}$: $X^+$ is Fano and $X$ is not, as Proposition 3.6.15 found. The class $K = (-4, 5)$ pairs with $b = r_1 + r_2$ to $1 > 0$ and with $-r_2$ to $-5 < 0$, so the only $K$-negative extremal ray of $X$ is the blow-down, and the $K$-MMP of $X$ crosses into $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}^3}$ and ends there with the fibre-type ray $r_1$, whose facet $(0, 1)$ is on the boundary of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$. For $D = D_3$, with class $(1, -3)$, the walk goes the other way. In $X$, $\langle d, r_1 + r_2 \rangle = -2 < 0$ and $\langle d, -r_2 \rangle = 3 > 0$, so the first step is the flip to $X^+$. In $X^+$, $\langle d, -(r_1 + r_2) \rangle = 2$ and $\langle d, 2 r_1 + r_2 \rangle = -1$, so the second step contracts $D^+_3$, and the walk stops in $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,2,3)}$, which contains $d = \beta_3$.

**Example 3.7.17 (the Hirzebruch surfaces).** In Example 3.7.11 the relations $b_1 = (1, -a, 1, 0)$ and $b_2 = (0, 1, 0, 1)$ of Example 3.7.5 are the wall relations of the walls $\operatorname{Cone}(u_2)$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1)$, the classes of the negative section $D_2$ and of a fibre, and the coordinates of $G_U = \mathbb{R}^2$ are $g \mapsto (\langle g, b_1 \rangle, \langle g, b_2 \rangle)$. The cone of curves of $\mathbb{F}_a$ is $\operatorname{Cone}(b_1, b_2)$. The ray of $b_1$ has $J_- = \{2\}$, so it is divisorial, and its facet $\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_a} \cap b_1^\perp$ is the ray of $\beta_4 = (0, 1)$, the common facet with $\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}(1,1,a)}$ (Theorem 3.7.13, part 4). The contraction is $\mathbb{F}_1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ for $a = 1$ and the contraction $\mathbb{F}_a \to \mathbb{P}(1, 1, a)$ of the negative section to the point $\frac1a(1, 1)$ for $a \ge 2$. The ray of $b_2 \ge 0$ is of fibre type, the ruling $\mathbb{F}_a \to \mathbb{P}^1$, and its facet is the ray of $\beta_1 = (1, 0)$, on the boundary of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ (part 5). The canonical class is $K = -\sum_\rho \beta_\rho = (a - 2, -2)$. Its second coordinate is negative, so it is not in $\operatorname{Cone}(B) = \operatorname{Cone}((1, 0), (-a, 1))$, and every $K$-MMP ends with a Mori fibre space. For $a \ge 2$ only the ruling is $K$-negative, since $\langle K, b_1 \rangle = a - 2 \ge 0$,, and for $a = 1$ the first step may also be the blow-down to $\mathbb{P}^2$.

### Proof

*Proof of Theorem 3.7.13.* Part 1. By Theorem 3.7.8, part 1, $[a] \in \Gamma_\Sigma$ if and only if $\varphi_a$ is convex and $\varphi_a(u_\rho) \ge -a_\rho$ for every unused $\rho$. By Theorem 3.4.13 the first condition is $D_a \cdot V(\tau) \ge 0$ for every wall $\tau$, and $D_a \cdot V(\tau) = \sum_\rho a_\rho b^\tau_\rho = \langle [a], b^\tau \rangle$ by Theorem 3.4.20, part 3. For an unused $\rho$ with $u_\rho = \sum_{i \in \gamma(1)} \lambda_i u_i$, linearity of $\varphi_a$ on $\gamma$ gives $\varphi_a(u_\rho) = -\sum_i \lambda_i a_i$, so $a_\rho + \varphi_a(u_\rho) = \langle [a], c^\rho \rangle$.

Part 2. By Theorem 3.7.8, part 3, $\Gamma_\Sigma$ has dimension $r - n$. Let $P = \sum_{\rho \in S} p_\rho D_\rho$ be a supporting divisor of $R$ (Lemma 3.5.4) and $g_0 = \iota_\Sigma[P] + \sum_{\rho \notin S} \beta_\rho$. Let $p \in \mathbb{R}^r$ be the vector with $p_\rho$ at $\rho \in S$ and $-\varphi_P(u_\rho) + 1$ at $\rho \notin S$; by the formula in the proof of Theorem 3.7.8, part 4, $g_0 = [p]$. For a wall $\tau$, $\langle g_0, b^\tau \rangle = P \cdot V(\tau)$, since $b^\tau$ is supported on $S$. It is $0$ if $\tau$ is an $R$-wall and positive otherwise (Lemma 3.5.4). For an unused $\rho$, $\langle g_0, c^\rho \rangle = p_\rho + \varphi_p(u_\rho) = 1$, since $\varphi_p = \varphi_P$. The wall relations of the $R$-walls are positive multiples of $b^R$ (Lemma 3.5.2, part 2). So by part 1 there is a neighbourhood $V$ of $g_0$ with $\Gamma_\Sigma \cap V = \{ g \in V : \langle g, b^R \rangle \ge 0 \}$, and $F_R \cap V = V \cap (b^R)^\perp$, which has dimension $r - n - 1$. Since $b^R$ is nonnegative on $\Gamma_\Sigma$ by part 1, $F_R$ is a face, and it is a facet.

Now let $F$ be a facet. By part 1 and Theorem 3.1.3, $\Gamma_\Sigma^\vee$ is the cone spanned by the vectors $v_j$, the $b^\tau$ and the $c^\rho$, and $F = \Gamma_\Sigma \cap w^\perp$ for some $w = \sum_j \mu_j v_j$ with $\mu_j \ge 0$ (Proposition 3.1.4). Then $F \subseteq \Gamma_\Sigma \cap v_j^\perp$ whenever $\mu_j > 0$. No $v_j$ is $0$, and $\Gamma_\Sigma$ spans $G_U$, so $\Gamma_\Sigma \cap v_j^\perp$ is a proper face containing the facet $F$, and equals $F$. If one such $v_j$ is some $c^\rho$, we are done. Otherwise $F = \Gamma_\Sigma \cap (b^\tau)^\perp$ for a wall $\tau$. The space of linear forms that vanish on $F$ is the line $\mathbb{R} b^\tau$, since $F$ has dimension $r - n - 1$. If $b^\tau = x + y$ with $x, y \in NE(X) = \operatorname{Cone}(b^{\tau'})$ (Theorem 3.4.21), then $x$ and $y$ are nonnegative on $\Gamma_\Sigma$ and sum to $0$ on $F$, so they vanish on $F$ and lie on the line $\mathbb{R} b^\tau$. Since $NE(X)$ contains no line (Theorem 3.4.21), they are nonnegative multiples of $b^\tau$. So $R = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} b^\tau$ is an extremal ray, $b^R$ is a positive multiple of $b^\tau$, and $F = F_R$.

Part 3. By Theorem 3.5.20, parts 1 and 4, $\Sigma^+$ is complete and simplicial with the same rays as $\Sigma$, and $X^+$ is projective, so $\Sigma^+$ is a projective $U$-fan with used set $S$. By part 3 of that theorem the walls of $\Sigma^+$ in the interiors of the maximal cones of $\Sigma_R$ have wall relations that are positive multiples of $-b^R$, and such walls exist (Lemma 3.5.11, part 4, applied to $T_-(W_C)$ with $|J_-| \ge 2$). By part 1 applied to $\Sigma^+$, $\langle g, -b^R \rangle \ge 0$ on $\Gamma_{\Sigma^+}$. So $\Gamma_\Sigma \cap \Gamma_{\Sigma^+} \subseteq \Gamma_\Sigma \cap (b^R)^\perp = F_R$.

Conversely, let $g = [a] \in F_R$, and let $\varphi_a$ be the support function of $\Sigma$ with forms $m_\sigma$. It is convex, and $D_a \cdot V(\tau) = 0$ for every $R$-wall $\tau$. By Theorem 3.4.4 and the wall inequality, $D_a \cdot V(\tau)$ is a positive multiple of $a_{n+1} + \langle m_\sigma, u_{n+1} \rangle$ in the standard labels, which vanishes if and only if $m_\sigma = m_{\sigma'}$, since $m_\sigma$ and $m_{\sigma'}$ agree on $\tau$ and $\sigma'$ is spanned by $\tau$ and $u_{n+1}$. So $m_\sigma = m_{\sigma'}$ across every $R$-wall. By Theorem 3.5.6, part 6, each maximal cone $C$ of $\Sigma_R$ is the union of maximal cones of $\Sigma$ joined by chains of $R$-walls, so $\varphi_a$ is linear on $C$. Every maximal cone $\sigma^+$ of $\Sigma^+$ lies in a maximal cone $C$ of $\Sigma_R$ (Theorem 3.5.20, part 1). The support function $\varphi^+_a$ of $\Sigma^+$ with values $-a_\rho$ at the used rays is linear on $\sigma^+$ and agrees with $\varphi_a$ at the rays of $\sigma^+$, which span $N_{\mathbb{R}}$; since $\varphi_a$ is linear on $C \supseteq \sigma^+$, $\varphi^+_a = \varphi_a$ on $\sigma^+$. So $\varphi^+_a = \varphi_a$ on $N_{\mathbb{R}}$. It is convex, and for unused $\rho$, $\varphi^+_a(u_\rho) = \varphi_a(u_\rho) \ge -a_\rho$. By Theorem 3.7.8, part 1, $g \in \Gamma_{\Sigma^+}$.

Part 4. By Theorem 3.5.15, part 4, $\Sigma_R$ is simplicial with rays $\Sigma(1) \setminus \{\rho_k\}$, and $Z_R$ is projective, so $\Sigma_R$ is a projective $U$-fan with used set $S \setminus \{k\}$. The vector $u_k$ lies in the circuit $Z$, so in a maximal cone $C = \operatorname{Cone}(W_C)$ of $\Sigma_R$ of the second kind, and $W_C \setminus \{u_k\}$ is a basis (Lemma 3.5.11, part 1) that spans the maximal cone $C$ of $\Sigma_R$. The relation $b^R$ is supported on $W_C$ and gives $u_k = \sum_{i \neq k} (b^R_i / |b^R_k|) u_i$, a sum over $W_C \setminus \{u_k\}$ with nonnegative coefficients, since $J_- = \{k\}$. By uniqueness of coordinates in a basis, these are the coefficients $\lambda_i$ that define $c^k$ for $\Sigma_R$, and
$$c^k = e_k - \sum_{i \neq k} \frac{b^R_i}{|b^R_k|} e_i = -\frac{1}{|b^R_k|} \Big( b^R_k e_k + \sum_{i \neq k} b^R_i e_i \Big) = -\frac{b^R}{|b^R_k|}.$$
By part 1 applied to $\Sigma_R$, $\langle g, -b^R \rangle \ge 0$ on $\Gamma_{\Sigma_R}$, and as in part 3 the intersection lies in $F_R$. Conversely, for $g = [a] \in F_R$, the argument of part 3 shows that $\varphi_a$ is linear on each maximal cone of $\Sigma_R$, so it equals the support function of $\Sigma_R$ with values $-a_\rho$ at the rays of $\Sigma_R$, which is therefore convex. For the indices unused by $\Sigma$ the inequality is unchanged, and for $k$, $\varphi_a(u_k) = -a_k$ because $k$ is used by $\Sigma$. So $g \in \Gamma_{\Sigma_R}$.

Part 5. If $J_- = \emptyset$, then $b^R \neq 0$ has nonnegative coordinates, and $\langle \beta_\rho, b^R \rangle = b^R_\rho \ge 0$ for every $\rho$, positive for some. So $b^R$ is nonnegative on $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ and not identically zero on it, $\operatorname{Cone}(B) \cap (b^R)^\perp$ is a proper face, and it contains $F_R$. Every chamber lies in $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ (Theorem 3.7.9, part 1), so no chamber contains a point with $\langle g, b^R \rangle < 0$. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 3.7.14.* Part 1. By Theorem 3.6.8, part 1, each $\Sigma_k$ is complete and simplicial and $X_k$ is projective. Its rays are among those of $\Sigma_0$, since a flip keeps the rays and a divisorial contraction removes one (Theorem 3.5.20, part 1, and Theorem 3.5.15, part 4). So $\Sigma_k$ is a projective $U$-fan. The strict transform keeps the coefficients of the remaining divisors, and the pushforward drops the contracted one, so $D_k = \sum_{\rho \in S(\Sigma_k)} d_\rho D_\rho$ by induction. We apply Theorems 3.7.8 and 3.7.13 to $\Sigma_k$ and the fixed vector $(d_\rho)_{\rho = 1, \dots, r}$. Its support function on $\Sigma_k$ is $\varphi_{D_k}$.

Part 2. For a flip, $\varphi_{D_{k+1}} \ge \varphi_{D_k}$ is Proposition 3.6.1, part 1. Let the step be a divisorial contraction $\pi \colon X_k \to X_{k+1}$ with exceptional divisor $E = D_j$, $J_- = \{j\}$, and let $C$ be a curve with class in $R_k$. The divisor $\pi^* D_{k+1}$ is the torus-invariant divisor on $X_k$ with support function $\varphi_{D_{k+1}}$, since $\Sigma_k$ refines $\Sigma_{k+1}$ (Section 1 of Chapter 6). Its coefficient at $D_\rho$, $\rho \neq j$, is $d_\rho$, so $D_k = \pi^* D_{k+1} + c E$ for a number $c$. Since $\pi^* D_{k+1} \cdot C = 0$, $D_k \cdot C = c \, E \cdot C$. Here $D_k \cdot C < 0$, and $E \cdot C < 0$ because $j \in J_-$ (Lemma 3.5.2, part 2, and Theorem 3.4.20, part 3). So $c > 0$. Let $x = \sum_{i \in \sigma(1)} \lambda_i u_i$ with $\lambda_i \ge 0$ lie in a maximal cone $\sigma$ of $\Sigma_k$. Linearity on $\sigma$ gives
$$\varphi_{D_{k+1}}(x) - \varphi_{D_k}(x) = -\sum_{i \in \sigma(1)} \lambda_i \big( (d_i - c \, \delta_{ij}) - d_i \big) = c \lambda_j \ge 0,$$
where $\delta_{ij}$ is $1$ if $i = j$ and $0$ otherwise. In particular $\varphi_{D_{k+1}}(u_j) = -d_j + c > -d_j$. So $\varphi_{D_{k+1}} \ge \varphi_{D_k}$ in both cases. If $\rho$ is unused by $\Sigma_k$, it was contracted at some step $i < k$, so $\varphi_{D_k}(u_\rho) \ge \varphi_{D_{i+1}}(u_\rho) > -d_\rho$. No index is unused by $\Sigma_0$.

Part 3. By Theorem 3.7.8, part 1, $d \in \Gamma_{\Sigma_k}$ if and only if $\varphi_{D_k}$ is convex and $\varphi_{D_k}(u_\rho) \ge -d_\rho$ for the unused $\rho$. The second condition holds by part 2, and the first is the nefness of $D_k$ (Theorem 3.4.19).

Part 4. The relation $b^{R_k}$ is supported on $S(\Sigma_k)$, so $\langle d, b^{R_k} \rangle = \sum_{\rho \in S(\Sigma_k)} d_\rho b^{R_k}_\rho$, which is a positive multiple of $D_k \cdot R_k < 0$ (Theorem 3.4.20, part 3). By Theorem 3.7.13, parts 3 and 4, $\Gamma_{\Sigma_{k+1}}$ lies in $\{\langle g, b^{R_k} \rangle \le 0\}$, the side of $d$, and meets $\Gamma_{\Sigma_k}$ in the facet $F_{R_k}$.

Part 5. By Theorem 3.6.8, part 3, the MMP is finite and ends with a minimal model or a Mori fibre space. If it ends with a minimal model $X_\ell$, then $d \in \Gamma_{\Sigma_\ell} \subseteq \operatorname{Cone}(B)$ by part 3 and Theorem 3.7.9, part 1. If it ends with a Mori fibre space, then $J_- = \emptyset$ for $R_\ell$ (Theorem 3.5.6, part 5), so $b^{R_\ell} \ge 0$, and $\langle d, b^{R_\ell} \rangle < 0$ by the argument of part 4, while $b^{R_\ell}$ is nonnegative on $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ by Theorem 3.7.13, part 5. So $d \notin \operatorname{Cone}(B)$. The two cases exclude each other, and $\operatorname{Cone}(B) = \operatorname{Eff}(X)$ by Theorem 3.7.3, part 3. This cone is closed, so it is also the pseudo-effective cone. $\square$

The remark after the corollary needs one more line. The vector $(1, \dots, 1)$ has positive coordinates, so $\sum_\rho \beta_\rho = [-K_X]$ is an interior point of $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$, by the argument at the start of the proof of Theorem 3.7.9, part 2, read backwards: a small ball around it consists of classes $\sum_\rho (1 + \epsilon_\rho) \beta_\rho$ with $|\epsilon_\rho| < 1$, because the $\beta_\rho$ span $G_U$. If $K_X$ were in $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ too, $\operatorname{Cone}(B)$ would contain the line through $\pm [K_X]$, against Lemma 3.7.2, part 5, since $[K_X] \neq 0$ when $r > n$.

### Notes and history

Cox-Little-Schenck describe the toric MMP as a walk through the GKZ chambers in Chapter 15, using the GKZ cones of Chapter 14, and that is the picture of this section. Fujino and Sato (2004) run the toric MMP for an arbitrary $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor, as in Chapter 6, without the secondary fan. Reid's paper of 1983, which set up the toric MMP, was not read for this section, and the handbook makes no claim about what it says on chambers. The general version, for a variety whose effective cone is cut by finitely many such chambers, is the theory of Mori dream spaces of Hu and Keel, in which a projective $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial toric variety is the basic example.

The proofs here use only the results of Chapters 4 to 6 and Section 2. The chamber of the flip is found by the observation that a class on the facet $F_R$ has a support function that is linear on the cones of the contraction fan, so the same function is the support function on every fan that refines the contraction fan. In Chapter 8 the same two chambers appear as the two sides of the variation of a GIT quotient by $\mathbb{C}^*$.
