# Variation of GIT for $\mathbb{C}^*$

Chapter 7 put all the projective simplicial toric varieties with rays among a fixed list $U$ into one space of classes $G_U$, cut into chambers, and the steps of the toric minimal model program became passages from one chamber to the next. This chapter finds the same picture in a quotient construction. A list of $r$ integers $q = (q_1, \dots, q_r)$ is an action of the multiplicative group on $\mathbb{A}^r$, with $t \cdot x = (t^{q_1} x_1, \dots, t^{q_r} x_r)$. There is no good quotient of all of $\mathbb{A}^r$: the orbits are not closed, and the ring of invariants forgets most of them. Geometric invariant theory chooses a character $\chi$, removes the points where every semi-invariant of weight a positive multiple of $\chi$ vanishes, and takes the quotient of the rest. For $\mathbb{G}_m$ the character is an integer, and only its sign matters. So there are three quotients: $X_+$, $X_0$ and $X_-$, with proper morphisms $X_+ \to X_0 \leftarrow X_-$.

Section 1 defines the three quotients as Proj of rings of semi-invariants, covers $X_\pm$ by charts, and shows that the points of $X_+$ are the orbits of the semistable points. The examples are projective space, the weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1,1,2)$ and the hyperbola $q = (1, -1)$, where the affine quotient identifies three orbits. Section 2 proves that the quotients are the toric varieties of Chapter 5: $X_0$ is the affine toric variety of the cone of the circuit with relation $q$, and $X_\pm$ are the toric varieties of its two triangulations $T_\pm$. The class group of $X_\pm$ is the character group $\mathbb{Z}$, and the divisor $D_i$ has class $q_i$. The examples are the blow-up of the plane at a point, $q = (1, 1, -1)$; the resolution of $\frac1n(1,1)$, $q = (1, 1, -n)$; the Atiyah flop, $q = (1, 1, -1, -1)$; and the Francia flip, $q = (1, 1, -1, -2)$. Section 3 puts the two quotients on the two sides of the wall $\chi = 0$ of the character line, which is the space of classes of Chapter 7, and proves that crossing the wall is the flip, the divisorial contraction or the Mori fibre space of Chapter 5, according to the number of negative weights. It states without proof the version for a torus of higher dimension, in which the chambers of the secondary fan are the GIT chambers of the Cox quotient.

The spine is Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapter 14, for the quotient construction and its GIT chambers, and Chapter 15 for the relation with the toric MMP. Thaddeus and Dolgachev-Hu introduced the variation of GIT quotients in general. The proofs here are written for the handbook from Chapters 2, 5 and 7 and use only graded rings and semigroups. No node of the map covers this chapter, and none of it is in the Lean library.
