## The quotients are toric

### Statement

The charts of Proposition 3.8.2 are spectra of semigroup algebras, so the quotients are toric varieties. This section finds their fans: they are the cone of a circuit and its two triangulations from Chapter 5.

Keep the notation of Section 1, and assume from now on that $r \ge 3$, $J_0 = \emptyset$, and that the weights are *well formed*: $\gcd(q_i : i \neq l) = 1$ for every $l$. In particular $q$ is primitive. Let
$$N = \mathbb{Z}^r / \mathbb{Z} q, \qquad u_i = \text{the image of } e_i, \qquad W = \{u_1, \dots, u_r\}.$$
Since $q$ is primitive, $N$ is a lattice of rank $n = r - 1$, and its dual is $M = \{ m \in \mathbb{Z}^r : \langle q, m \rangle = 0 \}$, with $\langle m, u_i \rangle = m_i$. So $M$ is the lattice of exponents of the invariant Laurent monomials.

**Lemma 3.8.7 (the circuit of the weights).** The vectors $u_i$ are distinct and primitive, they generate $N$, and $\sum_i q_i u_i = 0$. So $(W, q)$ is circuit data in the sense of Definition 3.5.10 when $J_+$ and $J_-$ are both nonempty, and $\operatorname{mult} \operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\}) = |q_j|$ for every $j$.

**Theorem 3.8.8 (the quotients are toric).** Let $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}(W)$ when $J_+$ and $J_-$ are nonempty.

1. If $J_+, J_- \neq \emptyset$, then $X_0 = U_\sigma$, the affine toric variety of the cone of the circuit.
2. If $J_+, J_- \neq \emptyset$, then $X_+ = X_{T_+}$ and $X_- = X_{T_-}$, the toric varieties of the two triangulations of Definition 3.5.10. The chart $D_+(x_j)$, $j \in J_\pm$, is $U_{\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})}$, and $\pi_\pm \colon X_\pm \to X_0$ are the toric morphisms of Theorem 3.5.14.
3. If $J_- = \emptyset$, then $X_0 = \operatorname{Spec} k$, $X_- = \emptyset$, and $X_+$ is the toric variety of the fan whose cones are spanned by the proper subsets of $W$, the weighted projective space $\mathbb{P}(q_1, \dots, q_r)$ of Example 3.7.4.
4. Let $u_i$ be a ray of the fan of $X_+$. Then the image under $\phi_+$ of $V(x_i) \cap \mathbb{A}^r_+$ is the divisor $D_{u_i}$.
5. If every $u_i$ is a ray of the fan of $X_+$, which holds if and only if $|J_+| \ge 2$ or $J_- = \emptyset$, then $\operatorname{Cl}(X_+) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, with $[D_{u_i}] \mapsto q_i$. So the class of the divisor of $x_i$ is the weight of $x_i$, and the class group is the group of characters of $\mathbb{G}_m$.

### Proof in words

A Laurent monomial is invariant exactly when its exponent lies in $M$, and it is regular on the chart $x_j \neq 0$ exactly when its exponent is nonnegative at the other coordinates. Since $\langle m, u_i \rangle = m_i$, these exponents are the lattice points of the dual of $\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})$. So each chart is the affine toric variety of one cone, the gluing of the charts along $x_j x_{j'} \neq 0$ is the gluing of the cones along their common face, and the cones that appear are those of the triangulation $T_+$ for the positive indices. The divisor $x_i = 0$ maps to the divisor of the ray $u_i$, and the exact sequence of Theorem 3.3.4 becomes $0 \to M \to \mathbb{Z}^r \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$, whose last map is the weight.

### Examples

**Example 3.8.9 (the blow-up of a point).** Let $q = (1, 1, -1)$ with variables $x, y, z$. The invariants are spanned by the $x^a y^b z^c$ with $a + b = c$, so $R_0 = k[xz, yz]$ is a polynomial ring, and $X_0 = \mathbb{A}^2$. As $J_- = \{3\}$, $X_-$ is the single chart $A_z = k[xz, yz]$ and $\pi_-$ is an isomorphism. The charts of $X_+$ are
$$A_x = k[y/x, xz], \qquad A_y = k[x/y, yz],$$
and in the coordinates $(s, t) = (xz, yz)$ of $X_0$ they are $k[t/s, s]$ and $k[s/t, t]$: $X_+$ is the blow-up of $\mathbb{A}^2$ at the origin (Example 3.2.40), with exceptional curve the image of $z = 0$, which is $\{(x, y) \neq 0\}/k^* = \mathbb{P}^1$. In $N = \mathbb{Z}^3/\mathbb{Z}q$, $u_3 = u_1 + u_2$, $T_+$ has the cones $\operatorname{Cone}(u_2, u_3)$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_3)$, and $T_-$ is the single cone $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2)$, in which $u_3$ is not a ray. By part 5, $\operatorname{Cl}(X_+) = \mathbb{Z}$ with $[D_{u_1}] = [D_{u_2}] = 1$ and $[E] = [D_{u_3}] = -1$.

**Example 3.8.10 (the cones $\frac1n(1,1)$).** Let $q = (1, 1, -n)$ with $n \ge 2$ and variables $x, y, z$. The invariants are spanned by the $x^a y^b z^c$ with $a + b = nc$, and $R_0$ is generated by $x^i y^{n - i} z$, $0 \le i \le n$: this is the cone $\frac1n(1,1)$ of Example 3.2.15. So $X_0 = X_-$ is the singular cone. The charts of $X_+$ are $A_x = k[y/x, x^n z]$ and $A_y = k[x/y, y^n z]$, two planes, glued along $y/x \mapsto (x/y)^{-1}$ and $x^n z = (x/y)^n \, y^n z$: the total space of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-n)$, the minimal resolution of Example 3.2.41. Its exceptional curve $E$ is the image of $z = 0$, and $[E] = -n$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(X_+) = \mathbb{Z}$, while the fibres $D_{u_1}$, $D_{u_2}$ have class $1$. On the smooth surface $X_+$, $E^2 = -n$ by Proposition 3.3.14, since $u_1 + u_2 = n u_3$.

**Example 3.8.11 (the Atiyah flop).** Let $q = (1, 1, -1, -1)$ with variables $x_1, x_2, y_1, y_2$. The invariants are generated by the four products $x_i y_j$, with the relation $(x_1 y_1)(x_2 y_2) = (x_1 y_2)(x_2 y_1)$, so $X_0$ is the cone $ad = bc$ over a quadric surface, the cone over a square of Example 3.5.16 after the relabelling $(u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4) \mapsto (u_1, u_3, u_2, u_4)$, which turns $(1, -1, 1, -1)$ into $q$. The chart $A_{x_1}$ is $k[x_2/x_1, x_1 y_1, x_1 y_2]$, a space $\mathbb{A}^3$, and $X_+$ is covered by two such charts: it is the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}$ over the line $\mathbb{P}^1$ with coordinates $[x_1 : x_2]$. The curve contracted by $\pi_+$ is the image of $y_1 = y_2 = 0$, the line $\{(x_1, x_2) \neq 0\}/k^*$. In the same way $X_-$ is the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}$ over the line with coordinates $[y_1 : y_2]$. These are the two small resolutions of Example 3.5.23.

**Example 3.8.12 (the Francia flip).** Let $q = (1, 1, -1, -2)$ with variables $x_1, x_2, y, z$, as in Example 3.5.24. The charts of $X_+$ are $D_+(x_1)$ and $D_+(x_2)$, of multiplicity $1$ by Lemma 3.8.7, so $X_+$ is smooth. The charts of $X_-$ are $D_+(y)$, smooth, and $D_+(z)$, of multiplicity $2$. On the slice $z = 1$ the invariants are the polynomials in $x_1, x_2, y$ of weight divisible by $2$, which are the invariants of $(x_1, x_2, y) \mapsto (-x_1, -x_2, -y)$ so $X_-$ has one point of type $\frac12(1,1,1)$, at the image of $(0, 0, 0, 1)$. The curve contracted by $\pi_+$ is the image of $y = z = 0$, a line $\mathbb{P}(1, 1)$, and the curve contracted by $\pi_-$ is the image of $x_1 = x_2 = 0$, the weighted projective line $\mathbb{P}(1, 2) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, which passes through the singular point.

### Proof

*Proof of Lemma 3.8.7.* The relation holds since $\sum_i q_i e_i = q$. The $e_i$ generate $\mathbb{Z}^r$, so the $u_i$ generate $N$. Suppose $u_l = c v$ with $v \in N$ and an integer $c \ge 2$, and let $p$ be a prime dividing $c$. Then $e_l - c v' \in \mathbb{Z} q$ for a lift $v'$ of $v$, so $e_l + a q \in p \mathbb{Z}^r$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}$. The coordinates $i \neq l$ give $p \mid a q_i$ for all $i \neq l$, and the coordinate $l$ gives $p \mid 1 + a q_l$, so $p \nmid a$, and $p$ divides $\gcd(q_i : i \neq l) = 1$, which is impossible. So $u_l$ is primitive. If $u_i = u_l$ for $i \neq l$, then $e_i - e_l \in \mathbb{Z} q$, so $q$ is supported on $\{i, l\}$, which contradicts $r \ge 3$ and $J_0 = \emptyset$. The vectors span $N_\mathbb{R}$ and there are $n + 1$ of them, so with $J_\pm \neq \emptyset$ they are circuit data. Proposition 3.5.13 gives the multiplicities. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 3.8.8.* For a set $A \subseteq W$, the semigroup $S_{\operatorname{Cone}(A)} = \operatorname{Cone}(A)^\vee \cap M$ is the set of $m \in \mathbb{Z}^r$ with $\langle q, m \rangle = 0$ and $m_i = \langle m, u_i \rangle \ge 0$ for $u_i \in A$.

Part 1. For $A = W$ this is the set of exponents of the invariant monomials, so $R_0 = k[S_\sigma]$ and $X_0 = U_\sigma$ (Definition 3.2.10). The cone $\sigma$ is strongly convex by part 2 of Lemma 3.5.11.

Part 2. For $j \in J_+$ and $A = W \setminus \{u_j\}$, the semigroup is the set of exponents of the monomials of $A_j$ in Proposition 3.8.2, so $D_+(x_j) = \operatorname{Spec} A_j = U_{\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})}$. These are the maximal cones of $T_+$. For $j \neq j'$ in $J_+$, $D_+(x_j) \cap D_+(x_{j'}) = D_+(x_j x_{j'})$, whose ring $(R_{x_j x_{j'}})_0$ is spanned by the invariant Laurent monomials that are nonnegative outside $j, j'$, which is $k[S_\tau]$ for $\tau = \operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j, u_{j'}\})$. The cone $\tau$ is the intersection of the two maximal cones: $T_+$ is a simplicial fan by part 3 of Lemma 3.5.11, so the intersection is a common face, spanned by a subset of $W \setminus \{u_j, u_{j'}\}$ that contains it. The inclusions $A_j \subseteq (R_{x_j x_{j'}})_0$ are the inclusions $k[S_{\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})}] \subseteq k[S_\tau]$ of Proposition 3.2.12. So $X_+$ is glued from the affine toric varieties of the maximal cones of $T_+$ along the varieties of their intersections, which is the construction of $X_{T_+}$ in Definition 3.2.18. Every cone of $T_+$ is a face of a maximal one, so the variety glued from the maximal cones is $X_{T_+}$. The morphism $\pi_+$ is given on each chart by $R_0 = k[S_\sigma] \subseteq k[S_{\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})}]$, which is the toric morphism given by the identity of $N$. For $X_-$ use $J_-$.

Part 3. If $J_- = \emptyset$, every monomial of weight $0$ is constant, so $R_0 = k$, and $X_- = \emptyset$ by part 3 of Proposition 3.8.2. The argument of part 2 applies to every $j$, now with $J_+ = \{1, \dots, r\}$, and gives the fan whose maximal cones are the $\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})$.

Part 4. Let $j \in J_+$ with $j \neq i$, so that $u_i$ is a ray of $\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})$. On $U = D_+(x_j)$, the divisor $D_{u_i}$ is the closure of the orbit of the ray $u_i$ (Theorem 3.2.29), which is the zero set of the monomials $\chi^m$, $m \in S_{\operatorname{Cone}(W \setminus \{u_j\})}$, with $\langle m, u_i \rangle = m_i > 0$. Such a monomial vanishes on $\{x_i = 0, x_j \neq 0\}$. Conversely, if a point of the slice $x_j = 1$ maps into $D_{u_i}$, then the monomial $x_i^{|q_j|} x_j^{-q_i \operatorname{sgn} q_j}$ of $A_j$ vanishes there, so $x_i = 0$. Every point of $U$ comes from the slice by Theorem 3.8.3, so $\phi_+(V(x_i) \cap \{x_j \neq 0\}) = D_{u_i} \cap U$. If $J_+ = \{i\}$, then $u_i$ is not a ray of $T_+$, and there is nothing to prove.

Part 5. The fan of $X_+$ has the rays $u_i$ for all $i$ if and only if every $u_i$ lies in some $W \setminus \{u_j\}$ with $j \in J_+$, which is the condition. Theorem 3.3.4 gives the exact sequence $M \to \mathbb{Z}^r \to \operatorname{Cl}(X_+) \to 0$, with $e_i \mapsto [D_{u_i}]$ and first map $m \mapsto \sum_i \langle m, u_i \rangle e_i = m$, the inclusion of $M$. The map $\mathbb{Z}^r \to \mathbb{Z}$, $m \mapsto \langle q, m \rangle$, is surjective as $q$ is primitive, with kernel $M$. So $\operatorname{Cl}(X_+) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r/M \cong \mathbb{Z}$, with $[D_{u_i}] \mapsto q_i$. $\square$

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $q = (1, 1, 1, -1)$. Show that $X_0 = X_- = \mathbb{A}^3$ and that $X_+$ is the blow-up of $\mathbb{A}^3$ at the origin, with exceptional divisor $\mathbb{P}^2$.
2. (A) Let $q = (1, 2, -1)$. Show that $X_0 = X_- = \mathbb{A}^2$, that $X_+$ has one singular point, of type $\frac12(1,1)$, and that $\pi_+$ contracts the curve $\mathbb{P}(1, 2)$. This is the weighted blow-up of the plane with weights $(1, 2)$.
3. (A) For $q = (1, 1, -1, -1)$, write the gluing of the two charts of $X_+$ and check that $X_+$ is the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$.
4. (B) Show that the image of $V(x_k : k \in J_-) \cap \mathbb{A}^r_+$ in $X_+$ is the weighted projective space $\mathbb{P}(q_j : j \in J_+)$, and that it is the orbit closure $V_+(\sigma_-)$ of Theorem 3.5.14.
5. (B) Let $q = (1, 1, 2)$, so $X_+ = \mathbb{P}(1,1,2)$. Using part 5, show that $\operatorname{Cl}(X_+) = \mathbb{Z}$ with the lines $D_{u_1}, D_{u_2}$ of class $1$ and the conic $D_{u_3}$ of class $2$, and compare with Example 3.3.17.
6. (C) The weights $q = (1, 2, 2)$ are not well formed. Show that $X_+ = \mathbb{P}(1, 2, 2) \cong \mathbb{P}^2$, but that $u_1$ is not primitive in $\mathbb{Z}^3/\mathbb{Z}q$. Explain how to pass to well formed weights in general without changing $X_+$.

### Notes and history

The description of a toric variety as a quotient of an open subset of affine space by a group of diagonal matrices is Cox's (1995), with earlier forms by Audin, Delzant and Musson; for a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial projective toric variety with $\operatorname{Cl} = \mathbb{Z}$ the group is $\mathbb{G}_m$, as here. Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapter 5, give the construction for every toric variety whose rays span $N_\mathbb{R}$, and Chapter 14 identifies the quotients for different characters with the GIT quotients. The proofs here identify the charts directly with the cones of Chapter 5 and do not use the general quotient construction.
