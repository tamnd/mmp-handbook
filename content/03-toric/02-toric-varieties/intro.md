# Toric varieties from fans

This chapter turns the convex geometry of Chapter 1 into algebraic geometry. A cone gives an affine variety, a fan gives a variety glued from these affine pieces, and a map of lattices that respects two fans gives a morphism. The torus acts on all of these varieties, and its orbits correspond to the cones of the fan. At the end of the chapter, every statement of Chapter 1 has a geometric meaning: a face is an open subset, the separation lemma is separatedness, a complete fan gives a complete variety, a smooth cone gives a smooth chart, and a refinement gives a proper birational morphism.

Section 1 sets up tori, characters and one-parameter subgroups, and the affine toric varieties $Y_{\mathcal{A}}$ of a finite set of characters. Section 2 builds the affine toric variety $U_\sigma$ of a cone, proves that it is normal, and proves that it is smooth if and only if the cone is smooth. Section 3 glues the $U_\sigma$ into $X_\Sigma$. Section 4 proves the orbit-cone correspondence. Section 5 treats toric morphisms, with toric resolution and the first toric contractions as examples.

Throughout, $k$ is an algebraically closed field of any characteristic, a variety is an integral separated scheme of finite type over $k$, and $N \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$ and $M = \operatorname{Hom}(N, \mathbb{Z})$ are as in Chapter 1. The running examples are $\mathbb{A}^n$, $\mathbb{P}^1$, $\mathbb{P}^2$, $\mathbb{F}_n$, the quotient singularity $\frac1n(1,1)$ with the quadric cone as $n = 2$, and the cone $xz = yw$ over a square. The spine is Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapters 1 and 3. The Lean library has no schemes of toric varieties yet: route R3 builds the comparison with $X_\Sigma$ as a later step, so the Lean parts of this chapter are short.
