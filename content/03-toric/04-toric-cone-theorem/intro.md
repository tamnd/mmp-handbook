# The toric cone theorem

The cone theorem is the first theorem of the minimal model program. It says which curves a divisor must be tested on to decide whether the divisor is nef. On a toric variety the answer is short: it is enough to test on the finitely many torus-invariant curves $V(\tau)$, one for each wall $\tau$ of the fan. Each such intersection number is read off a linear relation among the minimal generators of the rays, the wall relation. This chapter proves these facts for complete simplicial fans, first on the fan and then on the variety.

Section 1 shows that the curve $V(\tau)$ of a wall is a projective line, computes $D \cdot V(\tau)$ for every $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D$, and proves that the numbers $D_\rho \cdot V(\tau)$ are the coefficients of the wall relation. Section 2 attaches a support function $\varphi_D$ to a divisor and proves the combinatorial cone theorem: $\varphi_D$ is convex if and only if $D \cdot V(\tau) \ge 0$ for every wall. The proof is the generic segment argument, and it is also the proof in the Lean library. Section 3 passes to the variety: a Cartier divisor is nef if and only if its support function is convex, the cone of curves $\overline{NE}(X_\Sigma)$ is spanned by the classes $[V(\tau)]$, and $N_1(X_\Sigma)$ is the space of linear relations among the $u_\rho$.

The chapter uses Theorem 3.3.20 of Chapter 3: $X_\Sigma$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial if and only if $\Sigma$ is simplicial. So on a simplicial fan every torus-invariant divisor has an intersection number with $V(\tau)$, and the toric MMP of Chapters 5 and 6 works with simplicial fans. The examples are $\mathbb{P}^n$, the Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb{F}_n$, the weighted projective spaces $\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)$ and $\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,2)$, and the small resolution of the cone over a square. The spine is Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapter 6, with the account of Fujino and Sato (2004, Section 4) and Reid's paper of 1983. The Lean library proves the wall relation for smooth cones and the combinatorial cone theorem, `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.coneTheorem`. It does not yet have the variety $X_\Sigma$, so the geometric form of Section 3 is not in Lean.
