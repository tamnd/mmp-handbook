## Problems

Each problem is a computation by hand with wall relations, support functions and cones of curves.

1. (A) Let $\Sigma$ be the fan in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with the six rays $(1, 0)$, $(1, 1)$, $(0, 1)$, $(-1, 0)$, $(-1, -1)$, $(0, -1)$ in this cyclic order, the fan of the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at its three fixed points. Show that every wall relation is $u_{i-1} + u_{i+1} = u_i$, so that every $D_i$ is a $(-1)$-curve. Show that $\rho = 4$, that $NE(X_\Sigma)$ is spanned by the six classes $[D_i]$, and that $-K$ is ample with $K^2 = 6$.
2. (B) Let $\Sigma$ be the fan of $\mathbb{P}(1,2,3)$ with rays $u_1 = e_1$, $u_2 = e_2$, $u_0 = -2 e_1 - 3 e_2$, so that $u_0 + 2 u_1 + 3 u_2 = 0$. Show that the cones $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2)$, $\operatorname{Cone}(u_2, u_0)$, $\operatorname{Cone}(u_0, u_1)$ have multiplicities $1$, $2$, $3$. Write the wall relation at each ray with the normalization of Theorem 3.4.5, and deduce $D_0^2 = \frac16$, $D_1^2 = \frac23$, $D_2^2 = \frac32$. With $H = D_0$, show that $\operatorname{Cl} = \mathbb{Z} H$, $D_1 \sim 2H$, $D_2 \sim 3H$, $H^2 = \frac16$, and $K^2 = 6$.
3. (B) Let $X$ be the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^3$ at the fixed point $\gamma_{\operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_2, e_3)}$, the star subdivision at $v = e_1 + e_2 + e_3$. Show that the walls $\operatorname{Cone}(e_i, e_j)$ with $1 \le i < j \le 3$ have the relation $e_0 + v = 0$, and the walls $\operatorname{Cone}(v, e_i)$ have the relation $e_1 + e_2 + e_3 - v = 0$. Deduce that $NE(X)$ is spanned by the strict transform of a line through the point and a line in $E \cong \mathbb{P}^2$, that $E \cdot \ell = -1$ for a line $\ell \subset E$, and that $-K = 4H - 2E$ is ample.
4. (B) Let $X$ be the weighted blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^3$ at $\gamma_{\operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_2, e_3)}$ with weights $(1, 2, 3)$, the star subdivision at $v = e_1 + 2 e_2 + 3 e_3$. Show that the three new maximal cones have multiplicities $1$, $2$, $3$ and that the discrepancy of $E$ over $\mathbb{P}^3$ is $5$. With the basis $r_1 = (1, 1, 1, 1, 0)$ and $r_2 = (0, -1, -2, -3, 1)$ of $R(\Sigma)$ in the coordinates $(e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3, v)$, show that the wall curves have the classes $[C_{23}] = r_1 + r_2$, $[C_{13}] = r_1 + \frac12 r_2$, $[C_{12}] = r_1 + \frac13 r_2$, $[V(\operatorname{Cone}(v, e_3))] = -\frac12 r_2$, $[V(\operatorname{Cone}(v, e_2))] = -\frac13 r_2$, $[V(\operatorname{Cone}(v, e_1))] = -\frac16 r_2$ and $[V(\operatorname{Cone}(e_0, e_i))] = r_1$, where $C_{ij} = V(\operatorname{Cone}(e_i, e_j))$. Deduce that $NE(X)$ is spanned by $[C_{23}]$ and $-r_2$, the direction of the curves in $E$, that $K \cdot C_{23} = 1$, and that $-K$ is not nef. Find the two divisors $D_\rho$ that are negative on $C_{23}$. Chapter 5 shows that this makes the contraction of $C_{23}$ small.
5. (C) Let $\Sigma$ be complete simplicial and $X_\Sigma$ projective, and let $R$ be an extremal ray of $NE(X_\Sigma)$. Show that there is a nef torus-invariant $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$ with $D^\perp \cap NE(X_\Sigma) = R$. Show that the walls $\tau$ with $[V(\tau)] \in R$ are exactly the walls with $m_\sigma = m_{\sigma'}$ for the data of $D$ on the two sides. Deduce that the maximal cones on which $\varphi_D$ is given by one linear form $m$ form a convex set, and compute these sets for the two extremal rays of $\mathbb{F}_n$.
