# Cones and fans

A toric variety is built from a finite set of convex cones in a real vector space with a lattice. All of its geometry can be read from the cones: its affine charts, its orbits, its divisors, its singularities, its curves and, in the later chapters of this volume, its minimal model program. This chapter gives the convex geometry. It has no algebraic geometry at all, and a reader from combinatorics can start here.

Section 1 defines rational polyhedral cones, their duals and their faces, and proves the face correspondence between a cone and its dual. Section 2 proves Gordan's lemma: the lattice points of a rational polyhedral cone form a finitely generated semigroup. Its coordinate ring is the ring of functions of an affine toric variety in Chapter 2. Section 3 defines fans and proves the separation lemma, which makes the gluing of Chapter 2 separated. Section 4 refines fans into simplicial and smooth fans. In Chapter 2 this becomes toric resolution of singularities, and in Chapter 3 it becomes the statement that a simplicial fan gives a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial variety.

The running examples start here: the fans of $\mathbb{A}^n$, $\mathbb{P}^1$, $\mathbb{P}^2$, the Hirzebruch surfaces $\mathbb{F}_n$, the cone of the quotient singularity $\frac1n(1,1)$ (with the quadric cone as $n = 2$) and the cone over a square, which is the base of the Atiyah flop later in this volume. The spine is Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapters 1 and 3, with Fulton's book as a shorter parallel text. The Lean files `MinimalModel/Toric/Fan.lean`, `Divisor.lean` and `Examples.lean` of tamnd/mmp-lean contain the definitions of this chapter, and each section names them. The chapter repeats the material of P8 Chapter 6 in the form that the toric MMP needs, so the toric first path of Volume 0 can start here.
