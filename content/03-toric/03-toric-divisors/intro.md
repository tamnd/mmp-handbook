# Toric divisors and the canonical divisor

The minimal model program works with divisors: the canonical divisor $K_X$, its intersection numbers with curves, and the discrepancies of exceptional divisors. On a toric variety all of these are computed from the fan. This chapter does the computations that the toric MMP of the later chapters needs.

Section 1 attaches a prime divisor $D_\rho$ to each ray, computes the divisor of a character, and proves that the classes of the $D_\rho$ generate the class group, with the relations given by $M$. Section 2 decides which torus-invariant divisors are Cartier, by the data $\{m_\sigma\}$ of one character per cone, and computes the Picard group and the self-intersections of the invariant curves on a smooth toric surface. Section 3 proves that $X_\Sigma$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial if and only if $\Sigma$ is simplicial. Section 4 proves $K_{X_\Sigma} = -\sum_\rho D_\rho$ and gives the Gorenstein and $\mathbb{Q}$-Gorenstein criteria. Section 5 computes the discrepancy of the exceptional divisor of a star subdivision by the formula $a = \langle m_\sigma, v \rangle - 1$, the formula that the toric MMP and the toric singularity theory of later volumes rest on.

The examples are the same as before: $\mathbb{P}^n$, $\mathbb{F}_n$, $\frac1n(1,1)$, the quadric cone, $\mathbb{P}(1,1,n)$ and the cone over a square. Two failures run through the chapter. A plane through the vertex of $xz = yw$ is a Weil divisor that is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, and the cone with rays $e_1$, $e_2$, $e_3$, $(1, 1, -2)$ has a canonical divisor that is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier. The spine is Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapters 4 and 8, with Fulton's book, Chapter 3. The Lean file `MinimalModel/Toric/Divisor.lean` defines torus-invariant divisors on a fan and the canonical divisor $-\sum_\rho D_\rho$; the comparison with the divisors of the variety $X_\Sigma$ is a later step of route R3.
