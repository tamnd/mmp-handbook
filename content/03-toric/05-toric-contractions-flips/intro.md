# Toric contractions and flips

An extremal ray of the cone of curves is contracted by a morphism, and the minimal model program needs to know what that morphism does. On a projective toric variety with a simplicial fan the answer is combinatorial. The contraction is a toric morphism, given by the coarser fan of the domains of a supporting divisor, and its type is decided by the signs of one linear relation among the rays: the relation $b^R$ that every wall curve of the ray carries. If no coefficient is negative, the contraction is a fibration onto a variety of smaller dimension. If exactly one is negative, it contracts a divisor onto a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial variety. If two or more are negative, it is small, its target is not $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, and the program must flip.

Section 1 constructs the contraction of an extremal ray from a supporting divisor and proves that it contracts exactly the curves of the ray. Section 2 studies circuits, sets of $n + 1$ vectors with one relation, and their two triangulations; it proves that near the exceptional locus the fan is the triangulation $T_+$ of a circuit cone, and reads off the type of the contraction. Section 3 replaces $T_+$ by $T_-$ in every circuit cone, proves that the result is the fan of a projective $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial variety, and proves that it is the flip, unique by the Proj construction.

The examples are the Hirzebruch surfaces, the blow-up of a point of $\mathbb{P}^3$, the quotient singularities $\frac12(1,1)$ and $\frac13(1,1)$ as circuit cones, the Atiyah flop as the two triangulations of a square, the Francia flip as the circuit with relation $(1, 1, -1, -2)$, and the $(1, 2, 3)$ weighted blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^3$, which contains a Francia configuration. The spine is Fujino and Sato (2004, Section 4) and Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapter 15, after Reid's paper of 1983. The proofs were written for the handbook and checked against these accounts where the handbook has them. The Lean library has the vocabulary of contractions and steps in `MinimalModel/Toric/Program.lean`, but none of the theorems of this chapter.
