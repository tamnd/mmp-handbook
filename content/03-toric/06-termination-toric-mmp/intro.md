# Termination and the end of the toric MMP

Chapter 5 gave the steps of the toric minimal model program: the contraction of an extremal ray, and the flip of a small one. This chapter shows that the program cannot go on forever, assembles it, and runs it on examples. For toric varieties the program works for every torus-invariant $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$, not only for $K_X$, and every run ends.

Section 1 proves the toric negativity lemma. A $D$-flip raises the support function of $D$: on a circuit cone the two triangulations pick the two ends of the segment of expressions of a point, and the support function grows along that segment at the rate $-D \cdot R > 0$. A flip keeps the rays, and there are finitely many simplicial fans on a fixed set of rays, so no sequence of flips can be infinite (Fujino-Sato, Theorem 4.9). For $D = K_X$ the same comparison says that a flip raises discrepancies, as Example 3.6.5 shows for the Francia flip. Section 2 defines the toric $D$-MMP and proves that it ends with a minimal model or a Mori fibre space, and that the $K$-MMP always ends with a Mori fibre space. It also computes the minimal log discrepancy at a torus-fixed point as the minimum of the linear form $m_\Delta$ on the interior lattice points of the cone, with $\frac12(1,1)$, $\frac13(1,1)$, $\frac12(1,1,1)$ and the cone over a square as examples. Section 3 runs the program by hand on the weighted blow-ups of $\mathbb{P}^3$ with weights $(1, 1, 2)$ and $(1, 2, 3)$: it finds two $K$-MMPs with different end products, computes the cone of curves of the flip of the $(1, 2, 3)$ blow-up, and runs a $D$-MMP that consists of a flip followed by a divisorial contraction to $\mathbb{P}(1,1,2,3)$.

The spine is Fujino and Sato (2004, Sections 3 and 4) and Cox-Little-Schenck, Chapter 15, with Reid's paper of 1983 for the original program. The node MMAWP4SA44 of the map is the theorem of Section 2.
