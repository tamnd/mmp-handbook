# Rational singularities

A rational singularity is one whose resolution has no higher direct images of the structure sheaf. Volume P4 defined the notion and proved Kempf's criterion, and Volume P5 developed Artin's theory of rational surface singularities through the fundamental cycle. This chapter connects those results to the quotient and Du Val singularities of Chapters 9 and 10.

Section 11.1 states Kovács's splitting criterion and deduces from the Reynolds operator that quotients of rational singularities by finite groups are rational. It follows that rings of invariants are Cohen-Macaulay, that small subgroups of $SL_n$ give Gorenstein quotients with nonnegative discrepancies, and that the quotients of the plane by finite subgroups of $SL_2$ are Du Val. The cone over a smooth quadric surface shows that rational singularities need not be quotients.

Section 11.2 restates Artin's criterion for a surface singularity, proves that the exceptional curves of a rational surface singularity are smooth rational curves forming a tree, and computes the multiplicity and the embedding dimension from the fundamental cycle. For cyclic quotient singularities it gives both numbers in terms of the Hirzebruch-Jung continued fraction, and it exhibits a tree of rational curves that is not the resolution of a rational singularity.

The chapter follows Artin (Art66), Lipman (Lip69), Kovács (Kov00) and Kollár and Mori, Sections 4.1 and 5.1 (KM98).
