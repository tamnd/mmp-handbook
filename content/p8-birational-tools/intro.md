# Tools of birational geometry

The minimal model program in dimension at least three uses four sets of tools that the surface theory of P5 does not need in full. Rational curves are produced by Mori's bend-and-break, and they are the curves that span the extremal rays of the cone theorem. Resolution of singularities turns a singular variety or a pair into one with a smooth ambient space and a divisor with simple normal crossings, where discrepancies can be computed. Toric varieties give a class of examples in which every object of the program, from divisors and cones of curves to flips, is a finite computation with cones in a lattice. Finally, the singularities of the program, terminal, canonical, klt and log canonical, are defined by discrepancies, and their local theory controls what the steps of the program can produce. This volume collects these tools at the level the later volumes use them.

The volume has thirteen chapters in four parts.

- **Rational curves, Chapters 1 to 3.** Chapter 1 builds the scheme of morphisms from a curve to a variety and computes its tangent space. Chapter 2 proves bend-and-break and Mori's theorem that a variety with a curve on which the canonical class is negative contains a rational curve through each point of such a curve. Chapter 3 treats uniruled and rationally connected varieties.
- **Resolution, Chapters 4 and 5.** Chapter 4 proves that normalization resolves curves, computes the minimal log resolution of a curve on a smooth surface, and proves resolution of normal surfaces in characteristic $0$ by Jung's method, with the theorems of Zariski, Abhyankar and Lipman cited. Chapter 5 states Hironaka's resolution, embedded resolution and principalization, the functorial versions and weak factorization, derives log resolutions of pairs, and records what is known in positive characteristic and how the program uses log resolutions.
- **Toric varieties, Chapters 6 to 8.** Chapter 6 builds a toric variety from a fan and proves the orbit-cone correspondence and the criteria for smoothness and completeness. Chapter 7 computes the class group, the Picard group, the canonical divisor and the global sections of toric divisors. Chapter 8 resolves toric singularities, computes the intersection numbers of the torus-invariant curves and proves the toric cone theorem in the cases the volumes use.
- **Singularities, Chapters 9 to 13.** Chapter 9 treats quotients by finite groups, the Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem and cyclic quotient singularities. Chapter 10 classifies the Du Val singularities and proves that Du Val, crepant, rational double point and rational Gorenstein are the same condition. Chapter 11 proves that quotient singularities are rational and develops Artin's theory of rational surface singularities. Chapter 12 computes the class group, the canonical class and the discrepancy of the cone over a polarized variety. Chapter 13 defines discrepancies over a normal variety and the classes terminal, canonical, klt and log canonical, and computes them for surfaces.

The spine is Debarre's *Higher-dimensional algebraic geometry* (Deb01) and Kollár and Mori (KM98) for Parts A and D, Kollár's *Lectures on resolution of singularities* (Kol07) for Part B, Cox, Little and Schenck's *Toric varieties* (CLS11) and Fulton's *Introduction to toric varieties* (Ful93) for Part C, and Reid's *Young person's guide to canonical singularities* (Rei87) for Part D. Each section lists in `reads_with` the places of the spine texts that read in parallel.

The volume needs P3 for schemes, divisors and the canonical divisor, P4 for cohomology, P5 for the intersection theory of surfaces and the cone of curves, and P7 for the deformation theory used in Part A. Part C needs only P1 and P3, together with the intersection numbers of curves of P5 Ch. 5. Unless the text says otherwise, $k$ is an algebraically closed field. At present Parts C and D are drafted, and the other chapters are listed here so that the plan of the volume is visible.
