# Du Val singularities

The Du Val singularities are the surface singularities whose minimal resolution consists of $(-2)$-curves. They are the canonical singularities of surfaces, the singularities of the canonical models of surfaces of general type, and the quotients of the plane by finite subgroups of $SL_2$. This chapter classifies their resolution graphs, writes down the equations of the simplest ones, and proves that four different conditions single out the same class.

Section 10.1 proves that the dual graph of a Du Val singularity is one of the Dynkin diagrams $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, by excluding the extended diagrams, each of which carries an explicit null vector. It computes the resolutions of $A_n$, by the toric method of Chapter 8, and of $D_4$, by one blow-up that produces three ordinary double points on a line, and it states the full ADE table of equations and groups, citing the cases not proved here.

Section 10.2 proves that a singular point of a normal surface is Du Val if and only if its minimal resolution is crepant, if and only if it is a rational double point, if and only if it is rational and Gorenstein. The proof uses Artin's criteria from Chapter 8 of Volume P5 and the genus formula. The examples compute the fundamental cycle of $E_8$, and show by $\frac{1}{3}(1, 1)$ and the cone over a plane cubic that rationality and the Gorenstein condition are each needed.

The chapter follows Kollár and Mori, Section 4.2 (KM98), Reid's Young Person's Guide (Rei87), Artin (Art66) and Durfee's survey (Dur79).
