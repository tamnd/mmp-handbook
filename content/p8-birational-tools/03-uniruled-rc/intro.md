# Uniruled and rationally connected varieties

Bend-and-break produces rational curves, and this chapter sorts varieties by how many rational curves they carry. A variety is uniruled when rational curves cover it, and rationally connected when two general points lie on one rational curve. Both notions are birational invariants, and both appear at the end of the minimal model program: a Mori fibre space is covered by rational curves, and its general fibre has anti-ample canonical class, the condition that makes a smooth variety rationally connected in characteristic $0$.

Section 3.1 defines uniruled varieties through families of rational curves and proves, with the countability of the components of the scheme of morphisms, that over an uncountable field a projective variety is uniruled exactly when there is a rational curve through every point. A free rational curve makes a variety uniruled, and in characteristic $0$ a uniruled smooth variety has free curves through its general points, so its canonical class is not pseudo-effective and all its plurigenera vanish. The converses, the numerical criterion of Miyaoka and Mori and the theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Păun and Peternell, are stated with references. Hypersurfaces of low degree are uniruled, and varieties with nef canonical class in characteristic $0$ are not.

Section 3.2 defines rationally connected and rationally chain connected varieties, proves that a very free rational curve makes a smooth variety rationally connected, and deduces that such a variety has no nonzero global tensor forms. The theorem of Kollár, Miyaoka and Mori and of Campana that the two notions agree for smooth varieties in characteristic $0$ is stated. On a smooth cubic surface, seen as the plane blown up at six points, the preimage of a general line of the plane is a very free rational curve.

Section 3.3 states that smooth Fano varieties in characteristic $0$ are rationally connected, and the theorem of Graber, Harris and Starr that a family of rationally connected varieties over a curve has a section, with its corollary that rational connectedness is preserved under fibrations. Conic bundles illustrate both: over $\mathbb{P}^1$ they are rational, and over a curve of positive genus they are uniruled but not rationally connected.

The chapter follows Debarre, Chapter 4 (Deb01), and Kollár, Chapters IV and V (Kol96).
