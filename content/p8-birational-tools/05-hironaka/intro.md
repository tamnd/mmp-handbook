# Hironaka's theorem, as statements

Hironaka's theorem of 1964 says that in characteristic $0$ every variety has a resolution by a sequence of blow-ups with smooth centres, and that the same method makes a divisor or an ideal into one with simple normal crossings. The minimal model program uses it constantly: discrepancies, the classes of singularities and the log canonical threshold are computed on a log resolution, and the vanishing theorems are proved on one. Its proof is long and is not reproduced in this handbook. This chapter states the theorem in the forms the program cites, proves the consequences that follow from the statements by short arguments, and records what is known in positive characteristic.

Section 5.1 defines simple normal crossings in every dimension, strong, embedded and log resolutions, and principalization. It states Hironaka's resolution, embedded resolution and principalization, the functorial versions of Bierstone and Milman, Villamayor, Włodarczyk and Kollár, and the weak factorization theorem of Abramovich, Karu, Matsuki and Włodarczyk. It then proves that a functorial resolution is equivariant, that resolution and principalization together give log resolutions of pairs, and that the exceptional divisor of a sequence of smooth blow-ups carries a relatively antiample effective divisor.

Section 5.2 is on positive characteristic and on the use of log resolutions in the program. It states the theorems of Abhyankar for surfaces and for threefolds in characteristic greater than $5$, of Cossart and Piltant for threefolds in every positive characteristic, and de Jong's alterations. It proves that pairs on normal surfaces have log resolutions in every characteristic, using Chapter 4, and computes log discrepancies on a sequence of point blow-ups. Its Lean subsection explains why the formal definitions of the singularities of the program, in the repository, quantify over all divisorial valuations and so do not depend on a resolution.

The chapter follows Kollár's *Lectures on resolution of singularities* (Kol07), Chapter 3, Hironaka's paper (Hir64) and Kollár and Mori (KM98), Section 2.3. Simple normal crossings on surfaces are Definition P5.6.12 of Volume P5, and log resolutions of pairs are Definition 4.2.9 of Volume 4.
