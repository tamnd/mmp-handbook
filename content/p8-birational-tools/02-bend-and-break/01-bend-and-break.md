## Rigidity and the bend-and-break lemmas

### Statement

In this section $k$ is algebraically closed of any characteristic, $X$ is a projective variety over $k$, and a curve is an integral scheme of dimension $1$, separated and of finite type over $k$. For a morphism $f \colon C \to X$ from a projective curve, $f_* C$ is the $1$-cycle $\deg(C / f(C)) [f(C)]$, which is $0$ when $f$ is constant, and $\equiv$ is numerical equivalence of $1$-cycles, as in Definition P7.2.4: $Z \equiv Z'$ when $D \cdot Z = D \cdot Z'$ for every Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$. A *rational curve* on $X$ is a curve whose normalization is $\mathbb{P}^1$; by Theorem P5.2.12 the image of a nonconstant morphism $\mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ is a rational curve.

**Lemma P8.2.1 (rigidity).** Let $Y$ be a nonempty, connected, reduced scheme proper over $k$, $V$ a scheme of finite type over $k$, $Z$ a separated scheme of finite type over $k$ and $F \colon Y \times V \to Z$ a morphism. Let $v_0 \in V(k)$ with $F(Y \times \{v_0\})$ a single point, and fix $y_0 \in Y(k)$. Let $g \colon V \to Z$ be the morphism $v \mapsto F(y_0, v)$.

1. There is an open neighbourhood $V_0$ of $v_0$ with $F = g \circ p_2$ on $Y \times V_0$.
2. If $Y \times V$ is irreducible and reduced, then $F = g \circ p_2$ on $Y \times V$. In particular $F(Y \times \{v\})$ is a point for every $v \in V(k)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P8.2.2 (curves through a point).** Let $W$ be a quasi-projective variety of dimension $d \geq 1$, $w \in W$ a closed point and $U \subseteq W$ a dense open subset. Then there is a closed curve $T \subseteq W$ with $w \in T$ and $T \cap U \neq \emptyset$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P8.2.3 (indeterminacy and the exceptional curves).** Let $S$ be a smooth projective surface, $\varphi \colon S \dashrightarrow X$ a rational map, and $\pi \colon S' \to S$ a composite of point blow-ups such that $F' = \varphi \circ \pi$ is a morphism, as in Theorem P5.7.9. If $p \in S$ is a closed point at which $\varphi$ is not defined, then $\pi^{-1}(p)$ is connected and $F'(\pi^{-1}(p))$ is a connected curve.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P8.2.4 (limits of curves).** Let $C$ and $\bar T$ be smooth projective curves, $T^\circ \subseteq \bar T$ a nonempty open subset and $F \colon C \times T^\circ \to X$ a morphism, with $f_t = F|_{C \times \{t\}}$. Let $S = C \times \bar T$, let $\pi \colon S' \to S$ be a composite of point blow-ups such that $F$ extends to a morphism $F' \colon S' \to X$, and let $q = p_2 \circ \pi \colon S' \to \bar T$. For a closed point $t \in \bar T$ let $S'_t = q^*(t)$ be the fibre, as an effective divisor.

1. $S'_t = \widetilde C_t + \sum_j a_j E_j$, where $\widetilde C_t$ is the strict transform of $C \times \{t\}$, isomorphic to $C$ by $p_1 \circ \pi$, the $E_j$ are the integral curves of $S'$ in $\pi^{-1}(C \times \{t\})$ contracted by $\pi$, each isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, and $a_j \geq 1$.
2. The support of $S'_t$ is connected.
3. For every $t_0 \in T^\circ$ and every closed point $t \in \bar T$, $f_{t_0 *} C \equiv F'_*(S'_t) = f'_{t *} C + \sum_j a_j F'_* E_j$, where $f'_t$ is $F'|_{\widetilde C_t}$ composed with the isomorphism $C \cong \widetilde C_t$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.2.5 (bend-and-break, first form).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve, $c \in C$ a closed point and $f \colon C \to X$ a morphism with
$$\dim_{[f]} \operatorname{Hom}(C, X; f|_{\{c\}}) \geq 1.$$
Then there are a morphism $f' \colon C \to X$ and a nonzero effective $1$-cycle $Z = \sum_i a_i \Gamma_i$, with $a_i > 0$ and every $\Gamma_i$ a rational curve, such that
$$f_* C \equiv f'_* C + Z,$$
the point $f(c)$ lies in the support of $Z$, and $f'(C) \cup \operatorname{Supp} Z$ is connected.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.2.6 (bend-and-break, second form).** Let $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ be a nonconstant morphism with
$$\dim_{[f]} \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, X; f|_{\{0, \infty\}}) \geq 2.$$
Then $f_* \mathbb{P}^1 \equiv \sum_i a_i \Gamma_i$ with $a_i > 0$ integers, every $\Gamma_i$ a rational curve, and $\sum_i a_i \geq 2$. Equivalently, $f_* \mathbb{P}^1 \equiv Z_1 + Z_2$ with $Z_1$, $Z_2$ nonzero effective $1$-cycles whose components are rational curves.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Example P8.2.7 (conics breaking into two lines).** In $X = \mathbb{P}^2$ with coordinates $x, y, z$, the conics $C_t = V(x y - t z^2)$ for $t \neq 0$ are smooth and pass through $[1 : 0 : 0]$ and $[0 : 1 : 0]$. They are the images of $f_t \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$, $f_t([s : u]) = [s^2 : t u^2 : s u]$, with $f_t([1 : 0]) = [1 : 0 : 0]$ and $f_t([0 : 1]) = [0 : 1 : 0]$ for all $t$. As $t \to 0$ the conics tend to $V(x y)$, the union of the two lines $V(x)$ and $V(y)$, each through one of the two fixed points, while $f_0 = [s^2 : 0 : s u]$ is not a morphism: it is undefined at $[0 : 1]$. In $N_1(\mathbb{P}^2) = \mathbb{Z} \ell$ the conic has class $2 \ell$, equal to the sum of the classes of the two lines.

**Example P8.2.8 (lines do not break).** A line $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ has class $\ell$, the generator of $N_1(\mathbb{P}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ with $H \cdot \ell = 1$ for a hyperplane $H$, so it is not numerically equivalent to a sum of two nonzero effective $1$-cycles. By Theorem P8.2.6, $\dim_{[f]} \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^n; f|_{\{0, \infty\}}) \leq 1$ for a line $f$. Directly, a line is determined by two of its points, so the morphisms of degree $1$ with $f(0)$ and $f(\infty)$ fixed are the reparametrizations $f \circ \lambda$ with $\lambda([s : u]) = [\lambda s : u]$, a copy of $\mathbb{G}_m$ of dimension $1$, as in Exercise 2 of Section 1.1.

### Proof in words

Rigidity is the statement that a proper connected variety has no nonconstant functions, applied in families: near a fibre that is contracted, the morphism lands in an affine open set, and the global functions of $Y \times V_0$ are those of $V_0$.

For bend-and-break, take a curve $T$ of deformations of $f$ fixing $c$, and look at the surface $C \times \bar T$, with $\bar T$ a smooth completion. The family is a rational map from this surface to $X$ that sends the curve $\{c\} \times T$ to the point $f(c)$. If the rational map were defined along all of $\{c\} \times \bar T$, rigidity applied to the proper curve $\bar T$ would make all members of the family equal near $c$, hence equal. So there is a point $(c, t_\infty)$ of indeterminacy. Blowing up to resolve it, the exceptional curves over $(c, t_\infty)$ map onto a curve through $f(c)$, and the exceptional curves are rational. The fibre of the blown-up surface over $t_\infty$ is the strict transform of $C \times \{t_\infty\}$ plus exceptional curves, and every fibre maps to a cycle numerically equivalent to $f_* C$, which gives the first form.

The second form needs a little more, because a rational curve always moves with two points fixed: reparametrizing by $\lambda \mapsto [\lambda s : u]$ gives a one-dimensional family. Choosing the curve $T$ outside the closure of this orbit, the family either covers a curve, in which case all members are reparametrizations after all unless $f$ is a multiple cover, or covers a surface. In the surface case the two curves $\{0\} \times \bar T$ and $\{\infty\} \times \bar T$ are contracted, and the Hodge index theorem bounds the number of contracted curves on the blown-up surface. If every fibre had only one component that is not contracted, there would be too many contracted curves.

### Examples

*Example P8.2.7.* The map $f_t$ is a morphism for $t \neq 0$ because its three components $s^2$, $t u^2$, $s u$ have no common zero, and its image satisfies $x y = s^2 \cdot t u^2 = t (s u)^2 = t z^2$. It is injective, since $[s : u]$ is recovered as $[x : z]$ where $x \neq 0$ and as $[z : y/t]$ where $y \neq 0$, so it is birational onto $C_t$, and an isomorphism by part 3 of Theorem P3.10.29 since $C_t$ is smooth. In the surface $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{A}^1_t$ the family is undefined exactly at $([0 : 1], 0)$. On the chart $s = \sigma u$ near this point, $F = [\sigma^2 : t : \sigma]$, and blowing up $(\sigma, t) = (0, 0)$ with $t = \sigma \tau$ gives $F = [\sigma^2 : \sigma \tau : \sigma] = [\sigma : \tau : 1]$, a morphism near the exceptional curve $\{\sigma = 0\}$, which it maps isomorphically onto the line $V(x)$, as $\tau$ varies. The strict transform of the fibre $t = 0$ maps by $[s^2 : 0 : s u] = [s : 0 : u]$ onto $V(y)$, so the limit cycle is $V(x) + V(y)$, as Lemma P8.2.4 predicts, and $f(\infty) = [1 : 0 : 0] \in V(y)$ while $f(0) = [0 : 1 : 0] \in V(x)$, the line coming from the exceptional curve.

*Example P8.2.8.* The equality $\dim = 1$ for a line also follows from Corollary P8.1.13: a line is very free, $f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cong \mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(1)^{n-1}$, and the dimension is $-K \cdot L - n = (n + 1) - n = 1$.

### Proof

*Lemma P8.2.1.* (1) Let $z_0$ be the point $F(Y \times \{v_0\})$ and $U = \operatorname{Spec} B$ an affine open neighbourhood of $z_0$. The set $Y \times V \setminus F^{-1}(U)$ is closed, and since $Y$ is proper, its image $W$ under $p_2$ is closed in $V$. It does not contain $v_0$, because $Y \times \{v_0\} \subseteq F^{-1}(U)$. Let $V_0 = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open neighbourhood of $v_0$ in $V \setminus W$. Then $F$ maps $Y \times V_0$ into $U$, and a morphism to an affine scheme is a ring map $B \to \Gamma(Y \times V_0, \mathcal{O})$. By flat base change, Theorem P4.1.17 for $H^0$ and the flat map $k \to A$, $\Gamma(Y \times V_0, \mathcal{O}) = \Gamma(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y) \otimes_k A$, and $\Gamma(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y) = k$ by Theorem P3.4.21. So $p_2^\# \colon A \to \Gamma(Y \times V_0, \mathcal{O})$ is an isomorphism, the ring map of $F$ factors through it, and $F|_{Y \times V_0} = h \circ p_2$ for a morphism $h \colon V_0 \to U$. Composing with the section $v \mapsto (y_0, v)$ of $p_2$ gives $h = g|_{V_0}$.

(2) Both $F$ and $g \circ p_2$ are morphisms $Y \times V \to Z$, they agree on the open set $Y \times V_0$, which is nonempty and hence dense in the irreducible space $Y \times V$, $Y \times V$ is reduced and $Z$ is separated. Theorem P3.4.11 gives $F = g \circ p_2$.

*Lemma P8.2.2.* Induct on $d$. If $d = 1$, take $T = W$. Let $d \geq 2$, embed $W$ in some $\mathbb{P}^N$ and let $\bar W$ be its closure, a projective variety of dimension $d$ in which $U$ is still a dense open subset. Let $Z_1, \dots, Z_r$ be the irreducible components of $\bar W \setminus U$ of dimension $d - 1$, choose closed points $z_i \in Z_i$ with $z_i \neq w$, possible since $\dim Z_i \geq 1$, and a closed point $z_0 \in \bar W$ with $z_0 \neq w$. The hyperplanes through $w$ form a projective space $\mathbb{P}^{N-1}$, and those through $z_i$ form a hyperplane in it, since $z_i \neq w$. As $k$ is infinite, there is a hyperplane $H$ through $w$ containing none of $z_0, \dots, z_r$. On an affine open subset of $\bar W$ containing $w$, $H$ is cut out by a function that is not $0$, because $z_0 \notin H$ and $\bar W$ is irreducible (choose the affine set to contain $z_0$ as well, for instance the complement of a hyperplane missing $w$ and $z_0$), and not a unit, because it vanishes at $w$. By part 1 of Proposition P3.7.5, every irreducible component of $\bar W \cap H$ through $w$ has dimension $d - 1$; let $G$ be one. If $G \subseteq \bar W \setminus U$, then $G$ lies in an irreducible component of $\bar W \setminus U$, of dimension at most $d - 1$, so $G$ equals one of the $Z_i$; but $z_i \notin H$. Hence $G \cap U \neq \emptyset$. Then $G \cap W$ is a quasi-projective variety of dimension $d - 1$ containing $w$, in which $G \cap U$ is a nonempty open subset, dense since $G$ is irreducible. By induction there is a curve $T \subseteq G \cap W$ through $w$ meeting $U$.

*Lemma P8.2.3.* By Theorem P5.6.2, each blow-up is projective and birational, so $\pi$ is projective and birational, and $S$ is normal, being smooth. By Theorem P4.6.8, $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_{S'} = \mathcal{O}_S$ and the fibres of $\pi$ are connected; so $\pi^{-1}(p)$ is connected and $F'(\pi^{-1}(p))$ is connected. The morphism $\pi$ is an isomorphism outside the finitely many points of $S$ that are images of the centres of the blow-ups. Let $q_1, \dots, q_s$ be those images other than $p$, and $S_0 = S \setminus \{q_1, \dots, q_s\}$, an open neighbourhood of $p$. Suppose that $F'(\pi^{-1}(p))$ is a single point. Then $F'$ maps every fibre of $\pi_0 = \pi|_{\pi^{-1}(S_0)} \colon \pi^{-1}(S_0) \to S_0$ to a point, the fibres other than $\pi^{-1}(p)$ being points, and $\pi_{0*} \mathcal{O} = \mathcal{O}_{S_0}$. By Lemma P5.7.2 there is a morphism $h \colon S_0 \to X$ with $F'|_{\pi^{-1}(S_0)} = h \circ \pi_0$. Then $h$ agrees with $\varphi$ on the dense open set over which $\pi$ is an isomorphism and $\varphi$ is defined, so $h$ is a morphism extending $\varphi$ to a neighbourhood of $p$, a contradiction. So $F'(\pi^{-1}(p))$ is a closed connected subset of $X$, the image of a proper scheme of dimension at most $1$, with more than one point: a connected curve.

*Lemma P8.2.4.* (1) Induct on the number of blow-ups. Let $\sigma \colon S_2 \to S_1$ be the blow-up of a point $x$, with exceptional curve $E$, and $D$ an effective divisor on $S_1$ with multiplicity $m$ at $x$. In a neighbourhood of $E$, with local coordinates $u, v$ at $x$ and the chart $v = u v'$, a local equation of $D$ of order $m$ pulls back to $u^m$ times a function not divisible by $u$, so $\sigma^* D = \widetilde D + m E$, where $\widetilde D$ is the strict transform. Applying this at each step to the pullback of $C \times \{t\}$ gives $S'_t = \pi^* (C \times \{t\}) = \widetilde C_t + \sum_j a_j E_j$ with $a_j \geq 0$, and $a_j \geq 1$ for every curve $E_j$ contracted by $\pi$ into $C \times \{t\}$: the exceptional curve of a blow-up at a point of the support of the current total transform appears with coefficient at least the multiplicity there, which is positive, and pulling back further does not lower coefficients. Each $E_j$ is the strict transform of the exceptional curve of one blow-up, isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ by part 2 of Theorem P5.6.2, and the curves $C \times \{t\}$ and $\mathbb{P}^1$ are smooth. By Proposition P5.6.14, the strict transform of a smooth curve under a blow-up has the same normalization and $\delta$-invariant $0$, so it is again smooth and maps isomorphically onto the original curve, a birational morphism of smooth projective curves being an isomorphism by Theorem P5.1.3. So $\widetilde C_t \cong C \times \{t\} \cong C$ and every $E_j \cong \mathbb{P}^1$.

(2) The fibre $C \times \{t\}$ is connected, and $\pi$ is a closed surjective map whose fibres are connected by Theorem P4.6.8, as in the proof of Lemma P8.2.3. If $\pi^{-1}(C \times \{t\}) = A \sqcup B$ with $A$, $B$ closed, every fibre of $\pi$ lies in $A$ or in $B$, so $C \times \{t\} = \pi(A) \sqcup \pi(B)$ with both closed, and one of them is empty.

(3) The surface $S$ is smooth and projective by Proposition P3.12.23, so $S'$ is a smooth projective surface, and $q$ is flat by Theorem P3.13.4, being a dominant morphism from an integral scheme to a smooth curve. Let $D$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$ and $\mathcal{L} = F'^* \mathcal{O}_X(D)$. By Proposition P7.2.7, $D \cdot F'_*(S'_t) = (\mathcal{L} \cdot \mathcal{O}_{S'_t}) = \chi(\mathcal{L}|_{S'_t}) - \chi(\mathcal{O}_{S'_t})$. Both $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{O}_{S'}$ are flat over $\bar T$, so by Theorem P4.5.13 their Euler characteristics on the fibres are locally constant, hence constant on the connected curve $\bar T$. Let $t_1 \in T^\circ$ be a point over which $\pi$ is an isomorphism. Then $S'_{t_1} = C \times \{t_1\}$ and $D \cdot F'_*(S'_{t_1}) = \deg f_{t_1}^* \mathcal{O}(D) = D \cdot f_{t_1 *} C$ by Proposition P7.2.7, and $f_{t_1 *} C \equiv f_{t_0 *} C$ by Proposition P8.1.6 applied to $T^\circ$. The decomposition of part 1 and linearity of $F'_*$ give the last equality, with $F'_*(\widetilde C_t) = f'_{t *} C$.

*Theorem P8.2.5.* Write $x = f(c)$ and $H = \operatorname{Hom}(C, X; f|_{\{c\}})$, a quasi-projective scheme near $[f]$ by Theorem P8.1.4 and Proposition P8.1.5. Let $H_1$ be an irreducible component of $H$ through $[f]$ of dimension at least $1$, with its reduced structure. By Lemma P8.2.2 there is a curve $T' \subseteq H_1$ through $[f]$. Let $T^\circ \to T'$ be the normalization, a smooth curve, finite over $T'$ by Theorem P3.7.36, and $T^\circ \subseteq \bar T$ its smooth projective completion, by Theorem P5.1.3. Composing with the evaluation morphism of Theorem P8.1.4 gives $F \colon C \times T^\circ \to X$ with $F(c, t) = x$ for all $t$, with $f_{t_0} = f$ for a point $t_0$ over $[f]$, and with $f_t \neq f_{t'}$ when $t$, $t'$ lie over distinct points of $T'$. Let $S = C \times \bar T$ and $\varphi \colon S \dashrightarrow X$ the rational map defined by $F$.

Suppose that $\varphi$ is defined at every point of $\{c\} \times \bar T$. Its domain of definition is open and contains $C \times T^\circ$, and by part 2 of Theorem P3.10.29 its complement has codimension $2$, so it is a finite set of points $(c_i, t_i)$ with $c_i \neq c$. Let $C_0 = C \setminus \{c_i\}$. Then $\varphi$ is a morphism on $\bar T \times C_0$, with the factors swapped, and it maps $\bar T \times \{c\}$ to the point $x$. The scheme $\bar T \times C_0$ is smooth by Proposition P3.12.23, hence reduced by Lemma P3.12.10, and irreducible. Part 2 of Lemma P8.2.1, with $Y = \bar T$ and $V = C_0$, shows that $\varphi(t, y)$ does not depend on $t$. So $f_t|_{C_0} = f_{t'}|_{C_0}$ for all $t$, $t' \in T^\circ$, and $f_t = f_{t'}$ by Theorem P3.4.11, a contradiction. Hence $\varphi$ is not defined at some point $p = (c, t_\infty)$, and $t_\infty \notin T^\circ$.

Let $\pi \colon S' \to S$ be as in Theorem P5.7.9, with $F' = \varphi \circ \pi$ a morphism. By Lemma P8.2.3, $F'(\pi^{-1}(p))$ is a connected curve. Let $\Sigma$ be the strict transform of $\{c\} \times \bar T$; $F'$ maps a dense open subset of $\Sigma$ to $x$, hence all of $\Sigma$, and $\Sigma$ meets $\pi^{-1}(p)$ since $\pi(\Sigma) = \{c\} \times \bar T \ni p$. So $x \in F'(\pi^{-1}(p))$. The set $\pi^{-1}(p)$ is a connected union of some of the curves $E_j$ of Lemma P8.2.4 for $t = t_\infty$, and its image is the union of the curves $F'(E_j)$, for the $E_j \subseteq \pi^{-1}(p)$ not contracted by $F'$, and of points; since $\pi^{-1}(p)$ is connected and some $E_j$ in it is not contracted, every point of the image lies on such a curve $F'(E_j)$. Part 3 of Lemma P8.2.4, with $t = t_\infty$, gives $f_* C \equiv f'_{t_\infty *} C + \sum_j a_j F'_* E_j$. Let $f' = f'_{t_\infty}$ and $Z = \sum_j a_j F'_* E_j$. Each $F'_* E_j$ is $0$ or a positive multiple of the rational curve $F'(E_j)$, rational by Theorem P5.2.12 since $E_j \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, so $Z$ is an effective sum of rational curves. It is nonzero and its support contains $x$, by the choice of a non-contracted $E_j \subseteq \pi^{-1}(p)$ whose image contains $x$. Finally $f'(C) \cup \operatorname{Supp} Z = F'(S'_{t_\infty})$ is connected by part 2 of Lemma P8.2.4.

*Theorem P8.2.6.* Write $x_0 = f(0)$, $x_\infty = f(\infty)$ and $H = \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, X; f|_{\{0, \infty\}})$. Let $H_1$ be an irreducible component of $H$ through $[f]$ of dimension at least $2$, with its reduced structure. The group $G = \mathbb{G}_m$ acts on $\mathbb{P}^1$ by $\lambda \cdot [s : u] = [\lambda s : u]$, fixing $0 = [0 : 1]$ and $\infty = [1 : 0]$, and $\lambda \mapsto [f \circ \lambda]$ is a morphism $G \to H$, given by the family $\mathbb{P}^1 \times G \to X$, $([s : u], \lambda) \mapsto f([\lambda s : u])$. Let $\bar O$ be the closure of its image, of dimension at most $1$. By Lemma P8.2.2, with $U = H_1 \setminus \bar O$, which is dense open in $H_1$ since $\dim H_1 \geq 2$, there is a curve $T' \subseteq H_1$ through $[f]$ with $T' \not\subseteq \bar O$. As in the proof of Theorem P8.2.5, normalize and complete $T'$ to $T^\circ \subseteq \bar T$, and obtain $F \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \times T^\circ \to X$ with $F(0, t) = x_0$, $F(\infty, t) = x_\infty$ and $f_{t_0} = f$. Let $S = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \bar T$, $\pi \colon S' \to S$ a resolution of the indeterminacy of $F$ as in Theorem P5.7.9, $F' \colon S' \to X$ the resulting morphism, $q = p_2 \circ \pi$, and $Y = F'(S')$, an irreducible closed subset of dimension $1$ or $2$, since $f$ is nonconstant.

Suppose $\dim Y = 1$. Then $f_t(\mathbb{P}^1) = Y$ for every $t \in T^\circ$. Let $\nu \colon \widetilde Y \to Y$ be the normalization, a smooth projective curve dominated by $\mathbb{P}^1$, so $\widetilde Y \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ by Theorem P5.2.12. By Definition P3.7.35, $F'$ lifts to $\widetilde F \colon S' \to \widetilde Y$, and the restrictions $\tilde f_t \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \widetilde Y$ have a common degree $e$ by Proposition P8.1.6. Since $\nu$ is birational, $f_* \mathbb{P}^1 = e [Y]$ with $Y$ a rational curve; if $e \geq 2$ the theorem holds with one curve $\Gamma_1 = Y$ and $a_1 = e$. Let $e = 1$. Then each $\tilde f_t$ is an isomorphism. The morphism $T^\circ \to \widetilde Y$, $t \mapsto \widetilde F(0, t)$, takes values in the finite set $\nu^{-1}(x_0)$, and $T^\circ$ is connected, so it is constant; similarly at $\infty$. So $\lambda_t = \tilde f_{t_0}^{-1} \circ \tilde f_t$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^1$ fixing $0$ and $\infty$. By Example P8.1.8 it is given by a matrix in $\mathrm{PGL}_2$, and fixing $[0 : 1]$ and $[1 : 0]$ forces the matrix to be diagonal, so $\lambda_t \in G$. Then $f_t = \nu \circ \tilde f_{t_0} \circ \lambda_t = f \circ \lambda_t$, so the image of $T^\circ$ in $H$ lies in $\bar O$, and so does its closure $T'$, a contradiction.

Suppose $\dim Y = 2$. Fix a very ample divisor $A$ on $X$ and let $D = F'^* A$. By Theorem P7.1.15 and part 2 of Proposition P7.1.14, $D^2 = [K(S') : K(Y)] (A^2 \cdot Y) > 0$. For every closed point $t \in \bar T$, part 3 of Lemma P8.2.4 gives $f_* \mathbb{P}^1 \equiv F'_*(S'_t) = \sum_j b_j \deg(E_j / F'(E_j)) [F'(E_j)]$, the sum over the components $E_j$ of $S'_t$ not contracted by $F'$, with multiplicities $b_j \geq 1$, all components being isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ by part 1. If for some $t$ the integer $\sum_j b_j \deg(E_j / F'(E_j))$ is at least $2$, the theorem holds. It is at least $1$, since $A \cdot f_* \mathbb{P}^1 > 0$. Suppose, for a contradiction, that it is $1$ for every $t$: every fibre $S'_t$ has exactly one component not contracted by $F'$.

Let $r$ be the number of blow-ups in $\pi$. Each blow-up adds one exceptional curve, the earlier ones persisting as strict transforms, so $S'$ contains exactly $r$ curves contracted by $\pi$, and each lies in a fibre of $q$. Let $t_1, \dots, t_s$ be the points whose fibres contain them, and $r_i$ the number of them in $S'_{t_i}$, so $\sum_i r_i = r$ and $S'_{t_i}$ has $r_i + 1$ components. The curves contracted by $F'$ include the strict transforms $\Sigma_0$, $\Sigma_\infty$ of $\{0\} \times \bar T$ and $\{\infty\} \times \bar T$, mapped to $x_0$ and $x_\infty$, and $r_i$ components of each $S'_{t_i}$: in total at least $r + 2$ distinct curves $\Gamma_1, \dots, \Gamma_{r+2}$, all with $D \cdot \Gamma_i = 0$ by Theorem P7.1.15.

Their classes are linearly independent in $\operatorname{Num}(S')$. Let $\sum_i c_i \Gamma_i \equiv 0$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$, and write it as $A' \equiv B'$ with $A' = \sum_{c_i > 0} c_i \Gamma_i$ and $B' = \sum_{c_i < 0} (-c_i) \Gamma_i$, effective with no common component. Then $A'^2 = A' \cdot B' \geq 0$, because distinct integral curves meet nonnegatively. Since $D \cdot A' = 0$ and $D^2 > 0$, Corollary P5.5.23 gives $A'^2 \leq 0$ with equality only if $A' \equiv 0$. So $A' \equiv 0$, and for an ample divisor $H'$ on $S'$, $0 = H' \cdot A' = \sum_{c_i > 0} c_i H' \cdot \Gamma_i$ with every $H' \cdot \Gamma_i > 0$ by Lemma P5.5.20, so $A' = 0$; likewise $B' = 0$. The divisor $D$ is not in the span of the $\Gamma_i$, since $D^2 > 0$ while $D$ is orthogonal to that span. So $\operatorname{Num}(S')$ has rank at least $r + 3$. But $S = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \bar T = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\bar T}^{\oplus 2})$ has $\operatorname{Num}(S)$ of rank $2$ by part 2 of Theorem P5.9.4, and each blow-up raises the rank by $1$ by part 4 of Theorem P5.6.3, so $\operatorname{Num}(S')$ has rank $r + 2$, a contradiction.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $F \colon \mathbb{A}^1 \times \mathbb{A}^1 \to \mathbb{A}^1$ be $F(y, v) = y v$. Show that $F$ contracts $\mathbb{A}^1 \times \{0\}$ but no other fibre $\mathbb{A}^1 \times \{v\}$, and explain which hypothesis of Lemma P8.2.1 fails.
2. (B) Let $E$ be an elliptic curve and $f_a \colon E \to E$ the translation by $a \in E$. Show that $\dim_{[f_0]} \operatorname{Hom}(E, E) \geq 1$, that every $f_a$ has $f_{a *} E = E$, and that no $f_a$ with $a \neq 0$ fixes a point. Explain why this does not contradict Theorem P8.2.5, and why the fixed point is needed.
3. (A) Let $X$ be a projective variety that contains no rational curve, for example an abelian variety. Show that for every smooth projective curve $C$, every $c \in C$ and every $f \colon C \to X$, the scheme $\operatorname{Hom}(C, X; f|_{\{c\}})$ has dimension $0$ at $[f]$.
4. (B) Let $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be an isomorphism onto a smooth conic. Using Corollary P8.1.13 and the splitting $f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \cong \mathcal{O}(3)^{\oplus 2}$ of Section 1.3, show that $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^2; f|_{\{0, \infty\}})$ is smooth of dimension $4$ at $[f]$, and deduce from Theorem P8.2.6 that $2 \ell$ is a sum of two classes of rational curves, in agreement with Example P8.2.7.
5. (B) Let $F' \colon S' \to Y$ be a surjective morphism from a smooth projective surface onto a surface. Show, as in the proof of Theorem P8.2.6, that the classes of finitely many distinct integral curves contracted by $F'$ are linearly independent in $\operatorname{Num}(S')$, and that their number is less than the rank of $\operatorname{Num}(S')$.
6. (C) Let $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ be nonconstant and $\bar O \subseteq \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, X; f|_{\{0\}})$ the closure of the orbit $\{f \circ \varphi\}$ of the group of automorphisms of $\mathbb{P}^1$ fixing $0$, of dimension $2$. Show that the conclusion of Theorem P8.2.5 holds trivially for $C = \mathbb{P}^1$ when $f$ has degree at least $2$ onto its image, and explain why, for a rational curve, a deformation with one point fixed gives no information unless it leaves $\bar O$. This is the reason for the second form.

### Lean

The rigidity lemma, rational maps of surfaces and the elimination of indeterminacy are not in the repository, and neither is the scheme of morphisms. Curves and their intersection numbers appear in `MinimalModel/Statements/Basic.lean` as the type `MinimalModel.Curve` and the structure `MinimalModel.IntersectionData`, which takes intersection numbers of divisors with curves as data, so numerical equivalence of $1$-cycles can be stated there, but no statement of bend-and-break is pinned.

### Notes and history

Bend-and-break is due to Mori, who introduced it to prove Hartshorne's conjecture that a smooth projective variety with ample tangent bundle is a projective space (Mor79), and developed it in his paper on extremal rays (Mor82). The first form, with the rigidity lemma, is Kollár and Mori, Theorem 1.4 and Lemma 1.6 (KM98), and Debarre, Section 3.1 (Deb01); the second form, with the count of contracted curves on the blown-up surface, is Kollár and Mori, Corollary 1.7, and Debarre, Section 3.2. Kollár states both with control of the degree of the rational curves in Section II.5 (Kol96). The rigidity lemma for abelian varieties goes back to Mumford, and the version used here, for families over an arbitrary base, is in Debarre, Section 1.6. A stronger form of the second lemma, in which the cycle is connected and passes through the two fixed points, is in Kollár and Mori, Corollary 1.7; it is not needed for Theorem P8.2.13 below.
