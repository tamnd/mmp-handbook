# Cones over varieties

The affine cone over a polarized variety $(V, L)$ is the spectrum of the section ring $\bigoplus_m H^0(V, L^m)$. It has at most one singular point, the vertex, and the total space of $L^{-1}$ resolves it with a single exceptional divisor, a copy of $V$. Every invariant of the vertex that the volumes use, the class group, the index of the canonical class, the discrepancy and rationality, becomes a statement about $V$ and $L$. Cones are therefore the main source of examples for the classes of singularities of Chapter 13.

Section 12.1 constructs the cone and its resolution, proves that the cone is a normal affine variety smooth away from the vertex, and computes the normal bundle and the canonical class of the resolution. The examples identify the cyclic quotients $\frac{1}{n}(1, 1)$ and $\frac{1}{d}(1, \dots, 1)$ and the cones over hypersurfaces.

Section 12.2 proves that the class group of the cone is $\operatorname{Pic}(V)/\mathbb{Z} L$, that a cone over a divisor $D$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier exactly when $D$ is a rational multiple of $L$, and that if $K_V \sim_\mathbb{Q} r L$ then the exceptional divisor has discrepancy $-1 - r$. For a curve of genus $g$ and a line bundle of degree $d$ this is $-1 - \frac{2g - 2}{d}$. The examples compute the discrepancies of the cyclic quotients, the cones over hypersurfaces and the cone over a smooth quadric surface, and give a cone over a curve of genus $2$ whose canonical class is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier.

The chapter follows Kollár, Section 3.1 (Kol13), and Reid's Young Person's Guide (Rei87).
