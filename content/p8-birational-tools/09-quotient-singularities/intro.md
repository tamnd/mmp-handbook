# Quotient singularities

A finite group acting on a smooth variety produces a quotient whose singularities are the first examples of the singularities the minimal model program allows. They are the singularities of the moduli of curves, of weighted projective spaces and of the simplicial toric varieties of Part C, and in dimension two the klt singularities are exactly the quotient singularities. This chapter constructs the quotients, reduces their study to linear actions on affine space, and computes the cyclic case explicitly.

Section 9.1 constructs the quotient of an affine variety by a finite group whose order is invertible, proves that it is a normal variety whose points are the orbits, that the quotient of a factorial variety is $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial, and that the completed local ring of the quotient is the ring of invariants of the stabiliser. Cartan's linearization then shows that a quotient singularity is formally a quotient of affine space by a linear group.

Section 9.2 proves the Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem in the direction that a group generated by pseudo-reflections has a polynomial ring of invariants, by Chevalley's argument with divided differences, and cites the converse. It deduces that every quotient of affine space is a quotient by a group with fewer reflections, and that for a small group the quotient map is étale in codimension one, so that $K_X \sim 0$ when the group lies in $SL_n$.

Section 9.3 treats the cyclic quotient singularities $\frac{1}{r}(a_1, \dots, a_n)$, shows that they are the affine toric varieties of a lattice $\mathbb{Z}^n + \mathbb{Z} \cdot \frac{1}{r}(a)$, and collects for $\frac{1}{r}(1, q)$ the class group, the index of the canonical divisor and the Hirzebruch-Jung resolution from Chapters 7 and 8. The examples are $\frac{1}{2}(1, 1)$, $\frac{1}{n}(1, 1)$, $\frac{1}{5}(1, 2)$, the $A_{r-1}$ singularities and the non-small type $\frac{1}{4}(1, 2)$.

The chapter follows Reid's Young Person's Guide (Rei87), Chevalley (Che55), and Kollár and Mori, Section 5.2 (KM98).
