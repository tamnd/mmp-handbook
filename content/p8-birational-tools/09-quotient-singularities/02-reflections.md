## Reflections and the Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem

### Statement

Let $W = k^n$ and let $G \subseteq GL(W)$ be a finite subgroup with $|G|$ invertible in $k$. The group acts on the polynomial ring $k[W] = \operatorname{Sym}(W^*) = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ by $(g \cdot a)(w) = a(g^{-1} w)$, preserving degrees, and $\mathbb{A}^n / G = \operatorname{Spec} k[W]^G$.

**Definition P8.9.12 (pseudo-reflections and small groups).** An element $s \in GL(W)$ of finite order is a *pseudo-reflection* if $\operatorname{rank}(s - 1) = 1$, that is, if $s \neq 1$ fixes a hyperplane $H_s \subseteq W$ pointwise. A finite group $G \subseteq GL(W)$ is *small* if it contains no pseudo-reflection.

Since $|G|$ is invertible, every $g \in G$ is diagonalizable, and $g$ is a pseudo-reflection exactly when its eigenvalues are $1, \dots, 1, \zeta$ with $\zeta \neq 1$. If $H_s = V(l)$ for a linear form $l \in W^*$, then $l$ is determined up to a scalar.

**Lemma P8.9.13 (divided differences).** Let $s \in GL(W)$ be a pseudo-reflection with fixed hyperplane $V(l)$. For every $a \in k[W]$ the polynomial $s \cdot a - a$ is divisible by $l$, and if $a$ is homogeneous of degree $d \geq 1$, then $\Delta_s(a) = (s \cdot a - a)/l$ is homogeneous of degree $d - 1$ or zero.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.9.14 (Chevalley, Shephard and Todd).** Let $G \subseteq GL(W)$ be finite with $|G|$ invertible in $k$.

1. If $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ and $G$ is generated by pseudo-reflections, then $k[W]^G = k[f_1, \dots, f_n]$ for algebraically independent homogeneous invariants $f_1, \dots, f_n$, so $\mathbb{A}^n / G \cong \mathbb{A}^n$.
2. Conversely, if $k[W]^G$ is a polynomial ring, then $G$ is generated by pseudo-reflections.

Part 1 also holds when $\operatorname{char} k$ does not divide $|G|$. Part 1 in characteristic zero is proved below; the converse and the positive characteristic case are cited.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P8.9.15 (reduction to small groups).** Let $\operatorname{char} k = 0$, $G \subseteq GL(W)$ finite, and $H \subseteq G$ the subgroup generated by the pseudo-reflections in $G$. Then $H$ is normal, and there are a $G$-stable graded subspace $U \subseteq k[W]^H$ of dimension $n$ and isomorphisms
$$k[W]^H = \operatorname{Sym}(U), \qquad k[W]^G = \operatorname{Sym}(U)^{G/H},$$
where $G/H$ acts faithfully and linearly on $U$. So $\mathbb{A}^n / G \cong \mathbb{A}^n / (G/H)$ with $G/H$ acting linearly on $\mathbb{A}^n = \operatorname{Spec} \operatorname{Sym}(U)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P8.9.16 (small groups in codimension one).** Let $G \subseteq GL(W)$ be small, $X = \mathbb{A}^n / G$ and $\pi \colon \mathbb{A}^n \to X$ the quotient map, with $n \geq 2$.

1. The points of $\mathbb{A}^n$ with nontrivial stabiliser form a closed $G$-stable subset $Z$ of codimension at least $2$, and $\pi$ restricts to a finite étale morphism of degree $|G|$ from $\mathbb{A}^n \setminus Z$ onto $X^0 = X \setminus \pi(Z)$, whose complement in $X$ has codimension at least $2$.
2. Let $e$ be the order of the character $\det \colon G \to k^*$. Then $e K_X \sim 0$. In particular, if $G \subseteq SL(W)$, then $K_X \sim 0$ and $K_X$ is Cartier.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A reflection fixes a hyperplane, so it changes a polynomial only by a multiple of the equation of that hyperplane, and dividing by this equation lowers degrees. Chevalley's argument runs an induction on degree with these divided differences: a relation among minimal generators of the invariant ideal would produce, after differentiation, a relation that the divided differences force into a smaller ideal, which contradicts minimality. The reflections in an arbitrary group generate a normal subgroup whose invariants are again polynomial, so the quotient by the whole group is a quotient of affine space by a smaller group. A group without reflections has fixed loci of codimension at least two, so its quotient map is étale in codimension one, and an invariant volume form descends.

### Examples

**Example P8.9.17 (a group generated by reflections).** Let $G = \{ \operatorname{diag}(\pm 1, \pm 1) \} \subseteq GL_2(k)$, with $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$. It is generated by the reflections $\operatorname{diag}(-1, 1)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$, and $k[x, y]^G = k[x^2, y^2]$, since a monomial $x^a y^b$ is invariant exactly when $a$ and $b$ are even. The degrees of the generators are $2$ and $2$, with product $4 = |G|$. Removing one reflection gives the group $\{ \pm 1 \}$ of Example P8.9.8, which is small and has a singular quotient.

**Example P8.9.18 (a cyclic group with a reflection).** Let $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, let $i \in k$ with $i^2 = -1$, and let $G$ be the cyclic group of order $4$ generated by $g$ acting on the coordinate functions by $g \cdot x = i x$, $g \cdot y = -y$. The element $g^2$ acts by $x \mapsto -x$, $y \mapsto y$, a reflection, while $g$ and $g^3$ have no eigenvalue $1$, so $H = \{ 1, g^2 \}$. Then $k[x, y]^H = k[x^2, y]$, and $U$ is spanned by $u = x^2$ and $y$. The class of $g$ in $G/H$ acts by $u \mapsto -u$, $y \mapsto -y$, so Proposition P8.9.15 gives
$$\mathbb{A}^2 / G \cong \mathbb{A}^2 / \{ \pm 1 \},$$
the quadric cone of Example P8.9.8. In the notation of Section 9.3, $G$ gives the singularity $\frac{1}{4}(1, 2)$, and the example shows $\frac{1}{4}(1, 2) \cong \frac{1}{2}(1, 1)$.

**Example P8.9.19 (cyclic subgroups of $SL_2$).** Let $r \geq 2$ be invertible in $k$, $\zeta$ a primitive $r$-th root of unity and $G = \langle \operatorname{diag}(\zeta, \zeta^{-1}) \rangle \subseteq SL_2(k)$. A nontrivial element $\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^j, \zeta^{-j})$ has no eigenvalue $1$, so $G$ is small. A monomial $x^a y^b$ is multiplied by $\zeta^{\pm(a - b)}$, so it is invariant if and only if $a \equiv b \pmod r$. Such a monomial with $a \geq b$ is $(xy)^b (x^r)^{(a - b)/r}$, and similarly for $a < b$, so $k[x, y]^G$ is generated by $u = x^r$, $v = y^r$, $w = xy$, and
$$\mathbb{A}^2 / G \cong \{ uv = w^r \} \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3,$$
with the kernel computed as in Example P8.9.8. This is the singularity $A_{r-1}$ of Chapter 10. Proposition P8.9.16 gives $K_X \sim 0$, which agrees with the fact that a hypersurface is Gorenstein (Corollary P1.10.27).

### Proof

*Lemma P8.9.13.* For $w \in V(l)$, $s^{-1} w = w$, so $(s \cdot a - a)(w) = a(s^{-1} w) - a(w) = 0$. Choose coordinates with $l = x_1$ and write $b = s \cdot a - a$ as $b = x_1 b_1 + b_0(x_2, \dots, x_n)$. Then $b_0$ vanishes on $k^{n-1}$, so $b_0 = 0$ since $k$ is infinite, and $l$ divides $b$. If $a$ is homogeneous of degree $d$, so is $s \cdot a - a$, and the quotient by the linear form $l$ is homogeneous of degree $d - 1$ unless it is zero.

*Theorem P8.9.14, part 1.* Let $S = k[W]$, $R = S^G$, $R_+$ its part of positive degree, $\mathfrak{n} = (x_1, \dots, x_n) \subseteq S$, and $I = R_+ S$ the ideal of $S$ generated by the homogeneous invariants of positive degree. By the Hilbert basis theorem $I = (f_1, \dots, f_m)$ with $f_i \in R_+$ homogeneous, and the number $m$ may be taken minimal. By Lemma P1.13.6, applied to $I$ with the ideal $\mathfrak{n}$, the images of $f_1, \dots, f_m$ in $I / \mathfrak{n} I$ are then linearly independent over $k$: if one were a combination of the others modulo $\mathfrak{n} I$, the others would already generate $I$.

*Step 1: the $f_i$ generate $R$.* Let $f \in R$ be homogeneous of degree $d > 0$. Then $f \in I$, so $f = \sum_i a_i f_i$ with $a_i \in S$ homogeneous of degree $d - \deg f_i < d$. Applying the Reynolds operator of Lemma P8.9.2 gives $f = \sum_i R(a_i) f_i$ with $R(a_i) \in R$ homogeneous of degree less than $d$. By induction on $d$, each $R(a_i)$ is a polynomial in the $f_j$, and so is $f$.

*Step 2: a key lemma.* Let $h_1, \dots, h_r \in R$ be homogeneous with $h_1 \notin (h_2, \dots, h_r) R$, and let $a_1, \dots, a_r \in S$ be homogeneous with $\sum_i a_i h_i = 0$ and $\deg a_i h_i$ independent of $i$. Then $a_1 \in I$. First, $h_1 \notin (h_2, \dots, h_r) S$: if $h_1 = \sum_{i \geq 2} b_i h_i$ with $b_i \in S$, then applying $R$ gives $h_1 = \sum_{i \geq 2} R(b_i) h_i$. Induct on $\deg a_1$. If $\deg a_1 = 0$ and $a_1 \neq 0$, then $h_1 = -a_1^{-1} \sum_{i \geq 2} a_i h_i$, which is impossible, so $a_1 = 0 \in I$. If $\deg a_1 > 0$, let $s \in G$ be a pseudo-reflection with fixed hyperplane $V(l)$. Applying $s$ to the relation, subtracting, and dividing by $l$ in the domain $S$ gives $\sum_i \Delta_s(a_i) h_i = 0$, with $\deg \Delta_s(a_i) = \deg a_i - 1$ by Lemma P8.9.13. By induction $\Delta_s(a_1) \in I$, so $s \cdot a_1 - a_1 = l \Delta_s(a_1) \in I$. Every $g \in G$ is a product $s_1 \cdots s_t$ of pseudo-reflections. Put $b_j = s_{j+1} \cdots s_t \cdot a_1$, with $b_t = a_1$. Applying $s_{j+1} \cdots s_t$ to the relation gives a relation $\sum_i (s_{j+1} \cdots s_t \cdot a_i) h_i = 0$ of the same kind, because the $h_i$ are invariant, with first coefficient $b_j$ of degree $\deg a_1$, so the argument just given shows $s_j \cdot b_j - b_j \in I$. Then
$$g \cdot a_1 - a_1 = \sum_{j = 1}^{t} \left( s_j \cdot b_j - b_j \right) \in I.$$
Averaging, $a_1 - R(a_1) \in I$. Finally $R(a_1)$ is an invariant of positive degree, so it lies in $I$, and $a_1 \in I$.

*Step 3: the $f_i$ are algebraically independent.* Give $k[y_1, \dots, y_m]$ the grading $\deg y_i = d_i = \deg f_i$. Suppose $P \neq 0$ is a polynomial with $P(f_1, \dots, f_m) = 0$, of least possible degree; it may be taken weighted homogeneous of degree $D$. Put $h_i = (\partial P / \partial y_i)(f_1, \dots, f_m) \in R$, homogeneous of degree $D - d_i$. Since $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ and $P$ is not constant, some $\partial P / \partial y_i$ is a nonzero polynomial of smaller degree, so by minimality some $h_i$ is nonzero. Renumber so that $h_1, \dots, h_q$ is a minimal set of generators of the ideal $(h_1, \dots, h_m) R$ among the $h_i$, and write $h_j = \sum_{i \leq q} c_{ij} h_i$ for $j > q$, with $c_{ij} \in R$ homogeneous of degree $d_i - d_j$, zero if $d_i < d_j$. Differentiating $P(f_1, \dots, f_m) = 0$ with respect to $x_l$ gives $\sum_{i = 1}^{m} h_i \, \partial f_i / \partial x_l = 0$, that is,
$$\sum_{i = 1}^{q} h_i \Big( \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_l} + \sum_{j > q} c_{ij} \frac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_l} \Big) = 0.$$
Each bracket is homogeneous of degree $d_i - 1$, so Step 2 shows that the bracket for $i = 1$ lies in $I$, for every $l$. Multiplying by $x_l$, summing over $l$ and using Euler's formula $\sum_l x_l \, \partial f / \partial x_l = (\deg f) f$ gives
$$d_1 f_1 + \sum_{j > q} c_{1j} d_j f_j \in \mathfrak{n} I.$$
For $j > q$ with $d_j < d_1$, $c_{1j}$ has positive degree, so $c_{1j} \in R_+ \subseteq \mathfrak{n}$ and $c_{1j} d_j f_j \in \mathfrak{n} I$. For $d_j = d_1$, $c_{1j}$ is a scalar. So $d_1 f_1$ is congruent modulo $\mathfrak{n} I$ to a linear combination of the $f_j$ with $j > q \geq 1$, and $d_1 \neq 0$ in $k$. This contradicts the linear independence of the images of the $f_i$ in $I/\mathfrak{n} I$. So the $f_i$ are algebraically independent, $R = k[f_1, \dots, f_m]$ is a polynomial ring, and $m = \dim R = n$ by part 4 of Theorem P8.9.3.

*Theorem P8.9.14, part 2, and the positive characteristic case.* Both are in Bourbaki, Groupes et algèbres de Lie, Chapter V, Section 5, Theorem 4, under the hypothesis that $|G|$ is invertible in $k$. The case $k = \mathbb{C}$ is due to Shephard and Todd (ST54), who classified the finite groups generated by pseudo-reflections, and to Chevalley (Che55); Serre gave the uniform proof of part 2 reproduced by Bourbaki.

*Proposition P8.9.15.* A conjugate of a pseudo-reflection is a pseudo-reflection, so $H$ is normal. For $a \in R = k[W]^H$, $g \in G$ and $h \in H$, $h g \cdot a = g (g^{-1} h g) \cdot a = g \cdot a$, so $G$ acts on $R$ through $G/H$, preserving degrees. By part 1 of Theorem P8.9.14, $R = k[y_1, \dots, y_n]$ with $y_i$ homogeneous and algebraically independent. The ideal $R_+ = (y_1, \dots, y_n)$ and its square are graded and $G$-stable. In the polynomial ring $k[y_1, \dots, y_n]$ the elements of $R_+^2$ have no terms of degree one in the $y_i$, so the images of $y_1, \dots, y_n$ form a basis of $R_+ / R_+^2$. Since $|G|$ is invertible, Maschke's theorem gives, in each degree $d$, a $G$-stable complement $U_d$ of $(R_+^2)_d$ in $(R_+)_d$. Then $U = \bigoplus_d U_d$ maps isomorphically to $R_+ / R_+^2$, so $\dim U = n$. By Lemma P1.13.6 a homogeneous basis of $U$ generates the ideal $R_+$, and by the argument of Step 1 above it generates $R$ as a $k$-algebra. So $\operatorname{Sym}(U) \to R$ is a surjection of domains of the same dimension $n$, hence an isomorphism. The group $G$ acts faithfully on $k(W)$, and by Galois theory an element of $G$ fixes $k(W)^H = \operatorname{Frac}(R)$ pointwise if and only if it lies in $H$, so $G/H$ acts faithfully on $R$ and on $U$. Finally $k[W]^G = (k[W]^H)^{G/H} = \operatorname{Sym}(U)^{G/H}$.

*Proposition P8.9.16.* (1) A point $w$ has nontrivial stabiliser exactly when $w \in \ker(g - 1)$ for some $g \neq 1$, so $Z = \bigcup_{g \neq 1} \ker(g - 1)$, a finite union of linear subspaces, and $G$-stable. Since $G$ is small, $\operatorname{rank}(g - 1) \geq 2$ for $g \neq 1$, so $Z$ has codimension at least $2$. By part 2 of Theorem P8.9.3, $\pi^{-1}(\pi(Z)) = GZ = Z$, and $\pi(Z)$ is closed because $\pi$ is finite, of codimension at least $2$ because $\pi$ is finite and surjective. On $\mathbb{A}^n \setminus Z$ the action is free, so $\pi$ is étale there by Proposition P8.9.5. A finite étale morphism of varieties has a locally constant degree, equal to $[k(W) : k(W)^G] = |G|$ by part 4 of Theorem P8.9.3.

(2) Let $\omega = dx_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dx_n$. For $g \in G$, $g \cdot \omega = \det(g)^{-1} \omega$ with the action on functions above, so $\omega^{\otimes e}$ is $G$-invariant. Put $W^0 = \mathbb{A}^n \setminus Z$ and let $\pi^0 \colon W^0 \to X^0$. Since $\pi^0$ is étale, $\Omega^n_{W^0} = \pi^{0*} \Omega^n_{X^0}$, so $(\Omega^n_{W^0})^{\otimes e} = \pi^{0*} \mathcal{L}$ with $\mathcal{L} = (\Omega^n_{X^0})^{\otimes e}$, and by Lemma P8.9.2 and the projection formula
$$\left( \pi^0_* \pi^{0*} \mathcal{L} \right)^G = \mathcal{L} \otimes (\pi^0_* \mathcal{O}_{W^0})^G = \mathcal{L}.$$
So $\omega^{\otimes e}$ is the pullback of a section $\eta$ of $\mathcal{L}$ on $X^0$. The section $\pi^{0*} \eta$ vanishes nowhere and $\pi^0$ is surjective, so $\eta$ vanishes nowhere, and $\mathcal{L} \cong \mathcal{O}_{X^0}$. Thus $e K_{X^0} \sim 0$, by Definition P3.12.28 on the smooth variety $X^0$. Since $X \setminus X^0$ has codimension at least $2$ and $X$ is normal, $\operatorname{Cl}(X) = \operatorname{Cl}(X^0)$, and $e K_X \sim 0$. If $G \subseteq SL(W)$ then $e = 1$, and $K_X \sim 0$ is Cartier.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the symmetric group $S_n$ acting on $\mathbb{A}^n$ by permuting coordinates is generated by reflections, and identify the invariants given by Theorem P8.9.14.
2. (A) For the dihedral group of order $2m$ acting on $\mathbb{A}^2 = \mathbb{C}$ by rotations through multiples of $2\pi/m$ and the reflection $z \mapsto \bar z$, written in real coordinates, find two homogeneous invariants of degrees $2$ and $m$ that generate the ring of invariants.
3. (B) In Theorem P8.9.14, show that the product of the degrees $d_1 \cdots d_n$ equals $|G|$, by comparing the Hilbert series of $k[W]^G$ with Molien's formula $\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_g \det(1 - g t)^{-1}$.
4. (B) Show that the terms of Molien's formula with a pole of order exactly $n - 1$ at $t = 1$ are those with $g$ a pseudo-reflection, and deduce, by comparing the Laurent expansions at $t = 1$ of both sides of Exercise 3, that the number of pseudo-reflections in $G$ is $\sum_i (d_i - 1)$ when $G$ is generated by pseudo-reflections.
5. (B) Let $G \subseteq GL_n(k)$ be small and $X = \mathbb{A}^n / G$. Show that $X$ is smooth if and only if $G$ is trivial, using part 1 of Proposition P8.9.16 and the fact that a finite étale cover of the complement of a codimension two subset of $\mathbb{A}^n$ is trivial.
6. (C) Let $G = \langle g \rangle \subseteq GL_3$ be cyclic of order $6$ acting by $x \mapsto -x$, $y \mapsto \omega y$, $z \mapsto \omega^2 z$ with $\omega$ a primitive cube root of unity. Find the subgroup $H$ of Proposition P8.9.15 and identify $\mathbb{A}^3 / G$ as a quotient by a small group.

### Notes and history

Shephard and Todd (ST54) classified the finite complex reflection groups and observed that their invariant rings are polynomial; Chevalley (Che55) gave the general proof of part 1 reproduced here, and Serre proved the converse. The role of the theorem in the theory of quotient singularities is that a quotient $\mathbb{A}^n / G$ is a quotient by the small group of Proposition P8.9.15, and for small groups the canonical class behaves as in Proposition P8.9.16. That the image of $G/H$ in $GL(U)$ is small, so that every quotient singularity is a quotient by a small group, is due to Prill, Duke Mathematical Journal 34 (1967). The converse of part 2 of Proposition P8.9.16, that $\mathbb{A}^n / G$ with $G$ small is Gorenstein only when $G \subseteq SL_n$, is due to Watanabe, Osaka Journal of Mathematics 11 (1974); Reid uses both in the Young Person's Guide (Rei87, Section 4).
