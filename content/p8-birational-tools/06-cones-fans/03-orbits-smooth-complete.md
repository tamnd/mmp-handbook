## Orbits, smoothness and completeness

### Statement

In this section $k$ is algebraically closed, $\Sigma$ is a fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$, and a variety is identified with its set of closed points when this is harmless. A $k$-point of $U_\sigma = \operatorname{Spec} k[S_\sigma]$ is a homomorphism of $k$-algebras $k[S_\sigma] \to k$, that is, a map $\gamma \colon S_\sigma \to k$ with $\gamma(0) = 1$ and $\gamma(m + m') = \gamma(m) \gamma(m')$, a homomorphism of monoids into $k$ under multiplication. A point $t \in T_N(k) = \operatorname{Hom}(M, k^*)$ acts by $(t \cdot \gamma)(m) = t(m) \gamma(m)$. For a cone $\tau$, put
$$u_\tau = \sum_{\rho \preceq \tau \text{ a ray}} u_\rho \in \tau \cap N,$$
with $u_{\{0\}} = 0$.

**Definition P8.6.22 (distinguished points and orbits).** Let $\tau \in \Sigma$. The *distinguished point* $\gamma_\tau \in U_\tau$ is the homomorphism $S_\tau \to k$ with $\gamma_\tau(m) = 1$ for $m \in \tau^\perp \cap M$ and $\gamma_\tau(m) = 0$ otherwise. The *orbit* of $\tau$ is $O(\tau) = T_N \cdot \gamma_\tau \subseteq U_\tau \subseteq X_\Sigma$, and $V(\tau)$ is its closure in $X_\Sigma$. A subset $F \subseteq S_\sigma$ is a *face of the monoid* $S_\sigma$ if it is nonempty and, for $m, m' \in S_\sigma$, $m + m' \in F$ if and only if $m \in F$ and $m' \in F$.

The map $\gamma_\tau$ is a homomorphism of monoids because $\tau^\perp \cap S_\tau$ is a face of $S_\tau$: for $m, m' \in \tau^\vee$, $\langle m + m', u \rangle = 0$ on $\tau$ forces $\langle m, u \rangle = \langle m', u \rangle = 0$.

**Lemma P8.6.23 (faces of the monoid).** Let $\sigma$ be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone and $\tau \preceq \sigma$.

1. $S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp = \tau^* \cap M$ generates the group $\tau^\perp \cap M$, which is a direct summand of $M$ of rank $n - \dim \tau$.
2. $\tau \mapsto S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$ is a bijection from the faces of $\sigma$ to the faces of the monoid $S_\sigma$.
3. The vector $u_\tau$ lies in no proper face of $\tau$. If $\tau \in \Sigma$, a cone $\sigma' \in \Sigma$ contains $u_\tau$ if and only if $\tau \preceq \sigma'$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Lemma P8.6.24 (cones that are not full-dimensional).** Let $\sigma$ be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone spanning a subspace $W$ of dimension $d$, and let $N_\sigma = W \cap N$. Then $N = N_\sigma \oplus N''$ for some sublattice $N''$, and with $\sigma_0$ the cone $\sigma$ regarded in $(N_\sigma)_\mathbb{R}$,
$$U_\sigma \cong U_{\sigma_0} \times T_{N''} \cong U_{\sigma_0} \times \mathbb{G}_m^{n - d}.$$

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.6.25 (the orbit-cone correspondence).** Let $\Sigma$ be a fan.

1. For $\tau \in \Sigma$, $O(\tau)$ is a closed subvariety of $U_\tau$ isomorphic to the torus $\operatorname{Spec} k[\tau^\perp \cap M]$ of dimension $n - \dim \tau$. It consists of the points $\gamma \in U_\tau$ with $\gamma(m) \neq 0$ exactly for $m \in \tau^\perp \cap M$.
2. $X_\Sigma$ is the disjoint union of the orbits $O(\tau)$, $\tau \in \Sigma$, and these are all the $T_N$-orbits. For $\sigma \in \Sigma$, $U_\sigma = \bigsqcup_{\tau \preceq \sigma} O(\tau)$.
3. $V(\tau) = \bigsqcup_{\sigma \in \Sigma, \, \tau \preceq \sigma} O(\sigma)$, an irreducible closed subset of dimension $n - \dim \tau$. In particular $\tau \preceq \sigma$ if and only if $O(\sigma) \subseteq V(\tau)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P8.6.26 (limits of one-parameter subgroups).** Let $u \in N$.

1. If $u \in \sigma \in \Sigma$ and $u$ lies in no proper face of $\sigma$, the morphism $\lambda^u \colon \mathbb{G}_m \to T_N \subseteq X_\Sigma$ extends to $\mathbb{A}^1 \to X_\Sigma$, and $\lim_{t \to 0} \lambda^u(t) = \gamma_\sigma$. More generally, for $\tau \preceq \sigma$, $\lim_{t \to 0} \lambda^u(t) \cdot \gamma_\tau = \gamma_\sigma$.
2. If $u \notin |\Sigma|$, then $\lambda^u$ does not extend to a morphism $\mathbb{A}^1 \to X_\Sigma$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.6.27 (smoothness).** Let $\sigma$ be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone. Then $U_\sigma$ is smooth if and only if $\sigma$ is smooth, and in that case $U_\sigma \cong \mathbb{A}^r \times \mathbb{G}_m^{n - r}$ with $r = \dim \sigma$. A toric variety $X_\Sigma$ is smooth if and only if $\Sigma$ is smooth. If $\dim \sigma = n$, the embedding dimension of $U_\sigma$ at $\gamma_\sigma$ is the number of elements of the Hilbert basis of $S_\sigma$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.6.28 (completeness).** $X_\Sigma$ is proper over $k$ if and only if $\Sigma$ is complete.

Lean: `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsComplete` is completeness of the fan; the theorem is not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A point of $U_\sigma$ is a multiplicative map on the monoid $S_\sigma$, and the set where it does not vanish is a face of the monoid. Faces of the monoid are the lattice points of faces of $\sigma^\vee$, so they correspond to faces $\tau$ of $\sigma$, and the point is then a homomorphism from the group $\tau^\perp \cap M$ to $k^*$, on which the torus acts transitively. This gives the orbits, one for each cone. Moving a point of $O(\tau)$ along a one-parameter subgroup in the interior of a larger cone $\sigma$ sends all the characters that do not vanish on $\sigma$ to $0$, so the limit is $\gamma_\sigma$, and the closure of an orbit contains the orbits of the larger cones. A one-parameter subgroup outside the support has no limit, which together with the valuative criterion gives completeness. For smoothness, the fixed point of a full-dimensional cone has cotangent space spanned by the irreducible elements of $S_\sigma$, so smoothness forces the Hilbert basis to have $n$ elements, which then form a basis of $M$.

### Examples

**Example P8.6.29 (the orbits of the projective plane).** For the fan of $\mathbb{P}^2$ of Example P8.6.18, the zero cone gives the torus $\{ x_0 x_1 x_2 \neq 0 \}$, the three rays give the three lines minus their intersection points, and the three two-dimensional cones give the three fixed points $(1 : 0 : 0)$, $(0 : 1 : 0)$, $(0 : 0 : 1)$. For instance, the ray $\rho = \operatorname{Cone}(e_1)$ has $\rho^\perp \cap M = \mathbb{Z} e_2^*$, and $U_\rho = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_1/x_0, (x_2/x_0)^{\pm 1}]$. The point $\gamma_\rho$ is $x_1/x_0 = 0$, $x_2/x_0 = 1$, that is $(1 : 0 : 1)$. Its orbit is $\{ (a : 0 : c) : ac \neq 0 \}$, and its closure $V(\rho) = \{ x_1 = 0 \}$ also contains the orbits $(1 : 0 : 0)$ and $(0 : 0 : 1)$ of the two cones $\operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_2)$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_0)$ containing $\rho$. The one-parameter subgroup $\lambda^{e_1}(t) = (1 : t : 1)$ tends to $(1 : 0 : 1) = \gamma_\rho$, and $\lambda^{e_1 + e_2}(t) = (1 : t : t)$ tends to $(1 : 0 : 0) = \gamma_{\operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_2)}$, as in Proposition P8.6.26.

**Example P8.6.30 (the quadric cone and $\frac{1}{n}(1, 1)$).** Let $\sigma_n = \operatorname{Cone}(e_2, n e_1 - e_2)$ with $n \geq 2$. By Example P8.6.9, $k[S_{\sigma_n}] = k[x, xy, xy^2, \dots, xy^n]$ with $x = \chi^{(1, 0)}$ and $y = \chi^{(0, 1)}$. The map $k[z_0, \dots, z_n] \to k[S_{\sigma_n}]$, $z_j \mapsto x y^j$, is surjective, and its kernel contains the $2 \times 2$ minors of the matrix with rows $(z_0, \dots, z_{n-1})$ and $(z_1, \dots, z_n)$, since $z_i z_{j+1} \mapsto x^2 y^{i + j + 1} \leftarrow z_{i+1} z_j$. These minors cut out the affine cone over the rational normal curve of degree $n$, which is a surface, irreducible, and of dimension $2$, so the kernel is this ideal by a dimension count when one knows the minors generate a prime ideal (Exercise 5). The embedding dimension at $\gamma_{\sigma_n}$ is $n + 1 > 2$, so $U_{\sigma_n}$ is singular by Theorem P8.6.27. For $n = 2$ the relation is $z_0 z_2 = z_1^2$, the quadric cone $xy = z^2$ of Example P3.11.29. When $n$ is invertible in $k$, $U_{\sigma_n} \cong \mathbb{A}^2 / \mu_n$ for the action $\zeta \cdot (s, t) = (\zeta s, \zeta t)$ (Exercise 2 of Section 6.2); this is the cyclic quotient singularity $\frac{1}{n}(1, 1)$. Its orbits are the torus, two curves $O(\rho)$ for the two rays, which are the images of the coordinate axes, and the singular point $\gamma_{\sigma_n}$.

**Example P8.6.31 (the cone over a square).** For the cone $\sigma$ of Example P8.6.10, the four generators of $S_\sigma$ give $x = \chi^{e_1^*}$, $y = \chi^{e_2^*}$, $z = \chi^{e_3^* - e_1^*}$ and $w = \chi^{e_3^* - e_2^*}$ with $xz = yw$. The ring $k[a, b, c, d]/(ac - bd)$ is a domain of dimension $3$, as $ac - bd$ is irreducible, and it maps onto the domain $k[S_\sigma]$ of dimension $3$, so the kernel is a prime of height $0$ in a domain, which is $0$. So $U_\sigma = \{ ac = bd \} \subseteq \mathbb{A}^4$, the cone over the smooth quadric surface, singular at the vertex $\gamma_\sigma$, with embedding dimension $4$. Its orbits are the vertex, four curves for the four two-dimensional faces, four surfaces for the four rays, and the torus. The surface $V(\operatorname{Cone}(u_1))$ is the plane $\{ c = d = 0 \}$, since $e_1^*$ and $e_2^*$ vanish on $u_1$ while $e_3^* - e_1^*$ and $e_3^* - e_2^*$ are positive on it.

**Example P8.6.32 (a toric variety that is not complete).** The fan of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{A}^1$ of Exercise 4 of Section 6.2 has support the upper half-plane. The one-parameter subgroup $\lambda^{(0, -1)}(t) = (1, t^{-1})$ has no limit as $t \to 0$, by Proposition P8.6.26, since $(0, -1)$ is not in the support. By contrast the fans of $\mathbb{P}^n$, $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and the Hirzebruch surfaces are complete, so these varieties are proper by Theorem P8.6.28. For $F_n$ this recovers part 1 of Theorem P3.11.25.

### Proof

*Lemma P8.6.23.* (1) By Proposition P8.6.4, $\tau^* = \sigma^\vee \cap \tau^\perp$ is a rational cone of dimension $n - \dim \tau = \dim \tau^\perp$, so it contains a nonempty open subset of the rational subspace $\tau^\perp$, and therefore a lattice point $m_0$ with a neighbourhood in $\tau^\perp$ contained in $\tau^*$. For $m \in \tau^\perp \cap M$, $m + c m_0 \in \tau^*$ for a large integer $c$, as in the proof of Theorem P8.6.5, so $m = (m + cm_0) - c m_0$ is a difference of elements of $\tau^* \cap M = S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$. The quotient $M / (\tau^\perp \cap M)$ is torsion-free, because $cm \in \tau^\perp$ with $c \neq 0$ implies $m \in \tau^\perp$, so it is free and the subgroup is a direct summand. It spans $\tau^\perp$, which is a rational subspace of dimension $n - \dim \tau$, so its rank is $n - \dim \tau$.

(2) For $\tau \preceq \sigma$, $S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$ is a face of $S_\sigma$, by the argument after Definition P8.6.22. It determines $\tau$: its elements generate the rational cone $\tau^*$, and $\tau = \sigma \cap (\tau^*)^\perp$ by Proposition P8.6.4. Conversely, let $F$ be a face of $S_\sigma$. Then $0 \in F$, since $m = m + 0 \in F$ for some $m$. Let $G$ be a finite generating set of $S_\sigma$ and $G_F = G \cap F$. An element of $F$ is a sum of elements of $G$, and by the face property each summand lies in $F$, so $G_F$ generates $F$. Let $m_F = \sum_{g \in G_F} g \in F$ and $\tau = \sigma \cap m_F^\perp$, a face of $\sigma$. Each $g \in G_F$ is nonnegative on $\sigma$ and the sum vanishes on $\tau$, so $g \in \tau^\perp$, and $F \subseteq S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$. Conversely let $m \in S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$ and let $S \subseteq N$ be a finite set generating $\sigma$. For $s \in S \cap \tau$, $\langle c m_F - m, s \rangle = 0$, and for $s \in S \setminus \tau$, $\langle m_F, s \rangle > 0$, so $\langle c m_F - m, s \rangle \geq 0$ for a large integer $c$. Then $c m_F - m \in S_\sigma$, and $m + (c m_F - m) = c m_F \in F$, so $m \in F$.

(3) A face $\tau \cap m^\perp$ of $\tau$ containing $u_\tau$ has $\sum_\rho \langle m, u_\rho \rangle = 0$ with nonnegative terms, so it contains every $u_\rho$, and it is $\tau$ by Proposition P8.6.4 (5). If $u_\tau \in \sigma' \in \Sigma$, then $\sigma' \cap \tau$ is a face of $\tau$ containing $u_\tau$, so $\sigma' \cap \tau = \tau$ and $\tau \subseteq \sigma'$; and $\tau = \sigma' \cap \tau$ is a face of $\sigma'$ by the definition of a fan. The converse is clear.

*Lemma P8.6.24.* The quotient $N / N_\sigma$ is torsion-free, as in the proof of Lemma P8.6.23, so it is free and $N = N_\sigma \oplus N''$. Dually $M = M_\sigma \oplus M''$ with $M_\sigma = \operatorname{Hom}(N_\sigma, \mathbb{Z})$ and $M'' = \operatorname{Hom}(N'', \mathbb{Z})$, extended by $0$ on the other summand. Since $\sigma \subseteq W = (N_\sigma)_\mathbb{R}$, a form $m = m_\sigma + m''$ is nonnegative on $\sigma$ exactly when $m_\sigma$ is, so $S_\sigma = S_{\sigma_0} \oplus M''$, where $S_{\sigma_0} = \sigma_0^\vee \cap M_\sigma$. Hence $k[S_\sigma] = k[S_{\sigma_0}] \otimes_k k[M'']$, and $k[M'']$ is a Laurent polynomial ring in $n - d$ variables.

*Theorem P8.6.25.* (1) In $U_\tau$, the monoid $S_\tau$ contains the group $\tau^\perp \cap M$, because $\tau^\perp \subseteq \tau^\vee$. The span $I$ of the $\chi^m$ with $m \in S_\tau \setminus \tau^\perp$ is an ideal, since $S_\tau \setminus \tau^\perp$ is stable under adding elements of $S_\tau$, and $k[S_\tau]/I = k[\tau^\perp \cap M]$. So $Z = V(I) \subseteq U_\tau$ is a closed subvariety isomorphic to the torus $\operatorname{Spec} k[\tau^\perp \cap M]$, of dimension $n - \dim \tau$ by Lemma P8.6.23. Its points are the $\gamma$ vanishing on $S_\tau \setminus \tau^\perp$; on the group $\tau^\perp \cap M$ such a $\gamma$ is a homomorphism to $k^*$. The torus $T_N$ acts on $Z$ through the restriction $\operatorname{Hom}(M, k^*) \to \operatorname{Hom}(\tau^\perp \cap M, k^*)$, which is surjective because $\tau^\perp \cap M$ is a direct summand of $M$. So $T_N$ acts transitively on $Z$, and $\gamma_\tau \in Z$, so $Z = O(\tau)$.

(2) Let $\sigma \in \Sigma$ and $\gamma \in U_\sigma$. The set $F = \{ m \in S_\sigma : \gamma(m) \neq 0 \}$ is a face of $S_\sigma$, because $\gamma(m + m') = \gamma(m) \gamma(m')$ and $k$ is a domain, so $F = S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$ for a unique face $\tau \preceq \sigma$ by Lemma P8.6.23. Write $\tau = \sigma \cap m^\perp$ with $m \in S_\sigma$ (Proposition P8.6.13). Then $m \in F$, so $\gamma(\chi^m) \neq 0$, and $\gamma \in D(\chi^m) = U_\tau$. As a point of $U_\tau$, $\gamma$ is the extension to $S_\tau = S_\sigma - \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} m$ given by $\gamma(m' - cm) = \gamma(m') \gamma(m)^{-c}$, which is nonzero exactly on $\tau^\perp \cap M$. By (1), $\gamma \in O(\tau)$. The same computation shows that a point of $O(\tau)$, viewed in $U_\sigma$, has $F = S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$. So $U_\sigma$ is the disjoint union of the $O(\tau)$, $\tau \preceq \sigma$, and the face $\tau$ attached to a point does not depend on the chart $U_\sigma$ containing it, since for $\sigma, \sigma'$ both charts contain the point in $U_{\sigma \cap \sigma'}$, whose decomposition is the restriction of both. As $X_\Sigma = \bigcup U_\sigma$, it is the disjoint union of all the $O(\tau)$, which are orbits, so they are all the orbits.

(3) Let $Z_\tau = \bigsqcup_{\tau \preceq \sigma} O(\sigma)$. Its complement is the union of the $O(\sigma')$ with $\tau \not\preceq \sigma'$, which by (2) is $\bigcup_{\tau \not\preceq \sigma'} U_{\sigma'}$, since every face of such a $\sigma'$ also fails to contain $\tau$, and $O(\sigma') \subseteq U_{\sigma'}$. This union is open, so $Z_\tau$ is closed, and $V(\tau) \subseteq Z_\tau$. Conversely, for $\tau \preceq \sigma$, Proposition P8.6.26 (1), applied to $u = u_\sigma$, which lies in no proper face of $\sigma$ by Lemma P8.6.23, shows that $\gamma_\sigma$ is the image of $0$ under a morphism $\mathbb{A}^1 \to X_\Sigma$ that sends $\mathbb{G}_m$ into $O(\tau)$. The preimage of $V(\tau)$ is closed and contains $\mathbb{G}_m$, so it is $\mathbb{A}^1$, and $\gamma_\sigma \in V(\tau)$. Since $V(\tau)$ is stable under $T_N$, $O(\sigma) \subseteq V(\tau)$. So $V(\tau) = Z_\tau$, irreducible as the closure of the irreducible $O(\tau)$, and of dimension $\dim O(\tau) = n - \dim \tau$, because $O(\tau) = V(\tau) \cap U_\tau$ is a nonempty open subset of the irreducible space $V(\tau)$.

*Proposition P8.6.26.* (1) Write $\gamma = \gamma_\tau$ as a point of $U_\sigma$, so $\gamma(m) = 1$ for $m \in S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$ and $0$ otherwise. The morphism $t \mapsto \lambda^u(t) \cdot \gamma$ from $\mathbb{G}_m$ to $U_\sigma$ is given on rings by $\chi^m \mapsto \gamma(m) t^{\langle m, u \rangle}$ for $m \in S_\sigma$. Since $u \in \sigma$, all exponents are $\geq 0$, so it is a homomorphism $k[S_\sigma] \to k[t]$ and the morphism extends to $\mathbb{A}^1 \to U_\sigma$. At $t = 0$ the value is $m \mapsto \gamma(m)$ if $\langle m, u \rangle = 0$ and $0$ otherwise. For $m \in S_\sigma$, $\langle m, u \rangle = 0$ means that $\sigma \cap m^\perp$ is a face containing $u$, which is $\sigma$, so $m \in \sigma^\perp$. As $\sigma^\perp \subseteq \tau^\perp$, the value at $0$ is the map that is $1$ on $S_\sigma \cap \sigma^\perp$ and $0$ elsewhere, which is $\gamma_\sigma$. The case $\tau = \{0\}$, with $\gamma_{\{0\}} = 1$, is the first claim.

(2) Suppose $\lambda^u$ extends to $f \colon \mathbb{A}^1 \to X_\Sigma$. Then $f(0) \in U_\sigma$ for some $\sigma \in \Sigma$, and $f^{-1}(U_\sigma)$ is an open neighbourhood of $0$, so it contains $D(g) \ni 0$ for some $g \in k[t]$ with $g(0) \neq 0$. On rings, $f$ is $k[S_\sigma] \to k[t]_g$, $\chi^m \mapsto t^{\langle m, u \rangle}$, and $t^a \in k[t]_g$ forces $a \geq 0$. So $\langle m, u \rangle \geq 0$ for all $m \in S_\sigma$, and $u \in (\sigma^\vee)^\vee = \sigma$ by Theorem P8.6.3 (3), contradicting $u \notin |\Sigma|$.

*Theorem P8.6.27.* If $\sigma$ is smooth of dimension $r$, its ray generators extend to a basis of $N$, and Example P8.6.17 gives $U_\sigma \cong \mathbb{A}^r \times \mathbb{G}_m^{n - r}$, which is smooth. Conversely, assume $U_\sigma$ smooth. By Lemma P8.6.24, $U_\sigma \cong U_{\sigma_0} \times \mathbb{G}_m^{n - d}$, so $U_{\sigma_0}$ is smooth, since the local ring of $U_\sigma$ at a point $(x, 1)$ is regular and $U_{\sigma_0} \times \{1\}$ is cut out near it by the $n - d$ functions $t_i - 1$, which are part of a regular system of parameters, and $\sigma$ is smooth if $\sigma_0$ is. So assume $\dim \sigma = n$. Then $\sigma^\perp = 0$, and $\gamma_\sigma$ is the point with $\gamma_\sigma(m) = 0$ for all $m \neq 0$, whose maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ is spanned by the $\chi^m$ with $m \in S_\sigma \setminus \{0\}$. The ideal $\mathfrak{m}^2$ is spanned by the $\chi^{m + m'}$ with $m, m' \neq 0$, that is, by the $\chi^m$ with $m$ reducible. So the classes of $\chi^h$ for $h$ in the Hilbert basis $\mathcal{H}$ of Proposition P8.6.6 form a basis of $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$, which proves the last claim. The local ring at $\gamma_\sigma$ is regular of dimension $n$, so $|\mathcal{H}| = n$. By Theorem P8.6.5, $\mathcal{H}$ generates the group $M$ of rank $n$, so $\mathcal{H}$ is a basis of $M$, $S_\sigma = \sum_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} h$, and $\sigma^\vee = \operatorname{Cone}(\mathcal{H})$. Then $\sigma = (\sigma^\vee)^\vee$ is generated by the dual basis of $N$, which is smooth. For $X_\Sigma$, smoothness is local, $X_\Sigma$ is covered by the $U_\sigma$, and the faces of a smooth cone are smooth.

*Theorem P8.6.28.* Suppose $X_\Sigma$ proper over $k$, and let $u \in N$. Let $R = k[t]_{(t)}$, a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K = k(t)$. The morphism $\operatorname{Spec} K \to \mathbb{G}_m \xrightarrow{\lambda^u} X_\Sigma$ and $\operatorname{Spec} R \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ form a valuative diagram, and since $X_\Sigma \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is universally closed, it has a lift $h \colon \operatorname{Spec} R \to X_\Sigma$ by Theorem P3.4.32. The closed point of $\operatorname{Spec} R$ maps into some $U_\sigma$, and an open subset of $\operatorname{Spec} R$ containing the closed point is all of $\operatorname{Spec} R$, so $h$ factors through $U_\sigma$ and is given by $k[S_\sigma] \to R$, $\chi^m \mapsto t^{\langle m, u \rangle}$. As in the proof of Proposition P8.6.26, $u \in \sigma$. So $N \subseteq |\Sigma|$. The support is a union of cones, so it contains $N_\mathbb{Q}$, and it is closed by Theorem P8.6.3 (1), so it is $N_\mathbb{R}$.

Conversely, let $\Sigma$ be complete. By Theorem P8.6.15, $X_\Sigma$ is separated and of finite type over $k$. For a morphism of finite type between Noetherian schemes, it suffices to check the existence of lifts in the valuative criterion for discrete valuation rings (Grothendieck, EGA II, Théorème 7.3.8; Hartshorne, Exercise II.4.11). So let $R \supseteq k$ be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$ and valuation $v \colon K^* \to \mathbb{Z}$, and let $a \colon \operatorname{Spec} K \to X_\Sigma$. Then $a$ factors through some $U_\sigma$ and is given by $\psi \colon k[S_\sigma] \to K$. The set $F = \{ m \in S_\sigma : \psi(\chi^m) \neq 0 \}$ is a face of $S_\sigma$, so $F = S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp$ for a face $\tau \preceq \sigma$ by Lemma P8.6.23. By part (1) of that lemma, $\psi$ restricted to $F$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism $\tilde\psi \colon \tau^\perp \cap M \to K^*$, and $v \circ \tilde\psi \colon \tau^\perp \cap M \to \mathbb{Z}$ extends to a homomorphism $M \to \mathbb{Z}$, since $\tau^\perp \cap M$ is a direct summand. This is an element $\tilde u \in N$ with $\langle m, \tilde u \rangle = v(\tilde\psi(m))$ for $m \in \tau^\perp \cap M$.

The cones of $\Sigma$ not containing $u_\tau$ are closed and miss $u_\tau$, and the cones of $\Sigma$ cover $N_\mathbb{R}$, so a neighbourhood of $u_\tau$ is covered by the cones containing $u_\tau$. For a large integer $c$, $u_\tau + \tilde u / c$ is in this neighbourhood, so $\tilde u + c u_\tau \in \sigma'$ for a cone $\sigma' \in \Sigma$ containing $u_\tau$, and $\tau \preceq \sigma'$ by Lemma P8.6.23 (3). Define $\varphi \colon k[S_{\sigma'}] \to K$ by $\varphi(\chi^m) = \tilde\psi(m)$ for $m \in S_{\sigma'} \cap \tau^\perp$ and $\varphi(\chi^m) = 0$ otherwise. This is a ring homomorphism, because $S_{\sigma'} \cap \tau^\perp$ is a face of $S_{\sigma'}$. On the common open subset $U_\tau$ of $U_\sigma$ and $U_{\sigma'}$, both $\psi$ and $\varphi$ induce the homomorphism $k[S_\tau] \to K$ that is $\tilde\psi$ on $\tau^\perp \cap M$ and $0$ on $S_\tau \setminus \tau^\perp$, so $\varphi$ defines the same point $a$. For $m \in S_{\sigma'} \cap \tau^\perp$,
$$v(\varphi(\chi^m)) = \langle m, \tilde u \rangle = \langle m, \tilde u + c u_\tau \rangle \geq 0,$$
since $m$ vanishes on $u_\tau \in \tau$ and is nonnegative on $\sigma' \ni \tilde u + c u_\tau$. So $\varphi$ takes values in $R$, and defines a lift $\operatorname{Spec} R \to U_{\sigma'} \subseteq X_\Sigma$ of $a$. Hence $X_\Sigma$ is universally closed, and proper.

3. (B) Show that the distinguished point $\gamma_\sigma$ of a full-dimensional cone is the unique fixed point of $T_N$ on $U_\sigma$, and that $X_\Sigma$ has a fixed point of $T_N$ if and only if $\Sigma$ has a cone of dimension $n$.
4. (B) Show that $V(\tau)$ is itself a toric variety, whose fan is the set of images in $(N / N_\tau)_\mathbb{R}$ of the cones of $\Sigma$ containing $\tau$, where $N_\tau = N \cap \operatorname{span}(\tau)$. Compute this fan for $\tau$ a ray of $\mathbb{P}^2$.
5. (B) Show that the $2 \times 2$ minors of Example P8.6.30 generate the kernel of $k[z_0, \dots, z_n] \to k[S_{\sigma_n}]$, by showing that every monomial is congruent modulo the minors to one of the form $z_0^a z_j z_n^b$ or $z_0^a z_n^b$, and that these monomials have distinct images.
6. (C) Let $\Sigma$ be a fan whose support is convex. Show that $X_\Sigma \to U_{|\Sigma|}$, where $|\Sigma|$ is regarded as a cone, is proper if $|\Sigma|$ is a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone, by adapting the proof of Theorem P8.6.28 to the relative valuative criterion.

### Lean

The completeness of a fan is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsComplete`, and the lemma `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.exists_mem_of_isComplete` states that every point of $N_\mathbb{R}$ lies in a cone of a complete fan. The repository has no toric variety as a scheme, so the orbit-cone correspondence and Theorems P8.6.27 and P8.6.28 are not in Lean.

### Notes and history

The orbit-cone correspondence is due to Demazure in the smooth case and to Kempf, Knudsen, Mumford and Saint-Donat in general; Fulton, Section 3.1, and Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 3.2.6, are the standard references, and the literature state is theorem. The use of one-parameter subgroups and their limits goes back to the Hilbert-Mumford criterion of geometric invariant theory. The proof of Theorem P8.6.28 given here follows Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 3.4.6, and uses the form of the valuative criterion with discrete valuation rings; the form with arbitrary valuation rings, Theorem P3.4.32, gives the converse directly but needs a homomorphism from a lattice to an ordered group of higher rank to be pushed into a cone, which is the same argument with more bookkeeping.
