## Affine toric varieties and the toric variety of a fan

### Statement

In this section $k$ is a field, $N$ and $M$ are dual lattices of rank $n$ as in Section 6.1, and all cones are strongly convex rational polyhedral cones in $N_\mathbb{R}$. For a submonoid $S \subseteq M$, write $k[S]$ for the $k$-algebra with basis the symbols $\chi^m$, $m \in S$, and multiplication $\chi^m \chi^{m'} = \chi^{m + m'}$. So $k[M]$ is the ring of Laurent polynomials in $\chi^{e_1^*}, \dots, \chi^{e_n^*}$ for a basis $e_i^*$ of $M$, and $k[S]$ is the subring spanned by the monomials $\chi^m$ with $m \in S$. An element $f = \sum c_m \chi^m$ of $k[M]$ lies in $k[S]$ exactly when $c_m = 0$ for $m \notin S$.

**Definition P8.6.12 (tori and toric varieties).**

1. The *torus* of $N$ is $T_N = \operatorname{Spec} k[M]$, a group scheme with multiplication given by $\chi^m \mapsto \chi^m \otimes \chi^m$. A basis of $M$ identifies $T_N$ with $\mathbb{G}_m^n$, and $T_N(k) = \operatorname{Hom}(M, k^*) = N \otimes_\mathbb{Z} k^*$.
2. A *character* is a homomorphism $\chi^m \colon T_N \to \mathbb{G}_m$, given on rings by $t \mapsto \chi^m$, for $m \in M$. A *one-parameter subgroup* is a homomorphism $\lambda^u \colon \mathbb{G}_m \to T_N$, $u \in N$, given on rings by $\chi^m \mapsto t^{\langle m, u \rangle}$. So $\chi^m \circ \lambda^u$ is $t \mapsto t^{\langle m, u \rangle}$.
3. A *toric variety* is a normal variety $X$ over $k$ with an open immersion $T_N \hookrightarrow X$ with dense image and an action $T_N \times X \to X$ that restricts on $T_N \times T_N$ to the multiplication. A *toric morphism* is a morphism of toric varieties that restricts to a homomorphism of the tori and is equivariant for it.

**Proposition P8.6.13 (the affine toric variety of a cone).** Let $\sigma$ be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone, and let $U_\sigma = \operatorname{Spec} k[S_\sigma]$.

1. $k[S_\sigma]$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra and a domain with fraction field $k(M) = \operatorname{Frac} k[M]$, and $U_\sigma$ is an affine variety of dimension $n$.
2. $k[S_\sigma]$ is normal.
3. Every face $\tau \preceq \sigma$ is $\sigma \cap m^\perp$ for some $m \in S_\sigma$. For such $m$, $S_\tau = S_\sigma + \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(-m)$ and $k[S_\tau] = k[S_\sigma]_{\chi^m}$, so $U_\tau$ is the principal open subset $D(\chi^m)$ of $U_\sigma$.
4. In particular $T_N = U_{\{0\}}$ is a dense principal open subset of $U_\sigma$. The map $k[S_\sigma] \to k[M] \otimes_k k[S_\sigma]$, $\chi^m \mapsto \chi^m \otimes \chi^m$, defines an action of $T_N$ on $U_\sigma$ that extends the multiplication of $T_N$. So $U_\sigma$ is an affine toric variety.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P8.6.14 (fans).** A *fan* in $N_\mathbb{R}$ is a finite set $\Sigma$ of strongly convex rational polyhedral cones such that every face of a cone of $\Sigma$ lies in $\Sigma$, and the intersection of two cones of $\Sigma$ is a face of each of them. The *support* of $\Sigma$ is $|\Sigma| = \bigcup_{\sigma \in \Sigma} \sigma$. Write $\Sigma(r)$ for the set of cones of dimension $r$, so $\Sigma(1)$ is the set of rays, with minimal generators $u_\rho$ for $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$. The fan is *complete* if $|\Sigma| = N_\mathbb{R}$, and *smooth* or *simplicial* if every cone of $\Sigma$ is. For one cone $\sigma$, the set of its faces is a fan, again written $\sigma$.

Lean: `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan` is this definition, with `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.support`, `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsComplete` and `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsSimplicialFan`; `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.ofCone` is the fan of the faces of one cone.

**Theorem P8.6.15 (the toric variety of a fan).** Let $\Sigma$ be a fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$.

1. For $\sigma, \sigma' \in \Sigma$ with $\tau = \sigma \cap \sigma'$, $S_\tau = S_\sigma + S_{\sigma'}$. The open subsets $U_\tau \subseteq U_\sigma$ and $U_\tau \subseteq U_{\sigma'}$ of Proposition P8.6.13, part 3, are principal, and the map $k[S_\sigma] \otimes_k k[S_{\sigma'}] \to k[S_\tau]$ is surjective.
2. The affine varieties $U_\sigma$, $\sigma \in \Sigma$, glue along the open subsets $U_{\sigma \cap \sigma'}$, by the identity maps of $\operatorname{Spec} k[S_{\sigma \cap \sigma'}]$, to a scheme $X_\Sigma$ of finite type over $k$. It is a separated normal variety of dimension $n$, each $U_\sigma$ is an open subset of $X_\Sigma$ with $U_\sigma \cap U_{\sigma'} = U_{\sigma \cap \sigma'}$, and $X_\Sigma$ is covered by the $U_\sigma$ with $\sigma$ maximal in $\Sigma$.
3. The actions of $T_N$ on the $U_\sigma$ glue to an action on $X_\Sigma$, and $T_N = U_{\{0\}} \subseteq X_\Sigma$ is a dense open subset. So $X_\Sigma$ is a toric variety.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Remark P8.6.16 (all toric varieties come from fans).** Conversely, every separated toric variety in the sense of Definition P8.6.12 is $X_\Sigma$ for a unique fan $\Sigma$ in $N_\mathbb{R}$, up to an isomorphism that is the identity on $T_N$. The key input is Sumihiro's theorem that a normal variety with a torus action is covered by invariant affine open subsets; see Cox, Little and Schenck, Corollary 3.1.8, and Kempf, Knudsen, Mumford and Saint-Donat (KKMS73), Chapter I. This volume uses only the direction proved here, and works with $X_\Sigma$ throughout.

### Proof in words

The ring $k[S_\sigma]$ is a ring of monomials inside the Laurent polynomial ring, so it is a domain, and Gordan's lemma makes it finitely generated with the full fraction field. Normality comes from writing $S_\sigma$ as the intersection over the rays $\rho$ of $\sigma$ of the half-spaces $\{ \langle m, u_\rho \rangle \geq 0 \}$: each half-space gives a ring $k[x_1, x_2^{\pm 1}, \dots, x_n^{\pm 1}]$, which is factorial, and an intersection of normal rings with the same fraction field is normal. A face $\tau = \sigma \cap m^\perp$ allows the monomial $\chi^{-m}$ and nothing more, which is the localization at $\chi^m$. The separation lemma of Section 6.1 then shows that the intersection of two cones of a fan is cut out in each of them by one monomial and its inverse. This makes the gluing an identification of principal open subsets, and it makes the ring of $U_{\sigma \cap \sigma'}$ generated by the rings of $U_\sigma$ and $U_{\sigma'}$, which is the separatedness criterion for an affine cover.

### Examples

**Example P8.6.17 (affine space and the torus).** For the positive orthant $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}(e_1, \dots, e_n)$, Example P8.6.8 gives $S_\sigma = \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^n$, so $U_\sigma = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_1, \dots, x_n] = \mathbb{A}^n$ with $x_i = \chi^{e_i^*}$. More generally, for $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}(e_1, \dots, e_r)$,
$$U_\sigma = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_1, \dots, x_r, x_{r+1}^{\pm 1}, \dots, x_n^{\pm 1}] = \mathbb{A}^r \times \mathbb{G}_m^{n - r}.$$
The face $\operatorname{Cone}(e_i : i \in I)$ is $\sigma \cap m^\perp$ for $m = \sum_{i \leq r, i \notin I} e_i^*$, and its open subset is $D(\prod_{i \notin I} x_i)$, where the coordinates $x_i$, $i \notin I$, become units. For $\sigma = \{0\}$, $U_\sigma = T_N$.

**Example P8.6.18 (the projective line and projective space).** For $N = \mathbb{Z}$, the fan $\{ \{0\}, \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}, \mathbb{R}_{\leq 0} \}$ gives $U_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}} = \operatorname{Spec} k[x]$ and $U_{\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}} = \operatorname{Spec} k[x^{-1}]$, glued along $\operatorname{Spec} k[x^{\pm 1}]$. This is $\mathbb{P}^1$. In general, let $e_1, \dots, e_n$ be a basis of $N$, put $e_0 = -e_1 - \dots - e_n$, and let $\Sigma$ consist of the cones generated by the proper subsets of $\{ e_0, \dots, e_n \}$. Any $n$ of the $e_i$ form a basis of $N$, so all cones are smooth, and two cones meet in the cone of the common generators, which is a face of both. Let $\sigma_i = \operatorname{Cone}(e_j : j \neq i)$. For $i \geq 1$, the forms $e_j^* - e_i^*$ ($j \geq 1$, $j \neq i$) and $-e_i^*$ are the basis of $M$ dual to the basis $e_j$ ($j \geq 1$, $j \neq i$), $e_0$ of $N$, since $\langle e_j^* - e_i^*, e_0 \rangle = -1 + 1 = 0$ and $\langle -e_i^*, e_0 \rangle = 1$. With $\chi^{e_j^*} = x_j / x_0$, this gives
$$U_{\sigma_i} = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i] = D_+(x_i) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n,$$
also for $i = 0$, and the gluings of Theorem P8.6.15 are those of $\mathbb{P}^n$, because both are the identity on subrings of the function field $k(x_1/x_0, \dots, x_n/x_0) = k(M)$. So $X_\Sigma = \mathbb{P}^n$, and the fan is complete, as every $u = \sum a_i e_i$ lies in the cone $\sigma_j$ for an index $j \geq 1$ minimizing $a_j$, or in $\sigma_0$ if all $a_i \geq 0$: indeed $u = \sum_{i \neq j} (a_i - a_j) e_i + (-a_j) e_0$ when $a_j < 0$.

**Example P8.6.19 (products).** If $\Sigma \subseteq N_\mathbb{R}$ and $\Sigma' \subseteq N'_\mathbb{R}$ are fans, the cones $\sigma \times \sigma'$ form a fan $\Sigma \times \Sigma'$ in $(N \oplus N')_\mathbb{R}$, because the faces of $\sigma \times \sigma'$ are the products of faces. Since $(\sigma \times \sigma')^\vee = \sigma^\vee \times \sigma'^\vee$, $k[S_{\sigma \times \sigma'}] = k[S_\sigma] \otimes_k k[S_{\sigma'}]$, so $U_{\sigma \times \sigma'} = U_\sigma \times U_{\sigma'}$, and the gluings are compatible, so $X_{\Sigma \times \Sigma'} = X_\Sigma \times_k X_{\Sigma'}$. For two copies of the fan of $\mathbb{P}^1$, the fan of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ has the rays $\pm e_1, \pm e_2$ and the four quadrants as maximal cones.

**Example P8.6.20 (the Hirzebruch surfaces).** Let $n \geq 0$, $N = \mathbb{Z}^2$, and
$$u_1 = (1, 0), \quad u_2 = (0, 1), \quad u_3 = (-1, n), \quad u_4 = (0, -1),$$
in counterclockwise order. The fan $\Sigma_n$ has the four rays and the maximal cones $\sigma_{12} = \operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2)$, $\sigma_{23}$, $\sigma_{34}$ and $\sigma_{41}$, defined in the same way. Each pair of adjacent generators has determinant $1$, so the fan is smooth, and consecutive generators make angles less than $\pi$, so the cones cover $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the fan is complete. Write $x = \chi^{(1, 0)}$ and $y = \chi^{(0, 1)}$. The dual bases give
$$U_{\sigma_{12}} = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y], \quad U_{\sigma_{41}} = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y^{-1}], \quad U_{\sigma_{23}} = \operatorname{Spec} k[x^{-1}, x^n y], \quad U_{\sigma_{34}} = \operatorname{Spec} k[x^{-1}, x^{-n} y^{-1}].$$
For instance, $(n, 1)$ and $(-1, 0)$ are the basis of $M$ dual to $u_2, u_3$, because $\langle (n, 1), u_3 \rangle = -n + n = 0$. Compare with the Hirzebruch surface $F_n$ of Example P3.6.37 and Definition P3.11.24, with coordinates $t$ on $U_0 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^1$, $s = 1/t$, and fibre coordinates $r = b/a$, $w = 1/r$, $r' = t^n r$, $w' = 1/r'$. Setting $t = x$ and $r = y$, the four charts are
$$\operatorname{Spec} k[r, t], \quad \operatorname{Spec} k[w, t], \quad \operatorname{Spec} k[s, r'], \quad \operatorname{Spec} k[s, w'],$$
which are the four planes $V_0 \setminus C_+$, $V_0 \setminus C_-$, $V_1 \setminus C_+$ and $V_1 \setminus C_-$ of Theorem P3.11.25. All the gluings are identities on subrings of $k(x, y) = k(t, r)$, so $X_{\Sigma_n} \cong F_n$. The projection $N \to \mathbb{Z}$, $(a, b) \mapsto a$, sends $\sigma_{12}$ and $\sigma_{41}$ into $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ and $\sigma_{23}, \sigma_{34}$ into $\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}$, and on rings it is $t \mapsto x$, which is the bundle map $\pi \colon F_n \to \mathbb{P}^1$. For $n = 0$ this is the fan of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$.

**Example P8.6.21 (why the fan condition is needed).** The two cones $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_2)$ and $\sigma' = \operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_1 + e_2)$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ meet in $\sigma'$, which is not a face of $\sigma$, so they are not cones of one fan. Gluing $U_\sigma$ and $U_{\sigma'}$ along the open subsets $U_{\{0\}} = T_N$ alone gives a scheme in which the two copies of $T_N$ are identified but the two copies of $U_{\operatorname{Cone}(e_1)}$ are not, a non-separated scheme in the same way as the line with two origins. The fan condition guarantees that whenever two cones share a face, the corresponding open subsets are identified, and Lemma P8.6.7 guarantees that the identification is along a closed diagonal.

### Proof

*Proposition P8.6.13.* (1) By Theorem P8.6.5, $S_\sigma$ is finitely generated, so $k[S_\sigma]$ is generated by finitely many monomials. It is a subring of the domain $k[M]$. Since $S_\sigma$ generates $M$ as a group, every $\chi^m$ is a quotient of two elements of $k[S_\sigma]$, so $\operatorname{Frac} k[S_\sigma] = \operatorname{Frac} k[M]$, which is purely transcendental of degree $n$. By Theorem P1.4.31, $\dim k[S_\sigma] = n$.

(2) By Proposition P8.6.4, $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}(u_\rho : \rho \preceq \sigma \text{ a ray})$, so $\sigma^\vee = \bigcap_\rho \rho^\vee$ and $S_\sigma = \bigcap_\rho S_\rho$. By the description of $k[S]$ inside $k[M]$, $k[S_\sigma] = \bigcap_\rho k[S_\rho]$ inside $k[M]$. If $\sigma = \{0\}$, then $k[S_\sigma] = k[M]$, a localization of a polynomial ring, and it is factorial. Otherwise, a primitive vector $u_\rho$ extends to a basis $u_\rho = f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ of $N$, and with the dual basis $f_i^*$, $S_\rho = \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} f_1^* + \sum_{i \geq 2} \mathbb{Z} f_i^*$ by Example P8.6.8, so $k[S_\rho] = k[x_1, x_2^{\pm 1}, \dots, x_n^{\pm 1}]$ is factorial. Factorial rings are normal by Proposition P1.4.19. If $z \in k(M)$ is integral over $k[S_\sigma]$, it is integral over each $k[S_\rho]$, all of which have fraction field $k(M)$, so $z$ lies in each of them, and in their intersection.

(3) Let $\sigma^\vee = \operatorname{Cone}(m_1, \dots, m_s)$ with $m_j \in M$, and let $\tau = \sigma \cap m'^\perp$ with $m' = \sum \lambda_j m_j$, $\lambda_j \geq 0$. Then $\langle m', u \rangle = 0$ for $u \in \sigma$ exactly when $\langle m_j, u \rangle = 0$ for all $j$ with $\lambda_j > 0$, so $\tau = \sigma \cap m^\perp$ with $m = \sum_{\lambda_j > 0} m_j \in S_\sigma$. Now $-m$ vanishes on $\tau$, so $-m \in S_\tau$, and $S_\sigma \subseteq S_\tau$ since $\tau \subseteq \sigma$; hence $S_\sigma + \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(-m) \subseteq S_\tau$. Conversely let $m'' \in S_\tau$, and let $S \subseteq N$ be a finite set of generators of $\sigma$. For $s \in S$ with $\langle m, s \rangle = 0$, $s \in \tau$ and $\langle m'' + cm, s \rangle = \langle m'', s \rangle \geq 0$ for all $c$. For $s \in S$ with $\langle m, s \rangle > 0$, $\langle m'' + cm, s \rangle \geq 0$ for $c$ large. So $m'' + cm \in S_\sigma$ for some integer $c \geq 0$, and $m'' = (m'' + cm) + c(-m)$. On rings, $k[S_\tau] = k[S_\sigma][\chi^{-m}] = k[S_\sigma]_{\chi^m}$.

(4) Since $\sigma$ is strongly convex, $\{0\}$ is a face (Proposition P8.6.4), so by (3) $k[M] = k[S_{\{0\}}] = k[S_\sigma]_{\chi^m}$ for some $m \in S_\sigma$, and $T_N = D(\chi^m)$ is a nonempty open subset of the irreducible space $U_\sigma$, hence dense. The map $\chi^m \mapsto \chi^m \otimes \chi^m$ sends $k[S_\sigma]$ into $k[M] \otimes_k k[S_\sigma]$, it is a homomorphism of $k$-algebras, and the identities for an action (associativity and the unit) hold on each monomial because they hold for the group law of $T_N$. After inverting $\chi^m$ it becomes the comultiplication of $k[M]$, so the action extends the multiplication of $T_N$. Normality was proved in (2).

*Theorem P8.6.15.* (1) By Lemma P8.6.7 there is $m \in \sigma^\vee \cap (-\sigma')^\vee \cap M$ with $\tau = \sigma \cap m^\perp = \sigma' \cap (-m)^\perp$. By Proposition P8.6.13 (3) applied to $\sigma$ and $m$, $S_\tau = S_\sigma + \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(-m)$, and $-m \in S_{\sigma'}$, so $S_\tau \subseteq S_\sigma + S_{\sigma'}$. The reverse inclusion holds because $S_\sigma, S_{\sigma'} \subseteq S_\tau$. So $U_\tau = D(\chi^m) \subseteq U_\sigma$ and $U_\tau = D(\chi^{-m}) \subseteq U_{\sigma'}$, and every monomial of $k[S_\tau]$ is a product of a monomial of $k[S_\sigma]$ and one of $k[S_{\sigma'}]$, which is the surjectivity.

(2) For $\sigma, \sigma' \in \Sigma$, let $U_{\sigma \sigma'} = U_{\sigma \cap \sigma'} \subseteq U_\sigma$, an open subset by (1), and let $\varphi_{\sigma \sigma'} \colon U_{\sigma \sigma'} \to U_{\sigma' \sigma}$ be the identity of $\operatorname{Spec} k[S_{\sigma \cap \sigma'}]$. For three cones, $U_{\sigma \sigma'} \cap U_{\sigma \sigma''}$ is the intersection inside $U_\sigma$ of the open subsets of the faces $\sigma \cap \sigma'$ and $\sigma \cap \sigma''$. If these faces are $\sigma \cap m'^\perp$ and $\sigma \cap m''^\perp$ with $m', m'' \in S_\sigma$, the intersection is $D(\chi^{m'}) \cap D(\chi^{m''}) = D(\chi^{m' + m''})$, which is the open subset of $\sigma \cap (m' + m'')^\perp = \sigma \cap \sigma' \cap \sigma''$. So all the triple intersections are $U_{\sigma \cap \sigma' \cap \sigma''}$, all the maps are identities of affine schemes whose rings are subrings of $k(M)$, and the cocycle conditions hold. Theorem P3.2.3 gives the scheme $X_\Sigma$, with the $U_\sigma$ as open subschemes and $U_\sigma \cap U_{\sigma'} = U_{\sigma \cap \sigma'}$. It is of finite type, being covered by finitely many affine varieties. It is separated over $k$ by Proposition P3.4.3, parts 2 and 3, since the intersections of the cover are affine and (1) gives the surjectivity. All the $U_\sigma$ contain the same dense open subset $T_N$, so $X_\Sigma$ is irreducible, and it is reduced, normal and of dimension $n$ because each $U_\sigma$ is, by Proposition P8.6.13. Every cone of $\Sigma$ is a face of a maximal one, and a face gives an open subset, so the maximal cones suffice.

(3) For a face $\tau = \sigma \cap m^\perp$ of $\sigma$, the action map of $U_\sigma$ sends $\chi^{-m}$ to $\chi^{-m} \otimes \chi^{-m}$, so it is compatible with the localization at $\chi^m$ and restricts to the action of $U_\tau$. Hence the actions on the $U_\sigma$ agree on the overlaps and glue to a morphism $T_N \times X_\Sigma \to X_\Sigma$, and the identities for an action hold because they hold on each $T_N \times U_\sigma$. The subset $T_N = U_{\{0\}}$ is open, dense since $X_\Sigma$ is irreducible, and the action restricts to the multiplication on it by Proposition P8.6.13 (4).

6. (C) Show that $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n) = \operatorname{Proj} k[x_0, x_1, x_2]$ with degrees $1, 1, n$ is the toric variety of the fan in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with rays $(1, 0)$, $(-1, n)$ and $(0, -1)$ and the three two-dimensional cones they span in pairs, and that its chart at the point $(0 : 0 : 1)$ is $U_\sigma$ for $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}((1, 0), (-1, n))$, which is isomorphic to the cone of Example P8.6.9 with $q = 1$.

### Lean

The structure `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan` records a fan as a finite set of strongly convex rational polyhedral cones closed under faces, in which two cones meet in a face of each; `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.ofCone` is the fan of the faces of one cone and `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.affine` is the fan of $\mathbb{A}^n$, and the examples `MinimalModel.Toric.P1.fan` and `MinimalModel.Toric.P2.fan` are the fans of Example P8.6.18 for $n = 1, 2$, with completeness proved in `MinimalModel.Toric.P1.isComplete` and `MinimalModel.Toric.P2.isComplete`. The repository has no scheme $X_\Sigma$: the semigroup ring $k[S_\sigma]$, the gluing and Theorem P8.6.15 are not in Lean.

### Notes and history

Toric varieties were introduced in the early 1970s by Demazure (1970), who constructed smooth complete toric varieties from fans in his study of the Cremona group, and by Kempf, Knudsen, Mumford and Saint-Donat (KKMS73), who treated toroidal embeddings and normal affine toric varieties as $\operatorname{Spec} k[S_\sigma]$. Miyake and Oda developed the theory of fans, and Oda's book (Oda88) and Danilov's survey (Dan78) are the classical references. The literature state of all results in this section is theorem. Fulton, Chapter 1, and Cox, Little and Schenck, Chapters 1 and 3, give the same construction; Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 3.1.5, is Theorem P8.6.15. The identification of the Hirzebruch surface in Example P8.6.20 makes the computations of Chapters 7 and 8 checkable against those of P3 and P5.
