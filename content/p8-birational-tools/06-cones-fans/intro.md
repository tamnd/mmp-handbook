# Cones, fans and toric varieties

A toric variety is a normal variety that contains a torus $T \cong \mathbb{G}_m^n$ as a dense open subset, with an action of $T$ on the whole variety that extends the multiplication of $T$. The theory of toric varieties translates the geometry of such a variety into the combinatorics of a fan, a finite collection of rational polyhedral cones in a lattice of rank $n$ that meet along common faces. The translation is exact enough that properness, smoothness, divisors, line bundles, cones of curves and birational maps all become statements about cones, and it is this exactness that makes toric varieties the standard test case of the minimal model program in Volume 3. This chapter builds the dictionary from the start.

Section 6.1 treats rational polyhedral cones. It proves the duality theorem $(\sigma^\vee)^\vee = \sigma$, the theorem of Weyl and Minkowski that finitely generated cones are the same as finite intersections of half-spaces, the order-reversing bijection between the faces of a cone and the faces of its dual, and Gordan's lemma that the lattice points of a rational polyhedral cone form a finitely generated monoid. It ends with the separation lemma for two cones that meet along a common face, which is the combinatorial input for the gluing of the next section.

Section 6.2 defines the affine toric variety $U_\sigma = \operatorname{Spec} k[\sigma^\vee \cap M]$ of a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone, proves that it is a normal affine variety containing the torus as a principal open set, and shows that a face $\tau$ of $\sigma$ gives a principal open subset $U_\tau \subseteq U_\sigma$. A fan is a collection of such cones closed under faces and intersections, and the affine pieces glue along these open subsets to a separated normal variety $X_\Sigma$ with an action of the torus. The examples are affine space, the projective spaces, $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and the Hirzebruch surfaces.

Section 6.3 describes the points of $U_\sigma$ as monoid homomorphisms, proves the orbit-cone correspondence between the cones of $\Sigma$ and the torus orbits of $X_\Sigma$, with the closure of the orbit of $\tau$ being the union of the orbits of the cones containing $\tau$, and computes the orbits as limits of one-parameter subgroups. It proves that $X_\Sigma$ is smooth exactly when every cone is generated by part of a basis of the lattice, and complete exactly when the cones of $\Sigma$ cover the whole space. The examples are the quadric cone, the cyclic quotient singularity $\frac{1}{n}(1, 1)$ and the cone over a square.

The chapter follows Cox, Little and Schenck, Chapters 1 to 3 (CLS11), and Fulton, Chapters 1 and 2 (Ful93), with Oda's *Convex bodies and algebraic geometry* (Oda88) for the convex geometry.
