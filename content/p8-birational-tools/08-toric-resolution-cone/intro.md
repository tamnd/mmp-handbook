# Toric resolution and the toric cone

A toric morphism between toric varieties with the same torus is the same thing as a refinement of fans, and such a morphism is proper and birational. This makes resolution of singularities, for toric varieties, a matter of subdividing cones until every cone is generated by part of a basis. The curves fixed by the torus are equally explicit: each wall between two maximal cones gives a curve isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, and its intersection numbers with the invariant divisors are the coefficients of a linear relation among ray generators. This chapter proves these facts and ends with the toric cone theorem, which is the starting point of the toric minimal model program of Volume 3.

Section 8.1 proves that a refinement of fans induces a proper birational toric morphism, resolves the cyclic quotient singularities of surfaces by the Hirzebruch-Jung continued fraction, with $\frac{1}{5}(1, 2)$ and $\frac{5}{2} = [3, 2]$ as the worked case, and proves the multiplicity reduction that resolves every simplicial toric variety by star subdivisions. The Hirzebruch surface $F_n$ appears as the resolution of the weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$.

Section 8.2 proves that the closure of the orbit of a wall is a copy of $\mathbb{P}^1$, computes the degree of a Cartier divisor on it from the Cartier data of the two adjacent cones, and deduces the wall relation $\sum_\rho (D_\rho \cdot V(\tau)) u_\rho = 0$. In the smooth case the relation is $u_n + u_{n+1} = \sum b_i u_i$, and the intersection numbers are $1$, $1$ and $-b_i$. The examples are the self-intersections on the Hirzebruch surfaces and on the resolution of $\frac{1}{5}(1, 2)$, and the two small resolutions of the cone over a square.

Section 8.3 states the toric cone theorem for complete simplicial fans: the cone of curves is spanned by the classes of the wall curves, and a divisor is nef exactly when its support function is convex. It proves the theorem for smooth projective toric surfaces, by moving a curve to an effective sum of invariant curves with a vertex of its Newton polygon, and computes the cone of curves and the nef cone of $F_n$. The general case is cited.

The chapter follows Cox, Little and Schenck, Chapters 6, 10 and 11 (CLS11), and Fulton, Sections 2.6 and 5.1 (Ful93).
