## Wall curves and their intersection numbers

### Statement

Let $\Sigma$ be a fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$ of dimension $n$ and $X_\Sigma$ its toric variety.

**Definition P8.8.9 (walls and wall curves).** A *wall* of $\Sigma$ is a cone $\tau \in \Sigma$ of dimension $n - 1$ that is the intersection $\tau = \sigma \cap \sigma'$ of two cones $\sigma, \sigma' \in \Sigma(n)$; then $(\sigma, \sigma')$ is a *wall pair*. The *wall curve* is $V(\tau)$. Let $m_0$ be the primitive generator of the rank one group $\tau^\perp \cap M$ that is nonnegative on $\sigma$. When $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ are simplicial, write $\tau = \operatorname{Cone}(u_1, \dots, u_{n-1})$, $\sigma = \operatorname{Cone}(\tau, u_n)$ and $\sigma' = \operatorname{Cone}(\tau, u_{n+1})$.

**Proposition P8.8.10 (wall curves are projective lines).** Let $\tau$ be a wall. Then $\langle m_0, u \rangle > 0$ on $\sigma \setminus \tau$ and $\langle m_0, u \rangle < 0$ on $\sigma' \setminus \tau$. The curve $V(\tau) = O(\tau) \sqcup \{ \gamma_\sigma, \gamma_{\sigma'} \}$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, with $V(\tau) \cap U_\sigma = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$ and $V(\tau) \cap U_{\sigma'} = \operatorname{Spec} k[t^{-1}]$, where $t = \chi^{m_0}$, and $\gamma_\sigma$ is the point $t = 0$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P8.8.11 (degree on a complete curve).** For a Cartier divisor $D$ on a variety $X$ and a complete integral curve $C \subseteq X$, put $D \cdot C = \deg(\mathcal{O}_X(D)|_C)$. For a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor with $rD$ Cartier, put $D \cdot C = \frac{1}{r} (rD \cdot C)$. On a smooth projective surface this is the intersection number of Theorem P5.5.6.

**Proposition P8.8.12 (the degree on a wall curve).** Let $\tau = \sigma \cap \sigma'$ be a wall and $D = \sum_\rho a_\rho D_\rho$ a torus-invariant Cartier divisor with Cartier data $m_\sigma$, $m_{\sigma'}$. Then $m_\sigma - m_{\sigma'} = -(D \cdot V(\tau)) \, m_0$, and for every $u_n \in \sigma \setminus \tau$ that is the generator of a ray of $\sigma$,
$$D \cdot V(\tau) = \frac{\langle m_{\sigma'}, u_n \rangle + a_n}{\langle m_0, u_n \rangle}, \qquad a_n = a_{\operatorname{Cone}(u_n)} .$$
In particular $D \cdot V(\tau) \geq 0$ if and only if $\langle m_{\sigma'}, u \rangle \geq \varphi_D(u)$ for $u \in \sigma$, which is the convexity of $\varphi_D$ across the wall $\tau$.

Lean: `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.WallData.degree` is the right-hand side for real divisors on a wall of a fan, and `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.WallData.degree_nonneg_iff` is the last statement, with convexity across the wall `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsConvexAcross`; the identification with the degree of a line bundle is not in Lean as stated.

**Corollary P8.8.13 (the wall relation).** Let $\tau$ be a wall with $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ simplicial, so every $D_\rho$ with $\rho \in \sigma(1) \cup \sigma'(1)$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier near $V(\tau)$.

1. $D_\rho \cdot V(\tau) = 0$ for $\rho \notin \sigma(1) \cup \sigma'(1)$, $D_{u_n} \cdot V(\tau) = \operatorname{mult}(\tau) / \operatorname{mult}(\sigma)$ and $D_{u_{n+1}} \cdot V(\tau) = \operatorname{mult}(\tau) / \operatorname{mult}(\sigma')$.
2. $\sum_\rho (D_\rho \cdot V(\tau)) \, u_\rho = 0$, where $D_\rho$ stands for the generator $u_\rho$ of its ray.
3. If $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ are smooth, then $u_n + u_{n+1} = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} b_i u_i$ with $b_i \in \mathbb{Z}$, and
$$D_{u_n} \cdot V(\tau) = D_{u_{n+1}} \cdot V(\tau) = 1, \qquad D_{u_i} \cdot V(\tau) = -b_i \quad (1 \leq i \leq n - 1).$$

Lean: the existence and uniqueness of the relation is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.exists_unique_wall_relation`, and `MinimalModel.Toric.P2.wall_relation` checks it for $\mathbb{P}^2$; the intersection numbers are not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The two maximal cones of a wall lie on opposite sides of the hyperplane spanned by the wall, so the character $t = \chi^{m_0}$ that cuts out this hyperplane is a coordinate on one side and its inverse on the other, and the wall curve is two affine lines glued along $t \mapsto t^{-1}$. On the two charts $\mathcal{O}(D)$ is generated by $\chi^{m_\sigma}$ and by $\chi^{m_{\sigma'}}$, which differ by a power of $t$, and the degree of a line bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ is read off from its transition function. The relation among the ray generators comes from the fact that a principal divisor $\operatorname{div}(\chi^m)$ has degree zero on every complete curve.

### Examples

**Example P8.8.14 (the Hirzebruch surfaces).** In dimension two the walls are the rays, and $V(\rho) = D_\rho$. For $F_n$, with rays $u_1 = (1, 0)$, $u_2 = (0, 1)$, $u_3 = (-1, n)$ and $u_4 = (0, -1)$ in cyclic order, the wall relations are
$$u_4 + u_2 = 0 \cdot u_1, \qquad u_1 + u_3 = n \, u_2, \qquad u_2 + u_4 = 0 \cdot u_3, \qquad u_3 + u_1 = -n \, u_4 .$$
By Corollary P8.8.13 the self-intersections are $D_1^2 = D_3^2 = 0$, $D_2^2 = -n$ and $D_4^2 = n$, and $D_i \cdot D_{i+1} = 1$ for neighbouring rays. In the notation of Example P8.7.6, $F^2 = 0$, $C_-^2 = -n$, $C_+^2 = n$ and $C_- \cdot F = 1$, as in Theorem P3.11.25 and Example P5.12.8. With $K = -\sum D_i$, $K \cdot F = -2$ and $K \cdot C_- = -(1 - n + 1 + 0) = n - 2$, which agrees with adjunction for the rational curves $F$ and $C_-$.

**Example P8.8.15 (the resolution of $\frac{1}{5}(1, 2)$).** In Example P8.8.5 the relations at the rays $v_1$ and $v_2$ are $v_0 + v_2 = 3 v_1$ and $v_1 + v_3 = 2 v_2$, so $E_1^2 = -3$, $E_2^2 = -2$, $E_1 \cdot E_2 = 1$, and the strict transforms $D_{v_0}$ and $D_{v_3}$ of the two invariant curves through the singular point meet $E_1$ and $E_2$ respectively, once each. The canonical divisor $K = -\sum_{i=0}^3 D_{v_i}$ has $K \cdot E_1 = -(1 - 3 + 1) = 1$ and $K \cdot E_2 = -(1 - 2 + 1) = 0$, so $E_2$ is a $(-2)$-curve and $E_1$ is a $(-3)$-curve, as adjunction requires.

**Example P8.8.16 (the two small resolutions of the cone over a square).** Let $\sigma$ be the cone over a square of Example P8.6.10, with rays $u_1 = (0, 0, 1)$, $u_2 = (1, 0, 1)$, $u_3 = (1, 1, 1)$, $u_4 = (0, 1, 1)$. Subdividing along the diagonal $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_3)$ gives the fan $\Sigma_+$ with maximal cones $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_2, u_3)$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_3, u_4)$, both of determinant $\pm 1$, and subdividing along $\operatorname{Cone}(u_2, u_4)$ gives $\Sigma_-$. Both are refinements of $\sigma$ without new rays, so by Proposition P8.8.2 both $X_{\Sigma_\pm} \to U_\sigma$ are resolutions that are isomorphisms outside the vertex, with fibre over the vertex the wall curve $C_\pm \cong \mathbb{P}^1$. These are small resolutions: the exceptional set is a curve. For $\Sigma_+$ the wall is $\tau = \operatorname{Cone}(u_1, u_3)$, the opposite rays are $u_2$ and $u_4$, and the relation is $u_2 + u_4 = u_1 + u_3$. So
$$D_2 \cdot C_+ = D_4 \cdot C_+ = 1, \qquad D_1 \cdot C_+ = D_3 \cdot C_+ = -1, \qquad K \cdot C_+ = 0 .$$
For $\Sigma_-$ the roles are exchanged: $D_1 \cdot C_- = D_3 \cdot C_- = 1$ and $D_2 \cdot C_- = D_4 \cdot C_- = -1$. The birational map $X_{\Sigma_+} \dashrightarrow X_{\Sigma_-}$ over $U_\sigma$ is the Atiyah flop: the divisor $D_1$, which is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier on $U_\sigma$ (Example P8.7.14), is negative on $C_+$ and positive on $C_-$.

### Proof

*Proposition P8.8.10.* The cones $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ meet in the common face $\tau$, so by Lemma P8.6.7 there is $m \in \sigma^\vee \cap (-\sigma')^\vee \cap M$ with $\sigma \cap m^\perp = \tau = \sigma' \cap m^\perp$. Then $m \in \tau^\perp$, which is a line, so $m$ is a positive multiple of $m_0$, and $m_0$ is positive on $\sigma \setminus \tau$ and negative on $\sigma' \setminus \tau$. By the proof of Theorem P8.6.25, $V(\tau) \cap U_\sigma = O(\tau) \sqcup O(\sigma)$ is the closed subscheme $\operatorname{Spec} k[S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp]$, the quotient of $k[S_\sigma]$ by the prime spanned by the $\chi^m$ with $m \notin \tau^\perp$, and $S_\sigma \cap \tau^\perp = \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} m_0$. So $V(\tau) \cap U_\sigma = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, and $\gamma_\sigma$, at which every nonzero character vanishes, is $t = 0$. In the same way $V(\tau) \cap U_{\sigma'} = \operatorname{Spec} k[t^{-1}]$, and the two charts are glued along $O(\tau) = \operatorname{Spec} k[t^{\pm 1}]$. This is $\mathbb{P}^1$. The orbits in $V(\tau)$ are those of the cones containing $\tau$, which are $\tau$, $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$, since two cones of dimension $n$ that contain $\tau$ and lie on the same side of the hyperplane $m_0^\perp$ meet in a set of dimension $n$ near the relative interior of $\tau$, and in a fan such an intersection is a common face, so the two cones are equal.

*Proposition P8.8.12.* Both $m_\sigma$ and $m_{\sigma'}$ restrict to $\varphi_D$ on $\tau$, so $m_\sigma - m_{\sigma'} \in \tau^\perp \cap M$, and $m_\sigma - m_{\sigma'} = c \, m_0$ for an integer $c$. By Theorem P8.7.10, $s = \chi^{m_\sigma}$ generates $\mathcal{O}(D)$ on $U_\sigma$ and $s' = \chi^{m_{\sigma'}}$ generates it on $U_{\sigma'}$, and $s = t^c s'$. On $V(\tau) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ the restriction $\mathcal{L}$ of $\mathcal{O}(D)$ has the rational section $s'$, which generates $\mathcal{L}$ on $\operatorname{Spec} k[t^{-1}]$ and equals $t^{-c} s$ on $\operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, so its divisor is $-c$ times the point $t = 0$. Hence $D \cdot V(\tau) = \deg \mathcal{L} = -c$. Evaluating $m_{\sigma'} - m_\sigma = (D \cdot V(\tau)) m_0$ on $u_n$, with $\langle m_\sigma, u_n \rangle = -a_n$, gives the formula. Since $\langle m_0, u_n \rangle > 0$, $D \cdot V(\tau) \geq 0$ if and only if $\langle m_{\sigma'}, u_n \rangle \geq \langle m_\sigma, u_n \rangle$. The forms $m_{\sigma'}$ and $m_\sigma$ agree on $\tau$, and $\sigma$ is generated by $\tau$ and its rays outside $\tau$, on which $m_{\sigma'} - m_\sigma = (D \cdot V(\tau)) m_0$ has the sign of $D \cdot V(\tau)$; so this is the inequality $\langle m_{\sigma'}, u \rangle \geq \langle m_\sigma, u \rangle = \varphi_D(u)$ on $\sigma$. For $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier $D$, the same formulas hold with $m_\sigma \in M_\mathbb{Q}$, by applying them to $rD$.

*Corollary P8.8.13.* (1) If $\rho \notin \sigma(1) \cup \sigma'(1)$, then $D_\rho$ has Cartier data $m_\sigma = m_{\sigma'} = 0$, so $D_\rho \cdot V(\tau) = 0$. For $D = D_{u_n}$, Cartier data in $M_\mathbb{Q}$ on $\sigma'$ is $m_{\sigma'} = 0$, since $u_n$ is not a ray of $\sigma'$, so $D_{u_n} \cdot V(\tau) = 1 / \langle m_0, u_n \rangle$. The form $m_0$ is primitive in $M$, because $\tau^\perp \cap M$ is a direct summand (Lemma P8.6.23), so $m_0 \colon N \to \mathbb{Z}$ is surjective with kernel $N_\tau$. With $L_\tau = \mathbb{Z} u_1 + \dots + \mathbb{Z} u_{n-1}$ and $L_\sigma = L_\tau + \mathbb{Z} u_n$,
$$\operatorname{mult}(\sigma) = [N : L_\sigma] = [N : N_\tau + \mathbb{Z} u_n] \cdot [N_\tau + \mathbb{Z} u_n : L_\tau + \mathbb{Z} u_n] = \langle m_0, u_n \rangle \cdot \operatorname{mult}(\tau),$$
since $N / (N_\tau + \mathbb{Z} u_n) \cong \mathbb{Z} / \langle m_0, u_n \rangle$ through $m_0$, and $u_n \notin \operatorname{span}(\tau)$. The same argument with $-m_0$ applies to $u_{n+1}$.

(2) For $m \in M$, $\operatorname{div}(\chi^m) = \sum_\rho \langle m, u_\rho \rangle D_\rho$ is principal, so it has Cartier data $-m$ on every cone and degree $0$ on $V(\tau)$ by Proposition P8.8.12. By linearity of the degree, $\langle m, \sum_\rho (D_\rho \cdot V(\tau)) u_\rho \rangle = 0$ for all $m \in M$, and the vector is zero.

(3) If $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ are smooth, $\operatorname{mult}(\tau) = 1$ as $\tau$ is a face of a smooth cone, so $D_{u_n} \cdot V(\tau) = D_{u_{n+1}} \cdot V(\tau) = 1$ by (1). Then (2) gives $u_n + u_{n+1} + \sum_{i < n} (D_{u_i} \cdot V(\tau)) u_i = 0$. As $u_1, \dots, u_n$ is a basis of $N$, the coefficients of $u_n + u_{n+1}$ in it are integers, and the coefficient of $u_n$ is $\langle m_0, u_n + u_{n+1} \rangle = 1 + \langle m_0, u_{n+1} \rangle = 0$, since $\langle -m_0, u_{n+1} \rangle = \operatorname{mult}(\sigma') / \operatorname{mult}(\tau) = 1$ by the computation in (1), so $u_n + u_{n+1} = \sum_{i < n} b_i u_i$ with $b_i = -D_{u_i} \cdot V(\tau)$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute the wall relations of $\mathbb{P}^2$ and of $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, and check that the self-intersection of a line in $\mathbb{P}^2$ is $1$.
2. (A) Compute the intersection matrix of the invariant curves of the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at the three fixed points, the toric surface whose fan has the six rays $\pm e_1$, $\pm e_2$, $\pm (e_1 + e_2)$.
3. (B) Let $X_\Sigma$ be a smooth complete toric surface with rays $u_1, \dots, u_r$ in cyclic order. Show that $u_{i-1} + u_{i+1} = -D_i^2 \, u_i$, and deduce $\sum_i D_i^2 = 12 - 3r$ from Noether's formula, or prove it directly for $r = 3, 4$.
4. (B) For the weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$ of Example P8.7.16, compute $D_1 \cdot D_1$, $D_1 \cdot D_4$ and $D_4^2$ with Corollary P8.8.13, and check $D_4 \sim n D_1$.
5. (B) For the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^3$ along a line (Exercise 6 of Section 7.3), list the walls, compute the wall relations, and find the intersection numbers of the exceptional divisor with the wall curves.
6. (C) Show that in Example P8.8.16 the normal bundle of $C_+$ in $X_{\Sigma_+}$ is $\mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$, by restricting the invariant divisors $D_1$ and $D_3$ to $C_+$.

### Lean

A wall pair is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsWallPair`: two different maximal cones of a fan whose intersection has dimension $n - 1$. The structure `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.WallData` records the extra ray of $\sigma'$ and its expression in the rays of $\sigma$, and `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.WallData.degree` is the degree of a divisor on the wall, defined by the formula of Proposition P8.8.12. The theorem `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.WallData.degree_nonneg_iff` proves that the degree is nonnegative exactly when the support function is convex across the wall, `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.exists_unique_wall_relation` proves the existence and uniqueness of the relation among the ray generators of a wall pair, and `MinimalModel.Toric.P2.wall_relation` is the example of $\mathbb{P}^2$. Since the repository has no scheme $X_\Sigma$, the identification of the degree with the degree of a line bundle on $V(\tau) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ is not in Lean.

### Notes and history

The intersection numbers of invariant divisors with wall curves are in Oda's book and in Fulton, Section 5.1; Cox, Little and Schenck, Proposition 6.3.8 and Proposition 6.4.4, give the simplicial and smooth forms. The relation $u_{i-1} + u_{i+1} = -D_i^2 u_i$ for smooth complete toric surfaces is the starting point of the classification of these surfaces as blow-ups of $\mathbb{P}^2$ and of the Hirzebruch surfaces, Fulton, Section 2.5. In the toric minimal model program of Volume 3, the sign pattern of the wall relation of an extremal ray determines whether its contraction is a fibration, a divisorial contraction or a small contraction.
