## Lines on cubic surfaces and plane conics

### Statement

By Example P3.13.42, $\operatorname{Hilb}^{m+1}(\mathbb{P}^n) \cong \operatorname{Gr}(2, n + 1)$, and its $k$-points are the lines of $\mathbb{P}^n$. For a closed subscheme $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$, Theorem P8.1.2 makes $\operatorname{Hilb}^{m+1}(X)$ a closed subscheme of $\operatorname{Gr}(2, n + 1)$.

**Definition P8.1.17 (the Fano scheme of lines).** The *Fano scheme* of a closed subscheme $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ is $F(X) = \operatorname{Hilb}^{m+1}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Gr}(2, n + 1)$. Its $k$-points are the lines $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ with $L \subseteq X$.

**Proposition P8.1.18 (lines on a cubic surface are rigid).** Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth cubic surface, $L \subseteq S$ a line and $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to L$ an isomorphism. Then $N_{L/S} \cong \mathcal{O}(-1)$ and $f^* T_S \cong \mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$. The scheme $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, S)$ is smooth of dimension $3 = -K_S \cdot L + 2$ at $[f]$, and the connected component through $[f]$ has the $k$-points $f \circ \operatorname{PGL}_2$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.1.19 (the Fano scheme of a smooth cubic surface).** Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth cubic surface. Then $F(S)$ is a reduced finite scheme with $27$ points.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P8.1.20 (every cubic surface contains a line).** Let $\mathbb{P}^{19}$ be the projective space of cubic forms in $x_0, \dots, x_3$ up to scalars. The incidence set $I = \{ (L, [F]) : L \subseteq V_+(F) \} \subseteq \operatorname{Gr}(2, 4) \times \mathbb{P}^{19}$ is closed, irreducible of dimension $19$, and its projection to $\mathbb{P}^{19}$ is surjective. In particular every cubic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$, smooth or not, contains a line.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P8.1.21 (plane conics).** Let $\operatorname{Hom}_2 = \operatorname{Hom}_2(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^2)$ be the morphisms of degree $2$, an open subset of $\mathbb{P}^8$ by Example P8.1.7.

1. Every $f \in \operatorname{Hom}_2$ is either an isomorphism onto a smooth conic, and then $f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \cong \mathcal{O}(3)^2$, or a double cover of a line, and then $f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \cong \mathcal{O}(4) \oplus \mathcal{O}(2)$. In both cases $\operatorname{Hom}_2$ is smooth of dimension $8 = h^0(f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2})$ at $[f]$.
2. The double covers of lines form a closed subset $D \subseteq \operatorname{Hom}_2$ of dimension $7$.
3. The morphisms onto a given smooth conic $Q$ form one orbit $f_0 \circ \operatorname{PGL}_2$, of dimension $3$. For $f$ an isomorphism onto $Q$ there is an exact sequence $0 \to T_{\mathbb{P}^1} \to f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \to f^* N_{Q/\mathbb{P}^2} \to 0$, that is $0 \to \mathcal{O}(2) \to \mathcal{O}(3)^2 \to \mathcal{O}(4) \to 0$, and the count $8 = 3 + 5$ splits the tangent space into reparametrizations and deformations of the conic in the $\mathbb{P}^5$ of Example P3.13.40.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

A line on a cubic surface has self-intersection $-1$, so its normal bundle is $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, it has no sections, and the line cannot move. At the level of the Fano scheme, the tangent space at $[L]$ is the kernel of the map $(\ell_2, \ell_3) \mapsto \ell_2 q_2 + \ell_3 q_3$ from pairs of linear forms to cubic forms on $L$, where $q_2$, $q_3$ are the restrictions to $L$ of the two nonvanishing partial derivatives; smoothness of $S$ along $L$ says that $q_2$ and $q_3$ have no common zero, which makes the map injective. So the Fano scheme is reduced at each of its $27$ points. A cubic form vanishes on a given line under $4$ linear conditions, so the cubics through a line form a $\mathbb{P}^{15}$ and the incidence variety has dimension $4 + 15 = 19$; since some cubic has only finitely many lines, the projection to the $\mathbb{P}^{19}$ of all cubics is onto. A morphism of degree $2$ from $\mathbb{P}^1$ to $\mathbb{P}^2$ is birational onto a conic or $2$ to $1$ onto a line, and in both cases $H^1$ of the pulled back tangent bundle vanishes.

### Examples

**Example P8.1.22 (lines on a smooth quadric surface).** Let $Q = V_+(x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2) \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $L \subseteq Q$ a line, a fibre of one of the two rulings (Example P5.5.18). Then $L^2 = 0$, $N_{L/Q} \cong \mathcal{O}$, and the sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}(2) \to T_Q|_L \to \mathcal{O} \to 0$ splits because $H^1(\mathcal{O}(2)) = 0$; in fact $T_Q|_L \cong \mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}$ is the restriction of $T_{\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1} = p_1^* T_{\mathbb{P}^1} \oplus p_2^* T_{\mathbb{P}^1}$. So an isomorphism $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to L$ is free, $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, Q)$ is smooth at $[f]$ of dimension $-K_Q \cdot L + 2 = 2 + 2 = 4$, and modulo the $3$-dimensional group $\operatorname{PGL}_2$ this leaves a $1$-dimensional family of lines: $F(Q)$ is the disjoint union of two copies of $\mathbb{P}^1$, one for each ruling.

**Example P8.1.23 (a cone with infinitely many lines).** Let $\operatorname{char} k \neq 3$ and $S = V_+(x_0^3 + x_1^3 + x_2^3) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$, the cone over the Fermat plane cubic $E$ with vertex $v = [0 : 0 : 0 : 1]$. The partial derivatives $3 x_i^2$, $i \leq 2$, and $0$ vanish together only at $v$, so $S$ is singular exactly at $v$. Every line joining $v$ to a point of $E$ lies in $S$, so $F(S)$ contains a copy of $E$ and is not finite. In the notation of Theorem P8.1.19, for the line $L = \{ x_0 + x_1 = 0, \, x_2 = 0 \}$ through $v$ the two restricted partial derivatives both vanish at $v$, and the map on tangent spaces has a kernel. Proposition P8.1.20 is consistent with this example: over a singular cubic the fibre of the incidence variety may be positive dimensional.

### Proof

*Proposition P8.1.18.* By Example P5.5.18, $L^2 = 2 - 3 = -1$ and $K_S = -H$, so $-K_S \cdot L = 1$. The normal bundle is $N_{L/S} = \mathcal{O}_L(L)$, of degree $L^2 = -1$, so $N_{L/S} \cong \mathcal{O}(-1)$. Dualizing the conormal sequence of Theorem P3.12.14 gives $0 \to T_L \to T_S|_L \to N_{L/S} \to 0$, and its class lies in $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathcal{O}(-1), \mathcal{O}(2)) = H^1(\mathcal{O}(3)) = 0$ by parts 1 and 4 of Proposition P4.7.2 and Theorem P4.2.1. So $f^* T_S \cong \mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)$, with $H^1 = 0$ and $h^0 = 3$. Corollary P8.1.13 gives smoothness of dimension $3 = -K_S \cdot L + 2$ at $[f]$. Let $f'$ be a $k$-point of the connected component through $[f]$. By Proposition P8.1.6, $f'_* \mathbb{P}^1 \equiv L$, so $H \cdot f'_* \mathbb{P}^1 = 1$ and $f'$ is an isomorphism onto a line $L'$, by the projection formula. If $L' \neq L$, then $L \cdot L' \geq 0$, while $L \cdot L' = L \cdot L = -1$. So $L' = L$ and $f^{-1} \circ f' \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^1) = \operatorname{PGL}_2$ (Example P8.1.8).

*Theorem P8.1.19.* First, the points. By Corollary P3.12.31, $\omega_S \cong \mathcal{O}_S(-1)$, so $-K_S = H$ is ample and $K_S^2 = H^2 = 3$; $S$ is a del Pezzo surface of degree $3$ (Definition P5.9.24), and by Theorem P5.9.27 it is isomorphic to the blow-up $S_6$ of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in six points in general position. A line $L \subseteq S$ satisfies $L \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ and $L^2 = K_S \cdot L = -1$ (Example P5.5.18), so it is a $(-1)$-curve. Conversely, a $(-1)$-curve $C$ has $H \cdot C = -K_S \cdot C = 1$, and an integral curve of degree $1$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$ is a line: if $C$ lies in a plane, it is a plane curve of degree $1$; otherwise pick three non-collinear points of $C$ and the plane $\Pi$ through them, so that $\Pi \cap C$ is finite with at least three points, while its length is $H \cdot C = 1$. So the lines of $S$ are its $(-1)$-curves, and there are $27$ of them by part 2 of Theorem P5.10.5 transported along $S \cong S_6$.

Next, the scheme structure at a line $L$. Choose coordinates with $L = V_+(x_2, x_3)$. On the open subset $U \cong \mathbb{A}^4$ of $\operatorname{Gr}(2, 4)$ of lines complementary to $V_+(x_0, x_1)$ (the chart of Example P3.13.29), the point $(a, b, c, d)$ is the line $L_{a, b, c, d}$, the image of
$$\mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^3, \qquad [s : t] \mapsto [s : t : a s + c t : b s + d t],$$
and $L = L_{0,0,0,0}$. Over $U$ the universal line is the image of the closed immersion $\mathbb{P}^1_U \to \mathbb{P}^3_U$ given by the same formula. For a morphism $T \to U$ with $T = \operatorname{Spec} A$ affine, the pulled back line lies in $S_T$ exactly when $F(s, t, a s + c t, b s + d t) = 0$ in $H^0(\mathbb{P}^1_A, \mathcal{O}(3)) = A[s, t]_3$, by Theorem P4.2.1. So $F(S) \cap U$ is the closed subscheme of $\mathbb{A}^4$ cut out by the four coefficients $\phi_0, \dots, \phi_3 \in k[a, b, c, d]$ of the binary cubic $F(s, t, a s + c t, b s + d t)$. Since $L \subseteq S$, $F \in (x_2, x_3)$; write $F = x_2 Q_2 + x_3 Q_3$ with quadratic forms $Q_2$, $Q_3$, and let $q_i(s, t) = Q_i(s, t, 0, 0)$. Substituting $x_2 = \ell_2 = a s + c t$, $x_3 = \ell_3 = b s + d t$ and keeping the terms of degree $1$ in $(a, b, c, d)$ gives
$$F(s, t, \ell_2, \ell_3) \equiv \ell_2 \, q_2(s, t) + \ell_3 \, q_3(s, t) \pmod{(a, b, c, d)^2}.$$
So the $\phi_j$ vanish at the origin, and by Proposition P3.7.14 the tangent space $T_{[L]} F(S)$ is the kernel of the linear map $\lambda \colon (\ell_2, \ell_3) \mapsto \ell_2 q_2 + \ell_3 q_3$ from pairs of binary linear forms, a space of dimension $4$, to binary cubic forms.

The map $\lambda$ is injective. On $L$, $\partial F/\partial x_0 = x_2 \partial Q_2/\partial x_0 + x_3 \partial Q_3/\partial x_0$ and $\partial F/\partial x_1$ vanish, while $\partial F/\partial x_2 = q_2$ and $\partial F/\partial x_3 = q_3$. At a point $p \in L$ with $x_0(p) \neq 0$, the affine chart $x_0 = 1$ has coordinates $x_1, x_2, x_3$, and the Jacobian matrix of the dehomogenized equation at $p$ is $(\partial F/\partial x_1, \partial F/\partial x_2, \partial F/\partial x_3)(p) = (0, q_2(p), q_3(p))$; similarly on the chart $x_1 = 1$. Since $S$ is smooth along $L$, Proposition P3.7.14 and part 2 of Theorem P3.7.24 show that this row is nonzero, so $q_2$ and $q_3$ have no common zero on $\mathbb{P}^1$. Neither is zero, since a nonzero binary quadric has a zero over the algebraically closed field $k$ and $q_2 = 0$ would force $q_3$ to have none. So $q_2$ and $q_3$ have no common linear factor. If $\ell_2 q_2 = -\ell_3 q_3$, then $q_2$ divides $\ell_3 q_3$ in the factorial ring $k[s, t]$, hence divides $\ell_3$, which has degree $1 < 2$; so $\ell_3 = 0$, and then $\ell_2 = 0$. Therefore $T_{[L]} F(S) = 0$, that is, the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{F(S), [L]}$ satisfies $\mathfrak{m} = \mathfrak{m}^2$, and $\mathfrak{m} = 0$ by Nakayama's lemma. So $[L]$ is an isolated reduced point of $F(S)$, with local ring $k$. As this holds at each of the $27$ points, $F(S)$ is the reduced scheme of $27$ points.

*Proposition P8.1.20.* On $U \times \mathbb{P}^{19}$, with $U$ as above and $F$ the universal cubic form, $I$ is cut out by the four coefficients of $F(s, t, a s + c t, b s + d t)$, which are bihomogeneous; the charts $U$ for the six coordinate pairs cover $\operatorname{Gr}(2, 4)$, so $I$ is closed. The change of variables $x_2 \mapsto x_2 + a x_0 + c x_1$, $x_3 \mapsto x_3 + b x_0 + d x_1$ defines an automorphism of $U \times \mathbb{P}^{19}$ over $U$ that carries $I \cap (U \times \mathbb{P}^{19})$ to $U \times \Lambda$, where $\Lambda = \{ [F] : F \in (x_2, x_3) \}$ is the linear subspace of cubic forms vanishing on $L$. The restriction $k[x_0, \dots, x_3]_3 \to k[s, t]_3$ is surjective with $4$-dimensional target, so $\Lambda \cong \mathbb{P}^{15}$ and $I \cap (U \times \mathbb{P}^{19}) \cong \mathbb{A}^4 \times \mathbb{P}^{15}$ is irreducible of dimension $19$. The six such open subsets of $I$ meet pairwise, since $\operatorname{Gr}(2, 4)$ is irreducible, so $I$ is irreducible of dimension $19$. The variety $\operatorname{Gr}(2, 4) \times \mathbb{P}^{19}$ is projective, so the projection $\pi \colon I \to \mathbb{P}^{19}$ has closed image $Y = \pi(I)$ by Proposition P3.4.20, and $Y$ is irreducible. Smooth cubic surfaces exist, by Theorems P5.9.26 and P5.9.28, and over a smooth cubic $[F_0]$ the fibre $\pi^{-1}([F_0]) = F(V_+(F_0))$ is finite by Theorem P8.1.19. Applying part 2 of Theorem P3.7.6 to the dominant morphism $I_{\mathrm{red}} \to Y$ shows that every component of $\pi^{-1}([F_0])$ has dimension at least $19 - \dim Y$, so $\dim Y \geq 19$ and $Y = \mathbb{P}^{19}$.

*Proposition P8.1.21.* (1) Let $f \in \operatorname{Hom}_2$ and $C = f(\mathbb{P}^1)$, a curve. By the projection formula of Theorem P7.1.15, $2 = \deg f^* \mathcal{O}(1) = \deg(\mathbb{P}^1 \to C) \cdot \deg C$. If $\deg C = 1$, $C$ is a line $M$ and $f = \iota \circ \pi$ with $\pi \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to M$ of degree $2$ and $\iota$ the inclusion; then $f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} = \pi^*(T_{\mathbb{P}^2}|_M) = \pi^*(\mathcal{O}(2) \oplus \mathcal{O}(1)) = \mathcal{O}(4) \oplus \mathcal{O}(2)$ by Example P5.4.13 and Proposition P5.1.4. If $\deg C = 2$, then $\mathbb{P}^1 \to C$ is birational and $C$ is an integral conic. An integral plane conic is smooth: at a singular point $p$, a line through $p$ and a second point of $C$ would meet $C$ with total multiplicity at least $3 > 2$ and so lie in $C$. So $f$ is a birational morphism of smooth projective curves onto $C$, an isomorphism by Proposition P5.1.5, and $f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2} \cong \mathcal{O}(3)^2$ by Example P5.4.14. In both cases all summands are at least $2 \geq -1$, so $H^1 = 0$, and Corollary P8.1.13 gives smoothness of dimension $h^0 = 4 + 4 = 5 + 3 = 8$.

(2) Write $f = [F_0 : F_1 : F_2]$ with binary quadrics $F_i$, and let $M_f$ be the $3 \times 3$ matrix of their coefficients. The image of $f$ lies in a line exactly when the $F_i$ are linearly dependent, that is, when $\det M_f = 0$. So $D = \operatorname{Hom}_2 \cap V_+(\det)$, a closed subset cut out by one equation in the irreducible $8$-dimensional $\operatorname{Hom}_2$; it is nonempty and not everything, so all its components have dimension $7$ by Corollary P1.5.43 and Theorem P3.7.5. The count agrees with the description: a line $M$ is one of a $2$-dimensional family, and the double covers $\mathbb{P}^1 \to M \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ form $\operatorname{Hom}_2(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^1)$, of dimension $5$ (Exercise 1 of Section 1.1).

(3) If $f_0$ and $f$ are isomorphisms onto $Q$, then $f_0^{-1} \circ f \in \operatorname{PGL}_2$. The differential $df \colon T_{\mathbb{P}^1} \to f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2}$ is injective with locally free cokernel, because $f$ is a closed immersion onto the smooth curve $Q$ and the conormal sequence of Theorem P3.12.14 is exact on the left and locally split; the cokernel is $f^* N_{Q/\mathbb{P}^2} = f^* \mathcal{O}_Q(Q) \cong \mathcal{O}(4)$, since $Q \cdot Q = 4$ in $\mathbb{P}^2$. Taking global sections, $H^0(\mathcal{O}(2)) \to H^0(\mathcal{O}(3)^2) \to H^0(\mathcal{O}(4)) \to H^1(\mathcal{O}(2)) = 0$ is exact with dimensions $3$, $8$, $5$. The image of $H^0(T_{\mathbb{P}^1})$ is the tangent space to the orbit $f \circ \operatorname{PGL}_2$, and $H^0(N_{Q/\mathbb{P}^2}) = H^0(\mathcal{O}_Q(2))$, of dimension $5$, is the tangent space at $[Q]$ to the open subset of smooth conics in $\mathbb{P}^5$.

### Exercises

1. (A) For the line $L = V_+(x_0 + x_1, x_2 + x_3)$ on the Fermat cubic surface of Example P5.10.7, change coordinates to $y_0 = x_0$, $y_1 = x_2$, $y_2 = x_0 + x_1$, $y_3 = x_2 + x_3$, compute the quadrics $q_2$, $q_3$ of the proof of Theorem P8.1.19 and check that they have no common zero when $\operatorname{char} k \neq 3$.
2. (A) Let $p_0, p_1, p_\infty \in \mathbb{P}^2$ be non-collinear. Show that the morphisms $f \in \operatorname{Hom}_2(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^2)$ with $f(0) = p_0$, $f(1) = p_1$, $f(\infty) = p_\infty$ that are isomorphisms onto smooth conics are in bijection with the smooth conics through the three points, and that both sets have dimension $2 = 8 - 3 \cdot 2$.
3. (B) Complete Example P8.1.22: show that the lines on $V_+(x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2)$ are $V_+(x_0 - \lambda x_1, x_2 - \lambda x_3)$ and $V_+(x_0 - \mu x_2, x_1 - \mu x_3)$ with $[\lambda : 1], [\mu : 1] \in \mathbb{P}^1$ in the appropriate sense, and that $F(Q)$ is the disjoint union of two conics in the Klein quadric.
4. (C) Let $L$ be a line on a smooth cubic surface $S$, with $F = x_2 Q_2 + x_3 Q_3$ as in the proof of Theorem P8.1.19. Show that the planes $\Pi_\lambda = \{ x_3 = \lambda x_2 \}$ through $L$ cut $S$ in $L$ plus a conic, that the conic is singular for the roots of a binary form of degree $5$ in the coordinates of $\lambda$, and, using Theorem P5.10.5, that exactly $10$ lines of $S$ meet $L$, in $5$ pairs.
5. (B) Let $\Sigma \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3 \times \mathbb{P}^{19}$ be the set of pairs $(p, [F])$ with $V_+(F)$ singular at $p$. Show that $\Sigma$ is irreducible of dimension $18$, and, using the surface $x_0 x_1 x_2 = x_3^3$ in characteristic different from $3$, that the singular cubic surfaces form a hypersurface in $\mathbb{P}^{19}$.
6. (B) Let $f \in D$ be a double cover of a line, with $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$. Show that $df \colon T_{\mathbb{P}^1} \to f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2}$ vanishes at the two ramification points of $f$, so that its cokernel is not locally free, and compare $h^0(f^* T_{\mathbb{P}^2}) = 8$ with the dimension $7$ of $D$.

### Notes and history

Cayley and Salmon found the $27$ lines on a smooth cubic surface in 1849, and Clebsch and Schläfli described their configuration; the account by blowing up six points is in Chapter 10 of P5. The variety of lines on a hypersurface goes back to Fano, and Altman and Kleiman studied its scheme structure in arbitrary characteristic; for a general hypersurface of degree $e$ in $\mathbb{P}^n$ it is smooth of the expected dimension $2n - 3 - e$ when this number is nonnegative, which for $n = e = 3$ is the finiteness of Theorem P8.1.19. Kollár (Kol96, Chapter V) and Debarre (Deb01, Chapter 2) use lines on hypersurfaces as the first examples of families of rational curves; for $e \leq n - 1$ every point of the hypersurface lies on a line, which is the source of the uniruledness of such hypersurfaces in Chapter 3.
