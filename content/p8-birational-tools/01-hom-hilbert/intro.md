# Hom and Hilbert schemes

Rational curves are found by deforming curves, and a deformation of a morphism $f \colon C \to X$ is a point of a parameter space near $[f]$. This chapter builds that space, the scheme $\operatorname{Hom}(C, X)$ of morphisms, describes its tangent space, and proves the lower bound on its dimension that drives every later argument in Part A.

Section 1.1 states Grothendieck's theorem that the Hilbert functor of a projective scheme is represented by a projective scheme, and deduces the scheme $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X)$ as an open subscheme of the Hilbert scheme of $Y \times X$, through graphs. Morphisms with prescribed values on a closed subscheme form a closed subscheme, and the degree of a family of curves is locally constant, so that all members of a connected family have the same class in $N_1(X)$. The morphisms $\mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^n$ of degree $d$ form an open subset of a projective space of dimension $(n + 1)(d + 1) - 1$.

Section 1.2 identifies the tangent space of $\operatorname{Hom}(C, X)$ at $[f]$ with $H^0(C, f^* T_X)$ by dual numbers, states the deformation theoretic result that the local ring is cut out by at most $h^1(C, f^* T_X)$ equations in $h^0(C, f^* T_X)$ variables, and proves the dimension estimate $\dim_{[f]} \operatorname{Hom}(C, X) \geq -K_X \cdot f_* C + n \chi(\mathcal{O}_C)$, with its variant for morphisms with fixed points. A $(-2)$-curve on a surface shows that the estimate can be strict.

Section 1.3 works out two classical cases. A line on a smooth cubic surface has normal bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ and does not deform, and the Fano scheme of lines of a smooth cubic surface is $27$ reduced points; a dimension count shows that every cubic surface contains a line. The morphisms $\mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^2$ of degree $2$ form a smooth variety of dimension $8$, fibred over the $\mathbb{P}^5$ of conics with fibres $\operatorname{PGL}_2$ over the smooth conics.

The chapter follows Kollár, Rational curves on algebraic varieties, Chapters I and II (Kol96), and Debarre, Higher-dimensional algebraic geometry, Chapter 2 (Deb01).
