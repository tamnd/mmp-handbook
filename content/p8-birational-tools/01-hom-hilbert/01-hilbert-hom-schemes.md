## Hilbert schemes and Hom schemes

### Statement

In Part A, $k$ is an algebraically closed field, a variety is an integral separated scheme of finite type over $k$, and a curve is a variety of dimension $1$. A projective scheme over a scheme $S$ is a closed subscheme $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_S$ for some $N$, and for an $S$-scheme $T$ write $X_T = X \times_S T$. The Hilbert polynomial of a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^N_{\kappa(t)}$ is taken with respect to $\mathcal{O}(1)$, as in Section 13.2 of P3. A *rational curve* on a variety $X$ is a curve $C \subseteq X$ whose normalization is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$; by Proposition P5.1.2 and Theorem P5.2.12 these are exactly the images of nonconstant morphisms $\mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ when $X$ is proper.

**Definition P8.1.1 (the Hilbert functor of a projective scheme).** Let $S$ be a Noetherian scheme, $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_S$ a projective $S$-scheme and $P \in \mathbb{Q}[m]$. For an $S$-scheme $T$, let $\operatorname{Hilb}^P_{X/S}(T)$ be the set of closed subschemes $Z \subseteq X_T$ that are flat over $T$ and whose fibre $Z_t \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_{\kappa(t)}$ has Hilbert polynomial $P$ for every $t \in T$. For $g \colon T' \to T$ put $g^* Z = Z \times_T T'$. This is a functor on $S$-schemes, and $\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S} = \coprod_P \operatorname{Hilb}^P_{X/S}$ is the *Hilbert functor* of $X$ over $S$. For $S = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ and $X = \mathbb{P}^N_\mathbb{Z}$ this is Definition P3.13.32.

**Theorem P8.1.2 (Grothendieck).** Let $S$ be a Noetherian scheme and $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_S$ a projective $S$-scheme. For every $P \in \mathbb{Q}[m]$ the functor $\operatorname{Hilb}^P_{X/S}$ is represented by a projective $S$-scheme $\operatorname{Hilb}^P(X/S)$, which is a closed subscheme of $\operatorname{Hilb}^P(\mathbb{P}^N) \times S$, and there is a universal family $\mathcal{Z} \subseteq X \times_S \operatorname{Hilb}^P(X/S)$. Hence $\operatorname{Hilb}_{X/S}$ is represented by the disjoint union $\operatorname{Hilb}(X/S) = \coprod_P \operatorname{Hilb}^P(X/S)$, a scheme locally of finite type over $S$ with countably many connected components, each projective over $S$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P8.1.3 (the Hom functor).** Let $S$ be a Noetherian scheme, and $Y$, $X$ projective $S$-schemes with $Y$ flat over $S$. For an $S$-scheme $T$ let $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X)(T)$ be the set of $T$-morphisms $Y_T \to X_T$, with pullback along $T' \to T$ by base change. For a closed subscheme $B \subseteq Y$ and an $S$-morphism $g \colon B \to X$, let $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X; g)(T)$ be the subset of the morphisms $f$ with $f|_{B_T} = g_T$. When $S = \operatorname{Spec} k$ the subscript is omitted.

**Theorem P8.1.4 (the Hom scheme).** In the situation of Definition P8.1.3, the functor $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X)$ is represented by an $S$-scheme $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X)$, locally of finite type over $S$. Sending a morphism $f \colon Y_T \to X_T$ to its graph $\Gamma_f \subseteq Y_T \times_T X_T = (Y \times_S X)_T$ identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X)$ with an open subscheme of $\operatorname{Hilb}(Y \times_S X / S)$, where $Y \times_S X$ is embedded by the Segre embedding. In particular $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X)$ is a disjoint union of countably many quasi-projective $S$-schemes $\operatorname{Hom}^P_S(Y, X)$, one for each Hilbert polynomial $P$ of graphs. When $S = \operatorname{Spec} k$, the $k$-points of $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X)$ are the morphisms $Y \to X$, and the universal family is the *evaluation* morphism $\operatorname{ev} \colon Y \times \operatorname{Hom}(Y, X) \to X$, so that morphisms $T \to \operatorname{Hom}(Y, X)$ correspond to morphisms $F \colon Y \times T \to X$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Proposition P8.1.5 (morphisms with fixed values).** Let $Y$ and $X$ be projective schemes over $k$, $B \subseteq Y$ a closed subscheme and $g \colon B \to X$ a morphism. Then $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X; g)$ is represented by a closed subscheme of $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X)$: the fibre over the $k$-point $[g]$ of the restriction morphism $\rho \colon \operatorname{Hom}(Y, X) \to \operatorname{Hom}(B, X)$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

For a morphism $f \colon C \to X$ from a smooth projective curve to a projective scheme, write $f_* C$ for the $1$-cycle $f_*[C]$ of Proposition P7.2.7: it is $\deg(C \to f(C)) \, [f(C)]$ if $f$ is nonconstant and $0$ otherwise. By Proposition P7.1.7 and the projection formula of Proposition P7.2.7, $L \cdot f_* C = \deg f^* L$ for every line bundle $L$ on $X$.

**Proposition P8.1.6 (degree in families).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve, $X$ a projective scheme over $k$, $T$ a connected scheme of finite type over $k$ and $F \colon C \times T \to X$ a morphism, with $f_t = F|_{C \times \{t\}}$ for $t \in T(k)$. For every line bundle $L$ on $X$ the degree $\deg f_t^* L$ does not depend on $t \in T(k)$. Hence the class of $f_{t*} C$ in $N_1(X)$ is the same for all $t \in T(k)$, and the connected components of $\operatorname{Hom}(C, X)$ are open and closed subschemes on which the class of $f_* C$ is constant.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The Hilbert scheme is the deepest input of Part A and is cited: Grothendieck's construction embeds the Hilbert functor of $\mathbb{P}^N$ in a Grassmannian by taking the degree $m$ part of the ideal for one large $m$, and the conditions for a subscheme to lie in $X$ are closed. A morphism is the same as its graph, and a closed subscheme of $Y \times X$ is a graph exactly when its projection to $Y$ is an isomorphism, which is an open condition in flat families; this makes $\operatorname{Hom}$ an open piece of a Hilbert scheme. Fixing values on $B$ is a fibre of the restriction to $B$, so it is closed. In a flat family the Euler characteristic of a line bundle is locally constant, and on a curve the Euler characteristic of a line bundle determines its degree by Riemann-Roch.

### Examples

**Example P8.1.7 (morphisms from the line to projective space).** Let $d \geq 1$. By Theorem P3.6.21, a morphism $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^n$ with $f^* \mathcal{O}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}(d)$ is given by $n + 1$ binary forms $F_0, \dots, F_n$ of degree $d$ in $s, t$ without common zero on $\mathbb{P}^1$, and two tuples give the same morphism exactly when they differ by a common nonzero scalar, since $\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}) = k$. The tuples form the projective space $\mathbb{P}^M$ with $M = (n + 1)(d + 1) - 1$, and the tuples with a common zero form the image of the closed incidence set $\{ (F, p) : F_0(p) = \dots = F_n(p) = 0 \} \subseteq \mathbb{P}^M \times \mathbb{P}^1$ under the first projection, which is closed by Proposition P3.4.20. So the morphisms of degree $d$ are the points of a nonempty open subset $U_d \subseteq \mathbb{P}^M$, and the component $\operatorname{Hom}_d(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^n)$ of $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^n)$ formed by them is isomorphic to $U_d$ (Deb01, Section 2.1). Its dimension is $(n + 1)(d + 1) - 1 = (n + 1) d + n$. Since $-K_{\mathbb{P}^n} = (n + 1) H$ and $H \cdot f_* \mathbb{P}^1 = d$, this is $-K_{\mathbb{P}^n} \cdot f_* \mathbb{P}^1 + n \chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1})$, the bound of Section 1.2 with equality.

**Example P8.1.8 (automorphisms of the line).** For $n = d = 1$, a pair of linear forms $(a s + b t, c s + d t)$ has a common zero exactly when $a d - b c = 0$. So $\operatorname{Hom}_1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^1)$ is the complement of the quadric $\{ a d - b c = 0 \}$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$, the group $\operatorname{PGL}_2 = \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^1)$, of dimension $3 = -K_{\mathbb{P}^1} \cdot \mathbb{P}^1 + 1$. If $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ is any morphism, composition $\varphi \mapsto f \circ \varphi$ is a morphism $\operatorname{PGL}_2 \to \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, X)$, which is injective on $k$-points when $f$ is birational onto its image. So a nonconstant morphism $\mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ birational onto its image never lies on a component of $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{P}^1, X)$ of dimension less than $3$.

### Proof

*Theorem P8.1.2.* This is Grothendieck's theorem (Gro61), in the form of Kollár, Theorem I.1.4 (Kol96); for $X = \mathbb{P}^N$ it is Theorem P3.13.39. The proof is not given in this handbook. The countability of the components holds because there are countably many polynomials $P \in \mathbb{Q}[m]$ and each $\operatorname{Hilb}^P(X/S)$ is of finite type over the Noetherian scheme $S$.

*Theorem P8.1.4.* The representability and the openness are Kollár, Theorem I.1.10 (Kol96), which rests on Theorem P8.1.2. The part that is formal is the following. Let $f \colon Y_T \to X_T$ be a $T$-morphism. Since $X_T$ is separated over $T$, the graph $(\mathrm{id}, f) \colon Y_T \to Y_T \times_T X_T$ is a closed immersion by Proposition P3.4.10, its image $\Gamma_f$ is isomorphic to $Y_T$ by the first projection $p_1$, so it is flat over $T$, and its fibres have Hilbert polynomials that are locally constant on $T$ by Theorem P3.13.16. The formation of $\Gamma_f$ commutes with base change. Conversely, a closed subscheme $Z \subseteq (Y \times_S X)_T$ is the graph of a morphism exactly when $p_1|_Z \colon Z \to Y_T$ is an isomorphism, and then $Z = \Gamma_f$ with $f = p_2 \circ (p_1|_Z)^{-1}$, which determines $f$. So $f \mapsto \Gamma_f$ is an injective natural transformation $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X) \to \operatorname{Hilb}_{Y \times X / S}$. The cited input is that for the universal family $\mathcal{Z}$ over $\operatorname{Hilb}(Y \times_S X / S)$, flat over the base, the points over which $p_1|_{\mathcal{Z}}$ restricts to an isomorphism of fibres form an open subscheme $H$, and that a morphism $T \to \operatorname{Hilb}(Y \times_S X / S)$ factors through $H$ exactly when $p_1|_{\mathcal{Z}_T}$ is an isomorphism; this uses that $Y$ is flat and proper over $S$ (Kol96, proof of Theorem I.1.10). Then $H$ represents $\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y, X)$. An open subscheme of the projective $S$-scheme $\operatorname{Hilb}^P(Y \times_S X / S)$ is quasi-projective over $S$. Over $S = \operatorname{Spec} k$, a $k$-point is a morphism $Y \to X$, and the identity of $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X)$ corresponds to the universal morphism $\operatorname{ev}$.

*Proposition P8.1.5.* Restriction $f \mapsto f|_{B_T}$ is a natural transformation $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X) \to \operatorname{Hom}(B, X)$ of functors on $k$-schemes, so by Theorem P8.1.4 and the Yoneda lemma it is a morphism $\rho$ of the representing schemes; $B$ is projective and flat over $k$. A morphism $T \to \operatorname{Hom}(Y, X)$, that is a family $f$ over $T$, factors through $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X; g)$ exactly when $\rho \circ f$ is the constant family $g_T$, that is, when $\rho \circ f$ factors through the point $[g] \colon \operatorname{Spec} k \to \operatorname{Hom}(B, X)$. So $\operatorname{Hom}(Y, X; g)$ is represented by $\rho^{-1}([g]) = \operatorname{Hom}(Y, X) \times_{\operatorname{Hom}(B, X)} \operatorname{Spec} k$. A $k$-point of a scheme locally of finite type over $k$ is closed by Proposition P3.7.3, so $\operatorname{Spec} k \to \operatorname{Hom}(B, X)$ is a closed immersion, and its base change $\rho^{-1}([g]) \to \operatorname{Hom}(Y, X)$ is a closed immersion by Proposition P3.3.20.

*Proposition P8.1.6.* The projection $p \colon C \times T \to T$ is projective, $C \times T \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_T$, and $F^* L$ is a line bundle on $C \times T$, flat over $T$ because $C \times T$ is flat over $T$. The fibre of $C \times T$ over a closed point $t$ is $C$, with $F^* L$ restricting to $f_t^* L$. By Theorem P4.5.13, $t \mapsto \chi(C, f_t^* L)$ is locally constant on $T$, so constant because $T$ is connected. By the Riemann-Roch theorem P5.3.10, $\chi(C, f_t^* L) = \deg f_t^* L + 1 - g(C)$, so $\deg f_t^* L$ is constant. Since $L \cdot f_{t*} C = \deg f_t^* L$, the intersection numbers of $f_{t*} C$ with all line bundles are independent of $t$, which is the statement about $N_1(X)$ (Definition P7.2.4). Applied to the evaluation morphism over a connected component of $\operatorname{Hom}(C, X)$, which is locally of finite type over $k$, and to its open subschemes of finite type, the same argument shows that the class of $f_* C$ is constant on each connected component. Connected components of a scheme locally of finite type over $k$ are open, since such a scheme is locally Noetherian.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Hom}_d(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{P}^1)$ has dimension $2d + 1$, and that the morphisms of degree $d$ that fix the three points $0, 1, \infty$ form a set of dimension $2d - 2$.
2. (A) Let $p \neq q$ be points of $\mathbb{P}^n$. Show that the morphisms $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^n$ of degree $1$ with $f(0) = p$ and $f(\infty) = q$ are the maps $[s : t] \mapsto [\lambda s \, p + t \, q]$ for $\lambda \in k^*$, with $p$, $q$ written as vectors, so that they form a copy of $\mathbb{G}_m$.
3. (A) Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve and $X$ a projective variety with an ample line bundle $H$. Using Proposition P8.1.6, show that the constant morphisms form a union of connected components of $\operatorname{Hom}(C, X)$ and that their $k$-points are in bijection with $X(k)$.
4. (B) Let $f \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^n$ have degree $d$. Embed $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^n$ by $\mathcal{O}(1) \boxtimes \mathcal{O}(1)$. Show that the graph $\Gamma_f \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ has Hilbert polynomial $(d + 1) m + 1$. More generally, for $f \colon C \to X$ from a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ and $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$, show that the graph has Hilbert polynomial $(\deg \mathcal{O}_C(1) + \deg f^* \mathcal{O}_X(1)) m + 1 - g$.
5. (B) Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$ be projective and $C$ a smooth projective curve. Show that for each $e$ the morphisms $f \colon C \to X$ with $\deg f^* \mathcal{O}_X(1) = e$ form a quasi-projective open and closed subscheme of $\operatorname{Hom}(C, X)$, using Exercise 4 and Theorem P8.1.4.
6. (C) In Example P8.1.7, show that the complement of $U_d$ in $\mathbb{P}^M$ is irreducible of codimension $n$, by computing the dimension of the incidence set with Theorem P3.7.6 and showing that its projection to $\mathbb{P}^M$ is generically injective.

### Lean

Hilbert schemes, Hom schemes and flat families of closed subschemes are not in the repository. The predicates of `MinimalModel/Statements/Basic.lean`, such as `MinimalModel.IsSmooth` and `MinimalModel.CanonicalDivisor`, concern a single variety and do not involve parameter spaces.

### Notes and history

Grothendieck constructed Hilbert and Quot schemes in his Bourbaki talks of 1960 and 1961, collected in the Fondements de la géométrie algébrique (Gro61), and deduced the Hom scheme from them. Mori's proof of Hartshorne's conjecture and his work on extremal rays brought the Hom scheme into birational geometry, and Kollár's book (Kol96) gives the standard account: Chapter I constructs Hilbert, Chow and Hom schemes, and Chapter II treats their deformation theory. Debarre (Deb01, Chapter 2) gives a shorter account, with the example of morphisms from $\mathbb{P}^1$ to $\mathbb{P}^n$.
