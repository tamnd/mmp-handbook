## The canonical divisor, global sections and positivity

### Statement

Let $\Sigma$ be a fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$ and $X_\Sigma$ its toric variety. For a Cartier divisor $D = \sum_\rho a_\rho D_\rho$ with Cartier data $(m_\sigma)$, $\varphi_D$ denotes its support function (Definition P8.7.9) and $P_D$ its polyhedron (Definition P8.7.1). Write $\Sigma(n)$ for the set of cones of dimension $n$.

**Theorem P8.7.17 (the canonical divisor).** Let $m_1, \dots, m_n$ be a basis of $M$ and
$$\omega = \frac{d\chi^{m_1}}{\chi^{m_1}} \wedge \dots \wedge \frac{d\chi^{m_n}}{\chi^{m_n}},$$
a rational $n$-form on $X_\Sigma$. Then $\omega$ depends on the basis only up to sign, and
$$K_{X_\Sigma} = \operatorname{div}(\omega) = -\sum_{\rho \in \Sigma(1)} D_\rho .$$

Lean: `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.canonical` is the divisor $-\sum_\rho D_\rho$, with coefficients computed in `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.canonical_apply`; the theorem identifying it with the canonical divisor of the variety is not in Lean as stated.

**Definition P8.7.18 (convexity).** Assume $|\Sigma|$ is convex. A support function $\varphi$ of $\Sigma$ is *convex* if
$$\varphi(t u + (1 - t) v) \geq t \varphi(u) + (1 - t) \varphi(v) \quad \text{for } u, v \in |\Sigma|, \ 0 \leq t \leq 1 .$$
It is *strictly convex* if in addition, for each maximal cone $\sigma$, the linear function $\langle m_\sigma, \cdot \rangle$ that agrees with $\varphi$ on $\sigma$ satisfies $\varphi(u) < \langle m_\sigma, u \rangle$ for $u \in |\Sigma| \setminus \sigma$.

This is the convention of Cox, Little and Schenck. In the usual language of convex analysis such a function is concave, and the sign is forced by the sign in $\varphi_D(u_\rho) = -a_\rho$.

**Lemma P8.7.19 (the polytope of a divisor).** If $\Sigma$ is complete, then every cone of $\Sigma$ is a face of a cone in $\Sigma(n)$, and for every torus-invariant divisor $D$ the polyhedron $P_D$ is bounded, so $\Gamma(X_\Sigma, \mathcal{O}(D))$ is finite-dimensional, with basis the $\chi^m$ for $m \in P_D \cap M$.

Lean: not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.7.20 (global generation).** Let $\Sigma$ be complete and $D$ a torus-invariant Cartier divisor, with Cartier data $m_\sigma$ for $\sigma \in \Sigma(n)$. The following are equivalent.

1. $\mathcal{O}(D)$ is globally generated.
2. $m_\sigma \in P_D$ for all $\sigma \in \Sigma(n)$.
3. $\varphi_D(u) = \min_{\sigma \in \Sigma(n)} \langle m_\sigma, u \rangle$ for all $u \in N_\mathbb{R}$.
4. $\varphi_D$ is convex.

In that case $P_D$ is the convex hull of the $m_\sigma$, $\sigma \in \Sigma(n)$.

Lean: condition 4, for real coefficients, is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsNef`; the equivalence with global generation is not in Lean as stated.

**Theorem P8.7.21 (ampleness).** Let $\Sigma$ be complete and $D$ a torus-invariant Cartier divisor. Then $D$ is ample if and only if $\varphi_D$ is strictly convex. In that case $X_\Sigma$ is projective, $kD$ is very ample for $k \geq n - 1$, and if $X_\Sigma$ is smooth then $D$ itself is very ample.

Lean: strict convexity on a complete fan is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsAmple`, and a complete fan carrying such a divisor is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsProjectiveFan`; the theorem is not in Lean as stated.

### Proof in words

The form $\omega$ is the invariant volume form of the torus. Along each $D_\rho$ one of the characters in an adapted basis is a coordinate $x$ vanishing on $D_\rho$, and $\omega$ contains $dx / x$, so it has a simple pole there and no other zeros or poles. For global generation, on $U_\sigma$ the sheaf $\mathcal{O}(D)$ is generated by $\chi^{m_\sigma}$, and a global section, which is a combination of characters $\chi^m$ with $m \in P_D$, generates at the fixed point $\gamma_\sigma$ only if $\chi^{m_\sigma}$ occurs in it. The base locus is closed and stable under the torus, so if it is nonempty it contains a fixed point. Condition 2 says that each linear piece $\langle m_\sigma, \cdot \rangle$ lies above $\varphi_D$ on all rays, hence everywhere, which is the statement that $\varphi_D$ is the minimum of its linear pieces, and a minimum of linear functions is convex in the sense above. Ampleness is the strict version, and its proof needs the projective embedding by the lattice points of $P_D$, which is only cited here.

### Examples

**Example P8.7.22 (projective space).** For $D = d D_0$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$, Cartier data is $m_{\sigma_0} = 0$ on $\sigma_0 = \operatorname{Cone}(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ and $m_{\sigma_i} = d e_i^*$ on $\sigma_i = \operatorname{Cone}(e_j : j \neq i)$ for $i \geq 1$, since $\langle d e_i^*, e_j \rangle = 0$ for $j \neq 0, i$ and $\langle d e_i^*, e_0 \rangle = -d$. These are the vertices of the simplex $P_D = d \Delta_n$ of Example P8.7.5 when $d \geq 0$. So $\mathcal{O}(d)$ is globally generated exactly when $d \geq 0$, and the $m_\sigma$ are distinct, which makes $\varphi_D$ strictly convex, exactly when $d > 0$; this is the ampleness of $\mathcal{O}(1)$. The anticanonical divisor $-K = \sum_{i = 0}^n D_i \sim (n + 1) D_0$ is ample, so $\mathbb{P}^n$ is a Fano variety.

**Example P8.7.23 (nef and ample divisors on the Hirzebruch surfaces).** For $D = b D_1 + a D_2 \sim a C_- + b F$ on $F_n$, Example P8.7.15 gives the Cartier data, and
$$P_D = \{ (x, y) : x \geq -b, \ y \geq -a, \ -x + n y \geq 0, \ y \leq 0 \}.$$
Condition 2 of Theorem P8.7.20 reads: $m_{34} = (0, 0) \in P_D$ if and only if $a, b \geq 0$; $m_{41} = (-b, 0) \in P_D$ if and only if $a, b \geq 0$; $m_{23} = (-na, -a) \in P_D$ if and only if $b \geq na$ and $a \geq 0$; and $m_{12} = (-b, -a) \in P_D$ if and only if $b \geq na$ and $a \geq 0$. So $aC_- + bF$ is globally generated exactly when $a \geq 0$ and $b \geq na$, and these classes form the cone spanned by $F$ and $C_- + nF \sim C_+$. For a complete fan, $\varphi_D$ is strictly convex exactly when $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle > -a_\rho$ for every $\sigma \in \Sigma(n)$ and every ray $\rho$ not in $\sigma$ (Cox, Little and Schenck, Lemma 6.1.13, or Exercise 6 of Section 8.3). Here these strict inequalities are $a, b > 0$ at $m_{34}$, $a, b > 0$ at $m_{41}$, $b > na$ and $a > 0$ at $m_{23}$, and $b > na$ and $a > 0$ at $m_{12}$, so $D$ is ample exactly when $a > 0$ and $b > na$. This agrees with the nef and ample cones of Example P5.12.8, and, for $n = 0$, with $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$.

**Example P8.7.24 (the anticanonical divisor of $F_n$).** By Theorem P8.7.17 and Example P8.7.6,
$$-K_{F_n} = D_1 + D_2 + D_3 + D_4 \sim F + C_- + F + (C_- + nF) = 2 C_- + (n + 2) F .$$
By Example P8.7.23 with $a = 2$, $b = n + 2$, the divisor $-K_{F_n}$ is ample if and only if $n + 2 > 2n$, that is $n \leq 1$, and globally generated if and only if $n \leq 2$. So $F_0 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $F_1$, the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at a point, are Fano, while on $F_2$ the anticanonical divisor is nef but not ample, as $-K_{F_2} \cdot C_- = 0$.

### Proof

*Theorem P8.7.17.* The map $m \mapsto d\chi^m / \chi^m$ is a homomorphism from $M$ to the rational $1$-forms, since $d(\chi^m \chi^{m'}) / (\chi^m \chi^{m'}) = d\chi^m / \chi^m + d\chi^{m'} / \chi^{m'}$. So for another basis $m_i' = \sum_j g_{ij} m_j$, the wedge product changes by $\det(g_{ij}) = \pm 1$. On $T_N = \operatorname{Spec} k[t_1^{\pm 1}, \dots, t_n^{\pm 1}]$ with $t_i = \chi^{m_i}$, $\omega = (t_1 \cdots t_n)^{-1} dt_1 \wedge \dots \wedge dt_n$, a generator of $\Omega^n_{T_N}$, so $\operatorname{div}(\omega)$ is supported on $\bigcup_\rho D_\rho$. For a ray $\rho$ choose, as in the proof of Lemma P8.7.2, a basis $m_1', \dots, m_n'$ of $M$ with $U_\rho = \operatorname{Spec} k[x, y_2^{\pm 1}, \dots, y_n^{\pm 1}]$, $x = \chi^{m_1'}$, $y_i = \chi^{m_i'}$. Then $U_\rho$ is smooth, $\Omega^n_{U_\rho}$ is generated by $dx \wedge dy_2 \wedge \dots \wedge dy_n$, and
$$\omega = \pm \frac{1}{x y_2 \cdots y_n} \, dx \wedge dy_2 \wedge \dots \wedge dy_n,$$
which has order $-1$ along $D_\rho = V(x)$. The smooth locus of $X_\Sigma$ contains $T_N \cup \bigcup_\rho U_\rho$, so Proposition P3.12.29 gives $K_{X_\Sigma} = \operatorname{div}(\omega) = -\sum_\rho D_\rho$.

*Lemma P8.7.19.* Let $\tau \in \Sigma$. As in the proof of Theorem P8.6.28, a neighbourhood of $u_\tau$ is covered by the cones of $\Sigma$ containing $u_\tau$. Cones of dimension less than $n$ lie in hyperplanes and a finite union of them has empty interior, so one of these cones, $\sigma$, has dimension $n$, and $\tau \preceq \sigma$ by Lemma P8.6.23 (3). For the boundedness, the recession cone of $P_D$ is $\{ m : \langle m, u_\rho \rangle \geq 0 \text{ for all } \rho \}$. Each $u \in N_\mathbb{R}$ lies in a cone of $\Sigma$, so it is a nonnegative combination of ray generators, and a form in the recession cone is nonnegative on all of $N_\mathbb{R}$, hence zero. If $P_D$ were unbounded, there would be $m_k \in P_D$ with $|m_k| \to \infty$, and a limit point $m$ of $m_k / |m_k|$ would satisfy $|m| = 1$ and $\langle m, u_\rho \rangle \geq \lim -a_\rho / |m_k| = 0$ for all $\rho$, so $m$ would be a nonzero element of the recession cone. So $P_D$ is bounded, $P_D \cap M$ is finite and Proposition P8.7.3 gives the rest.

*Theorem P8.7.20.* (1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2). By Theorem P8.7.10, $\mathcal{O}(D)|_{U_\sigma} = \chi^{m_\sigma} \mathcal{O}_{U_\sigma}$. A global section is $f = \sum_{m \in P_D} c_m \chi^m$, and $f / \chi^{m_\sigma} = \sum_m c_m \chi^{m - m_\sigma}$ is a regular function on $U_\sigma$, so each $m - m_\sigma$ lies in $S_\sigma$. For $\sigma \in \Sigma(n)$, the point $\gamma_\sigma$ is the homomorphism $S_\sigma \to k$ that vanishes on $S_\sigma \setminus \{0\}$, so the value of $f / \chi^{m_\sigma}$ at $\gamma_\sigma$ is $c_{m_\sigma}$, which is zero unless $m_\sigma \in P_D$. So $\mathcal{O}(D)$ is generated by global sections at $\gamma_\sigma$ if and only if $m_\sigma \in P_D$, and (1) implies (2). Conversely, the base locus of $\mathcal{O}(D)$ is the intersection of the zero loci of the sections $\chi^m$, $m \in P_D \cap M$, and the zero locus of $\chi^m$ is the support of $\operatorname{div}(\chi^m) + D$, a union of divisors $D_\rho$. So the base locus is closed and stable under $T_N$. If it is nonempty, it contains a point of some orbit $O(\tau)$, hence $O(\tau)$ and its closure $V(\tau)$. By Lemma P8.7.19, $\tau$ is a face of some $\sigma \in \Sigma(n)$, and $\gamma_\sigma \in V(\tau)$ by Theorem P8.6.25. Under (2), $\gamma_\sigma$ is not a base point, so the base locus is empty.

(2) $\Leftrightarrow$ (3). For $u$ in a cone $\sigma'$, $\varphi_D(u) = \langle m_{\sigma'}, u \rangle$, so the minimum in (3) is at most $\varphi_D(u)$, and (3) says $\langle m_\sigma, u \rangle \geq \varphi_D(u)$ for all $\sigma$ and $u$. Since $\varphi_D(u_\rho) = -a_\rho$, this implies (2). Conversely, under (2), $\langle m_\sigma, u_\rho \rangle \geq -a_\rho = \langle m_{\sigma'}, u_\rho \rangle$ for every ray $\rho$ of $\sigma'$, and both sides are linear on $\sigma'$, which is generated by its rays, so $\langle m_\sigma, u \rangle \geq \varphi_D(u)$ on $\sigma'$. As $\Sigma$ is complete, this holds on $N_\mathbb{R}$.

(3) $\Rightarrow$ (4). A minimum of linear functions satisfies the inequality of Definition P8.7.18. (4) $\Rightarrow$ (3). Let $\sigma \in \Sigma(n)$, $\ell = \langle m_\sigma, \cdot \rangle$, $u_0$ an interior point of $\sigma$, and $u \in N_\mathbb{R}$. The function $g(t) = \varphi_D(u_0 + t(u - u_0))$ on $[0, 1]$ satisfies the convexity inequality and agrees with the linear function $h(t) = \ell(u_0 + t(u - u_0))$ on $[0, \varepsilon]$ for some $\varepsilon > 0$. For $\varepsilon < t \leq 1$, the inequality for the point $\varepsilon$ between $0$ and $t$ gives
$$h(\varepsilon) = g(\varepsilon) \geq \Big( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon}{t} \Big) g(0) + \frac{\varepsilon}{t} g(t) = \Big( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon}{t} \Big) h(0) + \frac{\varepsilon}{t} g(t),$$
and since $h$ is linear, $h(\varepsilon) = (1 - \varepsilon/t) h(0) + (\varepsilon / t) h(t)$, so $g(t) \leq h(t)$. At $t = 1$ this is $\varphi_D(u) \leq \langle m_\sigma, u \rangle$, which is (3).

Finally, under (2) the convex hull $Q$ of the $m_\sigma$ lies in $P_D$. If $m \in P_D \setminus Q$, the separation theorem for a point and a compact convex set gives $u \in N_\mathbb{R}$ with $\langle m, u \rangle < \langle m_\sigma, u \rangle$ for all $\sigma$. Let $u \in \sigma'$; then $\langle m, u \rangle < \langle m_{\sigma'}, u \rangle = \varphi_D(u)$. But $m \in P_D$ means $\langle m, u_\rho \rangle \geq \varphi_D(u_\rho)$ for the rays of $\sigma'$, hence $\langle m, u \rangle \geq \varphi_D(u)$ on $\sigma'$, a contradiction.

*Theorem P8.7.21.* This is Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 6.1.14 and Theorem 6.1.15 together with Theorem 2.2.12 for the very ampleness of $(n-1)D$, and Fulton, Section 3.4, for the smooth case; the proof is not given here. The direction from ample to strictly convex follows from the methods of this chapter and the next: if $D$ is ample, some multiple $kD$ is very ample (Theorem P3.6.25), hence globally generated, so $\varphi_{D}$ is convex by Theorem P8.7.20; and for a simplicial fan, if the inequality of Definition P8.7.18 were an equality across some wall, the curve of that wall would have degree $0$ against $D$ by the intersection formula of Section 8.2, which contradicts ampleness.

### Exercises

1. (A) Draw the polytope $P_{-K}$ for $\mathbb{P}^2$, $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $F_1$, and count its lattice points to compute $h^0(-K)$.
2. (A) Show that on $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ the divisor $a D_1 + b D_2$, with $u_1 = e_1$ and $u_2 = e_2$, is globally generated exactly when $a, b \geq 0$, and ample exactly when $a, b > 0$.
3. (B) Show that the canonical divisor of the weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1, 1, n)$ is $-(n + 2) D_1$ in $\operatorname{Cl} = \mathbb{Z}$, and that $-K$ is ample in the sense that a multiple is an ample Cartier divisor.
4. (B) Let $\Sigma$ be complete and $D$ globally generated. Show that the morphism to $\mathbb{P}^{|P_D \cap M| - 1}$ given by the sections $\chi^m$ contracts the curve $V(\tau)$ of a wall $\tau = \sigma \cap \sigma'$ to a point exactly when $m_\sigma = m_{\sigma'}$.
5. (B) Show that for $\Sigma$ complete, a torus-invariant Cartier divisor $D$ with $P_D = \emptyset$ has $h^0(\mathcal{O}(D)) = 0$, and that $P_{D + \operatorname{div}(\chi^m)} = P_D - m$.
6. (C) Let $\Sigma$ be the fan in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with rays $e_1, e_2, e_3, -e_1 - e_2 - e_3$ and a fifth ray $e_1 + e_2$, subdividing two maximal cones of the fan of $\mathbb{P}^3$. Show that the resulting toric variety is the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^3$ along the line $V(\operatorname{Cone}(e_1, e_2))$, and compute its nef cone from Theorem P8.7.20.

### Lean

The canonical divisor $-\sum_\rho D_\rho$ is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.canonical`, with `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.canonical_apply` stating that each coefficient is $-1$; `MinimalModel.Toric.P1.coeffSum_canonical` computes its degree for $\mathbb{P}^1$. Convexity of a support function, in the sign convention of Definition P8.7.18, is `ConcaveOn` in Mathlib, and `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsNef` asks for a convex support function of a divisor with real coefficients. Strict convexity is `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsAmple`, and `MinimalModel.Toric.Fan.IsProjectiveFan` is a complete fan with an $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor that is strictly convex. Theorems P8.7.17, P8.7.20 and P8.7.21 are not in Lean, since their statements involve the scheme $X_\Sigma$.

### Notes and history

The formula $K = -\sum D_\rho$ is in Demazure's paper and in Danilov's survey, and is Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 8.2.3. The characterization of globally generated and ample line bundles by convexity of the support function is due to Demazure for smooth complete fans and to Kempf, Knudsen, Mumford and Saint-Donat in general; Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorems 6.1.7 and 6.1.14, and Fulton, Section 3.4, give the proofs. On a complete toric variety a Cartier divisor is nef if and only if it is globally generated, so the toric nef cone is the cone of convex support functions; this is a consequence of the toric cone theorem of Section 8.3 together with Theorem P8.7.20.
