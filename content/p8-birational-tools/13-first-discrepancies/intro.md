# First discrepancies

The singularities allowed in the minimal model program are defined by comparing the canonical divisor of a variety with the canonical divisors of its models. For every divisor $F$ over $X$ the discrepancy $a(F, X)$ is the coefficient of $F$ in $K_Y - g^* K_X$, and the classes terminal, canonical, klt and log canonical are defined by lower bounds on these numbers. This chapter introduces the definitions and computes them in the examples of Chapters 9 to 12.

Section 13.1 defines divisors over $X$ and their discrepancies, proves that a discrepancy depends only on the valuation, proves the transitivity formula for a tower of models, and shows that over a smooth variety every exceptional divisor has discrepancy at least $1$, with $c - 1$ for the blow-up of a smooth centre of codimension $c$. It defines the four classes and proves that on a resolution with simple normal crossings it suffices to check the exceptional divisors of the resolution. The examples are the quadric cone, the cones $\frac{1}{n}(1, 1)$, the cones over plane curves and the terminal singularity $\frac{1}{2}(1, 1, 1)$.

Section 13.2 treats surfaces. On the minimal resolution the discrepancies solve a linear system with the intersection matrix and are nonpositive, so singular points of surfaces are never terminal. The main theorem is that a surface singularity is canonical if and only if it is Du Val. For toric surfaces the discrepancy of the divisor of a ray $v$ is $\langle m_\sigma, v \rangle - 1$, and it follows that cyclic quotient singularities are klt.

The chapter follows Kollár and Mori, Sections 2.3 and 4.1 (KM98), and Reid's Young Person's Guide (Rei87).
