# Resolution of curves and surfaces

A resolution of a variety $X$ is a proper birational morphism $Y \to X$ from a smooth variety. In dimension one the normalization is a resolution, and it is the only one. In dimension two a resolution exists, but it is not unique: any blow-up of a point of a resolution is again a resolution, and the minimal resolution of Volume 2 is the one through which all others factor. This chapter proves both facts in the generality the program uses, together with the embedded version for curves on a smooth surface. For a curve on a smooth surface the embedded version is a sequence of point blow-ups after which the total transform has simple normal crossings.

Section 4.1 proves that the normalization of an integral curve over a perfect field is a resolution and that every resolution of the curve is isomorphic to it. The examples are the node, the cusp and the three coordinate axes in $\mathbb{A}^3$. Over a field that is not perfect, a regular curve can fail to be smooth, and then it has no resolution at all.

Section 4.2 takes the embedded resolution of curves on a smooth projective surface from Section 6.2 of Volume P5 and proves what that section does not: the numerical data of a sequence of point blow-ups (the multiplicities of the exceptional curves in the total transform and in the relative canonical divisor), and the existence of a minimal log resolution through which every log resolution factors. The worked examples are the cusp $y^2 = x^3$, which needs three blow-ups with multiplicities $2$, $3$, $6$ and discrepancies $1$, $2$, $4$ and gives the log canonical threshold $5/6$ of Volume 4, then the node and the tacnode $y^2 = x^4$.

Section 4.3 is on surfaces. It states the theorem of Walker, Zariski, Abhyankar and Lipman. It then proves resolution for normal projective surfaces in characteristic $0$ by Jung's method: project to the plane, take a log resolution of the branch curve, normalize, recognize the singularities that remain as cyclic quotient singularities by Abhyankar's lemma, and resolve these by the toric method of Chapter 8. It proves that the minimal resolution is an isomorphism over the smooth locus and that every curve on a normal surface has a log resolution. The examples run Jung's method on the $A_2$ singularity $z^2 = y^2 - x^3$ and on the quadric cone.

The chapter follows Kollár's *Lectures on resolution of singularities*, Chapters 1 and 2 (Kol07), Hartshorne, Section V.3 (Har77), and Cutkosky's *Resolution of singularities* (Cut04). The minimal resolution of a normal surface is Definition 2.3.7 and Theorem 2.3.8 of Volume 2, and is used here, not proved again.
